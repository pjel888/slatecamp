**Date:** April 2026
**Purpose:** How the Calculator Suite makes money and feeds SlateCamp's growth engine

## The Market Opportunity

The Philippine hardware and building materials market generated approximately $15.3 billion in revenue in 2025 and is growing at roughly 13% annually through 2029. The broader construction market sits at $45.5 billion, with residential capturing 42% of that value. Major retail players — Wilcon Depot, AllHome, CW Home Depot, Ace Hardware, Handyman — compete aggressively for the architect and contractor purchasing decision, yet none of them offer online estimation tools.

The only notable calculator in the Philippine market is Boysen's paint estimator (web + app), which Pacific Paint built specifically to influence the purchasing moment — help architects and homeowners compute how much Boysen paint to buy, then recommend exact Boysen products. It works because the calculator output IS the shopping list.

That's the entire competitive landscape: one paint brand, one 2D calculator, zero 3D visualization. SlateCamp's Calculator Suite would be the first interactive 3D material estimation tool in the Philippine construction market.

## Five Revenue Streams

The Calculator Suite is not a single revenue model — it's a platform that generates money five different ways, each reinforcing the others.

### Stream 1: Brand Sponsorship ("Powered by")

This is the primary near-term revenue source. Each calculator naturally maps to one product category, and construction material brands have strong motivation to sponsor estimation tools because the calculator output directly drives purchasing.

**Why brands pay for this:**
The most powerful marketing moment in construction materials is when the architect or contractor decides HOW MUCH to buy. A calculator that says "You need 47 boxes of 60×60 porcelain tile" is functionally a purchase order — and the brand that appears next to that recommendation captures the sale. Boysen already proves this model works in the Philippines: their calculator recommends specific Boysen products and quantities, effectively converting estimation into sales.

Interactive calculators convert at approximately 5x the rate of simple contact forms because users exchange personal data (email) for personalized, high-value results. That makes calculator-generated leads far more qualified than typical ad traffic.

**Sponsor integration design:**
- Sponsor's product data prefills the calculator (tile sizes, coverage rates, can sizes)
- Results page shows a "Recommended Products" section with sponsor SKUs matching the estimate
- "Buy at [Sponsor]" button links to sponsor's e-commerce or nearest store locator
- Sponsor logo appears as "Powered by [Brand]" — never inside the calculation trail
- All sponsor values remain user-editable to preserve tool credibility
- Monthly analytics report sent to sponsor: impressions, calculations completed, product recommendations viewed, outbound clicks

**Pricing tiers:**

| Tier | Scope | Monthly price | What sponsor gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tool Sponsor | 1 calculator | ₱15,000–₱30,000 | Logo, product data, "Powered by," analytics, outbound links |
| Category Sponsor | 1 phase (2–3 tools) | ₱40,000–₱80,000 | All Tool benefits + featured placement across phase, co-marketing rights, lead sharing |
| Suite Sponsor | All 11 tools | ₱100,000–₱200,000 | Exclusive across suite, white-label option, quarterly business reviews, first-party lead data |

**Conservative Year 1 projection (3 Phase 1 tools launched):**
- 3 Tool Sponsors at ₱20,000/month average = ₱60,000/month = ₱720,000/year
- If upgraded to Category: ₱60,000/month = ₱720,000/year
- Total sponsor revenue target Year 1: ₱720,000–₱960,000

**Year 2 projection (8 tools live):**
- 5 Tool Sponsors + 1 Category Sponsor = ₱160,000–₱200,000/month = ₱1.92M–₱2.4M/year

### Stream 2: ALE Exam Funnel (Lead Generation for SlateCamp Pro)

Every calculator page is a top-of-funnel acquisition channel for SlateCamp's paid ALE prep product. The connection is natural — material estimation IS tested in the Architecture Licensure Examination (Building Technology & Utilities subject, ADSP design scenarios).

**The funnel:**
```
Free calculator traffic (SEO, social, sponsor co-marketing)
  → User completes estimation
    → Soft CTA: "This calculation is tested in the ALE.
       SlateCamp helps you master it."
      → Email capture on Save/Export (see Stream 3)
        → Nurture sequence: 
           Email 1: "Here's your estimate PDF" (value delivery)
           Email 2: "3 ALE questions about [topic]" (education)
           Email 3: "Your Readiness Score — take the diagnostic" (activation)
           Email 4: "SlateCamp Pro — master every estimation topic" (conversion)
```

**Why this works:**
The calculator demonstrates competence. A student who uses the tile calculator and sees accurate estimation logic thinks: "If SlateCamp's free tools are this good, what does their paid platform look like?" The calculator is the proof that SlateCamp knows the material — and knowing the material is exactly what the ALE tests.

**Conversion modeling:**
- Target: 10,000 monthly calculator users (achievable with SEO + sponsor co-marketing)
- Email capture rate: 15% (users who save/export) = 1,500 emails/month
- Nurture-to-diagnostic rate: 20% = 300 diagnostic completions/month
- Diagnostic-to-Pro conversion: 10% = 30 new Pro subscribers/month
- Pro Monthly at ₱399 or Exam Pass at ₱1,999
- Monthly revenue from funnel: ₱12,000–₱60,000
- Annual funnel revenue: ₱144,000–₱720,000

This is conservative. The real value is that these leads are pre-qualified — they're already architecture students or practicing architects who are actively doing estimation work. They're the exact SlateCamp target customer.

### Stream 3: Email Capture + Data Monetization (Ethical)

The calculator's most valuable business asset isn't the estimate — it's the data about who is estimating what, and when.

**Email capture triggers (value exchange):**

| Trigger | What user gets | What SlateCamp gets |
|---|---|---|
| Save project | Estimate saved to account, retrievable later | Email, project type, material preferences |
| Export to PDF BOQ | Formatted PDF bill of quantities | Email, estimate details, sponsor product exposure |
| Compare estimates | Side-by-side comparison of different scenarios | Usage patterns, material preference data |
| Get price estimate | Approximate material cost based on current prices | Email, budget range, purchase intent signal |

**Why sponsors pay more for this data:**
A construction brand knowing that 500 architects in Metro Manila estimated tile quantities for 60×60 porcelain in the last 30 days is actionable market intelligence. It tells them demand trends, preferred sizes, seasonal patterns, and geographic concentration — information they currently get only from distributor sell-through reports, months after the fact.

**Data product (Phase 2, anonymized + aggregated):**
- Monthly "Calculator Insights" report to sponsors: estimation volume by material type, most-used tile sizes, average room dimensions, geographic distribution
- This is anonymized, aggregated data — no individual user data shared without consent
- Pricing: included in Category/Suite sponsor tiers, or sold separately at ₱10,000–₱25,000/quarter

**Why freemium works here:**
Rectangular rooms with 2 openings covers most simple estimation needs. But real architectural projects have L-shapes, multiple doors and windows, and require PDF documentation. The jump from "quick estimate" to "professional tool" is the natural freemium gate.

**Revenue modeling:**
- 10,000 monthly users × 3% premium conversion = 300 premium subscribers

## Competitive Moat

### Why this is defensible

1. **3D visualization is hard to copy.** Building interactive 3D calculators in R3F requires specialized frontend engineering. Neither hardware retailers nor construction brands have this capability in-house. The closest competitor (Boysen's 2D paint calculator) took years to build and is still a simple form.

2. **Sponsor relationships are exclusive.** Once Mariwasa sponsors the tile calculator, their competitor (Niro Granite, FCB) can't easily launch a competing 3D tool — they'd need to build from scratch or find another platform. First-mover lock-in.

3. **Data compounds over time.** Every calculation trains the system on Filipino construction patterns — preferred tile sizes, typical room dimensions, common paint systems. This data becomes the foundation for smarter defaults, better recommendations, and market intelligence products.

4. **ALE exam integration is unique.** No other estimation tool connects to Licensure Exam preparation. The "learn this for the exam AND use it at work" positioning is something only SlateCamp can do.

5. **Network effects through sponsors.** Each sponsor co-markets the calculator to their own audience (architects, contractors, homeowners), driving traffic that benefits all other calculators in the suite. More tools = more sponsors = more traffic = more tools.

| Boysen builds a 3D paint calculator | Boysen is a paint company, not a tech company. Their calculator took years to build in 2D. SlateCamp's engineering advantage is the moat. |
| Students use free tools but don't convert to Pro | The freemium gate (L-shapes, export, save) ensures professionals need Pro. Students who use calculators during school become Pro users before the exam. |

## Decision Framework

When evaluating any Calculator Suite feature or business decision, apply these four tests:

1. **Does it drive a calculation to completion?** More completed calculations = more sponsor impressions, more email captures, more ALE funnel entries.

2. **Does it make the sponsor's product the obvious choice?** The sponsor pays because the calculator output becomes a shopping list. If the feature doesn't help users decide what to buy, it's not sponsor-valuable.

3. **Does it create a reason to come back?** Saved projects, comparison history, and price updates create return visits. One-time-use calculators have low lifetime value.

4. **Does it connect to the ALE exam?** Every feature that bridges "professional tool" and "exam preparation" strengthens SlateCamp's unique position and drives Pro conversions.
