The main Philippine references on stair dimensions are: PD 1096 (National Building Code), its guidelines, BP 220 (socialized/housing standards), BP 344 (Accessibility Law), and the Fire Code/compendia that tabulate them together. [scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/626143472/PD1096-1)

Below is a concise synthesis focused on riser/tread and related key dimensions.

## PD 1096 (National Building Code)

From PD 1096 guidelines/tables used in practice: [scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/559529819/Pd-1096-Guidelines-Table)

- Minimum stair clear width (general): **750 mm** (for small private/use ≤10 persons; wider for higher occupant loads as per tabulations). [studylib](https://studylib.net/doc/26212637/tabulation-of-design-standards)
- Maximum riser height: **200 mm**. [scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/559529819/Pd-1096-Guidelines-Table)
- Minimum tread depth: **200 mm** (measured exclusive of nosing). [scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/559529819/Pd-1096-Guidelines-Table)
- Headroom over stairs: specified as a minimum clear height (commonly 2.0 m or more in tabulated guidelines, though exact value should be checked on the official NBCP text used for permitting in your LGU). [scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/626143472/PD1096-1)

These NBCP values are the default baseline for typical residential and most building types unless a more specific law (housing, accessibility, fire) is stricter for your case. [fr.scribd](https://fr.scribd.com/document/657405626/PD1096-BP344-RA9514)

## BP 220 (Housing / Socialized Housing)

BP 220 and its standards for interior stairs in economic/socialized housing: [scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/559583005/standards-list-bp220)

- Minimum clear stair width (interior stairs): **0.60 m**. [scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/559583005/standards-list-bp220)
- Maximum riser height: **0.25 m**. [scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/559583005/standards-list-bp220)
- Minimum tread width: **0.20 m** (exclusive of nosing and other projections). [scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/559583005/standards-list-bp220)
- Headroom clearance: provided in the same standard set (commonly not less than about 2.0 m; check the BP 220 standard file or HLURB/DHSUD manual for the exact figure your reviewer uses). [scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/559583005/standards-list-bp220)

When designing mass housing projects under BP 220, these can be more permissive (e.g., narrower stairs) than PD 1096, but you must follow the specific standard that your housing permit and developer accreditation require. [fr.scribd](https://fr.scribd.com/document/657405626/PD1096-BP344-RA9514)

## BP 344 (Accessibility Law)

BP 344 focuses more on accessibility features (widths, landings, handrails) than on specific riser/tread limits, but it effectively imposes stricter minimum widths and handrail rules on stairs along accessible routes: [uapmakati.files.wordpress](https://uapmakati.files.wordpress.com/2007/07/bp344-comments-on-bp-344-irr-amendments-complete-part-1-july-10-2013.pdf)

Key stair-related points:

- Accessible stairs clear width: at least **1.20 m** is commonly required for accessible routes (aligned with corridor width and accessible stairway provisions in BP 344 checklists/IRR). [scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/890867591/BP344-Questionnaire)
- Landings for accessible stairs: minimum **1.50 m** length/clear dimension at landings is used in many BP 344 guides (mirroring ramp landing dimensions). [scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/890867591/BP344-Questionnaire)
- Handrails:
  - Two levels: **700 mm** and **900 mm** above the stair nosings. [youtube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3cyK1WRRa7A)
  - Extensions: handrails to extend **300 mm** beyond top and bottom risers. [uapmakati.files.wordpress](https://uapmakati.files.wordpress.com/2007/07/bp344-comments-on-bp-344-irr-amendments-complete-part-1-july-10-2013.pdf)
  - Continuous along full length of stairs. [youtube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3cyK1WRRa7A)

BP 344 does not usually override riser/tread numbers from PD 1096, but any stair that forms part of an accessible route must also meet these width, landing, and handrail rules. [uapmakati.files.wordpress](https://uapmakati.files.wordpress.com/2007/07/bp344-comments-on-bp-344-irr-amendments-complete-part-1-july-10-2013.pdf)

## Consolidated guideline (NBCP, BP 344, Fire Code)

Code-compilation tables used in architectural/civil review notes summarize minimum stair widths and riser/tread with occupancy-based stair widths from NBCP and Fire Code, and accessibility adjustments: [studylib](https://studylib.net/doc/26212637/tabulation-of-design-standards)

Typical consolidated figures (verify against your latest review material):

- Minimum stair width:
  - **750 mm** for occupant load **≤10** (private stairs, small units). [studylib](https://studylib.net/doc/26212637/tabulation-of-design-standards)
  - **900 mm** for occupant loads above this range, especially 10–50 and above (Fire Code/NBCP exit stairs). [youtube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbgUPN-3d-8)
  - **1.20 m** or more for accessible stairs and main accessible routes per BP 344. [scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/890867591/BP344-Questionnaire)
- Maximum riser (NBCP baseline): **200 mm**. [fr.scribd](https://fr.scribd.com/document/657405626/PD1096-BP344-RA9514)
- Minimum tread (NBCP baseline): **200 mm**. [scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/559529819/Pd-1096-Guidelines-Table)

These tables are what many reviewers and board exam references use when cross‑checking compliance across PD 1096, BP 344, and the Fire Code. [fr.scribd](https://fr.scribd.com/document/657405626/PD1096-BP344-RA9514)
