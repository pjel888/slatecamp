Section 02
Research Foundations
What the world's best EdTech platforms got right — and how the science of learning, behavioral psychology, and habit formation applies directly to SlateCamp's design decisions.

What Duolingo Got Right (and How We Apply It)

Duolingo achieved a 37% DAU/MAU ratio — one in three monthly users showing up daily. This wasn't luck. It was behavioral engineering. Here's what they built and how SlateCamp adapts each principle:

🔥
Streaks as Loss AversionP1 NOW
Duolingo users who maintain a 7-day streak are 3.6× more likely to stay engaged long-term. The Streak Freeze feature reduced churn 21% for at-risk users. The psychology: people are more motivated to avoid losing progress than to gain rewards.
Loss Aversion · Kahneman & Tversky, 1979
SlateCamp Adaptation:
Study streak = consecutive Camp Trail days completed. Show streak on dashboard. One "Grace Day" per week (free), additional grace days are Pro. Streak is never shown as a number-chase — framed as "consistency building" to match our coach voice.
⚡
Variable Reward LoopsP2
Duolingo's XP leaderboards drove 40% more engagement. The variability — you never know exactly where you'll land — keeps users curious and emotionally invested. The slot-machine psychology applied ethically to learning.
Variable Reward Schedules · Skinner, 1938
SlateCamp Adaptation:
After each session, show a "Discovery" — one insight about performance the student didn't know. "You answered fastest in Building Laws. Slowest in Utilities. The Pacing Map shows why." This is genuine, non-manipulative variable reward: real data with surprise value.
📈
Progress VisualizationP1 NOW
Duolingo's skill trees and progress rings make abstract learning concrete and visible. Users engage more when progress is tangible. Badges boost completion rates by 30%. The Readiness Score trajectory IS SlateCamp's version of this.
Progress Principle · Amabile & Kramer, 2011
SlateCamp Adaptation:
The Heatmap turning from red to green IS the progress artifact. Show the delta prominently after every session. "2 nodes moved amber → green since yesterday." Make the Heatmap the emotional center of the app, not just analytics.
🎯
Implementation IntentionsP1 NOW
Duolingo's daily goal setting (5 min / 10 min / 20 min) increases commitment dramatically. Research shows that specifying WHEN + WHERE + HOW you'll do something increases follow-through by 65%. Camp Trail already does this — but it must be framed this way explicitly.
Implementation Intentions · Gollwitzer, 1997 · d=0.65
SlateCamp Adaptation:
During Camp Trail setup: "When do you usually study?" + "What device do you prefer?" → The Trail sends reminders at that exact time, on that device. "Tomorrow at 8am: Day 15 · Structural Systems · 35 min."

Learning Science: What Actually Improves Exam Performance

Learning Science · Effect Sizes
The following interventions are all implemented or planned in SlateCamp. Effect sizes (Cohen's d) represent the research consensus on how much each technique improves test performance vs. passive re-reading (the default study behavior of most ALE candidates).
Technique Effect Size (d) SlateCamp Implementation Status
Testing Effect (Retrieval Practice) d = 0.60–1.20 Camp Mode quiz engine — answers questions from memory, gets immediate feedback Built
Spaced Repetition d = 0.46–0.80 SRS via SM-2 algorithm on user_node_mastery — items resurface at optimal intervals Partial
Interleaved Practice d = 1.05 Later Camp Trail weeks mix subjects; early weeks block by subject (Rohrer et al.) Camp Trail
Elaborative Interrogation d = 0.40–0.60 "Why?" prompts mid-Wiki article that require explanation before reveal Phase 1
Metacognitive Calibration d = 0.35–0.55 Pre-session confidence ratings vs actual accuracy — calibration chart post-session Phase 1
Feynman Teach-Back (Self-Explanation) d = 0.55–1.00 Post-study "explain this" with AI evaluation — highest effect size in literature Phase 3
Implementation Intentions (Structured Plan) d = 0.65 Daily Camp Trail prescription: "Complete these 3 activities today" In Progress
Anxiety Inoculation d = 0.30–0.50 Proof Simulation + deliberate pressure protocol in final 2 weeks Phase 3
Key Insight for Product Decisions
The ALE candidate's default behavior is passive re-reading of reviewer notes — an approach with near-zero effect size for retention. Every feature SlateCamp builds should replace passive re-reading with active retrieval. The Quiz Engine is the primary vehicle. The Wiki is secondary — it supports understanding but must drive back to quizzing through every interaction.

Onboarding & Assessment
The onboarding flow is the most consequential 25 minutes in SlateCamp. A user who completes the assessment and sees their Score Reveal has a fundamentally different psychological relationship with the product than one who skips it. Design every step to earn the completion.
💡 Design Principle for Onboarding
Duolingo's most powerful insight: let users experience value before asking them to create an account. SlateCamp's version: let users answer 5–10 diagnostic questions before the "create account" gate. They've already invested. The account creation becomes "protecting your progress," not starting paperwork.

    SCREEN: Quiz Interface — Camp Mode
    Clean, minimal interface: question number/total, subject breadcrumb, question text, 4 answer options (full-width tappable rows), flag button, progress bar. No timer in Camp Mode.

    • Subject breadcrumb: "Building Laws · Foundation Design" — shows student where they are in the taxonomy
    • Flag button: top right, single tap, no confirmation — flags for review
    • Confidence: tiny optional "Rate your confidence on this question" (1–3) per question — opt-in, never blocking

  • 3
    Answer selected → Confirm
    SCREEN: Quiz Interface — Pre-Confirm
    User taps answer. Option highlights (Cobalt Blue tint). "Confirm Answer" button activates. This two-step prevents mis-taps on mobile — important for accurate diagnostic data.
    📐 UX NOTE: The two-tap design (select → confirm) is not friction — it is data quality protection. A mis-tapped answer corrupts the mastery data for that node. The confirm step is 0.3 seconds of friction that protects the entire analytics engine.
  • 4
    Feedback Reveal — Correct Answer
    SCREEN: Feedback State
    All 4 options remain visible. Correct = green reveal + ✓. Wrong = red reveal + ✗ on user's choice, green reveal on correct. Rationale appears below options (does not replace — student sees their choice AND the correct answer simultaneously). "Next Question" CTA appears.
    📐 UX REQUIREMENTS — Feedback State:
    • Show chosen answer AND correct answer simultaneously — never just the correct one
    • Rationale text: body size, Camp Voice, references the specific code section
    • "Why does this trip people up?" — one sentence on the most common misconception
    • "Deep dive →" link to the relevant Wiki article for this topic (auto-linked via topic_id)
    • Wrong answer: show Error Taxonomy prompt (Phase 1): "What happened?" → [Knowledge gap] [Misread] [Two options similar] [Code version] — single tap, optional, takes 1 second
    📚 RESEARCH: Explanation feedback ("why the correct answer is right") is more effective than just showing the correct answer (Butler et al., 2013). The rationale + misconception pattern doubles retention value of each wrong answer.
  • 5
    Session Completion
    SCREEN: Session Complete
    Factual summary: X/Y correct, accuracy %, average time, comparison to previous session on same topics. Heatmap delta: "2 nodes moved amber → green." Three CTAs: "View Heatmap Changes" / "Start Next Session" / "Back to Trail."
    📐 UX REQUIREMENTS — Session Complete:
    • Accuracy bar fills in over 600ms — satisfying visual
    • Heatmap delta is the most important element — nodes that changed, shown with before/after colors
    • Phase 1: Show Metacognitive Calibration chart if confidence was rated pre-session: "You predicted 80% on Building Laws. You scored 41%." — honest, specific, non-judgmental
    • NO confetti, badges, or celebration for routine sessions — save celebration for milestones

Section 07 · Feature Deep-Dive
Camp Trail — The Retention Engine
Camp Trail is the product's primary retention mechanic. It solves the cold-start problem (what do I study today?), creates the habit loop (return daily), and is the most direct driver of DAU. It must be the most polished UX in the entire product.
Retention Science Behind Camp Trail
The research is clear: cohort-based structured programs achieve 85–96% completion rates vs. 3–15% for self-paced (French medical school spaced repetition study, 2025). Camp Trail is SlateCamp's version of this: a daily prescription that removes the decision of "what to study" and replaces it with a simple action — "complete today's 3 activities." Implementation intentions research (Gollwitzer, 1997) shows that when people specify WHEN, WHERE, and HOW they'll do something, completion rates increase by 65%.

Section 09 · Strategy
Gamification Strategy
SlateCamp's design constraints prohibit badges, confetti, virtual currency, and social leaderboards — for good reasons: the ALE is a real high-stakes exam, and manufactured urgency or gamification that doesn't serve learning is a brand betrayal. But tasteful, research-backed gamification that directly improves performance is not just acceptable — it's required for commercial viability.
✅ GUIDING PRINCIPLE
Every gamification element must answer YES to: "Does this make the student study more effectively or more consistently?" If a mechanic only drives engagement without improving outcomes — it's not SlateCamp. If a mechanic drives both engagement AND outcomes — it's mandatory.

Approved Gamification Elements (Research-Backed)

🔥
Study Streak P1 NOW
What: Consecutive days with at least one completed Camp Trail step. Displayed on dashboard and Day Complete screen as a dot-ribbon showing past 7 days.

Why SlateCamp version: Called "Trail Streak" not "Streak." Frame: consistency building. Never shown as a number-chase — shown as a visual ribbon. One "Grace Day" per week (free). Additional grace days = Pro benefit (1 additional/week). Protects against anxiety from a single missed day.
Loss Aversion · d=0.52 · Duolingo: 3.6× retention at 7-day streak
📊
Readiness Score Progress P1 NOW
What: The score trajectory chart IS the gamification. Watching 48% become 67% become 79% over weeks is the most intrinsically motivating feedback loop in the product. It is honest, earned, and directly correlated with the goal (passing).

Why this works: Unlike artificial XP points, the score means something. A student who gets a +2 score feels genuine achievement because that number represents real mastery improvement.
Progress Principle · Amabile & Kramer, 2011 · Flow Theory · Csikszentmihalyi
🗺️
Heatmap Color Transitions P1 NOW
What: Watching a red node turn amber and amber turn green IS the level-up moment for SlateCamp. This is the visible artifact of progress. It must be celebrated appropriately when it happens.

How to celebrate it: On Day Complete, show nodes that changed color with a brief animation. "Building Laws · Foundation Design moved to green." No fanfare — just the factual statement with the color change visible.
Progress Visualization · Visible artifacts of growth
🎯
Metacognitive Calibration Score P2
What: A separate "Calibration Score" alongside the Readiness Score. Shows how accurately the student predicts their own performance. Students with high mastery but poor calibration know the material but can't tell when they know it — a different and important problem.

Gamification angle: The Calibration Score creates a second achievement dimension. A student at 74% readiness with 40% calibration has a new goal: improve calibration. This drives engagement with the pre-session confidence widget.
Dunning-Kruger Effect · Metacognition research · d=0.35–0.55
⏱️
Pacing Gauge Progress P2
What: Show the student's average time/question trending toward the ALE's required pace. "You averaged 104 sec/question last week. ALE requires 96 sec. This week: 98 sec." The pacing gap closing is a form of progress that drives Storm Mode engagement specifically.

Why it works: It frames Storm Mode practice as pacing training, not just knowledge testing. Students who understand they're racing toward an objective pace (96 sec) will do more timed sessions.
Mastery Goals · Self-Efficacy Theory · Bandura
🏕️
Trail Completion Milestones P2
What: At Week 1, Week 2, Week 4, and Week 8 checkpoints on the Camp Trail, show a milestone summary: score at start vs. now, nodes changed, sessions completed. Not a badge — a data report with a brief acknowledgment.

Voice example: "Week 2 complete. 14 sessions. Score: 48% → 57%. Building Laws: 2 nodes moved to amber. The trail continues."
Fresh Start Effect · Dai et al., 2014 · +33–50% motivation at temporal landmarks
🌙
Session Discovery (Variable Reward) P2
What: After each session, one unique insight about the student's performance pattern that they didn't know. "You answer Building Laws 40% faster than Utilities. The Pacing Map shows why." "Your accuracy on 3rd-attempt questions is 92%. You're learning from wrong answers." Each discovery is different — genuine variable reward.
Variable Reward Schedules · Skinner · Curiosity Gap · Loewenstein
💬
Campfire Social Proof (Passive Social) P3
What: Not a leaderboard. Not competition. Campfire shows: "8 students with your score passed the ALE in June 2025" and "3 students who started with 44% are now at 71% after 6 weeks." Social proof, not social competition. Community, not comparison.

Why not a leaderboard: Leaderboards demotivate the bottom 80% — exactly the students who most need motivation. Campfire is social proof that the path works, not that you're behind.
Social Proof · Cialdini · Research shows social comparison increases anxiety in high-stakes contexts
🚫 PROHIBITED GAMIFICATION
XP Points / Virtual Currency: Disconnected from real exam readiness. Creates the illusion of progress without the substance.
Leaderboards / Rankings: Demotivate the bottom 80%. In Philippine context with hiya culture, public ranking is actively harmful for struggling students.
Confetti / Bouncy Animations: Inconsistent with camp voice. The ALE is serious. The product respects that.
Daily Quests / Badges: Trivialize the actual goal. "Complete 3 sessions today for a badge" is not why students are here.
Streaks as Punishment: Never make a student feel they've "broken" something. Missed days happen. The product responds with: "Pick up where you left off."
Manufactured Urgency Copy: "Only X days left!" "You're falling behind!" — Dark patterns that create anxiety, not action.
Section 10 · Strategy
Retention & DAU Engine
Retention is the product. Acquisition brings students in — retention determines if they pass. The DAU goal is not vanity: daily active users are daily studying students. Every retention intervention here is simultaneously a product quality improvement.

The Retention Stack — Layered Interventions

Layer 1: Habit Formation (0–7 days)

The first 7 days are the most critical for forming the study habit. Focus here: reduce friction to zero, maximize first-value moments, build the trail routine.

  • Day 0: Assessment + Score Reveal + Trail enrollment all in one session. Student leaves with a score, a plan, and Day 1 ready to go.
  • Day 1: Trail Day 1 must be achievable in 30 minutes. No friction. First Wiki + First Quiz should produce visible heatmap movement.
  • Day 2: Push notification at preferred study time. "Day 2 is ready. [Session title] · 35 min." Zero extra copy.
  • Day 3–7: Streak ribbon builds. Day 7: "One week on the trail" acknowledgment with score delta.
  • Streak Freeze offer: After Day 5 streak, introduce Grace Day concept. User understands what it is before they need it.

Layer 2: Habit Reinforcement (7–30 days)

After the first week, the key is making the habit visible and the progress unmistakable. Students who see proof that the trail is working don't churn.

  • Week 2: Show Week 1 → Week 2 heatmap delta. The map is visibly changing. This is the most powerful retention proof.
  • SRS review sessions: Items from Week 1 start coming due. This creates a new habit: "review day." Framed as "7 items ready for review" — feels like care, not chore.
  • Calibration chart: After 5 sessions with confidence ratings, show the calibration trend. Creates meta-engagement: students study to improve their calibration score, not just their mastery.
  • Blindspot notification: After 3 weeks of data, Blindspot Protocol activates. "Building Laws has been Red for 3 weeks. A targeted trail is ready." This is re-engagement with urgency that's earned, not manufactured.

Push Notification Strategy

Channel Protection Principle (Duolingo's Key Insight)
Duolingo treats push notifications as a protected channel. Volume limits, timing tests, suppression rules. The insight: over-sending permanently damages opt-in health. One ignored notification is forgivable. Seven ignored notifications in a week causes opt-out — and opt-out is permanent. SlateCamp rule: max 1 push/day, only during preferred study window, only with genuinely useful content.
