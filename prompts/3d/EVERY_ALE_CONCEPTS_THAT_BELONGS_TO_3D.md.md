# Every ALE concept that belongs in SlateCamp's 3D Lab

**No existing platform combines interactive 3D visualization with Philippine architecture licensure content — SlateCamp's 3D Lab would be the first.** The ALE's three-domain structure (History & Theory at 30%, Structural/Utilities at 30%, and Architectural Design at 40%) contains over 200 distinct concepts where spatial understanding is either essential or dramatically superior to text-based study. This report maps every 3D-ready concept across all 13 code and subject areas, organized by interaction type and tagged with specific parameters that drive slider controls, cutaways, and code-cited hotspots.

The ALE syllabus has remained stable through the 2024–2026 cycle, with the PRBoA making no publicly announced overhaul since the 2011 Table of Specifications. Passing requires a **70% weighted average** with no subject below 50%. The exam runs across two days with approximately 450 questions. The design subject (Area C, 40%) shifted to a non-drafting, multiple-choice format in 2009, making code-based spatial reasoning — exactly what 3D models reinforce — even more critical for examinees.

## 1. PD 1096 National Building Code — the largest single source of 3D content

The National Building Code generates the richest vein of 3D Lab modules because nearly every provision defines a three-dimensional spatial relationship. The concepts below are grouped by the interaction pattern best suited to each.

### Parametric room/building models with slider controls

**Ceiling heights and room sizes** form the foundation module. A single residential room model should let users toggle between natural ventilation (**2.70 m** minimum ceiling) and artificial ventilation (**2.40 m**), with multi-storey mode showing first-floor 2.70 m, second-floor 2.40 m, and third-floor-and-above 2.10 m minimums. Minimum habitable room area of **6.00 sq.m** (2.00 m least dimension), kitchen at **3.00 sq.m** (1.50 m), and bath/toilet at **1.20 sq.m** (0.90 m) should display as adjustable floor plans with red/green compliance feedback. Mezzanine floors require **1.80 m** clear above and below — a perfect cutaway scenario.

**Development controls (PSO/TGFA/AMBF)** need a parametric lot model. Users should input lot area and zoning classification, then watch the Allowable Maximum Building Footprint calculate in real time. Key parameters: PSO ranges from 50–90% depending on zoning and firewall conditions; Total Open Space for Lot (TOSL) ranges 15–50%; setbacks for R-1 zones are **4.50 m front, 2.00 m side, 2.00 m rear**. The model should show how AMBF = PSO × TLA, with the lesser-of-two-calculations rule enforced visually.

**Court requirements** scale with building height: **2.00 m** minimum horizontal dimension for 1–2 storeys, adding **300 mm per additional storey** up to the 14th floor. An interactive building section showing courts growing wider as floors are added is a high-impact module.

**Window openings** at minimum **10% of floor area** (not less than 1.00 sq.m) should animate as draggable window panels on room walls, with real-time percentage calculation. Vent shafts need their own model: minimum 0.10 sq.m cross-section per meter of height, least dimension 600 mm, minimum area 1.00 sq.m.

### Exploded assembly models with code hotspots

**Stairway design** is one of the highest-value 3D modules. Parameters for the interactive model: maximum riser **200 mm**, minimum tread **250 mm**, maximum **5 mm** riser variation per flight, headroom **≥ 2.00 m** from nosing, maximum **3.60 m** vertical between landings, minimum widths of **1.10 m** (>50 occupants), **0.90 m** (≤50), or **0.75 m** (private, <10). Handrails at **800–900 mm** above nosing, intermediate handrails every **3.00 m** of width, minimum **38 mm** wall clearance. A slider should let users adjust rise-run ratios and see compliance indicators change.

**Firewall construction** requires a wall section cutaway: minimum **2-hour** fire-resistive rating, projecting **400 mm** above roof. Abutment rules vary by residential zone: R-1 allows no abutments; R-2 allows one side only (max 80% of property line length); R-3 allows two sides and rear. Carport abutments max **3.20 m** high, with perforated concrete blocks above 1.50 m.

**Five construction types** should display as exploded building sections: Type I (wood, no fire resistance), Type II (wood + fire-resistant, 1-hour), Type III (masonry + wood, 1-hour, incombustible exterior), Type IV (steel/concrete, 2-hour), and Type V (steel/concrete, 4-hour throughout). Each shows fire-resistance ratings for bearing walls, structural frames, floors, and roofs.

### Dimensional parking and circulation models

**Parking slots** at precise dimensions: car perpendicular **2.50 m × 5.00 m**, parallel **2.15 m × 6.00 m**, truck **3.60 m × 12.00 m**, PWD **3.70 m × 5.00 m**. The 3D model should show a parking floor with toggle switches for each vehicle type, with parking ratios displayed per building use (1 slot per 100 sq.m GFA for shopping centers, 1 per 125 sq.m for offices, 1 per 10 rooms for hotels, etc.).

## 2. Fire Code RA 9514 — egress systems demand spatial visualization

Fire egress is inherently three-dimensional, making it ideal for immersive 3D floor-plan walkthroughs with travel-distance overlays.

**Travel distance visualization** is the flagship module. A multi-storey building model should display maximum travel distances as glowing path lines: **46 m** for assembly/business without sprinklers, **61 m** with sprinklers; **30 m** for mercantile unsprinklered, **46 m** sprinklered; **31 m** for industrial unsprinklered, **46 m** sprinklered. A toggle switches between sprinklered and unsprinklered states, updating all distances. Dead-end corridor limits (**6 m** for educational/institutional/assembly, **15 m** for all others) should highlight as red zones.

**Occupant load factor calculator** embedded in floor plans: users select a use type, and the model divides floor area by OLF to show capacity. Key values: assembly concentrated **0.65 sq.m/person**, assembly less concentrated **1.40**, standing space **0.28**, classrooms **1.80**, offices **9.30**, mercantile street floor **2.80**, residential **18.60**, healthcare sleeping **11.10**. The model dynamically calculates required exits (2 exits for 10–499 occupants, 3 for 500–999, 4 for 1,000+).

**Exit door mechanics** as an animated 3D door model: minimum clear width **710 mm**, maximum single leaf **1,220 mm**, swing direction toward egress for 50+ occupants, maximum **178 mm** projection into corridor when open, maximum **7 kg** opening force, panic hardware activation animation, re-entry mechanism signage every 4 storeys.

**Stairwell enclosure section model**: 1-hour rating for ≤3 storeys, 2-hour for 4+ storeys, self-closing fire doors, no storage permitted. New stair dimensions differ from existing: new stairs require **1,120 mm** minimum width, **180 mm** max riser, **280 mm** min tread; existing stairs allow **915 mm** width, **205 mm** max riser, **230 mm** min tread. Handrails at **760–865 mm**.

**Fire suppression systems** as a building section: automatic sprinklers required for buildings **15 m+** in height; standpipe classes I (2½-inch, fire department), II (1½-inch, occupants), III (combined); wet standpipe minimum **64 mm** diameter; all building portions within **6 m** of a nozzle attached to **22 m** hose; fire service connections **460–1,220 mm** above grade. Sprinkler heads, standpipe risers, and hose cabinets should be clickable hotspots.

**Smoke control systems** as an animated air-pressure diagram: stairwell pressurization, smoke partitions, and the sprinkler-vent method (minimum **91.5 m/min** downdraft for 30 minutes) visualized with particle effects.

## 3. Technical codes deliver the richest cutaway and installation models

### Philippine Electrical Code

An **electrical room context model** should show a typical residential room with: convenience outlets at **300 mm** from floor (bottom of box), switches at **1,200 mm** (center of plate), kitchen counter outlets above countertop level (~1,050 mm), panel boards with maximum handle grip at **1,980 mm**. The spacing rule — no point along any wall more than **1.8 m** from an outlet, effectively one outlet every **3.6 m** — should visualize as a "coverage ring" around each outlet. Any wall section **≥ 600 mm** wide requires its own outlet.

**Circuit and conductor sizing** as a schematic 3D panel: 15A circuits with 2.0 mm² wire, 20A with 3.5 mm², 30A with 5.5 mm². Branch circuit voltage drop ≤ 3%, total ≤ 5%. GFCI protection zones (kitchens, bathrooms, outdoors, garages, laundry) highlighted in the room model.

**Illumination levels** by room type overlaid as light-intensity heatmaps: offices **300–500 lux**, classrooms **300–500**, corridors **100–200**, hospitals/ORs **300–1,000**, residential living rooms **150–300**, parking **50–100**.

### Plumbing Code (NPCP)

**Fixture installation cutaway** is the core module. Each fixture type gets a 3D model showing trap size and fixture unit value: water closet (flush tank) with **76 mm** trap and **4 DFU**, lavatory with **32 mm** trap and **1 DFU**, kitchen sink **38 mm**/2 DFU, bathtub **38 mm**/2 DFU, urinal (flush valve) **51 mm**/4 DFU. Trap seal depth range: **51–102 mm**.

**Pipe slope visualization** as a transparent building section: pipes ≥100 mm at **1% slope** (10.4 mm/m), pipes ≤75 mm at **2% slope** (20.8 mm/m). Vent pipes graded to drip back by gravity, with no more than 1/3 of total length installed horizontally. Vent diameter ≥ half the drain diameter it serves.

**Septic tank design** as a parametric 3D model with bedroom-count slider: 1–2 bedrooms → **2.84 m³** capacity, 3 bedrooms → **3.79 m³**, scaling up to 10 bedrooms → **11.36 m³**. Standard dimensions table: 10 persons = 1.20 m depth × 0.90 m width × 1.80 m length, plus 1.00 m for secondary chamber. Minimum **15 m** from water distribution, never under the house.

**Water supply system** as a traced pipe diagram: water service pipe → meter → main distribution → risers → branches → fixture supply pipes. Building sewer minimum **150 mm** diameter. Backflow prevention devices (air gaps, vacuum breakers, check valves, RPZ) as exploded assembly models.

### NSCP 2015 Structural Code

**Load path visualization** is the most conceptually powerful 3D module. An animated building frame should show dead loads (reinforced concrete at **23.6 kN/m³**, steel at **77.0 kN/m³**) flowing from slab to beam to column to footing. Live loads toggle by occupancy: residential **1.9 kPa**, office **2.4 kPa**, retail first floor **4.8 kPa**, assembly movable seats **4.8 kPa**, heavy manufacturing **12.0 kPa**, library stacks **7.2 kPa**.

**LRFD load combinations** as stacked visual overlays on a building section: U1 = 1.4D, U2 = 1.2D + 1.6L + 0.5Lr, through U7 = 0.9D + 1.0E. Each combination activates different colored force arrows.

**Seismic parameter explorer** as an interactive Philippine map: Zone 2 (Z=0.20) vs Zone 4 (Z=0.40); soil profiles SA through SF shown as soil column cutaways (SA hard rock Vs>1,500 m/s down to SE soft soil Vs<180 m/s). Near-source factors Na and Nv displayed as distance rings from fault lines. Structural systems compared by R-factor: Special SMRF at R=8.5, Ordinary MRF at R=3.5, Bearing wall at R=4.5–6.5.

**Reinforced concrete fundamentals** as cross-section cutaways: minimum cover of **75 mm** cast against earth, **50 mm** exposed to weather (bars ≥20 mm), **40 mm** beams/columns not exposed, **20 mm** slabs/walls. Rebar shown at standard Philippine sizes (10 mm at 0.617 kg/m through 32 mm at 6.313 kg/m). **Steel connection types** as exploded joint models: bolted vs. welded, moment connections vs. shear connections.

**Wind load analysis** with a parametric building model: basic wind speed varies **200–280 kph** across the Philippines; exposure categories B (urban), C (open terrain), D (coastal flat). Velocity pressure equation qz = 0.613 × Kz × Kzt × Kd × V² visualized as pressure distribution on building faces.

### Mechanical Code

**Air changes per hour** as animated airflow in room sections: offices/stores/factories at **3 ACH**, bakeries/commercial kitchens at **10 ACH**, auditoriums at **0.30 m³/min per person**, operating rooms **15–25 ACH** (industry standard). Cross-ventilation and mechanical systems shown as particle-based airflow.

**Elevator/hoistway model** with dimensional callouts: minimum cab **1,100 mm** wide, pit depth with **610 mm** clearance below car on compressed buffers, fire-resistant enclosure walls, ventilation through main roof for shafts spanning 2+ storeys. Escalator maximum inclination **30°**, draft curtain **300 mm** below ceiling, sprinklers within **600 mm** of curtain at **1.8 m** spacing.

## 4. BP 344 Accessibility Law — the 2024 revised IRR changes everything

The **2024 Revised IRR** significantly tightened accessibility standards, making updated 3D models essential. The default ramp gradient changed from 1:12 to **1:20**, minimum width from 1,000 mm to **1,200 mm**, and landing length from 1,000 mm to **1,800 mm**.

**Accessible ramp configurator**: slider controls for gradient (1:20 default, 1:15 special), width (min 1,200 mm), rise per run (max 700 mm at 1:15), landing intervals. Dual handrails at **700 mm and 900 mm** from floor, diameter **30–40 mm**, extensions **300 mm** horizontal at top and bottom. Raised edge minimum **100 mm**. Protection railing **1,100 mm** where ramp exceeds 750 mm above grade. Tactile warning blocks (**600 mm** depth) placed **300 mm** from head and foot of ramp.

**Wheelchair maneuvering model**: turning circle of **1,500 mm** diameter, turnabout space **1,800 mm × 1,800 mm** at maximum **12,000 mm** intervals along corridors. Corridor widths: **1,500 mm** for single wheelchair + ambulant, **1,800 mm** for two wheelchairs passing. Bollard/obstacle passages minimum **900 mm**.

**Accessible toilet cutaway**: minimum cubicle **1,800 mm × 1,800 mm**, toilet seat at **450 mm** height, WC centerline **450 mm** from sidewall. L-type grab bar on side wall extending **300 mm** from front of WC, vertical leg **750 mm** above horizontal, grab bar height **300 mm** above seat. Flip-up grab bar on opposite side extending **100 mm** from front, supports **200 kg**. Lavatory at **800 mm** height, **650 mm** vertical leg clearance, **450 mm** centerline from wall. Emergency call button **400–600 mm** from floor.

**Three tactile block types** as detailed 3D objects: directional (4+ parallel bars, 34 mm wide), warning (25 truncated domes in 5×5 square grid, 35 mm diameter), and positional (25 staggered domes, 23 mm diameter). All blocks **300 × 300 mm**, elements **5 mm** raised, minimum **50%** luminance contrast.

**Accessible parking**: **4,000 mm** wide (includes 1,500 mm wheelchair passage) × **5,000 mm** long. Scaling table from 1 slot per 1–25 total spaces up to 2% for 501–1,000 spaces.

**Elevator cab**: minimum **1,200 mm × 1,400 mm** clear (2024 standard, up from 1,100 × 1,400), door opening **900 mm** (up from 800), controls at **900–1,200 mm**, buttons minimum **20 mm** diameter, Braille on all buttons, handrails at **900 mm** on both sides and rear.

## 6. Building materials as dimensioned 3D objects with assembly animations

**CHB/CMU blocks** at actual scale: 4" (100 × 200 × 400 mm), 5" (125 × 200 × 400 mm), 6" (150 × 200 × 400 mm), 8" (200 × 200 × 400 mm). Laying factor **12.5 pieces per sq.m**, mortar joints **10 mm**, grout cells every 5 layers. Bond pattern toggle: running, stack, Flemish, English.

**Standard material sheets**: plywood **1,220 × 2,440 mm** at 6/9/12/18 mm thickness; gypsum board same sheet size at 9.5/12.5/16 mm. Rebar at standard lengths (**6.0 m** most common) with all Philippine diameters from 8 mm (0.395 kg/m) through 36 mm (7.990 kg/m). Steel sections: wide flange W150×14 through W460×74, C-channels, angle bars, C-purlins with lip dimensions.

**Concrete mix proportions** as ingredient ratio visualizers: Class A (1:2:4, 9 bags/m³ at 40 kg, **3,500 psi**), Class AA (1:1½:3, **4,000 psi**), Class B (1:2½:5, **3,000 psi**), down to Class D (1:3:7, **2,000 psi**). Each shows proportional volumes of cement, sand, and gravel.

**Foundation type comparison**: isolated footing (typical 1×1 m to 2×2 m), combined footing, strip footing, mat/raft, driven piles, bored piles. PD 1096 minimums: footing thickness **250 mm**, depth **600 mm** below ground. Each as an exploded cross-section.

**Roof truss types** as structural 3D models: Fink (W-web, residential spans to 10 m), Pratt (diagonals toward center), Howe (diagonals away), Warren (equilateral triangles), King Post (short spans to 8 m), Queen Post (medium 8–12 m). Purlin attachment details with C-purlins welded to trusses.

**Wall assembly comparison**: solid CHB wall, cavity wall (double-layer with air gap), veneer wall, curtain wall (glass/metal on steel frame), drywall on metal studs at 400–600 mm spacing. Each as a cutaway showing all layers.

**Construction sequences** as step-by-step animated 3D builds:
- Foundation: site prep → excavation → lean concrete (75 mm) → rebar placement → formwork → pour Class A concrete → starter bars → curing → backfill
- Column: layout → vertical bars tied to starters with lateral ties → formwork (check plumb) → pour → cure (strip at 21 days) → 40 mm minimum cover
- Slab: shoring/props → stringers → formply (18 mm) → bottom rebar (short span lower, long span upper) → bar chairs (20–25 mm cover) → top rebar at supports → MEP sleeves → pour → cure 7 days → strip at 7+ days
- CHB wall: layout → dry first course → mortar bed (Class B, 10 mm joints) → running bond → plumb/level each course → vertical rebar → grout every 5 layers → lintels over openings → plastering (scratch coat + finish coat, 12–16 mm per side)

**Waterproofing systems**: membrane (torch-applied bituminous), cementitious (cement-polymer for toilets/tanks), crystalline (self-healing crystal formation), liquid-applied (polyurethane for terraces). Each as a wall/floor section cutaway.

## 7. Classical orders and architectural history — the visual core of Area A

**Five classical orders explorer** as detailed column-entablature models with proportional overlays:

- **Tuscan** (1:7 ratio): smooth unfluted shaft, simple echinus + abacus capital, plain single-fascia architrave, blank frieze
- **Doric** (1:8): 20 sharp-arris flutes, no base (Greek) or simple base (Roman), square abacus + echinus capital, **triglyphs and metopes** in frieze, mutules in cornice
- **Ionic** (1:9): 24 flutes with flat fillets between, two-tori base, **volute scrolls** on capital, three-fascia architrave, continuous frieze, **dentils** in cornice
- **Corinthian** (1:10): 24 sharp-edged flutes, tallest capital with **two rows of acanthus leaves** + caulicoli, three-fascia architrave, dentils + modillions
- **Composite** (1:10): combines **Ionic volutes at four corners with Corinthian acanthus leaves**

Each order should allow component isolation (base, shaft, capital, architrave, frieze, cornice), superposition demonstration (Doric → Ionic → Corinthian as on the Colosseum), and colossal order mode.

**Structural evolution timeline** as interactive building cross-sections:
- **Egyptian**: post-and-lintel, hypostyle hall columns (papyrus/lotus capitals), massive pylons
- **Greek**: peristyle temple with optical refinements (entasis, curved stylobate)
- **Roman**: arch, barrel vault, groin vault, dome; Pantheon cutaway showing **43.3 m** unreinforced concrete dome with coffers and oculus, varying concrete density
- **Byzantine**: Hagia Sophia pendentive dome — square plan to circular dome via triangular pendentives, **32 m** main dome at **55 m** height, 40 base windows
- **Romanesque**: barrel vault, groin vault, massive load-bearing walls, small windows
- **Gothic**: pointed arch, ribbed vault, flying buttress, tracery; Notre-Dame section showing how buttresses transfer thrust → thinner walls → larger windows
- **Renaissance**: Florence Cathedral double-shell dome — 8 major ribs + 16 minor, herringbone brickwork, self-supporting without centering, **42 m** span
- **Modernism**: Le Corbusier's Five Points as Villa Savoye model — pilotis, free plan, free facade, ribbon windows, roof garden
- **Contemporary**: Guggenheim Bilbao steel frame + titanium cladding, parametric geometry

### Philippine heritage buildings

**Bahay Kubo** exploded model: stilt posts (1–2 m elevation), split bamboo floor (showing airflow), nipa palm/cogon thatch roof (steep pitch for rain), windows on all sides (cross-ventilation), single/two-room plan. Subfloor storage zone labeled.

**Bahay na Bato** cutaway: stone/brick lower level (silong/zaguan) for storage/carriage, wooden upper level with **volada** (cantilevered balcony), **ventanillas** (small sub-windows for ventilation), **capiz shell** sliding windows (translucent, block rain), floor-to-ceiling openings. Regional variants toggle (Vigan coral stone, Ilocos plastered brick, Batanes thick stone).

**Earthquake Baroque church** section: Paoay Church showing massive side buttresses, lower/wider proportions for seismic resistance, thick coral stone/adobe walls.

## What the competitive landscape reveals about opportunity

The Philippine ALE prep market currently includes **VIZCODE** (2D illustrated code diagrams with AI chatbot), **Arkitekto** (8,000+ quiz questions with spaced repetition), and **Pinoy Reviewer** (300+ practice questions). None use interactive 3D WebGL models. VIZCODE comes closest with illustrated comparisons, but remains in 2D. Globally, platforms like Corinth3D and Archilogic demonstrate the technical feasibility of browser-based 3D education using React Three Fiber, Three.js, and GLTF model pipelines — but none target licensure exam content.

SlateCamp's 3D Lab would be the **first platform worldwide** to combine interactive WebGL visualization with code-specific architecture licensure content. The React Three Fiber ecosystem (with @react-three/drei for controls, gltfjsx for model conversion, and leva for GUI sliders) provides all the tooling needed. Performance considerations include LOD switching, texture compression, and render batching to maintain mobile compatibility.

8. **Seismic parameter map explorer** — zone factors, soil profiles, and near-source factors

These ten modules alone would cover concepts appearing in an estimated **35–40%** of ALE questions across all three domains, establishing SlateCamp's 3D Lab as an indispensable study tool with no direct competitor in the Philippine architecture licensure market.
