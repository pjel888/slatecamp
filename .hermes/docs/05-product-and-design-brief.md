# Product and Design Brief

## Product stance

Slatecamp should be understood as a product before it is treated as a codebase. Every technical change should map to user value, operational simplicity, or quality improvement.

## Product questions to resolve

- Who is the primary user?
- What problem does Slatecamp solve better than alternatives?
- What is the smallest valuable user flow?
- What should feel premium, fast, calm, or delightful?
- What must be avoided because it creates confusion or maintenance burden?

## Design standard

The UI should be coherent before it is decorative. Strong design choices should reduce cognitive load, clarify hierarchy, and make the system feel intentional.

## Design review lens

- Information hierarchy: can the user tell what matters first?
- Interaction cost: how many steps to complete the key task?
- Visual consistency: are spacing, type, color, and component behavior consistent?
- Empty/error/loading states: are they designed rather than incidental?
- Responsiveness: does the layout survive real device widths?
- Trust: does the interface clearly communicate status, risk, and next action?
