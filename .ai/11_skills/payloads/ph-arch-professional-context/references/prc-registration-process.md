# PRC Registration Process and CPD System

## LEA Application Process (Current as of 2025–2026)

### Step 1: Online Registration via LERIS
Platform: online.prc.gov.ph (LERIS — Licensure Examination and Registration Information System)
- Create PRC online account
- Fill out personal and educational information
- Select Licensure Examination for Architects
- Upload scanned documentary requirements
- Pay examination fee (₱900 as of last published rates — verify current amount)

### Step 2: Documentary Requirements
- PSA Birth Certificate (original)
- NSO/PSA Marriage Contract (for married female applicants)
- Transcript of Records with scanned photo, marked "For Board Examination Purposes"
- Valid NBI Clearance
- Certificate of good moral character (from school or barangay)
- DAE Documentation:
  - Affidavit of Experience signed and sealed by supervising RLA
  - At least 2 years of diversified experience (or 1 year if with Master's degree)
- For Master's degree credit: Official Transcript + School Certification

### Step 3: Notice of Admission (NOA)
- PRC releases NOA approximately 2–3 weeks before exam
- NOA specifies exam venue and schedule
- Must bring NOA to exam (for identification purposes)

### Step 4: The Examination
- Day 1: Area A and Area B (multiple choice)
- Day 2: Area C (Architectural Design and Site Planning)
- Results released 3 working days after the last examination day
- Results available at prc.gov.ph and published in major newspapers

### Step 5: Post-Exam Registration (for passers)
- Online registration at online.prc.gov.ph
- Requirements for oath-taking:
  - Downloaded duly accomplished Oath Form (Panunumpa ng Propesyonal)
  - NOA (for identification only)
  - 2 passport-sized colored photos with white background and name tag
  - 2 sets of documentary stamps
  - 1 short brown envelope
- Mass oath-taking date announced separately by PRC
- After oath: receive Certificate of Registration and Professional ID Card

### Step 6: UAP Membership
- After receiving PRC Certificate of Registration, architect must register with UAP
- UAP membership is mandatory under RA 9266 (IAPOA membership required of all RLAs)
- Initial UAP membership fee and annual dues apply
- UAP issues UAP membership ID separate from PRC ID

## CPD (Continuing Professional Development) System

### Legal Basis
- RA 10912 (CPD Act of 2016) — mandates CPD for all licensed professionals
- PRC Board Resolution implementing CPD for architects
- UAP as accredited CPD provider under PRC

### Current CPD Requirements (verify with PRC for most current rules)
- 45 CPD credit units per 3-year compliance period
- Required for PRC Professional ID card renewal
- Exemptions: architects 65 years and above (may apply for exemption)

### How CPD Units are Earned

**Formal Learning (maximum 30 units/period):**
- UAP national convention (NATCON): typically 15–20 units/attendance
- UAP chapter seminars: 3–8 units per seminar
- PRC-accredited university continuing education courses: varies
- Online CPD programs (PRC-accredited providers)

**Non-Formal Learning (maximum 15 units/period):**
- Self-directed learning with documentation
- Reading technical publications
- Participation in standards development committees

**Informal Learning (maximum 5 units/period):**
- Mentoring and coaching activities
- Community service related to architecture

### UAP NATCON (National Convention)
The UAP National Convention is held annually, typically October–November. It is the single largest CPD unit generator for Filipino architects:
- 3–5 days of seminars, lectures, keynotes
- Construction exhibits
- Award ceremonies (LIKHA Award, EMERITUS medallions)
- Oath-taking for newly registered architects
- Typically yields 15–20 CPD units in one event

**SlateCamp relevance:** NATCON is also a significant source of professional development content and a major touchpoint for the architect community. SlateCamp's B2B institutional strategy should consider NATCON as a launch/partnership platform.

## Post-LEA Career Pathways

Understanding typical career paths makes professional practice content more credible.

### First 1–3 Years (Post-licensure)
Most newly registered architects:
1. Continue with their current employer (often the firm where DAE was completed)
2. Join a mid-size architectural firm in a junior design or production role
3. Begin government service (DPWH, LGU positions)
4. Some pursue Master's degree in Architecture (MArch) or related fields

**Typical first-year salary range (2024–2025, Philippines):**
- Private architectural firm, Metro Manila: ₱25,000–₱40,000/month
- Government architect (starting): ₱35,000–₱45,000/month (Salary Grade 15–18)
- This is a known pain point — many fresh RLAs find the salary disappointing relative to the years of study invested

### 3–7 Years
- Senior architect / project architect roles
- Beginning of sole proprietorship or small firm formation
- Associates in established firms
- Specialization (healthcare, industrial, conservation)

### 7+ Years
- Principal architect / firm owner
- Consulting architect role
- Academic practice (teaching + practice)
- Government senior positions (OIC Building Official, DPWH Bureau Director)

## Why the License Matters Beyond Practice

For SlateCamp marketing context — the emotional reasons behind LEA motivation:

**Professional title:** "Registered Architect" vs. "Architecture graduate" — significant status difference in the Philippines, especially in professional family contexts

**Government positions:** Any government position primarily requiring architectural services must be filled by an RLA (RA 9266 mandate). Without the license, career advancement in government stops.

**Business registration:** An architectural firm cannot be registered with DTI and PRC without at least one RLA as principal

**Project signing authority:** Only RLAs can sign, seal, and date architectural documents. Without the license, an architect cannot legally author building permit documents — severely limiting professional capacity

**Social recognition:** In Philippine professional culture, the board exam is a significant milestone. Passing is a family achievement, often celebrated with a gathering. Failing carries social weight (hiya culture).

**Income ceiling:** Unlicensed architecture graduates are effectively capped in career advancement and earning potential in both private and government practice.
