## SlateCamp Revenue Context

**The core product promise:** *"Know you'll win."* — LEA candidates pay for certainty, not content.

**The asymmetry to exploit:** Students massively underweight the cost of failure (failed board exam = 6+ months delayed career, social shame, lost income) vs. the cost of SlateCamp Pro. This asymmetry is SlateCamp's pricing power.

**Market realities (Philippines, 2025–2026):**
- LEA candidate pool: ~8,000–12,000 per exam cycle
- Two exam cycles per year → ~16,000–24,000 annual addressable paying candidates
- Filipino students price-compare actively but will pay premium for proven outcomes
- Mobile payment penetration: GCash/Maya dominant — minimize friction in payment flow
- Average EdTech spend per Filipino student: ₱500–₱3,000 per exam cycle (benchmark from review centers)
- Review centers charge ₱8,000–₱25,000 for full program — SlateCamp sits far below this ceiling

## The Revenue Architecture Stack

### Stream 1: B2C Subscription (Primary, Launch)
The foundational revenue model. Direct-to-student, mobile-first, exam-urgency driven.

**Tier Structure:**
```
FREE — The Hook
- Diagnostic Quiz (full, one-time)
- Readiness Score (revealed once post-diagnostic)
- Taxonomy Heatmap (blurred after first view)
- 3 Camp Trail days only
- Limited Wiki access
- Purpose: Prove the product works. Create the "I need more of this" moment.

PRO MONTHLY — The Workhorse
Price target: ₱299–₱399/month
- Live Readiness Score (updated after every session)
- Full Heatmap with trajectory chart
- Full Camp Trail access
- Both Camp Mode + Storm Mode (full simulations)
- 3D Lab access
- Campfire community
- Wiki unlimited
- Best for: casual starters, early exam window (4–6 months out)

PRO EXAM PASS — The Cash Cow
Price target: ₱1,499–₱2,499 (one-time, 90-day access)
- Everything in Pro Monthly
- Pinned to the 90-day pre-exam window
- Includes: Endurance Cycle tracker + Blindspot Protocol
- Early access to newly added taxonomy content
- Priority support
- Best for: committed candidates 1–3 months before exam
- Psychology: one purchase removes pricing anxiety → full commitment to studying
```

**Why two paid tiers?**
- Monthly = lower friction entry, higher churn risk
- Exam Pass = higher upfront, lower churn (locked in), better LTV per cohort
- Together, they capture both the cautious buyer and the committed buyer

### Stream 2: B2B Institutional Licensing (Phase 2, 6–18 months post-launch)
The volume multiplier. Sell access in bulk to review centers, architecture schools, and universities.

**Institutional pricing model:**
```
School License (per semester):
- 50 students: ₱15,000–₱25,000/semester
- 100 students: ₱25,000–₱45,000/semester
- 200+ students: Custom (₱150–₱250/student/semester)

Review Center Bundle:
- Per-batch cohort access: ₱500–₱800/student (vs ₱399/month B2C)
- Includes: Admin dashboard, progress reports, educator view
- Review centers mark up SlateCamp as part of their total program

University Partnership:
- Integration into capstone or licensure prep courses
- Subsidized/grant-funded → DepEd/CHED partnership potential
```

**Why B2B eventually beats B2C in EdTech:**
- Larger contract size, lower per-customer sales cost
- Institutional renewals are stickier than consumer subscriptions
- Credibility signal: "Recommended by [University Name]" → organic B2C acquisition
- Insulates against consumer price sensitivity

### Stream 3: Topnotcher Premium (Phase 2–3)
Upsell to the highest-intent segment.

```
Topnotcher Track — ₱4,999–₱9,999 (one-time or installment)
- Everything in Pro Exam Pass
- 1:1 AI study coach (personalized weak-area curriculum)
- Cohort accountability group (8–12 people, LEA exam-aligned)
- Guaranteed score improvement or money back
- "Topnotcher Pledge" — featured in SlateCamp if you rank Top 10
- Live monthly Q&A with licensed architect mentors
```

### Stream 4: Content Marketplace (Phase 3)
Platform play: let licensed architects, top-passers, and review experts sell supplementary content on SlateCamp. Take 20–30% revenue share.

**Why this works:**
- Zero content cost to SlateCamp
- Creates content moat through creator ecosystem
- Drives organic acquisition (creators bring their own audiences)
- Generates revenue even from free-tier users

### Stream 5: Placement & Career Layer (Phase 3–4)
After passing the LEA, architects need: firms to join, PRC documentation help, CPD units, job boards. SlateCamp becomes the professional layer.

- Architect job placement board: ₱5,000–₱15,000/posting (employer-side)
- CPD course partnerships: revenue share with CPD providers
- PRC renewal automation: affiliate or tool revenue

## Freemium Architecture: The Gate Logic

The freemium gate must satisfy two competing requirements:
1. **Free tier must demonstrate value** — users must experience the "aha" moment before the paywall
2. **Free tier must leave them hungry** — they must feel limited enough to upgrade

**SlateCamp's core freemium gate:** The Readiness Score

```
Free: "Your Readiness Score is 34/100 — here's what that means."
     [Heatmap shown briefly, then blurred]
     [You need Pro to track your progress and act on this.]

Pro: Live score. Score history. Sub-scores by taxonomy.
     The Heatmap is your map. Pro makes it your GPS.
```

This gate works because:
- The score is delivered before the paywall (creates value)
- The score reveals a gap (creates anxiety)
- Pro is positioned as the solution to the anxiety (converts)

**What should NOT be behind the paywall:**
- The diagnostic itself — it's the acquisition hook
- The wiki (limit access, don't eliminate)
- Basic Camp Trail (give Day 1–3 free)

**What MUST be behind the paywall:**
- Live Readiness Score tracking
- Full Heatmap with trajectory
- Storm Mode (timed simulation — highest value feature)
- 3D Lab
- Campfire (community — FOMO creates conversions)

## Pricing Psychology for Filipino Students

1. **Anchoring to review centers:** Always position Pro Exam Pass at ~₱2,499 relative to ₱15,000+ review centers. "The cost of one month of classes."

2. **Installment framing:** ₱2,499 feels heavy; ₱499 × 5 feels manageable. Offer GCash installment via payment partners (BillEase, Plentina, Tendopay).

3. **Loss framing over gain framing:** "You're leaving ₱2,499 of protection on the table" outperforms "Get Pro for only ₱2,499" in Philippine student psychology.

4. **Social proof pricing:** Show "X students already enrolled for [exam month]" near the upgrade CTA. Scarcity + social proof = conversion catalyst.

5. **The ₱99 trial trick:** Offer 7-day Pro trial for ₱99. Dramatically lowers friction. Converts to full Pro at higher rates than a free trial because ₱99 signals commitment.

6. **Exam season urgency pricing:** Raise prices 15–25% in the final 8 weeks before the LEA. Early buyers saved money; late buyers pay urgency premium. Announce it in advance.

## Revenue Model Decision Framework

When choosing or adjusting any revenue decision, answer these four questions:
1. **Does it reduce friction to the first payment?** (If yes, strongly consider it)
2. **Does it increase ARPU without killing volume?** (If yes, strongly consider it)
3. **Does it create a new buyer segment we couldn't reach before?** (If yes, explore it)
4. **Does it align the student's success with our revenue?** (If yes, it's defensible long-term)

Revenue architecture that aligns with outcomes is the only sustainable model in EdTech. SlateCamp charges for certainty — and the business model must reflect that.

## Common Revenue Architecture Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Charging too early before value is proven** — the Readiness Score must land before the paywall
2. **Building too many tiers** — confusion kills conversion; 3 tiers max at any stage
3. **Making the free tier too good** — if free solves the problem, Pro never sells
4. **Making the free tier too bad** — if free is embarrassing, acquisition dries up
5. **Ignoring exam seasonality** — flat pricing during off-season costs you during peak demand
6. **Skipping B2B too long** — one school deal = 50–200 B2C subscriptions in a single contract
7. **Not testing pricing** — run A/B tests on price points every exam cycle; the market will tell you
