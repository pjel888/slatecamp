# Philippine Architecture & Construction Law Stack

Complete reference for every law, decree, and regulation governing architecture and construction in the Philippines. Organized by authority level.

---

## Tier 1 — Primary Statutes (Laws enacted by Congress or Presidential Decree)

### PD 1096 — National Building Code of the Philippines (NBCP)
- **Enacted:** February 19, 1977 by President Ferdinand E. Marcos
- **Nature:** Presidential Decree (has force of law)
- **Scope:** Minimum standards for building design, construction, use, occupancy, and maintenance
- **Implementing Rules:** 2004 Revised IRR (published April 2005 by DPWH)
- **Annotations:** PRBoA 2008 annotations (unofficial but widely used)
- **LEA relevance:** THE primary code. Open-book material is Rules VII and VIII of the 2004 IRR. Development controls, setbacks, light and ventilation, occupancy classification all come from here.
- **Enforced by:** Building Officials under DPWH

### RA 9514 — Fire Code of the Philippines of 2008
- **Enacted:** December 19, 2008
- **Repealed:** PD 1185 (old Fire Code)
- **Scope:** Fire safety, prevention, suppression, investigation
- **Implementing Rules:** 2019 Revised IRR
- **LEA relevance:** Travel distances, exit widths, occupant loads, fire safety inspection certificates, fire zone classifications
- **Enforced by:** Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP)

### BP 344 — Accessibility Law
- **Enacted:** 1982 (Batas Pambansa Blg. 344)
- **Scope:** Accessibility of public buildings for persons with disability
- **Implementing Rules:** 2024 Revised IRR (effective August 8, 2024, published in Official Gazette Vol. 120 No. 28, July 8, 2024)
- **Previous IRR:** 2013 Illustrated IRR (superseded for dimensional values)
- **LEA relevance:** Accessible design dimensions — doors, corridors, washrooms, ramps, signage. NOT open-book. Must be memorized.
- **Enforced by:** DPWH and DOTr, coordinated with NCDA
- **Penalties:** Come from RA 9442 (2007), NOT from BP 344 itself

### RA 9266 — Architecture Act of 2004
- **Enacted:** March 17, 2004
- **Scope:** Practice of architecture, registration, licensure, professional regulation
- **Implementing Rules:** IRR of RA 9266
- **LEA relevance:** Professional practice, architect-of-record rules, who signs what, scope of practice, DAE requirements, ethical standards
- **Enforced by:** Professional Regulatory Board of Architecture (PRBoA) under PRC

### RA 9442 — Magna Carta Amendment (2007)
- **Enacted:** 2007
- **Amends:** RA 7277 (Magna Carta for Disabled Persons, 1992)
- **Scope:** Adds criminal liability for non-compliance with accessibility requirements
- **LEA relevance:** Source of penalty provisions commonly (and wrongly) attributed to BP 344
- **Penalties:** First violation: ₱50,000-₱100,000 fine or 6 months-2 years imprisonment. Subsequent: ₱100,000-₱200,000 or 2-6 years. Architects explicitly named as liable parties.

### RA 7277 — Magna Carta for Disabled Persons (1992)
- **Enacted:** 1992
- **Scope:** Rights framework for persons with disability
- **Amended by:** RA 9442 (2007), RA 10524 (2013)
- **LEA relevance:** Establishes the legal basis for BP 344 compliance. Does not specify dimensions.

## Tier 2 — Referral Codes and Standards

### NSCP 2015 — National Structural Code of the Philippines
- **Edition:** 7th Edition (2015)
- **Based on:** ASCE 7-10 (loads), ACI 318-14 (concrete), AISC 360-10 (steel)
- **Scope:** Structural design: loads, seismic, wind, concrete, steel, timber, masonry
- **LEA relevance:** Structural design area — load combinations, seismic zones, allowable stresses
- **Published by:** Association of Structural Engineers of the Philippines (ASEP)

### PD 957 — Subdivision and Condominium Buyers Protective Decree
- **Enacted:** July 12, 1976
- **Scope:** Standards for subdivision development — road widths, lot sizes, open space, utilities
- **LEA relevance:** Subdivision questions — road right-of-way, minimum lot areas, open space percentages
- **Enforced by:** HLURB (now DHSUD)

### BP 220 — Economic and Socialized Housing
- **Enacted:** March 25, 1982
- **Scope:** Standards for socialized and economic housing — different from NBCP standards
- **LEA relevance:** Has its OWN setback values, minimum lot dimensions, and RROW requirements that differ from PD 1096. Students who study only PD 1096 get subdivision questions wrong.
- **CRITICAL:** BP 220 standards are LESS restrictive than PD 1096 for housing. Different PSO, different setbacks, different lot minimums.

### PD 1067 — Water Code of the Philippines
- **Enacted:** December 31, 1976
- **Scope:** Water resources management, easements along waterways
- **LEA relevance:** Easement provisions — 3-meter easement from banks of rivers/streams. This directly affects AMBF calculations when a lot is adjacent to a waterway.
- **Key provision:** 3-meter easement from the banks of all rivers, streams, and shores of seas and lakes

### Philippine Electrical Code (PEC)
- **Current edition:** 2017
- **Based on:** NEC (National Electrical Code, US)
- **LEA relevance:** Electrical utilities area — load computation, wiring methods, grounding

### Philippine Mechanical Engineering Code
- **LEA relevance:** HVAC, elevator requirements, mechanical systems

### Philippine Plumbing Code
- **Based on:** National Plumbing Code
- **LEA relevance:** Plumbing fixture counts, pipe sizing, drainage

## Hierarchy of Laws (when provisions conflict)

1. **Constitution of the Philippines** (supreme)
2. **Laws enacted by Congress** (RA series)
3. **Presidential Decrees** (PD series — have force of law)
4. **Batas Pambansa** (BP series — have force of law)
5. **Implementing Rules and Regulations** (subordinate to their parent law)
6. **Local ordinances** (must conform to national laws)
7. **Professional standards** (SPP — contractual, not statutory)

**When national and local provisions conflict:** The more restrictive provision governs for building compliance. But the national law takes precedence as a matter of legal hierarchy.

**When two national laws conflict on the same subject:** The later enactment governs (lex posterior derogat legi priori). Example: RA 9514 (2008) supersedes PD 1185 (old Fire Code).

**When an IRR and its parent law conflict:** The parent law governs. An IRR cannot exceed the authority of its parent statute.
