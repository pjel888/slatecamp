# LEA Exam Intelligence — High-Frequency Topics, Traps, and Patterns

---

## How the LEA is Structured (Exam Day Reality)

**Day 1 (Typically):**
- Area A: History & Theory; Planning; Practice — multiple choice
- Area B: Structural Design; Building Materials; Utilities — multiple choice

**Day 2 (Typically):**
- Area C: Architectural Design and Site Planning — design problem

**Format:** Primarily multiple-choice items. Area C involves a design drawing/problem.

**Duration:** The full LEA takes 2 days. Area C alone can run 3+ hours. Pacing is a real issue — students who cannot answer quickly on Area C drawing problems run out of time.

**Total items:** Approximately 500 points across all areas.

## Area A — High-Frequency Topics and Common Traps

### History of Architecture — Most Tested

**Trap #1: Confusing structural innovations across styles**
- The arch/vault → Roman (not Greek; Greeks used post-and-lintel)
- The pointed arch + ribbed vault + flying buttress → Gothic (all three together)
- The pendentive (dome on a square base) → Byzantine
- The squinch (earlier solution to dome-on-square) → also Byzantine period but predates pendentive

**Trap #2: Attribution errors**
- Pantheon → Roman (not Greek) — concrete dome, unreinforced
- Hagia Sophia → Byzantine (not early Christian; it was built AD 537)
- Parthenon → Greek (Doric order, not Ionic — common confusion)

**High-frequency architectural terms:**
- Hypostyle hall (Egyptian/Greek) — a hall with columns
- Triforium / Clerestory / Nave / Transept / Apse — Gothic/Romanesque church elements
- Pilotis / Free plan / Free façade / Horizontal window / Roof garden — Le Corbusier's 5 Points
- Entasis — the slight convex curvature of Greek columns (anti-optical illusion device)
- Battered walls — Egyptian temple walls that lean inward

**Philippine Architecture — Always Appears**
- Bahay kubo: bamboo walls, nipa palm roof, elevated floor — known specific dimensions are tested
- Bahay na bato: combination stone base + wood upper floor, typical Vigan house
- Juan Nakpil: National Artist for Architecture (the first)
- Leandro Locsin: National Artist for Architecture, known for "floating" structures (Cultural Center of the Philippines)
- Francisco Mañosa: vernacular modern architecture (Coconut Palace)

### Theory of Architecture — Most Tested

**The Golden Ratio (φ = 1.618):** Appears in Greek temples, Renaissance compositions. Know the value.

**Fibonacci Series:** 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21... approximates the golden ratio.

**Vitruvius' Three Principles:**
- Firmitas (Strength)
- Utilitas (Utility/Function)
- Venustas (Beauty)
These are foundational — "architecture is art, science, and profession."

**Edward Hall's Proxemic Zones (memorize these distances):**
- Intimate: 0–45 cm
- Personal: 45–120 cm
- Social: 1.2–3.6 m
- Public: 3.6 m and beyond

**Kevin Lynch's 5 Elements of Urban Image:**
1. Paths (channels of movement)
2. Edges (linear boundaries)
3. Districts (two-dimensional areas)
4. Nodes (strategic focal points)
5. Landmarks (point references external to observer)

### Architectural Practice — Most Tested

**The three most heavily tested practice topics:**

1. **Who signs and seals what:** Architects sign architectural documents only. Common wrong answer: "architects can sign structural drawings." No — RLCEs sign structural plans.

2. **SPP Document numbers:** Know which doc covers which service. The exam gives a service scenario and asks which SPP document governs.

3. **Fee calculation:** If total construction cost is ₱10,000,000 and the design fee is 6%, what is the architect's fee? = ₱600,000. Straightforward but students miss it under time pressure.

**RA 9266 provisions frequently tested:**
- 15-year civil liability for structural defects
- 2-year DAE requirement (and the Master's degree exception of 1 year)
- Board composition: 1 chairman + 2 members
- Grounds for suspension/revocation of certificate

## Area B — High-Frequency Topics and Common Traps

### Structural Design — Most Tested

**Seismic trap:** The Philippines is Seismic Zone 2. Some older review materials say Zone 4 — this is from the old UBC-based classification. NSCP 2015 uses Zone 2.

**Tributary area trap:** Students mix up one-way vs. two-way slab behavior. The short dimension governs for one-way slabs.

**Beam depth rule-of-thumb (approximate, for design guidance only):**
- Simple beam: span/12 to span/15 for depth
- Continuous beam: span/15 to span/20

**Column rule-of-thumb:** In preliminary design, column size is often about 1% of tributary floor area in sq.m. converted to column area in mm².

**LRFD vs. ASD:** NSCP 2015 uses LRFD as the primary method. ASD is used for timber and some older practice. Students sometimes confuse methods in exam questions.

---

### Building Materials — Most Tested

**CHB (Concrete Hollow Block) facts — frequently tested:**
- Standard sizes: 4", 5", 6" thick (width dimension)
  - 4" CHB = 100mm × 200mm × 400mm (nominal: 10 × 20 × 40 cm)
  - 6" CHB = 150mm × 200mm × 400mm
- Course height: each course = 200mm (including mortar joint)
- Reinforced CHB walls: vertical rebar at 400–600mm OC, horizontal at every 3rd course typically
- Grouting required at jambs, corners, and at vertical rebar locations

**Cement types (frequently confused):**
- Type I (Ordinary Portland Cement — OPC): standard use
- Type II: moderate sulfate resistance
- Type III: high early strength (used when formwork needs early stripping)
- Type IV: low heat of hydration (mass concrete)
- Type V: high sulfate resistance

**Concrete mix ratios (rough approximation — verify with NPC/NSCP):**
- 1:2:4 (cement:sand:gravel) ≈ 20 MPa
- 1:1.5:3 ≈ 25 MPa
- These are volumetric ratios for site-mixed concrete

**Glass trap:** Tempered glass cannot be cut after tempering. Safety glazing required by code at certain locations (shower enclosures, near doors, low sill heights).

### Utilities — Most Tested (frequently missed by retakers)

**Plumbing — DFU (Drainage Fixture Units):**
Common DFU values:
- Water closet (toilet): 4 DFU
- Lavatory (sink): 1 DFU
- Bathtub/shower: 2 DFU
- Kitchen sink: 1.5 DFU (residential)
- Floor drain: 0 DFU (unless receiving fixture discharge)
*DFU is used to size drain pipes. Higher DFU = larger pipe required.*

**Water heater trap:** Tankless heaters have essentially infinite hot water but high demand; storage heaters have limited capacity but lower demand rate. Exam sometimes tests sizing.

**Lighting calculation trap:**
- Students confuse footcandles (fc) and lux
- 1 footcandle = 10.76 lux (approximately 10 lux = 1 footcandle)
- Minimum illuminance for office tasks: ~500 lux (verify with IESNA standards)
- Emergency lighting minimum: 10 lux average, 1 lux minimum

**HVAC trap:** "1 ton of refrigeration" is a critical value:
- 1 TR = 3.517 kW = 12,000 BTU/hr = 3,024 kcal/hr
- Rough rule: 1 TR per 25–30 sq.m. of office space (varies enormously with climate, exposure, occupancy)
- The exam may ask: "What is the cooling capacity required for a 100 sq.m. office?" → approximately 3–4 TR (depends on assumptions)

**Elevator trap:** Traction (cable) elevators required for high-rise; hydraulic suitable for low-rise only (typically up to 4–6 floors). BP 344 requires minimum cab dimensions.

## Area C — Design Problem Intelligence

**Area C is 40% of the exam. It is where the exam is won or lost.**

### What the design problem tests:
1. Space programming and quantification (can you compute total GFA correctly?)
2. Bubble diagram → schematic floor plan (correct functional relationships)
3. Code compliance (setbacks, parking, exits, accessibility)
4. Structural logic (column grid, bay sizes)
5. Site planning (orientation, access, open space)
6. Graphic communication (legible, scaled drawings in limited time)

### Common Area C errors that fail students:

**Error 1: Not reading the problem statement carefully.**
The design brief specifies required spaces and areas. Students skip spaces or invent extra spaces. The brief is the scoring rubric.

**Error 2: Ignoring development controls.**
A building design that violates setbacks or exceeds AMBF/PSO fails even if it looks architecturally good. Always compute:
- AMBF = TLA × PSO% - setbacks applied
- Parking count required
- Fire egress compliance

**Error 3: Missing accessible design (BP 344).**
Accessible routes, ramps, accessible parking, and accessible toilet are almost always required. Forgetting them loses points.

**Error 4: Underestimating floor-to-floor height.**
For RC structures: typical floor-to-floor = 3.0m–3.6m for commercial/institutional; minimum is not just the code-required ceiling height.

**Error 5: Pacing — running out of time.**
Practice drawing a full schematic floor plan in under 90 minutes. The exam does not give infinite time.

## Retaker Profile — Why Students Fail the Second Time

Based on LEA performance patterns:

**Pattern 1: Over-reliance on memorization without understanding.**
Retakers often memorize code values but cannot apply them. The exam tests application, not recitation.

**Pattern 2: Weak Area C spatial skills.**
The design problem requires actual drawing under timed conditions. Students who only read don't practice drawing fast enough.

**Pattern 3: Professional Practice gaps.**
RA 9266 provisions, SPP document scope, and ethical conduct scenarios are perceived as "easy" and under-studied.

**Pattern 4: Structural design avoidance.**
Many architecture students are uncomfortable with structural calculations. They skip NSCP load combinations and rely on vague answers.

**Pattern 5: Code math errors.**
TGFA/AMBF/PSO calculations require careful setup. Students rush and make arithmetic errors under exam pressure.
