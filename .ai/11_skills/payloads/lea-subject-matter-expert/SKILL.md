## Operating Protocol

**Before writing any architecture content:**
1. Identify which LEA Area and Topic it belongs to (→ see Syllabus Reference)
2. Identify the primary source (law, code, standard, or doctrine)
3. Verify dimensional values, section numbers, and legal provisions against known sources

**When uncertain about a specific numerical value or section number:**
- Say: *"Verify against [specific document] Section [X] before publishing."*
- Never invent code provisions. A wrong code value = a wrong answer on the LEA = a failed student.

## The Philippine Architecture Education Pipeline

Understanding the full student journey is critical to producing content that meets students where they are.

### Stage 1: BS Architecture (5 Years)
**Governed by:** CHED Memorandum Order No. 61, Series of 2006 (updated 2017)
**Total units:** 232 credit units
**Structure:**
- General Education (GEC-B, CHED CMO 59 s. 1996)
- Technical Foundation: 13 units Mathematics, 9 units Natural Sciences, 18 units Basic Engineering Sciences
- Professional Core: 36 professional courses (115 units total)
- Specialization: 3 specialization courses (9 units)
- Architecture Comprehensive Course: 3 units (5th year)
- Non-academic: PE and NSTP

**Core design studio progression** — students take Architectural Design 1–5 (or equivalent) building from small residential to complex institutional programs. The thesis/comprehensive course caps the program.

**Key subjects feeding directly into LEA taxonomy:**
| Undergraduate Subject | LEA Area |
|---|---|
| History of Architecture 1–2 | Area A, Part I |
| Theory of Architecture | Area A, Part II |
| Architectural Practice 1–2 | Area A, Part III |
| Urban and Regional Planning | Area A, Part IV |
| Structural Theory / Reinforced Concrete Design | Area B, Part I |
| Building Materials and Construction | Area B, Part II |
| Plumbing/Sanitation, Electrical, Mechanical | Area B, Part III (Utilities) |
| Architectural Design 1–5 | Area C |
| Site Planning | Area C |

**Top architecture schools by LEA performance (recent data):**
- University of Santo Tomas (UST) — consistently top-performing; ~88-89% recent pass rates
- Polytechnic University of the Philippines - Sta. Mesa (PUP) — #1 in June 2024 (93.33%)
- University of the Philippines - Diliman (UP) — June 2023: 100% pass rate (50/50)
- University of San Carlos (USC) — #3 in June 2024 (85.29%)
- De La Salle - College of Saint Benilde (DLS-CSB) — consistent top performer

### Stage 2: Diversified Architectural Experience (DAE)
**Required:** Minimum **2 years** of diversified architectural experience under a Registered Licensed Architect
**Exception:** Master's Degree in Architecture credits **1 year** of the requirement
**Documentation:** Logbooks, certificates, affidavit signed and sealed by supervising RLA
**Source:** RA 9266, Section 13; IRR of RA 9266, Rule III

DAE must be "diversified" — exposure to different project types, phases, or scales. A student who worked only on interior fit-outs for 2 years may face scrutiny.

---

### Stage 3: Licensure Examination for Architects (LEA)
**Administered by:** Professional Regulatory Board of Architecture (PRBoA) under PRC
**Frequency:** Twice yearly (typically January and June)
**Testing centers:** Manila (NCR), Baguio, Cagayan de Oro, Cebu, Davao, Iloilo, Legazpi, Lucena, Pampanga, Rosales, Tacloban, Tuguegarao, Zamboanga
**Passing standard:** Weighted average ≥ 70%; no subject below 50%

**LEA Structure and Weights:**

| Area | Coverage | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Area A | History & Theory; Planning; Practice | 30% |
| Area B | Structural Design; Building Materials; Utilities | 30% |
| Area C | Architectural Design & Site Planning | 40% |

**Recent national pass rates:**
- January 2026: 1,998/2,434 = **82.09%**
- June 2025: 1,352/2,075 = **65.16%**
- January 2025: 1,052/1,792 = **58.71%**
- June 2024: 2,094/3,370 = **62.14%**
- January 2024: 1,809/2,933 = **61.68%**

**Pass rate analysis:** First-take pass rates are generally higher than the national average. Retakers (who appear in the "re-examination" category) pull the national average down. A student with a well-structured review can reasonably target 70%+ with 3–6 months of preparation.

## Content Accuracy Standards

### The Three Levels of Fact

**Level 1 — Statutory (highest):** Facts that come directly from a law (RA, PD, EO). These are non-negotiable. Examples: passing score is 70%, DAE is 2 years, architect must be Filipino citizen.

**Level 2 — Regulatory:** Facts from implementing rules, CHED memoranda, DPWH orders. Highly reliable but subject to amendment. Example: TGFA formula, setback calculations.

**Level 3 — Doctrinal/Professional:** Facts from professional standards, SPP documents, UAP guidelines, architectural theory. Reliable but may have interpretive variation. Example: design principles, historical attributions, structural formulas.

Always cite the level and source. For Level 1 and 2 facts, cite the specific section number.

## Common High-Stakes Content Areas

These are the topics that most frequently determine pass/fail:

**Building Code Mathematics (PD 1096 IRR):**
- TGFA, AMBF, OLF, DFU calculations — must be exact
- Parking requirements by occupancy — heavily tested
- Setbacks, easements, right-of-way provisions
- Stairway requirements: width, riser/tread dimensions, headroom
- Lot coverage, floor area ratio

**Fire Code (RA 9514):**
- Occupancy classifications and their fire ratings
- Travel distance to exits
- Sprinkler requirements
- Means of egress width calculations

**Structural Design:**
- Load combinations (NSCP 2015)
- Seismic zone classification of the Philippines
- LRFD vs. ASD methods
- CHB wall specifications and reinforcing

**Professional Practice:**
- SPP Documents 201–209 scope and compensation methods
- Fees: percentage-based, lump sum, MDPE, per diem
- Who signs what (architect-of-record rules)
- Stages of architectural services

**Planning:**
- Zoning ordinance concepts
- Land use categories
- Setback rules at different levels (national vs. local)
