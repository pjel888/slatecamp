## Core Unit Economics Framework

### The Four Numbers That Run SlateCamp

```
CAC  →  How much to acquire one paying user
LTV  →  How much that user generates over their lifetime
LTV:CAC Ratio  →  The single most important health metric
CAC Payback Period  →  How fast you recoup acquisition spend
```

**Target benchmarks (EdTech B2C, SE Asia emerging market):**
| Metric | Warning Zone | Healthy | Best-in-Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| LTV:CAC | < 2:1 | 3:1 – 5:1 | > 5:1 |
| CAC Payback | > 12 months | 4–8 months | < 4 months |
| Monthly Churn | > 10% | 5–8% | < 5% |
| Free→Paid Conv. | < 2% | 3–7% | > 8% |
| Gross Margin | < 55% | 65–75% | > 75% |

**Philippine context adjustments:**
- ARPU is lower than Western EdTech — this is not a bug. It's a feature if CAC is also low.
- Education has one of the fastest CAC payback periods globally (~3.8 months median for consumer education apps)
- Referral channels are disproportionately powerful in the PH market (high community trust, word-of-mouth dominates)

## The Formulas

### Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
```
CAC = Total Sales & Marketing Spend ÷ New Paying Customers Acquired

Fully-loaded CAC includes:
- Ad spend (Meta, TikTok, Google)
- Content creation costs attributable to acquisition
- Sales salaries (if any)
- Referral program costs
- Promotional discounts offered to convert
```

**SlateCamp early-stage target CAC:** ₱150–₱350 per paying user
- Organic/referral-driven: ₱50–₱150
- Paid social (FB/TikTok): ₱200–₱500
- Blended target: < ₱300

### Lifetime Value (LTV)
```
LTV = ARPU × Gross Margin % × (1 ÷ Monthly Churn Rate)

Where:
- ARPU = Average Revenue Per User per month
- Gross Margin % = (Revenue - Variable Costs) ÷ Revenue
- Monthly Churn Rate = % of paying users who cancel each month
```

**SlateCamp LTV model:**
- ARPU target: ₱399/month (blended monthly + seasonal pass)
- Gross margin: ~75–80% (software business, minimal marginal COGS)
- Monthly churn: ~8–12% (high due to exam completion = natural graduation)
- Expected average subscription length: 3–5 months per exam cycle
- LTV estimate: ₱399 × 0.77 × (1 ÷ 0.10) = ~₱3,072

**Important:** SlateCamp has a natural "graduation churn" — users leave because they passed the exam, not because the product failed. This is healthy churn. Distinguish it from dissatisfied churn in your analysis.

### CAC Payback Period
```
CAC Payback = CAC ÷ (Monthly Revenue per Customer × Gross Margin)

Example:
CAC = ₱250
Monthly Revenue = ₱399
Gross Margin = 77%

Payback = ₱250 ÷ (₱399 × 0.77) = ₱250 ÷ ₱307 = 0.81 months
```

At these numbers, SlateCamp pays back CAC in under 1 month — extraordinarily healthy. This is the structural advantage of exam prep EdTech: high urgency, upfront payment, short subscription = fast payback.

### LTV:CAC Ratio
```
LTV:CAC = ₱3,072 ÷ ₱250 = 12.3:1
```
This is best-in-class. It signals: **invest aggressively in growth** — but only after confirming these numbers with real cohort data, not projections.

## Cohort Analysis (The Real Test)

LTV:CAC is a theoretical model. Cohort analysis is the proof. Every quarter, SlateCamp should run:

**Monthly Cohort Retention Table:**
```
Cohort: Month 0 (100%)
Month 1: X% retained
Month 2: X% retained
...
Month 6: X% retained
```

**What to look for:**
- Does retention flatten? (Good — means a core loyal segment stays)
- Does it cliff immediately? (Bad — activation problem, users didn't see value fast enough)
- Where is the sharpest drop? (Month 1 → Month 2 is almost always the crisis point)

**SlateCamp-specific retention dynamics:**
- Expect natural drop-off post-exam (healthy)
- Watch for re-enrollment from the same user for next exam cycle (powerful LTV multiplier)
- Re-enrollment rate is a hidden metric: if 30% of users re-enroll for the next exam cycle, LTV doubles

## Contribution Margin

```
Contribution Margin per User = Revenue - Variable Costs per User

Variable costs for SlateCamp include:
- Payment processing fees (~2.5–3.5% of revenue)
- Cloud hosting incremental costs per user
- Customer support cost per user
- Content licensing (if any)

Fixed costs (do NOT subtract here):
- Salaries, rent, tools, software subscriptions
```

Target contribution margin: > 70%. At this level, every new user meaningfully funds overhead coverage.

## Burn Rate & Runway

```
Monthly Burn = Monthly Fixed Costs - Monthly Revenue
Runway (months) = Cash in Bank ÷ Monthly Burn
```

**Decision triggers:**
| Runway | Action |
|---|---|
| > 18 months | Growth mode: invest in CAC, hire |
| 12–18 months | Balanced: grow cautiously, extend runway |
| 6–12 months | Fundraise immediately or cut to profitability |
| < 6 months | Crisis: cut costs, find bridge capital |

**SlateCamp pre-revenue baseline:** Philip should know the monthly cash burn at all times. This number is the heartbeat of the company.

---

## Break-Even Analysis

```
Break-Even Users = Monthly Fixed Costs ÷ Contribution Margin per User

Example:
Monthly fixed costs = ₱300,000 (salaries, tools, hosting base)
Contribution margin per user = ₱300/month
Break-even = ₱300,000 ÷ ₱300 = 1,000 paying users
```

This is SlateCamp's first major milestone: 1,000 paying subscribers = operational break-even. At this point, the company does not need external capital to survive.

## The Path to Profitability Framework

### Stage 1: Validation (0 → 100 paid users)
- **North Star metric:** Free→Paid conversion rate
- **Goal:** Prove willingness to pay at the right price point
- **Financial target:** Understand real CAC from first paid campaign
- **Don't optimize:** Revenue yet — optimize for conviction in the model

### Stage 2: Repeatability (100 → 500 paid users)
- **North Star metric:** CAC Payback Period
- **Goal:** Find the acquisition channel with the best LTV:CAC
- **Financial target:** CAC payback < 6 months in your best channel
- **Don't optimize:** Scale yet — optimize for efficiency

### Stage 3: Scale (500 → 2,000 paid users)
- **North Star metric:** MRR growth rate month-over-month
- **Goal:** Prove you can grow without economics deteriorating
- **Financial target:** MRR growing > 15% MoM while LTV:CAC stays > 3:1

### Stage 4: Expansion (2,000+ users)
- **North Star metric:** NRR (Net Revenue Retention) — includes re-enrollments
- **Goal:** Multi-revenue-stream diversification (B2B institutional plays)
- **Financial target:** Operating profitability, or VC-fundable metrics for Series A

## Pricing Sensitivity Analysis

When evaluating a price change, model the following:
```
Revenue Impact = New Price × New Volume
vs.
Revenue Impact = Old Price × Old Volume

Breakeven Elasticity = % Volume Change ÷ % Price Change
(If < 1.0, the price increase was worth it)
```

**PH EdTech pricing context (2025):**
- Filipino students are price-sensitive but will pay for perceived exam-critical value
- ₱299–₱399/month = accessible, low friction
- ₱499–₱599/month = premium positioning, works if social proof is strong
- ₱1,499–₱2,999 one-time "exam season pass" = high-intent purchase, low churn risk
- Willingness to pay increases dramatically in the 6–8 weeks before exam → price accordingly

## Common Mistakes to Call Out

1. **Confusing gross revenue with net revenue** — Refunds, payment failures, discounts must be subtracted
2. **Using average ARPU when bimodal** — Monthly and one-time buyers behave differently; segment them
3. **Ignoring re-enrollment in LTV** — Candidates who fail and retry are multi-cycle users worth 2x–3x a first-timer
4. **Calculating CAC only from paid ads** — Include content, salaries, tools, referral rewards
5. **Over-optimizing LTV:CAC before PMF** — A 10:1 ratio means nothing if you only have 20 users
6. **Seasonal blindspot** — SlateCamp revenue is spiky; don't confuse exam-season MRR with baseline MRR
