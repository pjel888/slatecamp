# Subject Quick Strategies — The Topnotcher's One-Page Per Topic

## Area A Quick Strategies

### History of Architecture
**Winning approach:** For each period, master five things — structural innovation, spatial character, decorative element, key building, cultural driver. That five-item framework answers 80% of history questions.

**Your memory anchor per period:**
- Egyptian: massive stone, post-and-lintel, hypostyle halls → Karnak
- Greek: refined post-and-lintel, three Orders, Parthenon
- Roman: arch + vault + dome + concrete → Pantheon
- Byzantine: pendentive (dome on square base) → Hagia Sophia
- Gothic: pointed arch + ribbed vault + flying buttress → Notre Dame de Paris
- Renaissance: classical revival, dome reborn → Brunelleschi, Florence Cathedral
- Le Corbusier: 5 Points (pilotis, free plan, free façade, horizontal window, roof garden)
- Philippine: bahay kubo → bahay na bato → colonial stone churches → Leandro Locsin

**Trap to avoid:** The Pantheon is Roman, not Greek. Hagia Sophia is Byzantine, not Early Christian. The flying buttress is Gothic, not Romanesque.

### Theory of Architecture
**Winning approach:** Numbers and names. Know exact values, not approximations.

**Lock these in:**
- Golden Ratio: φ = 1.618 (or 1:1.618)
- Vitruvius: Firmitas, Utilitas, Venustas (Strength, Function, Beauty)
- Hall's Proxemics: Intimate 0–45cm / Personal 45–120cm / Social 1.2–3.6m / Public 3.6m+
- Kevin Lynch's 5 Urban Elements: Paths, Edges, Districts, Nodes, Landmarks

**One question that resolves most Theory items:** What is the purpose of this architectural element or principle? Form follows function. Every design element should have a reason. If you cannot identify the reason, keep reading.

---

### Architectural Practice (RA 9266 + SPP)
**Winning approach:** RA 9266 is the law. SPP is the implementation. Learn the law first, then the SPP makes sense.

**Six facts that appear in every practice exam:**
1. 2 years DAE required (1 year credited for Master's degree)
2. Passing: 70% GWA, no subject below 50%
3. PRBoA: 1 Chairman + 2 Members (3 total)
4. Civil liability: 15 years from building completion
5. Only RLAs sign and seal architectural documents
6. SPP Doc. 202 = Design Services (primary architectural service)

**SPP Documents in one sentence each:**
- 201: Pre-design (feasibility, programming, site selection) → MDPE fee
- 202: Design (SD, DD, CD, bidding, construction admin) → % of PCC
- 203: Specialized allied services → varies
- 204: Construction services → % of PCC
- 205: Post-construction → salary or management fee
- 206: Comprehensive (all of the above) → combined
- 207: Design-build → % of total project cost

### Principles of Planning
**Winning approach:** Connect planning theories to what they were reacting against. This makes them memorable and answerable.

- Garden City (Howard): reacting against industrial city overcrowding → low-density, green-ringed new towns
- Radiant City (Le Corbusier): reacting against Garden City sprawl → towers in parks, preserve ground
- New Urbanism: reacting against suburban sprawl → walkable, mixed-use, transit-oriented
- Kevin Lynch's Image of the City: reacting against uniform, unmemorable urban environments

**PD 957 vs. BP 220 distinction (always tested):**
- PD 957: commercial subdivisions (market-rate), stricter standards, higher lot minimums
- BP 220: socialized/economic housing, relaxed standards, smaller lots allowed

## Area B Quick Strategies

### Structural Design
**Winning approach:** Understand the concept, not just the formula. The exam tests structural logic more than structural calculation.

**The five structural concepts you need cold:**
1. The Philippines is Seismic Zone 2 (NSCP 2015) — all structures must be designed for seismic loads
2. LRFD is the current NSCP method (Load and Resistance Factor Design)
3. Load combinations: 1.2D + 1.6L is the key gravity combination
4. Soft-story irregularity is the most common Filipino building failure mode in earthquakes
5. Reinforced concrete requires minimum concrete cover: 40mm for beams/columns, 20mm for slabs

**When you see a structural question:** Ask first — what material? What failure mode? What design method? These three questions narrow the answer.

---

### Building Materials and Construction
**Winning approach:** Visualize what you have seen on actual construction sites. Your DAE was a study session for this.

**Quick-fire values to know:**
- 4" CHB: 100 × 200 × 400mm (nominal)
- 6" CHB: 150 × 200 × 400mm
- Portland cement Type I = OPC = standard use
- Portland cement Type III = high early strength (fast formwork stripping)
- Tempered glass: cannot be cut after tempering; required at shower enclosures, near doors at low sill heights

---

### Utilities (Plumbing, Mechanical, Electrical, Fire)
**Winning approach:** Fire code + plumbing = most tested. Start there.

**The utility numbers you must know:**
- 1 ton of refrigeration = 3.517 kW = 12,000 BTU/hr
- Standard sprinkler head activation: 74°C (ordinary rating)
- Intermediate sprinkler activation: 79–107°C
- WC (toilet) DFU: 4 drainage fixture units
- Lavatory DFU: 1 drainage fixture unit
- 1 footcandle = 10.76 lux
- Pipe slope for horizontal soil pipe: 1:50 (approximately 2%)

**RA 9514 Fire Code quick reference:**
- Travel distance to exit: varies by occupancy, approximately 30–60m depending on sprinklering
- Exit width: minimum 900mm
- Occupant load: office = 1 person per 9.3 sq.m. net area
- Exit doors: swing in direction of egress if occupant load > 50

## Area C Quick Strategy Summary

**The single most important Area C strategy:**
Finish before you refine. A complete rough design beats an incomplete polished design every time.

**The five questions to answer before drawing:**
1. What is the AMBF? (compute from lot area and setbacks)
2. How many parking spaces are required? (apply NBCP ratio for occupancy)
3. What is the occupant load? (for fire egress sizing)
4. What are the accessibility requirements? (BP 344 checklist)
5. What are the zone relationships? (bubble diagram — 5 minutes, always)

**The Area C scoring hierarchy:**
1. All required spaces present → most important
2. Code compliance (setbacks, parking, egress, accessibility) → critical
3. Logical spatial organization → important
4. Structural logic (rational column grid) → important
5. Environmental response (orientation, ventilation) → good to have
6. Aesthetic quality of drawings → least important given time pressure

If you have to sacrifice something for time — sacrifice aesthetic refinement last of all.
