# Area C Design Protocols — The Topnotcher's Playbook

## Time Budget for a 3-Hour Design Problem

| Phase | Time | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| Reading | 5 min | Read brief twice. Underline every constraint. |
| Computation | 10 min | AMBF, parking count, occupant loads |
| Bubble diagram | 5 min | Spatial relationships, zone separation |
| Schematic plan | 50–60 min | All floors. Rough but complete. |
| Elevations/sections | 20 min | Required views only. No decoration. |
| Verification check | 5 min | Accessibility, exits, required spaces |
| Buffer | 5 min | Labels, notes, dimensions |
| **Total** | **~100–110 min** | Leave 10 min margin |

**The most common Area C failure:** Running out of time with an incomplete design. A complete rough design always scores better than an incomplete polished one. Speed over beauty.

## The Computation Sequence (Do This Every Time)

Run this before drawing a single design line:

**1. Lot Analysis**
- Record TLA (Total Lot Area)
- Identify lot type: corner, interior, through
- Apply setbacks from NBCP IRR (front, sides, rear)
- Calculate AMBF (Allowable Maximum Building Footprint)
- Calculate allowable TGFA using FLAR if specified

**2. Parking**
- Identify occupancy type
- Apply NBCP parking ratio for that occupancy
- Calculate required parking count
- Allocate accessible parking spaces (minimum per BP 344)
- Determine if parking is at-grade or podium — affects AMBF usage

**3. Occupant Load (for fire egress)**
- Apply RA 9514 occupant load factor for the occupancy
- Calculate persons per floor
- Determine minimum exit width: total occupant load ÷ 60 persons/unit × 550mm
- Check travel distance to nearest exit does not exceed code maximum

**4. Program Area Check**
- List all required spaces from brief
- Sum total required floor areas
- Verify this fits within allowed TGFA
- If over TGFA: stack vertically, not horizontally

## Bubble Diagram Rules

A bubble diagram takes 5 minutes and prevents 30 minutes of redesign. Always do it.

**Zones to establish first:**
- Public vs. private (arrival, lobbies, reception = public; offices, patient areas, bedrooms = private)
- Wet vs. dry (toilets, kitchens, utility areas clustered together — plumbing runs efficiently)
- Noisy vs. quiet (mechanical rooms, assembly areas away from sleeping, study, clinical areas)
- Service vs. served (loading, waste, staff circulation separate from public/client circulation)
- Dirty vs. clean (especially in healthcare: soiled utility separate from clean supply)

**The adjacency test:** Before committing to a layout, ask: can a user move from Space A to Space B without passing through an unrelated space? If not — rethink the adjacency.

## Common Area C Building Type Strategies

### Residential (Low-to-Medium Rise)
- Separate unit circulation from service/emergency exits
- BP 344: if building has more than one floor, elevator must be accessible
- Double-loaded corridor maximizes efficiency
- Natural ventilation: units should have openings on at least two sides

### Office Building
- Typical structural grid: 7.5m × 7.5m or 8m × 8m (accommodates 3 parking stalls per bay)
- Core placement: central core (efficient floor plate) vs. end core (better natural light)
- Required: male/female toilets every floor, fire stairs at opposite ends of floor

### School / Educational
- DepEd standards: minimum classroom size 7m × 9m (63 sq.m.) for 40 students
- Classrooms face north or northeast in the Philippines (avoid direct afternoon sun)
- Administration at the main entrance for visibility and supervision
- Gymnasium, multi-purpose hall separate from academic wing (noise management)

### Hospital / Healthcare
- Strictly separate: clean, soiled, visitor, staff, emergency circulation
- Emergency entrance separate from main entrance
- ICU adjacent to operating rooms
- Pharmacy adjacent to nursing station
- Service/loading dock at rear, separate from all patient areas
- BP 344 compliance mandatory throughout

### Religious Building (Church/Chapel)
- Narthex (entry vestibule) → Nave → Sanctuary (altar) — traditional axis
- Sacristy adjacent to sanctuary
- Acoustic considerations: no hard parallel surfaces in nave
- Parking is critical — churches have high peak occupancy

### Hotel / Lodging
- Typical guest room: minimum 28–35 sq.m. (hotel room), 24–28 sq.m. (budget)
- Service core: linen, housekeeping, service elevator separate from guest elevator
- Ground floor: lobby, reception, F&B, function rooms
- Upper floors: guest room floors — double-loaded corridor standard

## The BP 344 Checklist for Every Design Problem

Run this before you submit:

- [ ] Accessible parking: minimum 3.7m wide, nearest to entrance, ISA symbol
- [ ] Access from parking to building: level or ramp (max 1:12), no steps in path
- [ ] Entry: level landing, lever hardware, 900mm clear door opening
- [ ] Ramp (if floor change): max 1:12 slope, 1,200mm clear width, handrails both sides, landings at top/bottom
- [ ] Elevator (if multi-floor): minimum 1,100 × 1,400mm cab, 800mm door opening
- [ ] Accessible toilet: 1,700 × 1,700mm minimum clear space, grab bars
- [ ] Signage: ISA symbol at all accessible facilities

Missing accessible design in Area C is one of the most common point deductions. Make this checklist automatic.

## Drawing Speed Techniques

**Lines:**
- Use a straightedge but draw fast — controlled speed, not drafting quality
- Walls: draw both lines together (parallel motion), not separately
- Doors: a swing arc takes 3 seconds. Draw a straight line with a small arc. Clear is enough.

**Text:**
- All capitals, architectural lettering style
- Write notes as you draw — do not leave annotation for the end
- Room names in all caps, area in sq.m. right below

**Scale:**
- Verify your scale before drawing. A common mistake is starting at the wrong scale and discovering the design does not fit the paper
- For a site plan, work at 1:500 or 1:200
- For floor plans, work at 1:100 or 1:200 depending on building size

**The most important drawing speed habit:** Practice drawing complete building programs in under 60 minutes — not once, but weekly in the months before the exam. Speed on exam day is trained, not found.
