# .ai System Principles

**Last Updated:** 2026-04-16

## Core principles

1. **Non-destructive by default**
   - Do not delete files or folders as part of organizational maintenance.
   - Prefer additive structure, indexes, and routing notes over relocation.

2. **Documentation-first operations**
   - Audit the workspace before reorganizing it.
   - Record intent, scope, and constraints before changing structure.

3. **Audit-before-change**
   - Inspect what already exists.
   - Identify alignment, gaps, ambiguities, duplicates, and naming drift before introducing new scaffolding.

4. **Traceability over tidiness**
   - Every structural addition should explain what it maps to.
   - If historical work remains in a legacy location, index it instead of hiding it.

5. **Preserve historical work**
   - Archived or superseded does not mean disposable.
   - Historical plans, prompts, research, and logs may still be required for recovery or reasoning.

6. **Active vs archived clarity**
   - Mark what is active, reference, historical, superseded, or experimental.
   - Avoid silent replacement of older material.

7. **Minimal-risk organization**
   - Prefer creating folders, README files, indexes, and standards docs.
   - Avoid renames and moves unless a documented migration plan exists first.

8. **Operational consistency**
   - Route future audits, plans, tasks, specs, prompts, research, and state updates through the numbered taxonomy.
   - Preserve existing directories until a human-approved migration explicitly consolidates them.
