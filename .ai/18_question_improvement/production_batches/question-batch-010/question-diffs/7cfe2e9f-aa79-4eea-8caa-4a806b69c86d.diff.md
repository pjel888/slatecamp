## Added explanation

Laboratory testing of timber is accurate; negative embodied carbon is a valid concept reflecting carbon sequestration, not measurement error. Timber processing requires energy (drying, milling), which adds positive embodied carbon. The net embodied carbon remains negative because sequestration exceeds processing emissions. Timber sustainability depends on harvest method (sustainable forestry vs clear-cutting), replanting, and transport distance. A negative embodied carbon value does not guarantee ecological soundness. Trees sequester atmospheric CO2 during growth through photosynthesis, storing carbon in cellulose and lignin. Timber retains this stored carbon for the duration of its service life. The negative value (carbon credit) offsets the manufacturing and transport emissions. Negative embodied carbon represents carbon sequestration in organic materials. Candidates must understand that timber stores atmospheric CO2, making it a carbon sink if sustainably harvested and managed.
