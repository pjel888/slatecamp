## Added explanation

Contamination significantly affects reprocessing of all material types. Assuming recyclability regardless of contamination is unrealistic. Contamination affects wood, gypsum, and other streams as much as metals. Adhesives, paint, and foreign materials reduce value. While some contamination is inevitable, planning and discipline can minimize cross-contamination. Assuming 100% contamination is defeatist and inconsistent with lean and waste reduction principles. Contamination reduces material value and increases sorting costs. Sawdust mixed with concrete, adhesives on lumber, and paint on drywall make reprocessing difficult or impossible. Prevention at source (separate work areas, careful handling) is preferable to end-of-life sorting. Contamination prevention at source through segregation procedures and controlled work areas is more cost-effective than post-generation sorting. Candidates must address contamination risks in waste management plans.
