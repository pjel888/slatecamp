Rococo is fundamentally different in intent and aesthetic, not just a color shift; it rejects theatrical intensity. Rococo does not eliminate decoration; it transforms it into lighter, more delicate forms. This describes intensified Baroque, not Rococo's lighter, more playful approach. Rococo 'emerged in France as a reaction against Baroque heaviness' with 'asymmetry and irregularity replacing Baroque symmetry' and 'lightness and delicacy rather than weight and spectacle.' It emphasizes playfulness and aristocratic pleasure.
