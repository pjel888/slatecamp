## Added explanation

Early modernism adopted glass and steel, which are now recognized as ill‑suited to the tropics; contemporary work rejects this approach. Modernism initially imported industrial materials; contemporary design prioritizes local materials and traditional knowledge. Modernism explicitly rejected ornament; neither tradition emphasizes decoration as a primary concern. Early postcolonial modernism (1960s–70s) often used glass curtain walls and flat roofs, which performed poorly in tropical climates (heat gain, leaks). Contemporary architects like Kéré merge modernist spatial clarity with indigenous passive cooling strategies, local materials, and climate‑specific solutions.
