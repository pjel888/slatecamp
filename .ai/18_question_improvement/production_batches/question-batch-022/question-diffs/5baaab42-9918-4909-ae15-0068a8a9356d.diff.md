## Added explanation

While some energy is absorbed by the isolator material (damping), this is secondary; the primary mechanism is frequency separation (tuning). Pure energy absorption would be inefficient. Raising the natural frequency would bring it closer to equipment vibration, reducing isolation effectiveness and potentially creating resonance amplification. Isolators are designed for selective frequency isolation, not flat damping; equal attenuation across all frequencies is neither achievable nor desired. Vibration isolation works by setting the system natural frequency (f_n) well below the equipment vibration frequency (f_e). When f_e >> f_n (typically f_e > 2.5 × f_n), attenuation ∝ (f_e/f_n)². At 2–5 Hz natural frequency with equipment vibration at 30+ Hz, this produces 20–30 dB reduction (CIBSE Guide E; ASHRAE). Effective vibration isolation requires frequency separation: f_n << f_e, not mass increase or frequency matching.
