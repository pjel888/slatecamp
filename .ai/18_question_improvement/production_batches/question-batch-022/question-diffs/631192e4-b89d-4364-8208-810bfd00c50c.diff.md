Building window orientation remains fixed; seasonal monsoon effects are meteorological (cloud cover) rather than architectural reorientation. During the Southwest Monsoon (June–September), cloud cover increases and diffuse illumination dominates, causing daylight factor to decrease 20–30% relative to dry-season baseline. During monsoon season, cloud cover increases diffuse illumination uniformly across all orientations, but the reduction in direct solar radiation (which normally penetrates deeper into spaces) results in lower overall daylight factors. North-facing facades benefit from diffuse light but lose the penetration depth advantage of direct solar radiation. Tropical daylighting design must account for monsoon-induced atmospheric changes; lighting control systems require seasonal setpoint adjustments (±15–20%) to maintain consistent illuminance through differing cloud conditions.
