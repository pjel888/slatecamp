## Added explanation

Door schedules specify door characteristics but not detailed hardware requirements. Electrical schedules address power and controls, not mechanical hardware. Finishes schedules address aesthetics, not operating characteristics. A hardware schedule centralizes hardware requirements and prevents confusion. Rather than embedding hardware specifications in door specifications, a dedicated hardware schedule lists each hardware set (e.g., 'Hardware Set A: Institutional Heavy-Duty') and assigns it to door types. This clarity prevents field substitutions. Hardware schedules are specialized documents. They complement door schedules and prevent field disputes by clearly specifying lock types, hinges, closers, and mounting heights.
