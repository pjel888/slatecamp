## Added explanation

Monitoring cannot prevent cloning; a cloned card presents the same card ID as the original and is indistinguishable at the reader level. Post-hoc detection via pattern analysis (e.g., impossible travel between two locations) is possible but not foolproof. Smart cards do cost 2–3x more but provide encryption. RFID monitoring (live field strength detection) cannot eliminate cloning—an attacker can still capture the unencrypted card number passively. Facial recognition cross-check adds cost and operational friction; it does not address the root cause (unencrypted credential broadcast). It is a compensating control, not a replacement for secure credentials. Proximity card cloning occurs because unencrypted card numbers are broadcast openly. Monitoring/alerting on duplicate swipes can detect unauthorized access after the fact (forensic value) but cannot prevent a cloned card from being used to gain access. The cloning vulnerability persists at the protocol level. Smart cards with on-card encryption and mutual authentication eliminate cloning risk at the credential level. Proximity card cloning is a fundamental protocol vulnerability; monitoring mitigates risk post-access but does not prevent breach. Credential upgrade (smart card or biometric) is the only technical fix.
