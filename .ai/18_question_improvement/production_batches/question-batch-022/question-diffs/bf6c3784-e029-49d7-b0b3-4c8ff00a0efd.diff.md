## Added explanation

The torogan is one of the most architecturally distinguished indigenous dwellings in the Philippines. Key features: (1) Okir carvings — elaborate geometric designs carved into posts, beams, and panels; okir is a Maranao art form with Islamic geometric roots; (2) High-peaked roof — dramatically steep, often described as the highest of all Philippine indigenous roof forms; (3) Raised on massive logs — the structure is elevated on thick hardwood posts; (4) No interior partitions — a single large open hall reflecting the communal nature of Maranao royal households. It is NOT a bahay kubo variant — it is a distinct royal typology of the Maranao people.
