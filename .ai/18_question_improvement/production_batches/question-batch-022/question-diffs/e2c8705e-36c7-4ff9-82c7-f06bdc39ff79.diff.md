## Added explanation

PoE does not replace UPS backup; a UPS still backs the PoE switch to ensure 4–8 hours of offline operation during mains failure. PoE reduces cable types (power + data consolidated) but does not reduce total runs by 50%. Conduit pathways are similar whether using separate power or PoE. PoE is an electrical power delivery standard; RF shielding is determined by reader frequency (125 kHz, 13.56 MHz, BLE), not by power delivery method. PoE infrastructure (IEEE 802.3af/at) combines power and data over a single Ethernet cable, eliminating the need for separate 24V DC power pairs in conduit. This simplifies future reader replacements and upgrades without re-running power cables. Category 5e cabling supports PoE infrastructure (15.4 W per 802.3af port, 30 W per 802.3at port), making upfront investment in Cat 5e future-proof. Specifying Category 5e with PoE-capable infrastructure during initial installation enables cost-effective future upgrades to IP-based PoE readers without major re-cabling efforts.
