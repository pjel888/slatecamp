## Added explanation

Horizontal overhangs provide minimal shadow at 84–90° altitude; at summer solstice, tan(84–90°) produces minimal overhang projection, leaving the window exposed to direct radiation. While egg-crate louvers combine horizontal and vertical control, they are optimal for equinox periods (65–68° altitude). At summer solstice extremes, simple vertical fins are more effective and economical. Interior shading allows 60–80% solar radiation penetration; at summer solstice, direct radiation penetrates horizontally into the building, defeating occupant comfort and thermal control goals. At 84–90° solar altitude (near-vertical), the sun approaches directly overhead; vertical fins are most effective because they block the lateral radiation while allowing overhead-reflected diffuse light to penetrate. Horizontal overhangs become ineffective at high altitudes, projecting minimal shadow. Egg-crate configurations are optimal for equinox periods, not summer solstice extremes. Shading device effectiveness is latitude- and season-specific; summer solstice vertical designs differ fundamentally from equinox egg-crate or winter horizontal configurations—understanding solar altitude geometry is essential for tropical daylighting control.
