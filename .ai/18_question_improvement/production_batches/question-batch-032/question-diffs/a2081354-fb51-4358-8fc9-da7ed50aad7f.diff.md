## Added explanation

While classical principles influenced architecture, Tschumi's strategy was deconstructivist fragmentation, not harmonious proportion. Tschumi's design actively intervened with red metal follies and strategic landscape restructuring. Tschumi explicitly rejected unified harmony; this contradicts his approach. Tschumi rejected the unified, hierarchically organized park in favor of a disjunctive system where points (follies), lines (paths), and surfaces (programmed zones) operate according to different logics...productive tensions between contradictory systems could generate spatial richness and experiential complexity.'
