## Added explanation

Friction in bearing connections is secondary; compression (bearing) dominates. Slip-critical demands high preload, not minimal. Neither tensile stress nor adhesion drives bearing or slip-critical behavior. Bearing connections: the bolt acts as a pin, transferring load through bearing (compression) on the bolt shank. The bolt may slip slightly, increasing deformation. Slip-critical connections: friction between mating surfaces (not bolt bearing) transfers all load. High preload (typically 70% of UTS for A325, 80% for A490) must be maintained throughout the structure's life to ensure friction capacity exceeds service loads.
