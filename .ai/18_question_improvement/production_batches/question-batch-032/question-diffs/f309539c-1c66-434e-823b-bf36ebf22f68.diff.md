## Added explanation

Philippine standards allow non-prequalified joints if properly qualified; they are not prohibited. Prequalified joints meet a defined standard; non-prequalified joints may be equally strong if properly qualified, but qualification is expensive. Material cost is not affected; the concern is qualification testing cost and schedule impact. Prequalified joints have been extensively tested and are approved by AWS D1.1 without requiring individual qualification. Non-prequalified joints must undergo custom testing (tension, bend, CVN impact tests) to demonstrate compliance—a costly process that delays fabrication. For cost and schedule efficiency, designers should default to prequalified joints unless there is a compelling reason otherwise.
