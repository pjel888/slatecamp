## Added explanation

While decorative, the triforium serves a practical function as a secondary light source and circulation space, only possible due to skeletal structure. The triforium's existence directly depends on the flying buttress system; it would not be possible with massive Romanesque walls. Gothic cathedrals do include triforia as part of their vertical articulation of interior space. The article defines the triforium as 'a secondary gallery above the main arcade, below clerestory.' Because flying buttresses allow walls to be thin screens rather than massive supports, secondary galleries like the triforium become architecturally possible.
