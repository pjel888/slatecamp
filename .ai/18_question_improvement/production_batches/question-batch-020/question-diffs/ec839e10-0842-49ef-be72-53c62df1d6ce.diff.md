## Added explanation

The Great Mosque of Córdoba is an earlier Umayyad mosque with hypostyle plan and horseshoe arches, not a palace. The Alcázar of Seville is a palace complex but dates to the 14th century and shows Islamic-Christian fusion rather than pure Andalusian apex. Qasr al-Hayr al-Sharqi is a Umayyad desert palace in Syria, not an Andalusian or Nasrid building. The Alhambra (1354–1391 CE) is explicitly identified as the 'apex of Andalusian palace design' with 'multiple interconnected courtyards...stucco and tile walls, muqarnas ceilings, water channels throughout, slender colonnades.'
