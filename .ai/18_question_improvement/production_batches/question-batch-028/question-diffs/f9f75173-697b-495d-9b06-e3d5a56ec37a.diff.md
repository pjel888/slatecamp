## Added explanation

High-efficiency AC is important but cannot replace the energy savings of passive design in tropical climates. Relying on mechanical systems increases operational costs and vulnerability to power outages (common in monsoon season). LED lighting reduces lighting energy (typically 10–15% of total), but climate control (heating/cooling) dominates office energy consumption (~40–60%). Addressing the larger load is more impactful. Double-glazing in tropical climates is less critical than in cold climates; it may increase cost without proportional benefit. Solar shading and ventilation are more cost-effective. In the Philippines' hot, humid climate, passive design—natural ventilation, orientation to catch prevailing breezes (NE/SW in many regions), solar shading (deep overhangs, louvers), and thermal mass (exposed concrete ceilings)—dramatically reduces cooling load. This is more cost-effective than active systems and improves resilience. Tropical climate design must prioritize passive cooling (ventilation, shading, mass) before active systems. This aligns with BERDE's emphasis on climate-responsive design.
