## Added explanation

Heat and compression describe thermal modification, not pressure treatment. Forced air and submersion describe dip treatment, which is less effective than pressure treatment. Steam and soak describe kiln drying or surface treatment, not pressure treatment. Pressure treatment uses a vacuum phase (extracting air from the wood) followed by a high-pressure phase (100—200 psi) to inject preservative solution deep into the lumber. Pressure treatment is a two-phase process: vacuum + high-pressure injection. This is more effective than any single-phase method.
