## Added explanation

Brutalism emerged from modernism but reasserted material expression and sculptural presence; it did not reject ornament absolutely but celebrated structural monumentality and concrete expressiveness. Deconstructivism fragmented geometric forms but did not organize around the principle of reduction; it celebrated complexity through non-Euclidean geometry. Postmodernism explicitly rejected the ban on ornament, reinstating decoration, historical reference, and semantic richness as legitimate design tools. Mies van der Rohe's 'less is more' became the philosophical core of International Style Modernism, which eliminated ornament, pursued functional reduction, and celebrated geometric essentiality as universal design principles.
