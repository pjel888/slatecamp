## Added explanation

Published standards provide foundation but don't eliminate the need for specific project criteria. The architect must bridge the standard to the project needs. Prescriptive specs naming brands are appropriate for certain items but not required here; the problem is lack of measurable criteria. ASHRAE 62.1 covers indoor air quality ventilation; it doesn't define energy efficiency (SEER) or noise levels. The architect must specify these independently. Citing a standard (ASHRAE 62.1) is not sufficient. The architect must also state measurable acceptance criteria: minimum SEER rating (e.g., 13), CFM output, filtration efficiency (MERV rating), and noise level (dB). The specification delegates too much judgment to the standards without stating how the contractor will demonstrate compliance. Reference standard specifications must still include project-specific performance metrics. Standards are tools, not substitutes for specification completeness.
