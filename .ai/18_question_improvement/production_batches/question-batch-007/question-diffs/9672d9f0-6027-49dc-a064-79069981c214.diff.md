## Added explanation

Romanesque flying buttresses are not primarily load-bearing; Gothic ones are. Location and structural function differ significantly between periods. Durham Cathedral and other Romanesque buildings do employ early flying buttresses, though modestly. The article explicitly contrasts them: 'Early flying buttresses appear in Romanesque (e.g., Durham Cathedral)...they remain internal half-arches or external structures of modest scale. They do not yet define the structure.' In contrast, 'Mistake 1: Confusing Romanesque flying buttresses with Gothic ones. Romanesque flying buttresses are internal or modest external structures; they do not dominate the structural frame. Gothic flying buttresses are external, load-bearing, and define the skeleton.' Gothic flying buttresses are external, load-bearing, and define the skeleton.'.
