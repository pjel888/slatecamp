## Added explanation

Regularity is the first pillar—verifying procedures were followed. Legality is the second pillar—verifying code and law compliance. Economy is the third pillar—verifying cost control and resource efficiency. Aesthetics, while important to architects, is not a statutory COA audit criterion. COA audits examine accounts for revenue/expenditures and promulgate rules against irregular, unnecessary, excessive, extravagant, or unconscionable uses of funds. Aesthetics is not an audit criterion. COA's three audit pillars are regularity, legality, and economy—aesthetics is not a COA audit measure.
