## Added explanation

Anodizing protects the aluminum surface but does not prevent galvanic corrosion at contact points where the oxide film is penetrated. Copper and stainless steel are more cathodic than aluminum; they accelerate galvanic corrosion, not inhibit it. Galvanic corrosion occurs between any dissimilar metals in an electrolyte (wet environment), regardless of ferrous/non-ferrous classification. Aluminum is more active (anodic) than stainless steel and copper (both cathodic). Direct contact without isolation creates galvanic cells; aluminum corrodes preferentially. Prevention requires EPDM gaskets, nylon washers, or non-conductive spacers at all metal interfaces.
