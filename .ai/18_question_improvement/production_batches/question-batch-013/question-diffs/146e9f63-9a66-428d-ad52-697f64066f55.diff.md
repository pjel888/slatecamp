## Added explanation

Social architecture may involve community labor but does not require it; the essential feature is shared decision-making authority, not labor division. Social architecture applies to all building types, not only public spaces. The distinction concerns decision-making process, not programmatic type. Extensive practical examples (Lucien Kroll's housing, participatory slum upgrading, Elemental's incremental housing). Social architecture is an epistemological shift: communities possess place-based knowledge equivalent to professional expertise. Rather than professional authority designing for passive users, social architecture is a process negotiated among stakeholders where communities shape design decisions fundamentally.
