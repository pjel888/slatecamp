## Added explanation

Type I (chromic acid) produces only thin films (5–10 µm) with limited corrosion protection—inadequate for exposed architectural work. Both Type I and Type II can achieve color uniformity; the distinction is film thickness and durability, not aesthetics. Cost is not the primary driver; corrosion protection is. Type II's superior film thickness justifies its standard use in architecture. Type II produces a medium-thickness oxide film (10–25 µm) that provides excellent corrosion resistance and aesthetic appearance. This is the standard choice for architectural extrusions exposed to weathering.
