## Added explanation

Vigan is the best-preserved Spanish colonial town in Asia and was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage City in 1999 precisely because it retains the Laws of the Indies urban structure: a central plaza surrounded by the cathedral, seminary, and municipal building, with streets radiating outward in a gridiron (quadricula) pattern. Calle Crisologo runs parallel to the plaza and exemplifies the arcade (portales) requirement of the Laws — arcaded walkways providing shade and shelter. The Laws also specified portales along the four principal streets — a tropical adaptation that 'provided considerable convenience to the merchants who generally gathered there.'
