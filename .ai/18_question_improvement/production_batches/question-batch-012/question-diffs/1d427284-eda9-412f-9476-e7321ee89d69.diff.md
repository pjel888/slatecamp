## Added explanation

While ashlar is lighter than some masonry, precision fitting was not primarily about weight reduction; it was about structural integrity through mechanical precision. Apadanas require columned support for their vast interiors; dry ashlar masonry does not eliminate the need for columns in open halls. While tight fitting might reduce moisture infiltration, this is not the primary structural advantage cited; the advantage is in the mechanical precision of fitting. The article defines: 'Dry ashlar: Ashlar masonry fitted so precisely that mortar is unnecessary; demonstrates precision in stone cutting.' This explains that the tight fit allows stones to remain in place through mechanical compression without adhesive.
