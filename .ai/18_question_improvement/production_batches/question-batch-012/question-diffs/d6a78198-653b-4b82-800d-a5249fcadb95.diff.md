## Added explanation

The Laws of the Indies included portales (arcaded walkways) as a specific tropical adaptation — the Spanish crown recognized that colonial urban planning in Southeast Asia required protection from sun and rain. The ordinance's own language stated the portales were 'of considerable convenience to the merchants who generally gathered there.' Streets had to open onto the plaza 'without encountering these porticoes' — meaning the porticoes were set back to maintain clear sidewalks. This is a key detail that distinguishes Laws of the Indies urbanism in Asia from its American applications.
