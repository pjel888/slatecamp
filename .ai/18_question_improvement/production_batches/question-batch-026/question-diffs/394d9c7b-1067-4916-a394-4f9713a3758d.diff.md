## Added explanation

18% moisture is actually slightly high, not low; 12—15% is the target. Modern glues (PVA, polyurethane, epoxy) do require clamping pressure to achieve full strength and proper surface contact. Mortise-and-tenon joints are commonly glued; pins or wedges are used as supplementary security, not as replacements for glue. Optimal gluing conditions require wood moisture of 12—15% and clamping pressure of 100—200 psi. At 18% moisture, the wood is wetter than optimal; without clamping pressure, the glue cannot develop full strength and may not penetrate the joint surfaces uniformly, resulting in weak spots and potential early failure. Glue strength is a function of moisture content, clamping pressure, and curing time; neglecting any factor reduces joint capacity significantly.
