## Added explanation

Both nails and bolts can rust; corrosion resistance depends on material, not form factor. Nails are used in Philippine hardwoods; the limitation is not material compatibility but load capacity. Threads contribute to bolt strength, but the primary load path in wood is bearing and friction, not thread-holding; nail strength is similarly fiber-based. Nails develop strength through friction against wood fibers and bearing at the nail shank. A single nail develops only 150—200 lbf in shear; multiple nails in staggered patterns are needed for higher loads. Bolts develop bearing strength across the full bearing area, which is larger, and transfer load through the bolt shank and threads together. Understand the mechanical basis of fastener strength: nails = friction + small bearing area; bolts = friction + larger bearing area + mechanical coupling.
