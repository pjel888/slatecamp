## Added explanation

Leandro Locsin (1928–1994, National Artist 1990) belongs to the First Wave of Filipino architecture (Modernist/Brutalist, 1950s–1970s). His signature concept is the 'floating volume' — the appearance of massive concrete forms levitating above the ground, seen most dramatically in the Tanghalang Pambansa (CCP Main Theater). The optical illusion is created by recessing the structural base and cantilevering the upper mass. Critically, this concept is inspired by the bahay kubo's raised floor — the under-floor ventilation space is reinterpreted as a visual void beneath a hovering concrete volume. Locsin's buildings are Brutalist concrete, not Neo-Vernacular. Mañosa is the Neo-Vernacular architect.
