## Added explanation

Domes are inherently non-planar; their efficiency comes from their three-dimensional curvature. Geodesic domes are rigid frame structures, not cable-based tensile structures. A dome is a 3D extension of an arch, but its geometry makes it more efficient at distributing loads without heavy external buttressing. Geodesic domes are 3D triangulated spheres (or hemispheres) where members follow great-circle paths. This geometry distributes loads evenly in all directions, minimizing bending moments and allowing efficient use of material. They can enclose very large areas with minimal self-weight. Geodesic domes use triangulation and spherical geometry to maximize span-to-weight ratios.
