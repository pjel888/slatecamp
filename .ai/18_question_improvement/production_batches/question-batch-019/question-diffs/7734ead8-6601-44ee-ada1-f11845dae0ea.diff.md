## Added explanation

Removing all vegetation and monoculturing climax species is costly, less resilient, and ignores successional processes. While natural succession occurs, it is slow (30–50+ years for mature forest). Active management achieves restoration faster. Active successional management accelerates restoration: remove invasive species that block succession, manage pioneer growth to create appropriate shade conditions, and introduce native shade-tolerant understory and eventual climax species. This harnesses natural succession while reducing timeline and ensuring diversity. Exotic fruit trees compete with native restoration goals and may become invasive.
