## Added explanation

While Palladio's *Four Books* influenced Renaissance and later revivals, they did not catalyze the shift to Neoclassicism; Brunelleschi did not write a major treatise. Baroque and Rococo publications were what Neoclassicism reacted against, not catalysts for the shift. Medieval and Renaissance works preceded Neoclassicism and were historical references, not catalysts for its specific emergence. The article lists key catalysts: 'Archaeological expeditions to Pompeii and Herculaneum (beginning 1748) revealed actual Roman buildings' and 'James Stuart and Nicholas Revett's *Antiquities of Athens*** (Volume I, 1762; continued through 1816) provided measured drawings of Greek temples.'
