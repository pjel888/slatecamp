## Added explanation

E is a constant material property of steel and does not fluctuate with grade designations[cite: 1]. Higher strength steel allows for higher loads before yielding, but it does not change the initial stiffness (slope of the elastic region)[cite: 1]. E is an intrinsic material property; geometry determines the moment of inertia (I), not the modulus of elasticity[cite: 1]. E = 200,000 MPa (200 GPa) is considered a material constant for all structural steel grades. While yield strength (Fy) and ultimate strength (Fu) vary by grade, the stiffness (E) does not[cite: 1]. Structural steel E is constant at 200,000 MPa regardless of grade[cite: 1].
