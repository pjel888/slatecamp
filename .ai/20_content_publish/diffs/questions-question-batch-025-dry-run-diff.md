| `244ccd5b-13d7-4db2-a33c-7cfb603e57f4` | explanation |  | ρ/g×h is dimensionally incorrect. Curing duration doesn't increase pressure; longer curing allows pressure reduction. Admixture dosage (accelerators/retarders) affects set time, not directly pressure. Concrete form pressure ≈ ρg×h (weight density × gravity × fluid height). For vertical forms, max pressure occurs at depth. Warmer concrete (~30°C) sets faster than cool concrete (~10°C), so strength develops quicker, reducing time the concrete acts as a fluid. Result: lower peak pressure on forms. Placement rate also affects pressure—rapid placement maintains higher fluid height longer. |

| `08787dff-085a-46c6-9592-85eafca7d1dd` | explanation |  | By definition, a non-bearing wall does not support the floors above. Shear walls are part of the lateral force-resisting system; a curtain wall is a non-structural component. NSCP Chapter 2 (Loads) and Chapter 6 (Wind) explicitly require seismic and wind design for non-structural components like cladding. Curtain walls must be designed to 'drift' with the building frame during an earthquake. The anchors must prevent the wall from falling off (out-of-plane forces) while allowing for horizontal movement relative to the floors. Curtain walls must accommodate inter-story drift and resist lateral pressures. |
