You open an LEA question booklet and see a black-and-white photograph of a white box elevated on slender columns, with a long strip window and a garden visible on the roof. The question asks you to identify all five architectural principles this house embodies. You are looking at the Villa Savoye — and you need to name every point and explain why each one matters.

## Core Concepts

- **The Five Points of Architecture (Les 5 points d'une architecture nouvelle, 1927):** Le Corbusier and his cousin Pierre Jeanneret published these five principles as a manifesto for a new architecture made possible by reinforced concrete frame construction. They represent the liberation of the building plan, section, and facade from the constraints of load-bearing masonry walls.

- **The Pilotis:** Slender reinforced concrete columns that elevate the building mass off the ground, freeing the ground plane for circulation, gardens, or parking. The building appears to float.

- **The Free Plan (Plan Libre):** Because the columns — not the walls — carry the structural loads, interior partitions can be placed anywhere, reconfigured, or removed entirely. Space becomes fluid and adaptable.

- **The Free Facade (Façade Libre):** The exterior wall is a non-load-bearing curtain hung from the floor slabs. It can be composed freely — punched with windows of any size, clad in any material — without structural consequence.

- **The Ribbon Window (Fenêtre en longueur):** Horizontal strip windows run uninterrupted along the facade, made possible by the free facade. They flood the interior with uniform natural light, eliminating the dark corners of traditional punched-window rooms.

- **The Roof Garden (Toit-terrasse):** The flat roof is reclaimed as usable outdoor space — a garden that compensates for the greenery displaced by the building's footprint. It also provides thermal insulation.

- **"Une maison est une machine-à-habiter" (A house is a machine for living in):** Le Corbusier's most famous aphorism, from Vers une Architecture (Toward an Architecture, 1923). It argued that houses should be designed with the same rational efficiency as ocean liners, airplanes, and automobiles — engineered for function, health, and modern life.

## LEA Exam Traps and Common Mistakes

- **Trap 1: Confusing "Ribbon Window" with "Curtain Wall."** A ribbon window (fenêtre en longueur) is a horizontal strip window within a wall, not a full-floor glass curtain wall. Curtain walls came later with Mies van der Rohe's glass skyscrapers. On the LEA, these are distinct concepts.

- **Trap 2: Attributing the Dom-ino system to Mies van der Rohe.** The Dom-ino frame (flat slabs + columns + stair = open plan) is Le Corbusier's invention (1914). Mies developed his own open-plan system using steel frames. Both enable a free plan, but the Dom-ino is concrete and belongs to Corbusier.

- **Trap 3: Thinking "Machine for Living" means the building looks like a machine.** The phrase means the house should work as efficiently as a machine — rational planning, good ventilation, proper light, hygienic design — not that it should literally resemble an engine or factory.

## Board-Prep Mnemonics

- **"Please Free Five Rooftop Friends" = Pilotis, Free plan, Free facade, Ribbon (fenêtre), Roof garden.** The first letter of each word maps to the five points in order. Visualize five friends standing freely on a rooftop to lock it in.

- **"Dom-ino Dominates" — Slab + Column + Stair = Freedom.** The Dom-ino diagram is the structural DNA behind all five points. If you can sketch the Dom-ino, you can derive every point from it: columns free the plan, slabs free the facade, flat roof becomes a garden.

## Active Recall Questions

**Question 1.** Le Corbusier's Maison Dom-ino (1914) is significant because it introduced:

A. The first curtain wall system using steel and glass
B. A reinforced concrete frame of flat slabs on columns, enabling the free plan and free facade
C. The first parametrically generated building structure
D. The concept of "streets in the sky" for mass housing

> **Correct Answer: B.** The Dom-ino system — flat reinforced concrete floor slabs supported on slender RC columns with a freestanding stair — is the structural prototype that made Le Corbusier's Five Points possible. It freed the plan from load-bearing walls and freed the facade from structural duty.

**Question 2.** Which building is considered the single most complete demonstration of all five of Le Corbusier's Five Points of Architecture?

A. Unité d'Habitation, Marseille
B. Chapelle Notre-Dame du Haut, Ronchamp
C. Villa Savoye, Poissy
D. Chandigarh Capitol Complex

> **Correct Answer: C.** The Villa Savoye (1931) in Poissy, France, is the textbook demonstration of all five points: pilotis elevate the house, the plan is free, the facade is free, ribbon windows wrap the volume, and a roof garden crowns the building. The Unité demonstrates some points but is primarily known for Brutalist material expression.

**Question 3.** Le Corbusier's phrase "une maison est une machine-à-habiter" was published in which foundational text?

A. Complexity and Contradiction in Architecture (1966)
B. Vers une Architecture / Toward an Architecture (1923)
C. Learning from Las Vegas (1972)
D. The International Style (1932)

> **Correct Answer: B.** The "machine for living" aphorism appears in Le Corbusier's manifesto Vers une Architecture (1923), where he argued that modern houses should be designed with the same rational efficiency as ships, airplanes, and automobiles. Option A is Venturi; Option C is Venturi & Scott Brown; Option D is Hitchcock & Johnson.
