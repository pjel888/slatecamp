## CPD Activity Types for Architects

### Structured Learning Activities

Structured learning encompasses formal courses, seminars, workshops, and conferences offered by accredited CPD providers. These activities include in-person training, online courses, webinars, and hybrid formats combining classroom and digital instruction. Structured learning typically awards higher credit units because it involves systematic curriculum, instructional expertise, and formal evaluation of learning outcomes. Architects completing structured activities receive certificates documenting credit hours and competency areas covered.

Structured learning activities address specific knowledge gaps identified in the professional track categories. Courses on building codes, structural design, sustainable architecture, and project management constitute structured learning. Professional conferences where architects attend presentations and seminars on current practice issues qualify as structured learning. Online platforms offering architecture-specific courses with proctored assessments and completion certification provide structured learning credits. The Board recognizes only programs delivered by accredited providers meeting minimum quality standards.

### Self-Directed Learning Activities

Self-directed learning allows architects to pursue professional development through independent study, research, and knowledge acquisition without formal instruction. Activities include reading professional journals, articles, and textbooks; participating in professional webinars or podcasts; conducting research on emerging architectural technologies; or studying new building standards and codes. Self-directed learning is flexible and individually tailored but typically awards modest credits reflecting the individual effort involved.

Self-directed learning requires documentation proving completion and relevance to professional development. Architects maintain records of materials studied, dates of completion, and time devoted to each activity. Learning outcomes must be identifiable and connected to architectural practice improvements. Professional magazines, industry publications, and online resources from recognized architectural organizations constitute acceptable self-directed learning materials. The key distinction is that self-directed learning is individually selected, self-paced, and self-evaluated, without external instruction.

### Work-Based Learning and Professional Experience

Work-based learning includes professional practice activities that develop skills and knowledge through actual work execution. Mentoring junior architects, leading complex projects with technical challenges, serving on professional committees, or contributing to professional code development count as work-based credits. This category recognizes that experienced architects develop knowledge through challenging professional assignments that require expanded competencies. Documentation typically includes project descriptions, committee participation records, or mentor affidavits confirming developmental activities.

Work-based learning credits require demonstration that the work activity enhanced professional competencies beyond normal practice execution. Supervising architects earn credits for mentoring junior staff, documenting skills transferred and professional growth stimulated. Architects leading innovative projects with new technologies or methodologies earn credits reflecting the knowledge development involved. Participation in professional committees establishing standards or policies constitutes work-based learning. Credits are modest relative to structured learning because the primary engagement is professional work, not formal instruction.

### Teaching and Presentation Activities

Architects who teach courses, deliver presentations at conferences, conduct workshops, or publish articles on professional topics earn CPD credits. Teaching a course at a university or professional organization awards significant credits reflecting the preparation, expertise, and time commitment. Delivering conference presentations on architectural research or innovation provides credits proportional to presentation scope. Publishing peer-reviewed articles or professional papers generates credits recognizing the intellectual contribution and expertise demonstration.

Teaching activities must be documented with course outlines, attendance records, or presentation materials. Architects demonstrating teaching expertise contribute to professional development of others while maintaining their own learning through preparation and engagement. Published articles or research in recognized professional journals provide evidence of intellectual contribution. The Board values these activities because educators and researchers advance the profession's knowledge base. Credits are substantial because teaching requires deep expertise and communication skills.

Research activities including conducting studies on architectural practice, testing new materials or methodologies, or investigating emerging design approaches generate CPD credits. Architects participating in professional research projects, contributing to professional organization studies, or conducting independent research on architectural topics earn credits. Innovation projects implementing new technologies, sustainable design methods, or construction processes that enhance professional knowledge qualify for credits.
