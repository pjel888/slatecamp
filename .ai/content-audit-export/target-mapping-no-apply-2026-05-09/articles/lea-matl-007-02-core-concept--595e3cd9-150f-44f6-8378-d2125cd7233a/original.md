## What You Need to Know for the LEA
## Learning Goal

Classify wall finishes (paint, wallpaper, gypsum board, plywood paneling, tile, stone cladding, fabric, metal panel, glass) by aesthetic effect, durability, installation method, and fire-resistance rating for interior wall applications.
## Core Concept

Wall finishes range from paint systems (latex, enamel, epoxy) to ceramic tile, wallpaper, natural stone veneer, wood paneling, and fiber cement board. Paint is the dominant finish globally and locally in the Philippines. Paint films must bond to properly prepared substrates and maintain adhesion through environmental cycling (humidity, temperature, UV).

Paint performance depends on film thickness and substrate preparation. Dry film thickness (DFT) is measured in micrometers (µm) and ranges from 50µm for primer to 75-150µm for topcoats. Surface preparation—sanding, priming, caulking—is non-negotiable; 90 percent of paint failures stem from substrate defects, not paint itself. Moisture in concrete or gypsum causes blistering and delamination within months.

The ASTM D3359 cross-hatch adhesion test quantifies bond strength. The test cuts a lattice pattern through the film to the substrate, applies tape, and measures adhesion loss on a 0-5 scale (5B = no loss, best; 0B = complete loss, worst). A rating of 5B (no adhesion loss) indicates excellent performance. A rating of 0B (complete adhesion loss) indicates a failed system. For interior drywall, ratings of 3 or better are typical; for concrete, ratings of 4 or better are expected if substrate is properly prepared.

In the Philippines, Boysen (Pacific Paint), Davies, and Island Paints dominate the market. Boysen's Permacoat (acrylic latex) and Acrytex (enamel/acrylic hybrid) are industry standards. Davies Paints are recognized for color range and premium positioning. These brands publish drying time, coverage rate (8-12 m²/L typical), and VOC limits that must match local air-quality requirements.

Wallpaper, while aesthetic, demands smooth drywall surfaces (sandpaper grit 220+) and proper adhesive formulation. Ceramic tile on walls follows the same adhesion principles as floor tile but with relaxed slip-resistance requirements. Natural stone veneer (marble, granite) requires moisture barriers on substrate to prevent salt efflorescence and spalling in tropical climates. Hardiflex (fiber cement board) offers moisture and fire resistance for wet rooms and exterior walls, avoiding traditional gypsum drywall susceptibility to mold.

## Philippine Construction Context

The Philippines' high humidity (60-95% year-round) and seasonal rain create moisture challenges for interior finishes. Boysen and Davies paint systems are formulated with fungicides to resist mold in damp conditions, a critical specification often overlooked. Construction timelines frequently push application in marginal conditions (high humidity, rain-threatening); insisting on ASTM D3276 conditions (23°C, &lt;50% RH) delays projects but prevents future delamination.

Gypsum drywall, the standard substrate, absorbs moisture rapidly. Hardiflex fiber cement board offers mold resistance (e.g., HardiFlex NexGen with MoldBlock Technology) for kitchens, bathrooms, and ground-floor walls in flood-prone areas. Hardiflex costs 20-30% more than drywall but eliminates replacement risk in moisture-heavy zones.

Stone veneer (marble, granite imported or quarried locally) requires moisture barriers and proper flashing to prevent salt efflorescence, visible as white crystalline deposits. Wallpaper, while aesthetically popular in residential projects, must be paired with fungicide-treated primers in high-humidity zones.

Boysen brands like KNOxOUT (marketed as air-purifying with claimed NOx-reducing properties via titanium dioxide) appeal to premium projects seeking air-quality claims, though durability remains tied to substrate preparation.

## How It's Tested

**Question Type 1 (Surface Preparation):** A painter applies latex topcoat to gypsum drywall sanded with 120-grit paper and not wiped. What will likely occur within 6-12 months? Students must recognize that inadequate surface prep (grit &lt;220) and dust contamination cause delamination (ASTM D3276 violations).

**Question Type 2 (Moisture Management):** A ground-floor bathroom in Cavite shows paint blistering 4 months after application. Concrete substrate moisture test shows 5% CM. What is the root cause and solution? Answer: moisture >3% CM per ASTM F2170 causes vapor-induced blistering. Require moisture barrier or defer painting until concrete cures.

**Question Type 3 (Adhesion Testing):** A project specification requires ASTM D3359 adhesion rating of 4 or better. A site test shows rating 2. Is the coating acceptable and why? Answer: No. Rating 2 indicates 5-15% adhesion loss, unacceptable per spec and likely to fail during service life.

**Question Type 4 (Material Selection):** Specify a finish system for a high-humidity kitchen in Metro Manila. Options: latex paint, enamel, or wallpaper. Which offers best moisture resistance? Answer: Enamel or latex with fungicide primer (Boysen formulations); wallpaper is unsuitable without mold-resistant base primer.
