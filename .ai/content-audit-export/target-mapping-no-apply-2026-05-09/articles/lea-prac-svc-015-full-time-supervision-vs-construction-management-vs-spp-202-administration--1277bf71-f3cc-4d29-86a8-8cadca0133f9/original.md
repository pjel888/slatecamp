## Full-Time Supervision vs. Construction Management vs. SPP 202 Administration
## Learning Goal

Understanding the distinctions among supervision, construction management, and design administration clarifies professional roles and contractual obligations. SPP 204 addresses full-time supervision, SPP 205 covers construction management, and SPP 202 establishes regular design services standards. Each service category addresses specific project phases and professional responsibilities with distinct compensation methods and liability frameworks. Architects must clearly communicate these distinctions to clients to establish appropriate engagement arrangements.

### Full-Time Supervision: Design Intent Verification

Full-Time Supervision services focus on quality control and design verification throughout construction. Supervisors ensure that materials, workmanship, and methods conform to design specifications and technical standards. Authority remains limited to technical matters within the design scope. Supervisors verify contractor compliance without managing overall project coordination or cost control. This service emphasizes architectural expertise in evaluating construction quality against design intent.

### Construction Management: Project Delivery Coordination

Construction Management services encompass broader project coordination beyond quality verification. Construction managers address schedule progression, cost control, multi-trade coordination, and resource management. Authority extends to business decisions affecting project delivery including contractor scheduling, cost management, and change order processing. Construction managers function as owner representatives managing relationships among all project participants. This service emphasizes project management expertise and business acumen alongside technical knowledge.

### SPP 202 Regular Design Services: Comprehensive Engagement Model

SPP 202 Design Administration establishes the architect's involvement from concept through occupancy. Regular design services encompass project definition, schematic design, design development, contract documents, bidding/negotiation, and construction phases. Supervisory functions form one component of comprehensive design administration. Administration services may include design development consultation, construction issue resolution, and building operation guidance beyond supervision alone. This integrated model maintains architect involvement across all project phases.

### Key Functional Differences

Supervision focuses exclusively on design compliance verification during construction. Construction management adds business coordination, cost control, and stakeholder management. Design administration integrates design decisions with construction execution and post-occupancy support. These distinctions establish different professional relationships, authority scopes, and compensation methods. Project requirements and owner preferences determine which service model best suits specific engagements.

### Compensation Method Distinctions

Full-time supervision typically employs percentage of construction cost or fixed fees based on hours and site presence. Construction management uses percentage, hourly, or performance-based methods reflecting broader responsibility scope. Design administration encompasses fees for entire project delivery from concept through occupancy. Compensation methods align with service scope and professional responsibility intensity. Clear fee structures prevent disputes and establish mutual understanding of value exchange.

### Legal Responsibility and Liability

Supervisors bear professional responsibility for identifying non-conformance and recommending correction. Construction managers assume coordination accuracy and cost control responsibilities within their authority scope. Design administrators maintain continuous professional involvement throughout project delivery. Article 1723 of the Civil Code imposes liability on the contractor and the architect/engineer who directed the structural work if it fails due to defects in materials or workmanship. Limitation of authority agreements protect professionals when operating within defined scope.
