## Obligations to Contractors and Suppliers — Independence and Impartiality
## Learning Goal
After reading this article, you should understand the key concepts tested in the LEA for this topic, including the prohibition on gifts and commissions from contractors, the market discount exception, and the architect's mediatory role.

The architect's role as mediator between client interests and contractor performance requires absolute independence from financial or personal entanglement with construction professionals. The Code of Ethical Conduct establishes strict prohibitions on gifts, commissions, and arrangements that would compromise professional judgment.

### The Mediatory Function

Code Section 3 establishes the architect as a fair safeguard of contractor interests while serving the client. This dual responsibility creates tension: the architect must ensure the client receives quality work at agreed terms while protecting the contractor from impossible requirements or unfair contract interpretation. This balance demands complete impartiality.

The architect exercises quasi-judicial functions between client and contractor. Similar to judges who recuse themselves from cases involving financial interest, architects cannot accept arrangements creating reciprocal obligations or debt.

### Prohibition on Gifts and Commissions

Code Section 3-d explicitly prohibits architects from accepting free engineering services, substantial gifts, commissions, or favors from any contractor or sub-contractor. The prohibition covers any benefit that would place the architect under moral obligation. The test asks whether the architect would feel indebted — not whether the benefit has objective monetary value.

### Distinction from Permitted Market Discounts

Code Section 4-b permits architects to accept market discounts on building materials or equipment — but with a critical requirement: these discounts must be credited to the client. The architect cannot personally retain the benefit.

## How It's Tested

**Pattern 1:** Gift prohibition — Contractor offers free software valued at $5,000. Acceptance violates §3-d because moral obligation is created.

**Pattern 2:** Discount credit — Supplier offers 12% discount. Architect must credit full discount to client through reduced cost estimates.

**Pattern 3:** Services vs. discounts — Free engineering from a supplier (violates §4-a) vs. volume discount credited to client (permissible).

**Pattern 4:** Bias appearance — Architect who accepted a contractor's gift in a prior project must recuse from evaluating that contractor's work on a new project.
