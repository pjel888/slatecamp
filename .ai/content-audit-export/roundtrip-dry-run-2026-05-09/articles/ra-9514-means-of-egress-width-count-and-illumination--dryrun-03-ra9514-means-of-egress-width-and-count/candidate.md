# RA 9514 Means of Egress: Width, Count, and Illumination

## Learning objective

Given a Fire Code question prompt about means of egress, decide whether the issue is exit sufficiency, minimum width, number of means of egress, capacity, or illumination. These are related life-safety controls, but they are not the same control. A correct answer must match the fact pattern to the exact requirement being tested.

## Governing source

Use RA 9514, 2019 Revised IRR, Rule 10. The general life-safety premise is in Rule 10, Division 2, General Requirements, PDF p.89. The minimum-width and number-of-egress provisions are in Division 5, Section 10.2.5.2, PDF p.95. Illumination is in Section 10.2.5.11, PDF p.113.

## Start with the life-safety premise

Rule 10 explains that every building or structure, new or old, designed for human occupancy must be provided with exits sufficient to permit the fast and safe escape of occupants in case of fire or other emergencies. The same general requirement says the design should not depend solely on any single fire-safety construction, and that additional safeguards are required in case one safeguard fails.

Source: RA 9514, 2019 Revised IRR, Rule 10, Division 2, General Requirements, PDF p.89.

This broad rule frames the topic. Means of egress is not just a doorway count. The Fire Code is asking whether people can move out safely when smoke, fire, panic, building height, occupancy character, and the number of exposed occupants are considered.

## Kind, number, location, and capacity belong together

The same general requirement states that every building or structure must have exits of a kind, number, location, and capacity appropriate to the individual building or structure. The listed considerations include the character of the occupancy, the number of persons exposed, the fire protection available, and the height and type of construction.

Source: RA 9514, 2019 Revised IRR, Rule 10, Division 2, General Requirements, PDF p.89.

A common wrong answer focuses only on quantity. Two exits may still be poorly located, too narrow, or inappropriate for the occupancy. Conversely, a width calculation cannot replace a required number of egress routes. Keep the categories separate, then combine them only after each one has passed.

## Division 5 is the general means-of-egress rule

Section 10.2.5.1 states that means of egress for both new and existing buildings must comply with Division 5, except as modified for individual occupancies by Divisions 8 through 20 of Rule 10. It also prohibits any change, alteration, or addition that would reduce the means of egress below the requirements for new buildings.

Source: RA 9514, 2019 Revised IRR, Rule 10, Division 5, Section 10.2.5.1, PDF p.94.

This is the reason many Fire Code answers include the phrase “unless a specific occupancy division provides otherwise.” The general rule is not the end of analysis if the problem statement gives a specific occupancy covered by Divisions 8 through 20.

## Minimum width is not capacity calculation

Section 10.2.5.2(F) states that the width of any means of egress must not be less than 915 mm, except when specifically provided under Divisions 8 through 20 of Rule 10. This is a minimum-width floor.

Source: RA 9514, 2019 Revised IRR, Rule 10, Division 5, Section 10.2.5.2(F), PDF p.95.

Do not confuse this 915 mm minimum with the capacity-factor table. Section 10.2.5.2(C) states that egress capacity for approved components is based on capacity factors, while the minimum-width rule states a separate minimum width. A component might satisfy one check and still fail another.

Source: RA 9514, 2019 Revised IRR, Rule 10, Division 5, Section 10.2.5.2(C), PDF p.94.

## Number of means of egress

Section 10.2.5.2(G) states that the number of means of egress from any balcony, mezzanine, storey, or portion thereof must not be less than two, except when specifically permitted in Divisions 8 through 20. It then adds thresholds: when occupant load for any storey or portion is more than 500 but not more than 1000, the means of egress must not be less than three; in excess of 1000, the means of egress must not be less than four.

Source: RA 9514, 2019 Revised IRR, Rule 10, Division 5, Section 10.2.5.2(G), PDF p.95.

Worked application: a storey with occupant load 750 falls in the “more than 500 but not more than 1000” band. The general number required is three means of egress, unless a specific occupancy division modifies the answer. Do not answer two just because two is the basic minimum.

## Illumination has its own number

Section 10.2.5.11 requires illumination of means of egress for every building and structure as required by the occupancy divisions. Illumination must be continuous during the time the conditions of occupancy require the means of egress to be available for use. The floors of means of egress must be illuminated at all points, including angles and intersections of corridors and passageways, landings of stairs, and exit doors, to not less than 10.7 lux.

Source: RA 9514, 2019 Revised IRR, Rule 10, Division 5, Section 10.2.5.11, PDF p.113.

## Exam traps

- **Trap 1: answering 915 mm for every egress question.** That number is minimum width, not exit count or illumination.
- **Trap 2: forgetting Divisions 8 through 20.** The general rule may be modified by occupancy-specific provisions.
- **Trap 3: using two exits for every case.** Occupant loads above 500 can trigger three or four means of egress.
- **Trap 4: treating lighting as optional.** The cited minimum is 10.7 lux at required egress points.
