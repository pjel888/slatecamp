# BP 220 Parks, Playgrounds, and Road Pavement

## Learning objective

Given an economic/socialized-housing subdivision stem, identify whether the question is asking for the hierarchy of basic needs, parks/playgrounds allocation, minimum park/playground area, or road-pavement standard under the Revised IRR of BP 220.

## The rule that decides the answer

The Revised BP 220 IRR lists the basic needs of human settlements in descending order: water, movement and circulation, storm drainage, solid and liquid waste disposal, park/playground, and power. The order matters because the exam often hides a site-planning priority question inside a subdivision-standard stem.

Source: BP 220 Revised IRR 2008, Rule II, Section 4/5 context, PDF p.9, extracted from `vitruvius-connection/001_Raw-Source-Layer/001-Laws/BP-220/Revised_IRR_BP220_2008.pdf`.

## Parks and playgrounds are density-linked

Table 1 allocates parks and playgrounds by density. For both economic and socialized housing, 150 lots/dwelling units per hectare and below uses 3.5% of gross area for parks/playgrounds. Higher density bands rise to 4%, 5%, 6%, 7%, and 9% above 225.

Source: BP 220 Revised IRR 2008, Rule II, Table 1: Parks and Playgrounds Allocation, PDF p.12.

## The floor is still 100 square meters

The same extracted table note says that in no case shall an area allocated for parks and playgrounds be less than 100 square meters. This prevents a small-site arithmetic answer from undercutting the explicit minimum.

Source: BP 220 Revised IRR 2008, Rule II, Table 1 note, PDF p.12.

## Road pavement trap

Major roads, minor roads, and motor courts for economic and socialized housing projects are paved with asphalt at minimum 50 mm thickness or concrete at minimum 150 mm thickness with 20.7 MPa compressive strength at 28 days. Sidewalks or alleys are treated differently in the same excerpt.

Source: BP 220 Revised IRR 2008, Rule II, Section 5.b.3.4 Road Pavement, PDF p.17.

## Board shortcut

For BP 220 subdivision questions, first identify whether the stem is about socialized/economic-housing minimum standards, not PD 957 open-market subdivision standards. Then ask: priority hierarchy, allocation percentage, absolute minimum, or material/thickness requirement?
