# PD 957 License to Sell: Registration Is Not Yet Selling Authority

## Learning objective

Given a subdivision or condominium buyer-protection question prompt, decide whether the fact pattern is about registration, license to sell, public-offering materials, or performance-bond requirements under the PD 957 IRR. The core exam habit is to separate project registration from actual authority to sell.

## Governing source

Use the PD 957 IRR. The license-to-sell rule is in Rule IV, Section 11, PDF p.47. The application evidence for public-offering materials appears in the documentary requirements excerpt, PDF p.43. The cited license rule also points to the work program, performance bond under Section 12, and prescribed license fee.

## The license-to-sell gate

Rule IV, Section 11 states that no owner or dealer shall sell any disposable subdivision lot or condominium unit in the registered project without a license to sell issued by the Board. This is the controlling rule when the question asks whether selling may begin.

Source: PD 957 IRR, Rule IV, Section 11, PDF p.47.

The word “registered” in the provision is important because it shows the trap. A project can be registered and still not be sellable if the license to sell has not been issued. Registration is not the same as authority to sell disposable lots or condominium units.

## Timing and requirements are attached to the license

The same Section 11 language states that the license to sell is issued within two weeks from registration of each project, upon proper application, submission of the required work program, performance bond as provided in Section 12, and payment of the prescribed license fee by the owner or dealer. The Board issues the license to sell the lot or unit in the project or portion covered by the performance bond, provided the registration statement and other documentary requirements establish that the proposed sale to the public is not fraudulent.

Source: PD 957 IRR, Rule IV, Section 11, PDF p.47.

This paragraph gives you a process sequence. Registration is followed by proper application and requirements. The performance bond matters because the license covers the project or portion covered by the bond. If the question prompt mentions a registered project but says the bond or license has not been completed, do not jump to “may sell.”

## Public-offering materials are part of the evidence

The application documentary requirements include a copy of any circular, prospectus, brochure, advertisement, or communication used or to be used for public offering of the subject project and for circulation upon approval by the Board.

Source: PD 957 IRR, application documentary requirement excerpt, PDF p.43.

This matters because some exam questions hide buyer-protection rules inside marketing facts. A brochure, advertisement, or circular is not just sales fluff. It can be part of the public-offering evidence reviewed in the application process. If the question asks which document type is explicitly included, circulars, prospectuses, brochures, advertisements, and communications are all in the cited phrase.

## Registration, advertising, and selling are different ideas

A strong answer keeps three ideas separate. Registration identifies the project in the regulatory process. Public-offering materials are documentary evidence submitted or circulated upon approval. License to sell is the authority gate for selling disposable lots or condominium units. The fact that a project has registration papers or marketing materials does not automatically mean the owner or dealer may sell.

Source: PD 957 IRR, Rule IV, Section 11, PDF p.47; application documentary requirement excerpt, PDF p.43.

This distinction is the main board-exam value of the topic. If the prompt asks “may the owner sell after registration but before license issuance?” the answer is no under the cited Section 11 rule. If the prompt asks “which item is part of public-offering materials?” the answer may be brochure or advertisement, but that is not the same as selling authority.

## Worked application

Suppose a condominium project has been registered, and the developer has prepared brochures for public offering, but no license to sell has been issued. Under Section 11, the owner or dealer may not sell disposable condominium units yet. The brochures may be relevant as application evidence, but they do not replace the license. If the prompt adds that a performance bond has been submitted and the Board has issued the license for the project or bonded portion, then the license-to-sell gate has been satisfied for that covered scope.

## Decision sequence

Use the facts in chronological order. First, identify whether the project is merely registered. Second, check whether the owner or dealer has properly applied for the license to sell and submitted the required work program, performance bond, and fee. Third, check whether the Board has issued the license for the project or the bonded portion. Fourth, treat circulars, prospectuses, brochures, advertisements, or communications as public-offering materials, not as substitutes for the license itself. This order keeps the answer tied to buyer-protection procedure.

Keep the verb in the question prompt clear. “Register,” “advertise,” “offer,” and “sell” are not interchangeable under the cited IRR sequence. The license-to-sell gate controls the selling act.

## Exam traps

- **Trap 1: registration equals license.** Section 11 keeps them separate.
- **Trap 2: marketing documents equal selling authority.** Public-offering materials are evidence; the license is the authority gate.
- **Trap 3: the performance bond is ignored.** The cited rule links license issuance to the performance bond under Section 12.
- **Trap 4: the Board’s role is skipped.** Section 11 requires a license to sell issued by the Board.
- **Trap 5: the project scope is overread.** The license covers the project or portion covered by the performance bond.
