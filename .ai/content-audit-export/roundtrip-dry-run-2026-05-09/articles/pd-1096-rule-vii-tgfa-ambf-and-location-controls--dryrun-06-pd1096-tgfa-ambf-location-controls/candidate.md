# PD 1096 Rule VII: TGFA, AMBF, and Location Controls

## Learning objective

Given a National Building Code development-control question prompt, separate three checks before computing: location on property, allowable building footprint, and allowable total gross floor area. Many wrong answers are correct-looking numbers applied to the wrong control.

## Governing source

Use PD 1096, 2004 Revised IRR, Rule VII. The location controls are in Section 704.1(a) through 704.1(c), PDF p.81. The AMBF discussion begins in Section 704.4, PDF pp.81-83. The TGFA controls are in Section 705.1 and 705.2, PDF p.83. Use the precise subsection when the fact pattern points to it.

## First check: location on property

Section 704.1(a) states that no building shall be constructed unless it adjoins or has direct access to a public space, yard, or street on at least one of its sides. This is a threshold condition. Before computing area, decide whether the building may be located on the property condition described.

Source: PD 1096, 2004 Revised IRR, Rule VII, Section 704.1(a), PDF p.81.

The wording is “at least one” side. If an option says the building must be surrounded by streets on all sides, that overstates the rule. If an option ignores access and jumps directly to a floor-area percentage, it is answering the wrong part of Rule VII.

## Adjacent property line and eaves

Section 704.1(b) states that, for purposes of Section 704, the centerline of an adjoining street or alley is considered an adjacent property line. Section 704.1(c) then states that eaves over required windows shall not be less than 750 mm from the side and rear property lines.

Source: PD 1096, 2004 Revised IRR, Rule VII, Section 704.1(b)-704.1(c), PDF p.81.

The 750 mm value is precise. It is not a generic setback value for every projection, every side of the lot, or every roof edge. The cited clause is specifically about eaves over required windows and their distance from side and rear property lines. A correct answer should keep that condition attached to the number.

## AMBF is about footprint, not total floor area

Section 704.4 introduces the rules for determining the Allowable Maximum Building Footprint, or AMBF, for buildings and related habitable structures. The source says the AMBF rules are compared with Rule VIII Table VIII.1 on Percentage of Site Occupancy and Maximum Allowable Construction Area, Rule VIII Tables VIII.2 and VIII.3 on setbacks, yards, and courts, and applicable stipulations under Rule VII and the Fire Code.

Source: PD 1096, 2004 Revised IRR, Rule VII, Section 704.4, PDF pp.81-83.

AMBF is the footprint control. In plain language, it asks how much of the site may be occupied by the building footprint after the relevant site-occupancy, setback, yard, court, firewall, and fire-safety cross-checks are considered. Do not answer an AMBF question with TGFA just because both appear in Rule VII.

## TGFA is the total gross floor-area control

Section 705.1 states that the Allowable Maximum Total Gross Floor Area, or TGFA, of any proposed building or structure shall only be as allowed under Rule VII. Section 705.2 then explains that, in Table VII.1, the percentages in the third through eighth columns, excluding the multiplier numbers 3, 5, 12, 18, and 30 that represent number of storeys or floors, are percentages of Total Lot Area used to initially determine the allowable maximum TGFA.

Source: PD 1096, 2004 Revised IRR, Rule VII, Section 705.1-705.2, PDF p.83.

This is the key distinction: TGFA is total gross floor area, while AMBF is footprint. A multi-storey building can have a footprint that passes one check and a total gross floor area that fails another. The abbreviation in the question prompt tells you which control is being tested.

## Worked application

Suppose a question gives a lot with direct access to a street on one side, asks about eaves over required windows near the side property line, and then provides area percentages. Do not begin with Table VII.1. First, Section 704.1(a) confirms the one-side direct-access condition. Second, Section 704.1(c) requires at least 750 mm for eaves over required windows from side and rear property lines. Only after the location and projection conditions are addressed should you move to AMBF or TGFA, depending on what the problem asks.

Suppose the prompt asks for Allowable Maximum Total Gross Floor Area. The controlling abbreviation is TGFA, and the source path is Section 705, not Section 704.4. If the prompt asks for maximum building footprint, use AMBF and keep it separate from total floor area.

## Decision sequence

Use a three-step sequence for Rule VII problems. Step one: ask whether Section 704.1(a) allows the building condition at all because it adjoins or directly accesses a public space, yard, or street on at least one side. Step two: if the issue is projection or property-line clearance, keep Section 704.1(c) attached to eaves over required windows and the 750 mm side/rear property-line distance. Step three: only after those controls are settled, decide whether the prompt asks for AMBF under Section 704.4 or TGFA under Section 705.

## Exam traps

- **Trap 1: broad Section 704 citation when the issue is precise.** Use Section 704.1(a) for the one-side access rule and Section 704.1(c) for the 750 mm eaves rule.
- **Trap 2: TGFA and AMBF are swapped.** TGFA is total gross floor area; AMBF is footprint.
- **Trap 3: the 750 mm value is detached from its condition.** It applies to eaves over required windows from side and rear property lines.
- **Trap 4: area arithmetic starts too early.** Location and property-line controls can decide the question before computation.
