## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Illuminance is the amount of luminous flux falling on a unit area. The checked handout identifies lux as the unit of illuminance and lumen as the unit of luminous flux. In LEA lighting questions, lux is the working-plane target; watts are the power input and should not be confused with illuminance.

The DOE Guidelines set energy-efficient lighting requirements in terms of illumination level, luminous efficacy, and lighting power density. The design objective is not maximum brightness. The lighting system should support the intended activity with the least practical energy requirement while still creating an effective visual environment.

DOE Section 3.3 requires the task to be analyzed by difficulty, duration, criticalness, and location. Illumination that is higher than necessary wastes energy; illumination below the task need can impair visual effectiveness. Table 3.1 gives recommended design illuminance levels: 50 to 150 lux for circulation areas and corridors, 100 to 200 lux for stairs and hotel escalators, 200 to 300 lux for infrequent reading and writing, 300 to 750 lux for general offices and conference rooms, 500 to 1000 lux for deep-plan offices, drawing offices, and proofreading, 750 to 1500 lux for designing, architecture, and machine engineering, and 1000 to 2000 lux for detailed and precise work.

Efficiency is a whole-system issue. DOE directs the designer to select efficient lamps, luminaires, and ballasts suited to the task and environment; the luminaire should provide appropriate distribution, uniformity, and glare control. High room surface reflectance and light finishes improve lighting efficiency because dark surfaces absorb light.

Controls are also part of the design answer. DOE requires controls for lighting systems except emergency, exit, or security lighting. Each enclosed space needs at least one control, task lighting needs its own control point, and larger enclosed areas with connected lighting load above 10 W/m2 must be controllable so the load can be reduced by at least 50 percent while maintaining reasonably uniform illuminance.

## LEA Memory Anchors

- Lux measures illuminance on the task surface; lumen measures luminous flux.
- Choose the illuminance level from the task, not from a desire for maximum brightness.
- Too much light wastes energy; too little light impairs visual effectiveness.
- Table 3.1 ranges rise from circulation to precise work.
- Efficient luminaires, reflectance, glare control, and switching are part of the answer.
