## Learning Goal

Apply recommended illumination levels (lux) by space type per the Philippine Lighting Standard (PNS / IESNA RP-1) for office, educational, healthcare, hospitality, residential, and industrial spaces, and design efficient lighting layouts for given room dimensions.
## Recommended Illumination Levels by Space Type

Illumination is measured in _lux_ (lumens per square meter). Each space type has distinct requirements based on visual task complexity and occupancy safety needs.

### Office Spaces

Office general work areas require **300–500 lux** according to IES (Illuminating Engineering Society) standards. General tasks such as reading printed documents, computer-based work at standard distances, and conference activities fall within this range. Office task areas—where detailed work occurs, such as engineering drawings, medical record entry, or precision assembly—require **500–750 lux** per IES and ASHRAE 90.1 recommendations. The higher illumination supports sustained visual accuracy over extended periods.

Classrooms require **300 lux** minimum per IES standards for reading printed materials, viewing instructional aids, and board visibility. Laboratory spaces, where precision measurements, specimen examination, and chemical analysis occur, demand **500 lux** per IES guidelines to ensure accuracy and prevent errors.

### Healthcare Facilities

Hospital wards—general patient rooms and recovery areas—require **100 lux** per IES standards to prevent glare while allowing nursing staff to assess patient conditions. Operating rooms present the most demanding illumination requirement: **10,000–100,000 lux** in the surgical field per IES surgical lighting standards. This extreme range accommodates different surgical procedures, magnification levels, and instrument requirements. The surgical field may require the upper limit during microsurgery, while general OR areas outside the surgical field operate at lower illumination levels.

### Circulation Spaces

CoCorridors require minimum 100 lux per IES standards to ensure wayfinding and safety. Stairways demand **150 lux** per IES standards—higher than corridors—because descending or ascending stairs presents increased fall risk if illumination is inadequate.

### Commercial and Industrial

Retail spaces require **300–750 lux** depending on merchandise type per IES standards. General retail (clothing, groceries) operates at 300–500 lux; jewelry, electronics, and cosmetics display require 500–750 lux for color discrimination and detail visibility. Industrial work areas require **200–500 lux** per IES, with task-specific illumination determined by object size, contrast, and movement speed.

### Parking Areas

Covered parking structures require **50–75 lux** per IES standards to support vehicle navigation and personal safety. This level allows occupants to identify exit routes and navigate to safety without maintaining normal visibility.

## Lighting Quality Metrics

Illumination quantity alone does not ensure lighting effectiveness. Three quality metrics determine overall lighting performance.

### Color Rendering Index (CRI)

CRI measures how accurately artificial light renders object colors compared to natural daylight (CRI = 100). Minimum **CRI 80** applies to all occupied spaces per Philippine Energy Efficiency Standards and IES recommendations. This threshold preserves color discrimination for visual tasks. Healthcare facilities and retail spaces require **CRI 90 or higher** per IES standards because accurate color identification is critical—nurses must distinguish patient skin tone changes, and retail customers must accurately assess product colors before purchase.

### Color Temperature

Color temperature (measured in Kelvin, K) affects visual perception and circadian response. Standard selections follow occupancy type:

- **3000K (warm white)**: Hospitality, residential, and reception areas. Warm light creates a welcoming atmosphere per IES design guidance.
- **4000K (neutral white)**: General office, retail, and education. Neutral light provides balanced visibility without warm or cool bias per ASHRAE 90.1.
- **5000–6500K (cool/daylight)**: Industrial work, healthcare, and precision task areas. Cool light enhances alertness and visual acuity per IES industrial and healthcare standards.

### Uniformity Ratio

Uniformity ratio (UR) compares minimum illumination to average illumination across a space. Minimum **UR 0.6** applies to general areas per IES standards, meaning the darkest point must receive at least 60% of the average illumination to prevent shadows and dark zones. Task areas require minimum **UR 0.7** per IES to reduce eye strain from significant brightness variation.

## Lighting Power Density (LPD) Compliance

Lighting power density—measured in watts per square meter (W/m²)—caps energy consumption per space type under ASHRAE 90.1 and Philippine Energy Efficiency Standards. LPD limits vary by occupancy:

- Office general: 9–12 W/m² (depending on occupancy configuration)
- Retail: 14–16 W/m²
- Warehouse: 4–6 W/m²
- Hospital: 12–16 W/m²

These allowances assume LED technology (>90 lumens per watt) per ASHRAE 90.1 baseline assumptions. Designers must verify LPD compliance by calculating total installed lighting power and dividing by floor area. Compliance reduces energy costs and operational carbon emissions while meeting illumination requirements.

## Practical Application: Designing for Compliance

Select illumination levels using this sequence:

1. _Identify occupancy type_ and visual task complexity from IES standards and PD 1096.
2. _Determine illumination target_ (lux) and quality requirements (CRI, UR, color temperature).
3. _Calculate fixture count and wattage_ to achieve lux while remaining within LPD limits.
4. _Verify uniformity_ across the space using illuminance calculation software (DIALux, AGI32) to confirm UR exceeds minimum thresholds.
5. _Specify LED fixtures_ with appropriate color temperature, CRI rating, and dimming capability to support occupancy-responsive control.
6. _Commission and verify_ installed illumination with a lux meter to confirm design intent.

Compliance with PD 1096 Rule VIII and Philippine Energy Efficiency Standards requires both adequate illumination and controlled energy consumption—a balance achieved through quality fixture selection and careful layout planning.

## Summary

Illumination levels range from 10 lux (emergency) to 100,000 lux (surgical field) depending on occupancy and task. Office general areas require 300–500 lux; task areas 500–750 lux. Retail and industrial spaces follow IES guidelines specific to merchandise type and object characteristics. Healthcare facilities demand high CRI (90+) and cool color temperature for accurate clinical assessment. Parking areas operate at lower illumination (20–75 lux) due to reduced visual complexity. Uniformity ratios (0.6–0.7) prevent dark zones. LPD limits ensure energy efficiency without sacrificing visibility. Designers achieving compliance combine appropriate illumination with CRI, color temperature, uniformity, and power density control, supported by PD 1096 and Philippine Energy Efficiency Standards requirements.
