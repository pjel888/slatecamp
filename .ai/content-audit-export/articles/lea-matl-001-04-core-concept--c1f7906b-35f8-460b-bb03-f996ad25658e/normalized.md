## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Wood preservation questions are safest when they stay with the source-audited mechanisms: moisture reduction by seasoning, decay conditions, termite behavior, and preservative application methods. Batch 082 removes unsupported regulatory claims and unsupported service-life claims.

## Core Concept

Seasoning removes moisture from wood. The checked Woods and Plastics Lecture source lists practical benefits of seasoned lumber: less shrinkage, checking, splitting, warping, and decay, plus better nail-holding and easier handling than green lumber. Wood decay is a biological moisture problem; fungi need favorable moisture and temperature conditions. Wood kept below about 20 percent moisture content is generally outside the usual moisture condition for fungal decay.

Termite control is handled by prevention and barriers as well as wood treatment. Subterranean termites build earthen tubes from the soil to reach wood. Source-safe protective measures include shields, termite-proofing wood close to earth, and poisoning the soil where appropriate.

Preservative application depth matters. Surface methods such as brushing, dipping, or hot-and-cold bath treatment do not equal pressure treatment. Pressure treatment forces preservative into the wood under pressure and is the source-safe answer when deeper penetration is the controlling idea.

## How It Is Tested

Expect items that ask for the best preservation method, the reason seasoning helps, or the organism behavior being controlled. If the item says subterranean termite, look for soil contact or earthen tubes. If it asks why brush treatment is limited, answer shallow penetration rather than inventing a legal prohibition.

## Common Mistakes

Do not state that a chemical is banned, required, or code-approved unless the item provides a current code source. Do not treat kiln drying as a chemical preservative. Do not assume surface brushing gives the same protection as pressure treatment.
