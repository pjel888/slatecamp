# Museums and Cultural Facilities: Occupancy and Life Safety

## Source Scope

This local repair uses the checked PD 1096 RIRR occupancy tables, RA 9514 2019 Revised IRR assembly provisions, and the local Fire Code source family. It does not rely on unsupported exhibit-area ratios, gallery climate-control rules, or museum-planning assumptions outside those raw PDFs.

## LEA Anchor

A museum question usually tests source discipline. Decide whether the item is asking about occupancy classification, assembly occupant load, exit arrangement, fire alarm, sprinkler protection, or hazard separation. Each of those answers comes from a different provision.

## Occupancy Classification

The checked PD 1096 matrix lists libraries, museums, exhibition halls, and art galleries in the education and recreation public-use context. RA 9514 also includes exhibition halls and museums in assembly occupancies when the space is used for gathering under the Fire Code definition.

## Assembly Life Safety

For assembly use, RA 9514 sets occupant load by the assigned use: concentrated areas without fixed seats, less concentrated areas such as exhibit rooms and lounges, standing or waiting spaces, or fixed seats. Assembly spaces require exit lighting and signs, emergency lighting, and fire alarm systems. Rooms or spaces with hazardous equipment, operations, processes, combustible supplies, hazardous materials, or maintenance shops must not be directly under or abutting required exits and must be separated by fire barriers with at least one-hour fire resistance.

## Exam Use

For LEA answers, do not treat a museum as only a collection of rooms. Classify the facility, then test the specific public area, exhibit room, auditorium, workshop, storage room, or support space named by the item. A gallery, theater, and conservation workshop can trigger different source paths.
