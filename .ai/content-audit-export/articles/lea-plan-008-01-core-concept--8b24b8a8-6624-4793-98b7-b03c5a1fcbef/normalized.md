## Core Concept

Climate resilience in planning refers to the ability of settlements and urban systems to withstand and recover from natural hazards and climate-related disturbances. Disaster-responsive planning goes beyond reactive recovery; it incorporates risk reduction into land use planning, infrastructure design, and community preparedness from the outset.

In the Philippines, disaster risk is not abstract—the World Bank estimates annual GDP loss to disasters at _1.9%_, driven by typhoons, floods, earthquakes, and storm surge. Legal frameworks, beginning with RA 10121 (Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act, 2010), mandate that Comprehensive Land Use Plans (CLUPs) mainstream disaster risk reduction and management (DRRM) into zoning and land allocation decisions.

Climate-resilient planning typically addresses:

1. **Hazard mapping and risk assessment** — identifying earthquake faults, flood zones, storm surge zones, landslide-prone slopes
2. **Setback requirements** — minimum distances from hazardous areas (active faults, water bodies, fault traces)
3. **Land use restrictions** — prohibiting residential or critical infrastructure in high-risk zones
4. **Building typology and resilience** — designing structures to withstand anticipated hazards

## Philippine Context

The Philippines ranks among the world's most disaster-prone countries, facing an average of 20 typhoons annually, persistent earthquake risk along major fault systems, and increasing flood risk from sea-level rise and intense rainfall. The 2013 Typhoon Yolanda (Haiyan) storm surge inundated coastal cities and resulted in policy reforms, including the 40-meter no-build zone standard. Planners working in Philippine LGUs must balance development demand against mandatory hazard setbacks, making DRRM-informed CLUP revision a continuous practice.

## How It's Tested

1. **Hazard identification from site context** — "A proposed residential subdivision is mapped on a PHIVOLCS fault trace. What minimum setback must be maintained?" → Answer: 5 meters (from PHIVOLCS standard). Test assesses knowledge of numeric standards and source.

2. **CLUP integration and legal mandate** — "Under RA 10121, what must all LGUs mainstream into their CLUPs?" → Answer: Disaster Risk Reduction and Management (DRRM); this tests the legal requirement under Philippine planning law.

3. **Coastal hazard zone application** — "A municipality is planning a new fishing settlement 25 meters from mean high tide. Does this comply with post-Yolanda zoning standards?" → Answer: No, the standard requires 40 meters. This tests application of the numeric standard to planning scenarios.

**Exam Tip:** Climate adaptation = adjusting to actual/expected climate effects. Mitigation = reducing greenhouse gas emissions. Resilience = ability to withstand and recover. Know which strategies are adaptation vs. mitigation — the LEA tests this distinction.
