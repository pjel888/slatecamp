# Climate-Resilient Planning and DRRM in CLUPs

## Board Exam Quick Check

For climate-resilience questions, avoid memorized numbers unless the item names a checked source. The safer LEA move is to identify the planning task: risk assessment, land-use allocation, CDRA, CLUP integration, zoning parameters, infrastructure design, or stakeholder participation.

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

The DHSUD climate-resilience reference tool says climate resilience should be a core part of urban plans and designs because cities and towns must deal with urban growth, climate risks, service access, land-use patterns, energy use, and transportation systems. It identifies urban planning and urban design as tools for reducing vulnerability and improving adaptation.

The same tool summarizes RA 10121 Section 2(g) as a policy to mainstream disaster risk reduction and climate change in development processes, including policy formulation, socio-economic development planning, budgeting, governance, land-use and urban planning, public infrastructure, and housing.

The reference tool also explains that the Supplemental Guidelines on Mainstreaming Climate and Disaster Risk in the CLUP introduced a six-step Climate and Disaster Risk Assessment process. CDRA helps LGUs translate risk information into land-use policies, zoning parameters, and safer, more resilient human settlements.

The HLURB CLUP guide already requires climate change and disaster-risk projections in the situation analysis. That means hazard and vulnerability information belongs inside the CLUP workflow, not as an isolated emergency plan.

The DHSUD resilient urban design guidelines connect resilience to compact and mixed-use development, transit-oriented development, open spaces, water-sensitive urban design, nature-based solutions, and complete streets. These strategies support safer and more adaptable urban form.

## Exam Focus

- Climate resilience is a planning and design principle, not only emergency response.
- CDRA links hazard/vulnerability information to CLUP and zoning decisions.
- DRRM and climate change are mainstreamed into development processes in the DHSUD reference tool's RA 10121 summary.
- CLUP situation analysis should include climate and disaster-risk projections.
- Use source-checked planning terms instead of unsupported hazard setback numbers.

## How It Is Tested

Expect questions asking why DRRM belongs in CLUPs, what CDRA contributes, how risk information becomes zoning parameters, or which urban design strategies improve climate resilience. The best answer integrates risk assessment, land use, infrastructure, open space, and stakeholder participation.
