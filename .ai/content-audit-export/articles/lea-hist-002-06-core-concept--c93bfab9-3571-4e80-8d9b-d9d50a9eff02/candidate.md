# Neoclassicism

## LEA Point
Neoclassicism is the 18th- and 19th-century return to antiquity through archaeology, Enlightenment reason, and public virtue. For the LEA, identify it by restraint, symmetry, straight lines, temple fronts, domes, porticoes, and Greek or Roman forms used as disciplined civic language.

## Source Anchors
The movement differs from Renaissance classicism because it is more archaeological and ideological. Renaissance designers reinterpreted ancient principles through humanist proportion; Neoclassical designers increasingly looked to measured antiquity, excavations, and Greek or Roman precedents as models of moral and civic order.

The Architect of the Capitol source-maps the U.S. Capitol as a neoclassical building whose Greek and Roman sources evoke founding ideals. Use this as the safest civic-symbolism anchor.

Monticello source-maps Thomas Jefferson's long design process for his mountaintop house. In LEA logic, Monticello is the American villa anchor: a personal but ideological classical composition associated with Jefferson, proportion, portico, dome, and republican taste.

Berlin.de source-maps the Brandenburg Gate as a classicist monument built from 1788 to 1791 and inspired by the Propylaea of the Athenian Acropolis. Use it for Greek Revival, Doric language, and national symbolism.

The Pantheon in Paris is the French neoclassical church-to-mausoleum anchor. It reads as a classical temple with a dome and a civic memorial role.

## Exam Pattern
Neoclassical questions often contrast the style with Baroque. Baroque uses curves, drama, illusion, and emotional persuasion; Neoclassicism uses straightness, restraint, rationality, archaeological reference, and civic clarity.

Another pattern asks why the style mattered politically. Classical Greece and Rome supplied a visual vocabulary for virtue, law, republicanism, and Enlightenment reason, especially in capitols, courthouses, and public monuments.

## Guardrails
Do not call Neoclassicism mere nostalgia. The source-safe answer is that it used old forms for new ideological work: reason, reform, civic identity, and public order.

Avoid treating Greek Revival and Roman classicism as identical. Greek Revival emphasizes Greek temple forms and orders more directly.

## Real World Scenario
In an image item, a white civic building with a portico, dome, and disciplined classical symmetry should suggest Neoclassical public virtue. A sandstone gate with Doric order and Propylaea inspiration should suggest the Brandenburg Gate and Greek Revival logic.
