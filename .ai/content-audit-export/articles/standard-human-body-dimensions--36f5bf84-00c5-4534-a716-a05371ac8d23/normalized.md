# Standard Human Body Dimensions

## Learning Goal

Use source-backed human-body dimension concepts in architectural design: know what anthropometrics means, know why body dimensions affect reach, furniture, and movement space, and use checked BP 344 dimensions when a standard legal dimension is required.

## Source-Audited Scope

The checked local sources do not include the primary anthropometric datasets named in the older draft. Therefore, this Batch 053 rewrite removes population-specific tables that were not checked and keeps only claims that can be traced to the selected local raw PDFs.

The Furniture Reviewer source identifies anthropometrics as the study dealing with human measurements. The Salvan design-theory source states that anthropomorphic proportioning is based on the dimensions and proportions of the human body, and that human dimensions affect things people handle, heights and distances people reach, furniture used for sitting, working, eating, and sleeping, and the volume of space needed for movement, activity, and rest.

## How to Use Human-Body Dimensions

1. Name the body relationship first: reach, clearance, seating support, sightline, movement, or transfer.
2. Use source-specific numbers. A BP 344 number answers an accessibility-code question; a furniture-vocabulary source answers a furniture-identification question.
3. Do not cite an unverified population table. If the primary data source is not in the raw source family, the value is parked.
4. Treat average-user design with caution. The checked sources support the principle that human dimensions vary by person and by task; they do not support the older draft's specific percentile tables.
5. Remember that architectural standards have context. A 1200 mm switch height is not a general reach table; it is the checked BP 344 rule for manual switches.

## Exam Traps

- Do not answer Batch 053 questions with Del Prado-Lu, NHANES, ANSUR, or BIFMA values; those raw sources were not checked in this batch.
- Do not turn every human-body question into a BP 344 question. BP 344 supplies legal accessibility minima, while anthropometrics explains why bodies, furniture, reach, and movement space matter.
- Do not use a single unqualified range such as 1.50 m to 1.85 m as if it were a standard human-body dataset.
