## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Passive environmental design in tropical Philippines uses building orientation, massing, and overhangs to minimize solar heat gain and maximize natural ventilation. The primary strategy is aligning the long axis East-West (not North-South) to reduce exposure to the intense midday sun.

## Learning Goal

Apply passive design strategies for tropical Philippines: orient long axis East-West, calculate overhang depth ratios for solar control (60% on east/west facades), use thermal mass and natural ventilation to reduce cooling loads, and recognize the ±15° orientation tolerance.

## Core Concept

Passive design harnesses climate forces—sun path, prevailing winds, thermal mass—without mechanical systems. In the Philippines at 14.5°N latitude, solar radiation is nearly vertical at solar noon, creating extreme thermal loads on east and west facades. A building oriented with its long axis East-West presents narrower exposures to these peak-radiation sides. Conversely, a North-South orientation exposes the long sides to morning (east) and afternoon (west) sun, maximizing heat gain.

Overhangs (horizontal fins) block direct solar radiation on vertical facades. A 60% overhang ratio on east and west facades means the overhang width is 0.6 times the floor-to-ceiling height. At 3 m floor height, this yields a 1.8 m deep overhang. In Philippine tropical conditions, this depth is empirically proven to reduce cooling loads by 20–40%, depending on latitude and occupancy pattern.

Thermal mass (concrete walls, tile floors) absorbs daytime heat and releases it at night, smoothing indoor temperature swings. Natural ventilation through operable windows, clerestories, and stack effect (warm air exiting high; cool air entering low) reduces dependence on mechanical air conditioning.

The LEA tests whether candidates understand the geometric and climatic reasoning behind these strategies, not detailed HVAC calculations. The key insight is directional: _long axis East-West for the tropics_.

## Key Provisions

**Optimal Building Orientation (Tropics, Philippines):**
- Long axis: East-West (minimize east/west facade area)
- Short axis: North-South (minimize direct radiation from peak sun angle)
- Deviation tolerance: ±15° acceptable; >30° becomes sub-optimal

**Solar Control Design:**
| Facade | Overhang Depth Ratio | Equivalent Depth (3m floor height) |
|---|---|---|
| East facade | 60% | 1.8 m |
| West facade | 60% | 1.8 m |
| South facade | 30–40% | 0.9–1.2 m (lower sun angle) |
| North facade | Minimal | Diffuse light only (no direct radiation) |

**Thermal Mass Strategy:**
- Exposed concrete, masonry: minimum 100 mm thickness
- Thermal lag: 8–12 hours (peak outdoor heat reaches interior well after sunset)
- Combined with nighttime cross-ventilation: peak indoor temperature reduced 3–5°C vs. lightweight construction

## Philippine Practice Context

The Philippines experiences mean annual temperatures of 25–28°C with relative humidity 60–80%. The monsoon cycle (southwest May–October, northeast November–April) dictates prevailing wind directions. South-facing facades receive morning/afternoon wind in both seasons; north-facing facades are typically lee-side. Designers integrate these wind paths into facade design, often using louvered screens or wind scoops. Indigenous architecture (bahay kubo) intuitively employs passive strategies: elevated wood frame (thermal mass under floor), large overhangs (amakan-thatched), cross-ventilation between open sides. Modern designs revisit these principles using contemporary materials (reinforced concrete, glass, metal louvers) for mixed-use and high-rise buildings.

## How It's Tested

**Test Pattern 1: Building Orientation**
A floor plan is rotated 45° off East-West cardinal directions. The exam asks if this orientation is compliant with passive design strategy. Answer: Compliant (within ±15° tolerance). If rotated 40° or more off axis, answer: Sub-optimal or non-compliant depending on code jurisdiction strictness.

**Test Pattern 2: Overhang Depth Calculation**
Given a 3.5 m floor-to-ceiling height, calculate the required overhang depth for a west-facing facade using 60% ratio. Answer: 3.5 × 0.6 = 2.1 m. Exam may ask if a 1.8 m overhang is adequate. Answer: No—insufficient for this floor height.

**Test Pattern 3: Facade Exposure Comparison**
Two building designs: (A) 50 m × 20 m footprint, E-W orientation; (B) 50 m × 20 m footprint, N-S orientation. Exam asks which has greater solar heat gain. Answer: (B)—the N-S orientation exposes the 50 m long sides to east/west radiation, quadrupling the affected area vs. (A)'s 20 m sides.

<Tip>**"Long axis East-West; short sides catch the sun."** In tropical design, _narrow_ your building to the sun's travel arc (east-west rotation). Orient the long sides north and south, where sun angles are less severe at midday.</Tip>

**Exam Tip — ERR-DSGN-012:** East-West building orientation is correct for tropical passive design (minimizes east/west exposure). North-South orientation is WRONG for tropical climate — the long facade should face north/south, NOT east/west.
