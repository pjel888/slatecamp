# Passive Design for Architectural Design

Passive design is an architectural decision sequence. The checked TOA 4 source defines it as design that works with the environment to exclude unwanted heat and use sun and breezes for comfort while avoiding or minimizing mechanical heating or cooling.

## Design Sequence

1. Reduce heat gain through orientation, shade, light-colored or reflective surfaces, and careful glazing.
2. Encourage natural ventilation by orienting windows and building form toward prevailing winds.
3. Use shaded daylight instead of unprotected glass.
4. Create cooler outdoor areas with verandas, balconies, shade, and landscape.
5. Coordinate solar control with airflow so comfort strategies do not fight each other.

## Exam Use

A passive design answer should identify the climate action and the architectural element that performs it. Shade handles sun. Openings and vents handle air paths. Landscape and semi-open spaces temper outdoor conditions. Mechanical equipment is not the first answer unless the item asks for active cooling.

## Common Trap

Do not list passive strategies as separate tricks. They work as a system: orientation, envelope, openings, shade, and landscape must support the same climate response.
