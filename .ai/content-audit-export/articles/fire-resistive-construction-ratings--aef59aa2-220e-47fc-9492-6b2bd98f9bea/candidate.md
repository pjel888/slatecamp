# Fire Resistive Construction Ratings

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to distinguish construction type from fire-resistive rating under the 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096, read the minimum rating requirements for major building elements, and avoid importing rating systems from foreign model codes into local LEA questions.

## Controlling Source

This article is source-audited against the DPWH official PDF of the 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096, especially Rule IV Sections 401 to 403 and Rule VI Sections 601 to 604.

## Construction Type Is the Starting Classification

Rule IV classifies construction into five types. The type identifies the general material and protection approach for the building, while the fire-resistive rating identifies the required time performance of elements such as walls, partitions, frames, floors, roofs, doors, and windows.

Do not answer a rating question from the construction-type name alone. First identify the building element, then the construction type, then the required rating or minimum protection thickness.

## Type I to Type V Under Rule IV

Rule IV Section 401 gives the basic construction-type framework:

- Type I: wood construction.
- Type II: wood construction with protective fire-resistant materials and one-hour fire-resistive construction throughout.
- Type III: masonry and wood construction with one-hour fire-resistive construction throughout; exterior walls must be incombustible fire-resistive construction.
- Type IV: steel, iron, concrete, or masonry construction with walls, ceilings, and permanent partitions of incombustible fire-resistive construction.
- Type V: structural elements of steel, iron, concrete, or masonry with four-hour fire-resistive construction throughout.

The rule is not a simple "higher number means every item gets the same number of hours" shortcut. Section 403 assigns different ratings by element.

## Minimum Fire-Resistive Requirements by Element

Rule IV Section 403 gives the minimum fire-resistive construction requirements:

| Element | Type I | Type II | Type III | Type IV | Type V |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Exterior bearing and non-bearing walls | No requirement stated in the table | 1 hour | 1 hour | 4 hours | 4 hours |
| Interior bearing walls | No requirement stated in the table | 1 hour | 1 hour | 1 hour | 3 hours |
| Permanent partitions | No requirement stated in the table | 1 hour | 1 hour | 1 hour | 1 hour |
| Structural frame | No requirement stated in the table | 1 hour | 1 hour | 2 hours | 3 hours |
| Floors | No requirement stated in the table | 1 hour | 1 hour | 1 hour | 1 hour |
| Roofs | No requirement stated in the table | 1 hour | 1 hour | 1 hour | 1 hour |
| Exterior doors and windows | 1 hour | 1 hour | 1 hour | 1 hour | 1 hour |

When an LEA item asks for "minimum rating" without naming an element, the item is incomplete. Exterior walls, interior bearing walls, frames, floors, roofs, and openings can have different answers.

## Fire-Resistive Rating and Time Period

Rule VI Section 601 defines fire-resistive rating as the time a material or construction resists a standard fire exposure. Section 602 defines the fire-resistive time period rating as the length of time a material or construction withstands the fire test prescribed by the Secretary.

The exam point is that the rating is performance over time, not simply material identity. A concrete element, masonry wall, steel member, partition, door, or window still needs the rating assigned by the applicable rule.

## Minimum Thickness Examples Under Section 603

Rule VI Section 603 lists minimum thicknesses for selected materials and assemblies. The values below are source-audited examples from the table and should be used only within their stated assembly category:

| Assembly category | 4 hours | 3 hours | 2 hours | 1 hour |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Solid reinforced concrete slab, cover/depth | 25/150 mm | 25/150 mm | 20/125 mm | 15/100 mm |
| Solid prestressed concrete slab, cover/depth | 65/150 mm | 50/150 mm | 40/125 mm | 25/100 mm |
| Solid concrete partition | 175 mm | 175 mm | 150 mm | 125 mm |
| Solid masonry partition | 200 mm | 175 mm | 150 mm | 125 mm |
| Hollow-unit masonry partition | 300 mm | 250 mm | 200 mm | 150 mm |
| Concrete protection for metal structural members | 75 mm | 50 mm | 38 mm | 25 mm |
| Masonry protection for metal structural members | 100 mm | 75 mm | 55 mm | 38 mm |
| Solid concrete exterior wall | 180 mm | 150 mm | 125 mm | 75 mm |
| Solid masonry exterior wall | 200 mm | 175 mm | 150 mm | 100 mm |
| Hollow masonry exterior wall | 300 mm | 250 mm | 200 mm | 150 mm |
| Reinforced concrete column, all faces exposed | 450 mm | 400 mm | 300 mm | 200 mm |

These are minimum thicknesses from the IRR table, not universal design recommendations. Use the exact assembly type named in the item.

## Standards and Tests

Rule VI Section 604 authorizes the Secretary to prescribe standards and tests for flame spread, fire tests, doors and windows, smoke and fire detectors, interior finishes, structural member protection, fire-resistive walls, partitions, floors, roofs, openings, and roof coverings.

For LEA purposes, that means the Code supplies both a rating vocabulary and an administrative source for test standards. Do not substitute an unverified foreign table when the question asks for PD 1096.

## LEA Recall Anchors

Use this decision path:

1. Identify the construction type: I, II, III, IV, or V.
2. Identify the element: exterior wall, interior bearing wall, partition, frame, floor, roof, door, or window.
3. Apply Rule IV Section 403 for required hours.
4. If the question asks for material thickness, apply Rule VI Section 603 for the specific assembly category.
