# PD 1096 Rule XII Egress and Corridors

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to apply the Rule XII egress rules that commonly appear in LEA questions: occupant load, required exit count, exit width, travel distance, exit door behavior, corridor width, dead-end corridors, stair widths, ramp widths, exit enclosures, smokeproof enclosures, and projection-room exits.

## Controlling Source

This article is source-audited against the DPWH official PDF of the 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096. The checked local raw conversion covers Rule XII Section 1207 for stairs, exits, and occupant loads; Section 1206 for roof-construction items that appear in linked questions; and Section 1214 for motion-picture projection rooms.

## The Core Rule: Egress Is Load-Driven

Section 1207 starts with a simple command: stairs and exits must conform to the occupant-load requirements of the building or portion served. The occupant load is generally determined by dividing the floor area assigned to the use by the unit area per occupant in Table XII.1, while fixed-seat areas use the number of fixed seats.

For exam work, do not treat exits as decorative or optional circulation features. Start with the use, compute or identify the occupant load, and then apply the exit-count, exit-width, and arrangement rules.

## Minimum Number of Exits

Every building or usable portion must have at least one exit. The required number increases as occupant load and floor position increase.

| Condition | Rule XII result |
| :-- | :-- |
| Floors above the first storey with occupant load over 10 | At least two exits |
| Mezzanine used for purposes other than storage, if over 185 sq. m or over 18.00 m in any dimension | At least two stairways to an adjacent floor |
| Storey or portion with occupant load 500 to 999 | At least three exits |
| Storey or portion with occupant load 1,000 or more | At least four exits |
| Floors above the second storey, basements, and cellars used for other than building service | At least two exits |

When only two exits are required, Section 1207 requires them to be separated by at least one-fifth of the perimeter of the area served, measured in a straight line between exits. Where three or more exits are required, arrange them so that if one exit becomes blocked, the others remain available.

## Travel Distance

Section 1207(2)(d) gives the travel-distance rule. No point in a building without a sprinkler system may be more than 45.00 meters from an exterior exit door, horizontal exit, exit passageway, or enclosed stairway, measured along the line of travel.

In a building equipped with a complete automatic fire-extinguishing system, the distance from exits may be increased to 60.00 meters.

That 45.00 m / 60.00 m distinction is the source-backed rule for common sprinkler bonus questions. Do not add a different value unless another specific code provision in the problem controls.

## Exit Width

The total width of exits in meters must not be less than the total occupant load served divided by 165. The required total width should be divided approximately equally among the separate exits.

When lower or upper floors exit through the level under consideration, Section 1207 also counts portions of adjacent floor occupant loads: 50% of the first adjacent storey above and first adjacent storey below, and 25% of the storey immediately beyond the first adjacent storey.

## Exit Doors

The exit-door rules apply to every exit door serving an area with occupant load over 10, or serving hazardous rooms or areas.

Key requirements:

- Exit doors serving hazardous areas or occupant load of 50 or more must swing in the direction of exit travel.
- Exit doors must be openable from the inside without a key or special knowledge or effort, subject to the stated Group E or F exterior-door exception.
- Required exit doorways must permit a door at least 900 mm wide and 2.00 m high.
- Installed exit doors must open at least 90 degrees and leave a clear exitway width of at least 700 mm.
- No leaf of an exit door may exceed 1.20 m in width.
- Revolving, sliding, and overhead doors may not be used as required exits.

## Corridors and Exterior Exit Balconies

The corridor and exterior exit balcony rules apply when they serve as required exits for occupant load over 10.

| Topic | Rule XII value or rule |
| :-- | :-- |
| Minimum corridor or exterior exit balcony width | 1.10 m |
| Trim, handrails, and fully opened doors | May not reduce required width by more than 200 mm |
| Doors in any position | May not reduce corridor width by more than one-half |
| Dead-end corridors or exterior exit balconies | Permitted only when the dead end does not exceed 6.00 m |
| Corridor walls and ceilings | Generally at least one-hour fire-resistive construction |
| Non-door interior openings in required one-hour corridor walls | Total non-door opening area may not exceed 25% of the corridor wall area of the room separated from the corridor |

The exam trap is width reduction. A corridor is not compliant merely because the architectural dimension is 1.10 m on plan. Door swing, handrails, and projections must still preserve the required unobstructed width.

## Stairways and Ramps

Section 1207 treats stairway width by occupant load.

| Stair served occupant load | Minimum stair width |
| :-- | --: |
| More than 50 | 1.10 m |
| 50 or less | 900 mm |
| Private stair serving less than 10 | 750 mm |

Other stairway anchors:

- Riser height may not exceed 200 mm.
- Tread run may not be less than 250 mm.
- Variation within one flight is limited to 5 mm.
- Handrails are generally required on each side.
- Handrail height must be at least 800 mm and not more than 900 mm above the nosing of treads.
- Required stairways must have at least 2.00 m headroom.
- Every building four or more storeys in height must have one stairway extending to the roof unless the roof slope is greater than 1 in 3.

A ramp may be used as an exit if it conforms to the Code. Its width is the width required for corridors.

## Exit Enclosures and Smokeproof Enclosures

Every interior stairway, ramp, or escalator must be enclosed as specified in the Code, subject to the stated exceptions. Enclosure walls must be at least two-hour fire-resistive construction, and openings into exit enclosures are limited to exit doorways and openings in exterior walls.

A smokeproof enclosure consists of a vestibule and continuous stairway enclosed from the highest point to the lowest point by two-hour fire-resistive walls. In buildings five storeys or more in height, one of the required exits must be a smokeproof enclosure.

## Exit Discharge, Courts, and Passageways

Every exit must discharge into a public way, exit court, or exit passageway. Every exit court must discharge into a public way or exit passageway. Exit passageways must be without openings other than required exits and must have fire-resistance at least equal to the relevant building walls, floors, and ceilings, but not less than one hour.

Exit courts and passageways must be at least as wide as the required total width of the tributary exits. Their required width must remain unobstructed except as permitted for corridors.

## Projection Rooms Under Section 1214

When linked questions involve motion-picture projection rooms, Section 1214 controls. For ribbon-type film over 22 mm with electric projection equipment, the projection equipment and related devices must be enclosed in a projection room.

Key values:

- Construction: at least one-hour fire-resistive throughout, with incombustible wall and ceiling finishes.
- Ceiling height: at least 2.40 m from finished floor.
- Floor area: at least 7.00 sq. m plus 3.50 sq. m for each additional machine.
- Exits: at least two doorways separated by not less than one-third the room perimeter.
- Doorway size: each at least 750 mm wide and 2.00 m high.
- Door protection: self-closing fire assembly with three-fourths-hour fire-resistive rating.

## LEA Recall Anchors

Remember the egress sequence as: occupant load, number of exits, exit width, exit arrangement, travel distance, doors, corridors, stairs or ramps, enclosure, discharge.

High-frequency values:

- 45.00 m: maximum travel distance without sprinkler system.
- 60.00 m: maximum travel distance with complete automatic fire-extinguishing system.
- 1.10 m: minimum corridor or required high-load stair width.
- 6.00 m: maximum dead-end corridor or exterior exit balcony length.
- 900 mm x 2.00 m: minimum required exit doorway door size.
- 700 mm: minimum clear exitway width when the door is installed.
- 800 mm to 900 mm: stair handrail height above tread nosing.
- 2.00 m: minimum required stair headroom.
