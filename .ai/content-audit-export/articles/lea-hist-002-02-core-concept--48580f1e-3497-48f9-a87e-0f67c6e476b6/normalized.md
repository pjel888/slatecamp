# Romanesque Architecture

## LEA Point

Romanesque architecture is the medieval round-arch and heavy-vault church tradition before Gothic. The safest exam anchors are semicircular arches, barrel vaults, groin vaults, thick walls, massive piers, small windows, fortress-like interiors, pilgrimage planning, Durham, Speyer, Pisa, Santiago de Compostela, and Cluny as a monastic recognition anchor.

## Source Anchors

- UNESCO Durham anchors a major Norman/Romanesque cathedral with important vaulting and transitional structural features.
- UNESCO Pisa, Speyer, and Santiago anchor major Romanesque and pilgrimage contexts.
- The local Fletcher conversion anchors Romanesque arches, vaults, massing, wall logic, and the transition toward Gothic structural systems.

## Structural Pattern

A barrel vault is a continuous semicircular vault. Its outward thrust pushes against the side walls, so Romanesque builders answer with thick masonry, heavy piers, and smaller openings. Groin vaults can concentrate loads more efficiently, but the overall Romanesque image remains heavy and enclosed compared with Gothic skeletal construction.

## Exam Guardrails

- Do not describe Romanesque churches as light, skeletal, and glass-filled; that is Gothic vocabulary.
- Do not rely on unsupported "first" or "largest" ranking claims. Use Speyer, Cluny, Durham, Pisa, and Santiago as recognition anchors unless the exact ranking is locally captured.
- Do not say Romanesque lacks structural experimentation. Durham and other examples show transitional development toward Gothic.

## Real World Scenario

A board item shows a semicircular barrel-vaulted nave with thick walls, small windows, and massive piers. The source-safe answer is Romanesque, not Gothic, because the structural system still depends on heavy masonry resisting vault thrust.
