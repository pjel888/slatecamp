### Historical Evolution and Rationale

Full-Time Supervision emerged from construction supervision traditions where architect representatives monitored contractor performance. The clerk-of-works model established foundational responsibility for construction oversight. Modern construction complexity, regulatory requirements, and quality standards have expanded supervision scope and intensity. Architect-in-charge responsibility for construction supervision remains a fundamental principle within Philippine architectural practice.

### Supervision Group Organization

Construction supervision teams comprise the architect-in-charge as principal authority and field personnel providing daily coordination. Organization structures vary based on project scale, complexity, and duration. Large projects justify full-time resident supervisors with support personnel for material testing and documentation. Smaller projects may employ periodic site visits by design firm staff supplemented by part-time field representatives. Clear authority hierarchies and communication protocols ensure effective coordination and decision-making.

### Scope of Full-Time Supervision Services

Quality control services verify that materials, workmanship, and construction methods conform to design specifications and standards. Evaluation of work assesses contractor performance against schedule, budget, and quality benchmarks. Daily reporting documents site activities, progress, variations, and issues requiring resolution. Document filing maintains organized records of specifications, submissions, drawings, and correspondence. Field inspections and material testing verify compliance with performance standards.

### Manner of Providing Supervision Services

Full-time supervision can operate under two distinct methods: (1) as Architect-of-Record (AoR) of the project, or (2) as Consulting Architect for full-time supervision services only or as the Construction Supervision Group (CSG). The architect-supervised model maintains separation between design authority and construction executor roles, ensuring design integrity. The architect-constructor model combines design and construction roles when the same organization provides both services. Both models establish clear responsibility frameworks, though authority limitations vary between arrangements.

### Method of Compensation for Supervision

Compensation methods for full-time supervision services may be based on one or more of the following: percentage of project construction cost (PCC), multiple of direct personnel expenses, hourly rates plus overhead, lump sums, and per diems. Percentage-based compensation aligns supervisor interests with project value and complexity. Fixed fees provide cost certainty and suit projects with defined scope and stable budgets. Hybrid arrangements combine percentage and fixed components based on specific project characteristics. The Architect's Guidelines provide detailed guidance on compensation methodology and benchmarks for full-time supervision services.

### Limitation of Authority and Professional Responsibility

Architects in supervisory roles exercise authority over specified construction matters while remaining subject to owner discretion on business decisions. Supervisors cannot exceed contractual authority or assume client obligations. Professional responsibility encompasses standard of care in technical matters, adherence to schedules, and accurate reporting. Supervisors bear professional responsibility for technical decisions affecting structural integrity, subject to applicable provisions of the Civil Code and professional standards of care.

### Legal Responsibilities and Standards of Care

Full-time supervisors assume responsibility for identifying non-conformance and recommending corrective action. Documentation of deficiencies protects both architect and client interests. Standard of care requires vigilant monitoring, prompt issue identification, and professional judgment in technical assessments. Supervisors must balance owner interests against contractor capabilities and construction realities.
