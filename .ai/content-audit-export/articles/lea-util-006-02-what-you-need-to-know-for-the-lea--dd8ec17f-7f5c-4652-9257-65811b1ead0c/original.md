## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal

Apply the basics of architectural lighting design — luminous flux (lumens), illuminance (lux), luminance (cd/m²), luminous efficacy (lm/W), color temperature (Kelvin), color rendering index (CRI) — to select appropriate lighting systems for Philippine building projects.

<ExamTip>
LEA questions on lighting design often test the difference between luminous flux (lumens, total output) and illuminance (lux, light on a surface). Remember: lumens = what the lamp produces; lux = what the room receives. Efficacy (lm/W) tells you how efficient the lamp is.
</ExamTip>

<Tip>
For quick exam recall: office spaces need 300–500 lx; residential living areas 150–300 lx; corridors 100–150 lx. Match color temperature to activity: 2700–3000 K for relaxation (residential), 4000–5000 K for focus (offices, classrooms).
</Tip>

<Warn>
Do not select a lamp based solely on high efficacy. The LEA may test whether you consider CRI for tasks requiring color accuracy (e.g., medical, retail, art galleries need CRI ≥ 80–90). A high-efficacy HID lamp with poor CRI is wrong for color-critical applications.
</Warn>
