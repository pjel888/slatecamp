# Parametricism

## LEA Point
Parametricism is tested as a digitally enabled design methodology associated with parametric relationships, continuous variation, and systematic responsiveness. The source-safe phrasing is that Patrik Schumacher theorized it and Zaha Hadid Architects produced key built examples; it is not universally accepted as the single dominant style of the 21st century.

## Source Anchors
- Patrik Schumacher is the source-safe theorist for Parametricism as a named style/methodology in late-2000s architectural discourse.
- Zaha Hadid Architects' Heydar Aliyev Centre is source-safe for continuous surface, plaza-building relationship, complex geometry, and advanced computing coordination.
- Zaha Hadid Architects' Serpentine North/Sackler Gallery work is source-safe for a 2009-2013 London conversion and tensile extension.
- BIM sources are source-safe for coordinated digital building information and parametric change management, but BIM should not be treated as identical to Parametricism.

## Exam Pattern
The common correct answers are Zaha Hadid Architects for Heydar Aliyev Centre and Serpentine Sackler/North Gallery, Schumacher for the theory, and BIM for coordinated model information and change propagation.

## Guardrails
Do not treat algorithmic complexity as inherently meaningful. The architect still selects parameters, evaluates outcomes, and gives design intent. Distinguish Deconstructivism's fragmented contradiction from Parametricism's continuous, relational variation.

## Real World Scenario
In a studio or office workflow, a facade whose panel sizes vary according to sun exposure, structure, and fabrication limits is a parametric logic example when those variables are linked and update systematically.

## Related Articles
Parametricism is best reviewed after Deconstructivism because both use complex forms, but Parametricism emphasizes coherent relational systems instead of visual rupture.
