# PD 1096 Rule VII Occupancy

## Source-Safe Scope

This local-first repair keeps the article inside checked primary sources: the 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096 for occupancy classification and mixed occupancy, with the Fire Code source family used only for life-safety coordination. It removes media blocks, derivative web citations, and claims that were not checked against local raw PDFs.

## Core Rule

PD 1096 Rule VII classifies buildings by use or occupancy into ten groups, A through J, subdivided into twenty-five divisions. The classification is not just a label. It drives dependent reviews for construction type, fire protection, exits, allowable floor area, building height, parking, and other code controls.

## Occupancy Groups To Keep Straight

- Group A covers residential dwellings. Division A-2 includes non-leasing residential occupants not exceeding ten persons and certain small school, clinic, library, museum, and similar uses listed in the IRR.
- Group B covers residentials, hotels, and apartments that accommodate more than ten persons.
- Group C covers education and recreation uses not classified under the large assembly groups.
- Group D covers institutional uses where care, restraint, or limited mobility changes evacuation risk.
- Group E covers business and mercantile uses, including offices and dining or drinking establishments with an occupant load below one hundred persons.
- Group F covers industrial uses involving incombustible and non-explosive materials.
- Group G covers storage and hazardous uses, including flammable or highly combustible materials and repair garages.
- Group H covers assembly uses below the Group I threshold.
- Group I covers assembly buildings with a stage and an occupant load of one thousand or more.
- Group J covers accessory structures such as agricultural structures, private garages, fences over 1.80 meters, stages, courts, tombs, vaults, and similar listed uses.

## Mixed Occupancy

When a building has more than one occupancy, the default PD 1096 rule is to apply the most restrictive requirement among the occupancies present. The local source records two key exceptions. First, in a one-storey building with more than one occupancy, each portion can conform to the requirement of the occupancy housed in that portion. Second, minor accessory uses may follow the major occupancy if they do not exceed ten percent of the area of any floor and do not exceed ten percent of the basic area allowed under the occupancy requirement.

## Review Sequence

1. Identify the principal use and closest listed occupancy group.
2. Check whether the building has multiple occupancies.
3. Apply the most restrictive requirement unless a checked exception applies.
4. Coordinate the occupancy result with egress, fire-resistive separation, height, area, parking, and other dependent controls.
