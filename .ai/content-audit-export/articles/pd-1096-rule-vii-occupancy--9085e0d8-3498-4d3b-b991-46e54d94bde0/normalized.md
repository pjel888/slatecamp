## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to:

Classify buildings by occupancy group (A through J) and division under PD 1096 Rule VII Table VII; apply the correct classification to mixed-occupancy buildings; identify when the strictest occupancy requirements govern the entire building vs when separated occupancies may have independent requirements; and determine allowable occupancy combinations in the same building.
---

A 30-storey BPO office tower in Makati built in reinforced concrete is governed by very different rules than a small wooden beach house in La Union. The **use/occupancy** classification dictates the minimum **construction type**, fire-resistive requirements, exits, and many other safety provisions.[file:17]

If you misclassify occupancy or construction type, every dependent computation (allowable area, height, exit widths, fire protection) can be wrong. This module aligns strictly with PD 1096 and the 2004 Revised IRR, not foreign codes.[file:17][file:18]

## Core Legal Framework

### 1. Classification by Use/Occupancy (Groups A to J)

| Element         | Detail                                                                                                                               |
| :-------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Legal Basis** | PD 1096, Chapter 7, Sec. 701; Rule VII, Sec. 701 (IRR)[file:17]                                                                      |
| **Definition**  | Buildings are classified by principal use or occupancy, reflecting fire risk, occupant vulnerability, and evacuation needs.[file:17] |
| **scale**       | 10 Groups (A–J), subdivided into 25 Divisions.[file:17]                                                                              |

**Reason for the Law:** A warehouse, hospital, school and jail present different life-safety risks. Grouping by occupancy allows the Code to match structural, fire and egress requirements to actual hazard levels.[file:17]

---

### 2. Mixed Occupancy (Sec. 703)

**Legal Basis:** PD 1096, Sec. 703; Rule VII, Sec. 703 (IRR).[file:17]

**General Rule:** A building with two or more occupancies shall comply with the requirements for the **most restrictive** occupancy involved, unless otherwise provided by the Code.[file:17]

**Exception (a) — One-storey buildings:**  
In **one-storey** buildings containing multiple occupancies, each portion may be treated and designed in accordance with the requirements of its own occupancy, subject to Code provisions on separation.[file:17]

**Exception (b) — Minor accessory uses (≤ 10%):**  
Where accessory uses occupy **not more than 10%** of the floor area of any storey (and within the basic allowable area), the **major occupancy** may govern the classification of the building.[file:17]

**Reason for the Law:** A small clinic or chapel inside a mall should not force the entire mall to follow hospital or church standards; the exceptions prevent over-regulation while still respecting risks in higher-hazard portions.[file:17]

---

### 3. Types of Construction (Types I to V)

| Element                | Detail                                                                                                                        |
| :--------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Legal Basis**        | PD 1096, Chap. 4, Sec. 401–403; Rule IV, Sec. 401–403 (IRR)[file:17]                                                          |
| **Definition**         | Types of Construction classify buildings according to the combustibility and fire-resistance of structural elements.[file:17] |
| **PH scale Direction** | **Type I = least fire-resistive → Type V = most fire-resistive**.[file:17]                                                    |

**Reason for the Law:** Higher-risk occupancies (hospitals, high-rises, assembly buildings) must use more fire-resistive construction than low-risk, low-occupant structures like sheds or small agricultural buildings.[file:17]

### PH vs US Convention (Awareness Only)

The Code does **not** reference US IBC, but for exam awareness:

| PD 1096 (Philippines) | Relative Fire Performance                                         |
| :-------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Type I**            | Wood construction — least fire-resistive.[file:17]                |
| **Type II**           | Wood, 1-hour fire-resistive throughout.[file:17]                  |
| **Type III**          | Masonry and wood, 1-hour, incombustible exterior walls.[file:17]  |
| **Type IV**           | Steel/iron/concrete/masonry, incombustible construction.[file:17] |
| **Type V**            | Fire-resistive construction, highest rating.[file:17]             |

In many US texts, Type I is the most fire-resistive; PD 1096 runs in the **opposite direction**, so do not import foreign mnemonics into LEA answers.[file:17]

### Construction Types — Summary Table

| Type    | Code Name (PD 1096)                             | Materials & Features (simplified)                                                                                                                        | Relative Fire-Resistance                                                                  | Typical Example (PH)                                                     |
| :------ | :---------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **I**   | Wood Construction                               | Structural elements may be any materials allowed, often predominantly wood; lightest protection.[file:17]                                                | Lowest; no general fire-resistive rating specified.                                       | Traditional wood dwellings, small temporary structures.                  |
| **II**  | Wood Construction with Fire-Resistive Treatment | Wood framing; members protected to provide approx. **1-hour fire-resistance**; limited in height/area by Code.[file:17]                                  | 1-hour fire-resistive throughout.                                                         | Treated timber buildings where code allows 1-hr rated wood.              |
| **III** | Masonry and Wood Construction                   | Interior: wood framing; **exterior walls of incombustible fire-resistive material (masonry/concrete)**.[file:17]                                         | 1-hour fire-resistive throughout; exterior walls incombustible.                           | CHB/concrete exterior walls with wood interior framing.                  |
| **IV**  | Steel, Iron, Concrete or Masonry Construction   | Structural components are of incombustible materials (steel/iron/concrete/masonry); non-bearing partitions may use treated wood.[file:17]                | Incombustible construction throughout (not necessarily the maximum fire rating).          | Mid-rise commercial/industrial buildings with exposed concrete/steel.    |
| **V**   | Fire-Resistive Construction                     | Structural members in steel/iron/concrete/masonry; walls, ceilings and permanent partitions are incombustible, **fire-resistive** construction.[file:17] | Highest; designed to maintain structural integrity for specified fire-resistance periods. | High-rise office towers, major hospitals, critical government buildings. |

**Key Distinction — Type II vs Type III:**  
Both provide roughly 1-hour fire-resistance, but **Type III requires incombustible exterior walls**; Type II allows protected wood exteriors within limits.[file:17]

**Key Distinction — Type IV vs Type V:**  
Both use incombustible materials, but **Type V is specifically fire-resistive**, with structural members designed/tested for required fire-resistance time periods; Type IV is incombustible but not necessarily at the strongest fire-resistive grade.[file:17]

## High-Yield Exam Traps

### Trap 1 — Importing US IBC Groups

US IBC uses Group B = Business, Group M = Mercantile, Group A = Assembly, etc. PD 1096 does **not**; in PD 1096:[file:17]

- **Group B** = Residentials, Hotels and Apartments
- **Group E** = Business and Mercantile
- **Group H/I** = Assembly
- There is **no Group M**.

Any choice using US-style group labels as if PH code adopts them is incorrect.

---

### Trap 2 — Reversing the Construction Type scale

PD 1096 runs from **Type I (wood, lowest)** to **Type V (fire-resistive, highest)**.[file:17]  
Answering “Type I is the strongest” is a direct sign you are thinking in US IBC, not PD 1096.

Mnemonic: **“One is flimsy, Five is a fortress.”**

---

### Trap 3 — A vs B: The 10-Person Cutoff

Group A Div. 2 covers residential buildings **for exclusive use of non-leasing occupants not exceeding 10 persons**.[file:17]

Once a similar building (e.g., boarding house, convent, dorm) accommodates **more than 10 persons**, it becomes **Group B**.[file:17] This 10-person threshold is critical in exam questions.

---

### Trap 4 — Group A Includes More Than Single-Family Houses

Group A includes not only single-family dwellings but also churches, community facilities, parks, clubhouses (not for gain), small schools (up to 16 classrooms), certain dormitories, convents and small clinics within the Div. 1–2 definitions.[file:17]

The exam often gives a specific building type (e.g., “16-classroom school on campus”) and asks you to identify its precise division.

---

### Trap 5 — Mis-citing Section 703

- **Sec. 703 / Rule VII, Sec. 703**: Mixed Occupancy (general rule + exceptions).[file:17]
- **Chap. 4, Sec. 401 / Rule IV**: Types of Construction.[file:17]

Citing “Section 703” as the legal basis for Types I–V is incorrect; it governs mixed occupancy, not construction type.

---

### Trap 6 — Group H vs Group I (Stage AND Headcount)

Group I requires **both**:

- a **stage**, and
- an occupant load of **1000 or more**.[file:17]

| Scenario                            | Correct Group                                              |
| :---------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------- |
| With stage + < 1000 occupants       | **Group H, Div. 1**[file:17]                               |
| Without stage + occupant load ≥ 300 | **Group H, Div. 2**[file:17]                               |
| Without stage + occupant load < 300 | **Group H, Div. 3**[file:17]                               |
| With stage + occupant load ≥ 1000   | **Group I**[file:17]                                       |
| Open stadium without stage          | **Group H, Div. 4** (if not otherwise in Group I)[file:17] |

Do not classify a large open stadium without a stage as Group I; it stays within Group H Div. 4.

## Quick-Reference Mnemonics

### Occupancy Alphabet (PH Edition)

```text
A = Anak's house      — Single-family dwellings, small residential/community uses
B = Bed spacers       — Hotels, apartments, boarding houses (>10 persons)
C = Classrooms        — Education and recreation not in H or I
D = Doctors & detainees — Hospitals, nurseries, jails, similar institutions
E = Enterprises       — Business, mercantile, offices, gas stations
F = Factories         — Industrial, incombustible and non-explosive materials
G = Gas & garages     — Storage and hazardous, flammable, repair garages
H = Hundreds          — Assembly under 1000 (with/without stage)
I = Infinite crowds   — Assembly with stage AND ≥1000 occupants
J = Just outside      — Accessory: garages, fences, tanks, courts, tombs
```

### Construction scale (PH Edition — Weakest to Strongest)

```text
Type I   = Wood — least fire-resistive
Type II  = Wood + 1-hour fire-resistive protection
Type III = Masonry + Wood; incombustible exterior walls
Type IV  = Steel / Iron / Concrete / Masonry; incombustible construction
Type V   = Fire-Resistive; highest fire-resistance for structural elements
```

One-liner: **“One is flimsy. Five is a fortress.”**

## Exam Tip

LEA Trap: The ten occupancy groups: A=Residential, B=Commercial, C=Industrial, D=Institutional, E=Educational, F=Assembly, G=Recreational, H=Storage, I=Business, J=Dangerous. For mixed-occupancy, the STRICTEST requirements govern the entire building UNLESS the uses are separated by 2-hour fire-rated construction. The 'separated vs non-separated' distinction is the key exam point.
