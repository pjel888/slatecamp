# Hydrology in Site Planning

## Board Exam Quick Check

Hydrology questions are about the movement, storage, and risk of water on and around the site. The checked local sources connect watershed planning, drainage, rainwater harvesting, open spaces, permeability, river easements, and erosion-control materials.

## Source-Grounded Core

The climate-resilience reference tool directs planners to consider the water cycle in urban planning and design. Plans should support watershed improvement and, where possible, consider alternative water sources, rainwater collection, and domestic water recycling.

The same source identifies disaster-risk actions such as diversifying water resources, improved drainage, storm and wastewater management, and transport or road infrastructure improvements. It also lists ecosystem and structural actions: watershed and reservoir management, maintaining wetlands and urban green spaces, improved drainage, water storage, flood levees, and coastal protection.

## Site Hydrology Moves

Source-backed site hydrology decisions include:

- Protecting drainage lines and waterways.
- Avoiding unnecessary alteration of natural topography.
- Increasing public open spaces along rivers to prevent future exposure.
- Using rainwater harvesting to augment water supply and reduce surface flow.
- Increasing permeability to recharge groundwater.
- Restoring riparian forests and increasing upland tree cover to delay or reduce flood flows.
- Using flood impoundment parks, detention parks, swales, bioswales, rainbeds, and pervious pavement where appropriate.

## Sitework Interface

The Siteworks sources give the construction-side vocabulary. Granular fill or filters help prevent movement of fine particles from soils and natural materials through which seepage occurs. Geotextiles are used for separation, filtration, and in-plane drainage. Subsurface filters may be used around underdrains, edge drains, or under paving. Geocomposite drains and geonets create void space for in-plane water flow while filter fabric keeps soil from filling the drainage path.

## LEA Answer Pattern

When a question asks for the best hydrology answer, prefer the choice that keeps the watershed visible: manage runoff near the source, retain or detain water where possible, preserve drainage and river corridors, increase permeability, and coordinate green and engineered drainage rather than simply pushing water off the site faster.
