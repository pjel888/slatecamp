# Rainwater harvesting and storm drainage

Rainwater harvesting questions in the LEA should be answered from the plumbing and storm-drainage rules first. The checked local source is the Revised National Plumbing Code of the Philippines. It treats roof drains, leaders, downspouts, gutters, and rainwater piping as part of the storm drainage system, separate from soil, waste, and vent piping.

## Exam Anchor

Start by classifying the pipe or device. A conductor or downspout is the vertical pipe that conveys rainwater. A drain carries ground water, surface water, storm water, or wastewater into the building drainage system. A roof-drain or rainwater-piping question is therefore not a sanitary-waste question unless the problem gives a code-approved connection.

## What The Code Supports

Rainwater piping is not used as soil, waste, or vent piping. Roofs, courts, vent shafts, light wells, and similar areas with rainwater drains discharge outside the building or to the gutter, and they are not connected to soil and waste pipe systems. The same separation appears again in the house-drain provisions: rain, surface water, and subsurface water are not connected to an excreta drainage system unless the Administrative Authority first approves it.

Roof drains need corrosion-resistant materials and strainers. Dome-type strainers have a net inlet area at least one and one-half times the outlet pipe area. Flat deck strainers used on occupied decks have a net inlet area at least two times the outlet pipe area. The exam trap is to size the pipe without checking the roof-drain inlet and the discharge path.

Rainwater piping is sized by maximum rainfall intensity and roof area. The code directs the designer to consult local rainfall figures for the maximum rainfall per hour. The same section states that a 102 mm per hour rainfall intensity is normally used around Manila. Table 11-1 is for roof drains and downspout piping by rainfall intensity and horizontal projected roof area. Table 11-2 is for horizontal rainwater piping, and the code gives a worked example using roof area, rainfall intensity, pipe diameter, and slope.

## Safe Sizing Workflow

1. Identify whether the item is a roof drain, vertical leader, downspout, gutter, horizontal rainwater pipe, or sanitary drain.
2. Use the local maximum rainfall intensity given in the problem. If the problem is Manila-based and gives no other local value, the local source supports 102 mm per hour as the normal Manila intensity.
3. Use the horizontal projected roof area, then add wall area only when the code rule for adjacent walls applies.
4. Select pipe or gutter size from the applicable rainwater table.
5. Keep rainwater discharge separate from excreta drainage unless the item states Administrative Authority approval.

## Common Mistakes

- Using a sanitary-drain slope rule as if it automatically sizes rainwater leaders.
- Connecting roof rainwater to soil and waste piping because both are "drainage."
- Importing first-flush depths, cistern freeboard, or runoff coefficients from outside the checked local plumbing-code scope.
- Treating the Rational Method as the controlling answer when the item is really asking for code table selection.

## How It Is Tested

LEA-style questions usually ask what must be separated, which rainfall value or area controls a table lookup, what a downspout or conductor is, or why storm water is not mixed with excreta drainage. If the question gives roof area, rainfall intensity, pipe slope, and diameter options, choose the option that follows the rainwater tables rather than a generic drainage shortcut.

## Checked Local Sources

- Revised National Plumbing Code of the Philippines, Storm Drainage System: connections, roof drains, sizing of rainwater piping, and rainwater separation from soil/waste systems.
