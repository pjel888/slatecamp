# Accessible Seating in Assembly Areas
### BP 344 (2024 IRR) — LEA Review Series

---

## REAL-WORLD HOOK

> You walk into a 750-seat cinema on opening night. The trailers begin. Somewhere in the back row, a person in a wheelchair cannot see the screen because the only "wheelchair area" is at the far left corner, blocked by the accessible route itself. Her companion stands awkwardly in the aisle. In front of her, another wheelchair user must pass through her space to reach his seat. The theater was built in 2005 under the old IRR. Now it's 2026. The new IRR is in force. **Would this theater pass inspection?** Almost certainly not — on at least four separate counts. This module tells you every one of them.

## CORE MNEMONIC FRAMEWORK

### **"DISC — WAH — 5P"**

| Letter | Stands For | What It Means |
|--------|-----------|---------------|
| **D** | **Dispersal** | Spaces spread throughout the seating bowl |
| **I** | **Independent Access** | No wheelchair space accessed through another |
| **S** | **Sightlines** | Unobstructed views; companion seats beside, not behind |
| **C** | **Circulation Separation** | Accessible route CANNOT overlap wheelchair spaces |
| **W** | **Width × Depth** | 900 mm × 1200 mm minimum footprint per space |
| **A** | **Adjoin Circulation** | Viewing areas must adjoin accessible circulation routes |
| **H** | **Handrails at 750 mm** | Front and sides of wheelchair positions |
| **5P** | **5% Priority Waiting** | 5% of waiting-area seats reserved for PWDs |

</LawCallout>

## REASON FOR THE LAW

### Why 3% and Not a Flat Number?
Because **sclea matters**. A 50-seat chapel needs far fewer WC spaces than a 10,000-seat arena. The old tiered table was an approximation; the 3% rule is proportional and eliminates the "cliff effect" (e.g., a 499-seat hall needed only 6 spaces, but a 501-seat hall needed 7).

### Why Can't an Accessible Route Overlap a Wheelchair Space? (Sec. 4.2)
Because when a wheelchair user occupies that space, a circulation route running through it would **trap or block them**. The space must be a safe haven, not a corridor.

### Why Can't You Access One WC Space Through Another? (Sec. 4.3)
Dignity and independence. If wheelchair user A must move to let wheelchair user B reach their space, **B's accessibility is contingent on A's cooperation** — which violates the principle of autonomous access enshrined in RA 7277.

### Why 750 mm Handrails at WC Positions? (Sec. 4.7)
Seated wheelchair users reach forward at approximately 750–800 mm. A 900 mm handrail (standard stair height) is **too high for lateral support** from a seated position. The 750 mm height aligns with the natural arm reach of a person seated in a wheelchair.

### Why 5% Priority Waiting Seats?
Waiting in a queue while standing is difficult or impossible for many PWDs. Designated seating ensures they do not have to choose between holding their place in line and physical exhaustion.

## COMPUTATION EXAMPLE: 750-SEAT CINEMA

**Given:** A multiplex cinema has 750 seats in a single auditorium.

**Step 1: Apply 2024 IRR 3% Rule**
$$750 \times 3\% = 22.5 → \textbf{Round up to 23 wheelchair spaces}$$

**Step 2: Apply Old IRR for Comparison**
- 6 spaces for first 500 seats
- Remaining 250 seats above 500: 250 ÷ 100 = 2.5 → 2 additional spaces (or 3, rounding up)
- **Old IRR total: 8 or 9 spaces**

**Step 3: Key Takeaway**
The 2024 rule **dramatically increases** the requirement: 23 vs. 8–9 spaces. This represents a ~2.5× increase in required accessible seating.

**Step 4: Footprint Impact**
- Each space: 900 mm × 1200 mm = 1.08 m²
- 23 spaces: **24.84 m² minimum dedicated to wheelchair seating**
- Plus companion seats, aisle integration, and accessible routes = significant planning impact

**Step 5: Priority Waiting Seats**
- If lobby waiting area has 50 seats: 50 × 5% = **3 priority seats** minimum

## SIGHTLINE DESIGN PRINCIPLES

```
TIERED CINEMA — PLAN VIEW (Schematic)

SCREEN
|==========================================|
|         GENERAL SEATING (rows A-Z)       |
|                                          |
|  [WC][C]  ← Row L  →  [WC][C]           |
|           ← Row M  →                    |
|  [WC][C]  ← Row R  →  [WC][C]           |
|_________________________________________|
        ↑ Accessible Circulation Route ↑
        (DOES NOT overlap WC spaces above)

[WC] = Wheelchair Space (900×1200mm)
[C]  = Companion Seat (standard seat width)
```

**Key Sightline Rules:**
- WC spaces at **multiple elevations** for unobstructed views
- Spaces **dispersed** across left, center, and right of seating bowl
- Not all grouped in one corner (dispersal requirement implied by "adjoin accessible circulation throughout")
- Companion seat is **beside** (same row), not behind or in front

## TRAP LEART BOXES

---

<Callout variant="lea-watch" title="Trap Leart">

### 🚨 TRAP LEART #1: "Round Down" Trick
The exam may present **3% of an odd number** and expect rounding. Always **round UP** to the nearest whole number for accessibility counts. Never round down — that would provide fewer accessible spaces than required.
- 3% of 750 = 22.5 → **23** (not 22)
- 3% of 333 = 9.99 → **10** (not 9)

</Callout>

---

<Callout variant="lea-watch" title="Trap Leart">

### 🚨 TRAP LEART #2: Old vs. New IRR
Questions may specify "under the 2004 IRR" or "under the current (2024) IRR." Read carefully. If a building was **designed in 2005**, the old tiered table may apply. If it is being designed **today**, the 3% rule applies.

</Callout>

---

<Callout variant="lea-watch" title="Trap Leart">

### 🚨 TRAP LEART #3: The 750 mm Handrail Height
"What is the required handrail height at accessible stairs?" = **900 mm** (BP 344 stair standard)
"What is the required handrail height at wheelchair seating areas?" = **750 mm** (App. D Sec. 4.7)
These are DIFFERENT heights for DIFFERENT contexts. The exam loves this distinction.

</Callout>

---

<Callout variant="lea-watch" title="Trap Leart">

### 🚨 TRAP LEART #4: Companion Seats Are Not Always Required
The **2024 IRR explicitly mandates companion seats in sports facilities** (Sec. 3.3.a). For theaters and cinemas, companion seating is best practice but the explicit mandate is sports-specific. Don't over-generalize.

</Callout>

---

<Callout variant="lea-watch" title="Trap Leart">

### 🚨 TRAP LEART #5: Route Overlap
"The accessible route may pass through wheelchair spaces if the spaces are unoccupied." — **FALSE.** Sec. 4.2 is absolute. The route shall NOT overlap wheelchair seating areas, occupied or not.

</Callout>

---

<Callout variant="lea-watch" title="Trap Leart">

### 🚨 TRAP LEART #6: 5% Priority ≠ 5% Accessible
The 5% priority waiting seats are for **waiting areas** (lobbies, queues). They are **separate** from the 3% wheelchair spaces in the seating bowl. Do not conflate the two percentages.

</Callout>

## PROTÉGÉ EFFECT CHALLENGE

You are now the reviewer. A classmate says:

> *"Our design has a 500-seat theater with 6 wheelchair spaces, all grouped in a single row at the back. An accessible corridor passes through three of the spaces. Each space is 900 mm × 1200 mm. The project was designed under the 2024 IRR."*

**Identify all the violations and explain why each one is wrong.**

*(Write your answer before checking below)*

---

**ANSWER KEY:**

1. **Wrong number of spaces:** 500 × 3% = 15 spaces required. Only 6 provided. **Violation: App. D Sec. 4.**
2. **All in one row at the back (dispersal failure):** Wheelchair spaces must adjoin accessible circulation throughout the seating bowl, implying dispersal. Clustering all spaces in one location denies PWDs equal choice of seating location. **Violation: App. D Sec. 4.4 + RA 7277 spirit.**
3. **Accessible corridor passing through WC spaces:** The accessible route SHALL NOT overlap wheelchair seating areas. **Direct violation: App. D Sec. 4.2.**
4. **Access through adjacent spaces:** If spaces are grouped in a row, accessing an interior space requires passing through the outer spaces. **Violation: App. D Sec. 4.3.**
