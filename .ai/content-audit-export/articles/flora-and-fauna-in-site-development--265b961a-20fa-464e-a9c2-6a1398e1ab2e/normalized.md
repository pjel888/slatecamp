# Flora and Fauna in Site Development
## Learning Goal
After reading this article, you should be able to:

Apply environmental regulations for tree preservation and habitat protection during site development: identify protected species under DENR regulations; calculate tree preservation zone radii; determine when an Environmental Compliance Certificate (ECC) is required; and apply DENR buffer requirements for developments near protected areas and waterways.
---

## The Real-World Scenario (The Hook)

You are the architect-of-record for a 2-hectare mixed-use development in Antipolo, Rizal. The client's lot borders a DENR-classified watershed reserve and contains over forty mature Narra (*Pterocarpus indicus*) and Molave (*Vitex parviflora*) trees — both premium-hardwood species under conservation watch. Before you can even submit a building permit application, the DENR-EMB regional office flags your project as requiring an Environmental Compliance Certificate (ECC). Your site plan must now demonstrate how you will preserve existing flora, protect adjacent habitats, and comply with the Philippine Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) System — or the project dies on paper.

## Core Concepts

- **Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) System (PD 1586 & DENR DAO 2003-30):** The Philippine EIS System, not PD 1096, is the governing framework for evaluating ecological impacts of development projects. Projects located in or adjacent to Environmentally Critical Areas (ECAs) — such as watersheds, mangrove swamps, coral reef areas, habitats of endangered species, and national parks — are required to secure an ECC from the DENR-EMB before construction.

- **Environmentally Critical Areas (ECAs) vs. Environmentally Critical Projects (ECPs):** The EIS System classifies triggers into two categories. ECAs are sensitive locations (e.g., within NIPAS areas, prime agricultural land, near water bodies). ECPs are project types that inherently carry high environmental risk (e.g., heavy industries, mining, major infrastructure). A project can be triggered by either or both.

- **Tree Preservation as a Site Planning Discipline:** Preserving existing mature trees is not merely aesthetic — it controls erosion, manages stormwater through root absorption, reduces the urban heat island effect, and maintains biodiversity corridors. Philippine practice integrates tree preservation into the site development plan through root protection zones (RPZs), adjusted building footprints, and transplanting protocols.

- **The DENR-EMB as the Lead Agency:** For flora and fauna concerns, the correct regulatory body is the Department of Environment and Natural Resources — Environmental Management Bureau (DENR-EMB), not the Office of the Building Official (OBO). The OBO handles the National Building Code (PD 1096); the DENR-EMB handles the EIS System and ECC issuance under PD 1586 and DAO 2003-30.

## Crucial Formulas & Computations

[SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK:ui_0003 component="ExamTip" status="preserved"]

OBO (Office of the Building Official) = Building permit under PD 1096. If a question says "who issues the ECC?" the answer is DENR-EMB, NOT the OBO.

While flora/fauna preservation is primarily regulatory and qualitative, two quantitative tools frequently appear in EIA studies for site development:

### 1. Tree Survey & Root Protection Zone (RPZ) Calculation

**Formula:**
```
RPZ Radius = 12 × DBH
```
Where:
- **DBH** = Diameter at Breast Height (trunk diameter measured at 1.4 m above ground level)
- **RPZ Radius** = The minimum undisturbed radius around the tree (no excavation, no compaction, no grade changes)

**Example:**
A mature Narra tree has a DBH of 0.45 m (45 cm).

RPZ Radius = 12 × 0.45 m = **5.40 m**

This means no construction activity, trenching, or soil compaction is permitted within a 5.40 m radius from the trunk center. Your site plan must show this circle as a protected zone.

### 2. Canopy Coverage Ratio (for Green Area Compliance)

**Formula:**
```
Canopy Coverage (%) = (Total Canopy Area / Total Lot Area) × 100
```

**Example:**
A 10,000 sq.m. lot has 25 mature trees with an average canopy spread of 8 m diameter each.

- Canopy area per tree = π × (4)² = 50.27 sq.m.
- Total canopy area = 25 × 50.27 = 1,256.6 sq.m.
- Canopy Coverage = (1,256.6 / 10,000) × 100 = **12.6%**

This metric is used in EIA reports to demonstrate the ecological value of existing vegetation and to set targets for post-development canopy retention or replacement planting.

## LEA Exam Traps and Common Mistakes

- **Trap 1: Citing PD 1096 for environmental/ecological matters.** The National Building Code governs building design, not ecological preservation. For flora, fauna, EIA, and ECC requirements, the correct references are PD 1586, DENR DAO 2003-30, RA 7586 (NIPAS), and RA 9147 (Wildlife Act). Do not force PD 1096 where it does not belong.

- **Trap 2: Confusing the ECC with the Building Permit.** The ECC is issued by the DENR-EMB and certifies environmental compliance. The Building Permit is issued by the local OBO under PD 1096. They are separate processes — the ECC is a prerequisite that must be obtained *before* applying for the building permit for projects in ECAs/ECPs.

- **Trap 3: Assuming all trees can simply be "transplanted."** On the LEA, the conservative/safe answer is always to preserve trees *in situ* (in their original location) by adjusting the building footprint. Transplanting is a last resort with low survival rates for mature Philippine hardwoods. The DENR Tree Cutting Permit process is separate and requires justification.

## Active Recall Questions

### Question 1
A developer plans to build a resort adjacent to a DENR-classified watershed reserve. Which document must be secured from the DENR-EMB **before** applying for a building permit?

A. Fire Safety Inspection Certificate (FSIC)  
B. Environmental Compliance Certificate (ECC)  
C. Locational Clearance from HLURB  
D. Barangay Business Permit  

> **Answer: B. Environmental Compliance Certificate (ECC)**  
> Projects in or adjacent to Environmentally Critical Areas (ECAs) such as watersheds are required to secure an ECC under PD 1586 and DENR DAO 2003-30. The ECC certifies that the project's Environmental Management Plan (EMP) has been reviewed and approved. The FSIC (A) is under RA 9514 (Fire Code) and is obtained after construction, not before. Locational Clearance (C) is a zoning tool, not an environmental one.

### Question 2
Under PD 1067 (Water Code of the Philippines), what is the required easement from the banks of rivers and streams in **urban areas** where no structures may be built?

A. 5 meters  
B. 20 meters  
C. 3 meters  
D. 40 meters  

> **Answer: C. 3 meters**  
> Article 51 of PD 1067 establishes easement zones of 3 meters in urban areas, 20 meters in agricultural areas, and 40 meters in forest areas from river/stream banks. These are no-build zones reserved for public use (recreation, navigation, salvage). The 5-meter figure (A) is a common confusion with the PHIVOLCS fault-line buffer, and 40 meters (D) applies to forest areas only.

### Question 3
An architect's site survey reveals a mature Molave tree (DBH = 0.60 m) directly in the path of a proposed access road. Using the standard Root Protection Zone formula, what is the minimum radius that must be kept undisturbed around this tree?

A. 3.60 meters  
B. 6.00 meters  
C. 7.20 meters  
D. 12.00 meters  

> **Answer: C. 7.20 meters**  
> The RPZ radius = 12 × DBH = 12 × 0.60 m = 7.20 m. This means no excavation, grading, or compaction within 7.20 m of the trunk center. Option A (3.60 m) would result from incorrectly using 6× DBH. Option D (12 m) confuses the multiplier (12) with the result. The architect must either reroute the access road or justify tree removal through a DENR Tree Cutting Permit — but on the LEA, the safe answer is always to preserve and reroute.
