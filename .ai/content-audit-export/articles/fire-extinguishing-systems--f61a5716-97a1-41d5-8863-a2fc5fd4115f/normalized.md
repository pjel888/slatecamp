# Fire Extinguishing Systems

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to identify when PD 1096 requires automatic fire-extinguishing systems, distinguish dry standpipes from wet standpipes, recall the basic sizing and supply rules in Section 1212, and connect those systems to BFP review, inspection, and occupancy clearance under RA 9514.

## Controlling Sources

This article is source-audited against the DPWH official PDF of the 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096, especially Rule XII Section 1212, and the 2019 Revised IRR of RA 9514, especially Rule 9 Sections 9.0.2.3, 9.0.3.3, and 9.0.4.1.

## What Counts as a Fire-Extinguishing System Topic

For local board-exam purposes, fire-extinguishing systems are not only sprinklers. Rule XII Section 1212 covers automatic fire-extinguishing systems, dry standpipes, wet standpipes, fire department connections, hose outlets, water supply, pumps, and periodic approval or testing.

Answer questions from the exact system named. A sprinkler rule, a dry standpipe rule, and a wet standpipe rule are not interchangeable.

## Automatic Fire-Extinguishing Systems

Section 1212 requires automatic fire-extinguishing systems in several conditions, including:

- Every storey, basement, or cellar with an area of at least 200 square meters when used for habitation, recreation, dining, study, or work and with an occupant load greater than 20.
- Dressing rooms, rehearsal rooms, workshops, factories, and other rooms with more than 10 occupants.
- Group H or Group I assembly halls with an occupant load over 500 when the next doors are more than 30 meters from the nearest safe fire dispersal area, opening to an exit court, or street.
- Rooms used for storing or handling photographic film, x-ray nitrocellulose film, or other inflammable articles.

The rule is conditional. Do not generalize it into "all buildings need sprinklers" or "all assembly occupancies need automatic sprinklers." The area, use, occupant load, occupancy group, and distance facts matter.

## Dry Standpipes

Section 1212 requires dry standpipes in every building four or more storeys in height.

Important dry standpipe details:

- Pipes must be wrought iron or galvanized steel.
- The system must withstand a test pressure of 20 kilograms per square centimeter.
- The size must be capable of delivering 900 liters per minute from each of any three outlets simultaneously under pressure created by one fire engine or pumper.
- The dry standpipe must extend from the ground floor to and over the roof.
- Outlets are located at each storey, with roof outlets also required.
- The fire department connection is placed on the street front and labeled "CONNECTION TO DRY STANDPIPE."

For recall, dry standpipes are about fire department supply into the building. They are required by height: four or more storeys.

## Wet Standpipes

Section 1212 requires wet standpipes by occupancy and height or area:

- Group H and Group I occupancies of any height.
- Group C occupancies two or more storeys in height.
- Groups B, D, E, F, and G occupancies three or more storeys in height.
- Group G and Group E occupancies over 1,800 square meters in area.

The IRR gives an exception for Group H buildings without a stage and with seating capacity under 500: they need not have wet standpipes.

Wet standpipes must have enough internal diameter to deliver 190 liters per minute at 2 kilograms per square centimeter at the hose connections. Group H and Group I systems must supply any two outlets simultaneously; the other listed occupancies must supply one outlet.

## Water Supply, Pumps, and Hose

Section 1212 gives the operational supply requirements:

- A street main of at least 100 millimeters may supply the system.
- If pressure is insufficient, tanks must furnish at least 1,500 liters per minute for at least 10 minutes.
- The required pressure at the topmost base outlet is at least 2 kilograms per square centimeter.
- Fire pumps must deliver at least 1,000 liters per minute and provide at least 2 kilograms per square centimeter at the topmost hose outlet.
- Hose must be at least 38 millimeters and not longer than 23 meters.

These values should be kept separate from occupant-load and travel-distance rules in other sections. Section 1212 is a system requirement section.

## Approval and Periodic Testing

Section 1212 requires fire-extinguishing systems, including sprinklers, wet and dry standpipes, automatic chemical extinguishers, basement pipe inlets, and related appurtenances, to meet Fire Department approval as to installation and location. They are also subject to periodic tests.

That approval requirement is why design compliance and inspection evidence matter. A system is not treated as complete only because it appears on drawings.

## Link to RA 9514 Occupancy Review

The 2019 Revised IRR of RA 9514 connects fire protection systems to the BFP review and occupancy process:

- Rule 9 Section 9.0.2.3 covers inspection after completion and before the Certificate of Occupancy.
- Rule 9 Section 9.0.3.3 requires a Fire Safety Compliance and Commissioning Report before beneficial occupancy of newly constructed, altered, or modified buildings.
- Rule 9 Section 9.0.4.1 treats the Fire Safety Inspection Certificate as a prerequisite for Certificate of Occupancy, business permits, permits to operate, licenses, and other permits issued by government offices.

For LEA questions, the systems rule and the occupancy clearance rule are related but distinct. PD 1096 Section 1212 tells you when and how the system is required. RA 9514 Rule 9 tells you how BFP review, commissioning, inspection, and FSIC fit into occupancy and permit clearance.
