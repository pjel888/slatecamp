# Lathing and Plastering

## Learning Goal

Identify the source-backed relationship among lath, plaster, gypsum wall board, fire-resistive requirements, and type of construction under PD 1096, then distinguish metal lath, gypsum lath, gypsum plaster, gypsum board, and stucco using checked local material sources.

## Source-Audited Scope

PD 1096 Section 1215 and the 2004 RIRR both state that installation of lath, plaster, and gypsum wall board shall conform to fire-resistive rating requirements and the type of construction of the building. This is the legal anchor for this article.

The 2004 RIRR fire-resistance table also lists metal lath with vermiculite or perlite gypsum plaster as a protection material for metal structural members. The OCR table provides minimum thickness values by fire-resistance duration, so treat the exact assembly as code-table data, not as a generic product claim.

The Metals Lecture source identifies steel mesh and wire cloth as materials used for concrete reinforcement, lath for plaster, stucco, and cement. It also describes welded wire fabric and self-centering laths made as flat or segmental sheets with ribs and open mesh.

The Salvan Building Materials source supplies material vocabulary: gypsum is a hydrated calcium sulfate from which gypsum plaster is made; gypsum wallboard is a gypsum-core board for interior walls and ceilings; gypsum lath is used as a base for plaster; expanded lath is open metal mesh used to admit mortar or plastered cement; gypsum plaster can be applied over gypsum lath, metal lath, fiberboard plaster base, or masonry; and stucco is portland-cement plaster applied to exterior surfaces.

## Core Rules

1. Start with Section 1215. Lath, plaster, and gypsum wall board are not free-floating finish choices; they must match fire-resistive rating requirements and the building's type of construction.
2. Separate legal compliance from material vocabulary. PD 1096 tells you the compliance relationship; Metals Lecture and Building Materials tell you what the materials are.
3. Know metal lath. Steel mesh and wire cloth may function as lath for plaster, stucco, and cement. Self-centering laths are open mesh forms produced from stamped or deformed metal sheets.
4. Know gypsum lath. Gypsum lath is a gypsum-core product used as a base for plaster and for adhesion of gypsum plaster.
5. Know gypsum wallboard. Gypsum wallboard is an interior wall and ceiling sheathing/panel product with a gypsum core and paper facing.
6. Know plaster and stucco. Gypsum plaster is an interior finish material; stucco is portland-cement plaster for exterior surfaces. Over wood sheathing, stucco needs a wire tie/backing relationship; over masonry it may be applied directly according to the checked material source.
7. Do not invent thicknesses. Use the checked PD 1096 RIRR table for fire-resistance assembly thicknesses, and use material sources only for material identity and use.

## Exam Traps

- Do not answer with Type X gypsum-board thicknesses from outside the checked source set from the older draft. Type X details were not source-backed in this Batch 053 raw PDF set.
- Do not say Section 1215 gives a single universal plaster thickness. It ties lath, plaster, and wall board to fire-resistive rating requirements and type of construction.
- Do not confuse gypsum lath with gypsum wallboard. Gypsum lath is a plaster base; gypsum wallboard is an interior wall/ceiling panel product.
- Do not call stucco a gypsum plaster. The checked material source identifies stucco as portland-cement plaster applied to exterior surfaces.
