# Space Programming Process Deliverables and Use in Design

## Learning Goal

Use SPP 201 to recognize architectural programming, space planning, and space management as pre-design services that organize requirements before design decisions are fixed.

## Architectural Programming

SPP 201 treats architectural programming as an analytical, problem-seeking process. The architect identifies horizontal and vertical requirements, then produces a space program with the ambiance and cost range needed by the client.

This is not contractor selection and it is not construction administration. It is a pre-design service that turns the client's needs into a usable basis for later planning and design.

## Methods Used

The source-supported methods are practical information-gathering tools: interviews, consultations, focus group discussions, surveys, space audits, and analysis. These methods help the architect test what the client says they need against actual use patterns, organizational relationships, and space requirements.

## Space Planning

Space planning is the related service that determines the size and configuration of spaces for a stated purpose. It translates the program into spatial quantities and relationships that can guide the architectural plan.

## Space Management Studies

Space management studies look beyond room counts. They analyze space requirements, organizational structure, linkages, and interactions. The resulting space program becomes the basis for the architectural plan and design.

## LEA Practice Guardrails

For the LEA, keep the sequence clear: programming defines and organizes requirements before detailed design decisions harden. Space planning translates those requirements into size and configuration. Space management checks the organization, relationships, and interactions that make the program usable.

Avoid treating programming as bidding, contract award, or site supervision. The checked SPP 201 source supports programming, space planning, and space management as pre-design services used to prepare the design basis.

## Real World Scenario

A clinic owner says, "We only need a room list," but the staff groups disagree about patient flow, storage, and department adjacency. Under SPP 201, the architect can use interviews, consultations, focus group discussions, surveys, space audits, and analysis to build the program.

The exam-safe answer is not to start final drawings. The architect first identifies horizontal and vertical requirements, prepares a space program with ambiance and cost range, then uses space planning and space management studies to test size, configuration, linkages, and interactions.
