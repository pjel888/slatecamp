Picture this: You are the lead architect for a high-density office tower in a CBD. The client wants to maximize profit by building as tall and as wide as the code allows. Your job is to thread the needle between **Building Height Limit (BHL)**, **Floor to Lot Area Ratio (FLAR)**, **Gross Floor Area (GFA)**, and **Total Gross Floor Area (TGFA)**.[file:17][file:18]

Miscalculate the FLAR and the building loses sleaable floor space. Confuse TGFA with building volume and the project fails compliance review. The board exam tests exactly this: which metric controls the _footprint_, which controls the _density_, and which sets the _vertical limit_.[file:17][file:18]

## PATTERN INTERRUPT — The Three Independent Controls

Before reading further, lock this into memory:[file:17][file:18]

| Control  | What It Limits                                       | Governing Table / Section                       |
| :------- | :--------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------- |
| **BHL**  | How HIGH the building can go                         | Rule VII, Sec. 707; Table VII.2                 |
| **FLAR** | How much GFA (floor area) can be built               | Guidelines on Building Bulk, Table VII.G.1      |
| **PSO**  | How much of the lot the building footprint can cover | Rule VIII, Sec. 803; Table VII.1 / Table VIII.1 |

These three controls are **independent**. High FLAR does not mean high BHL, and high PSO does not mean high FLAR. On the exam, all three apply simultaneously — and you must use the most restrictive result.[file:17][file:18]

## Core Concept 1: Building Height Limit (BHL)

**Definition (Rule VII, Sec. 707):** The maximum height permitted for a building or structure based on its proposed use or occupancy, after applying other development controls and site conditions.[file:17][file:18]

**How BHL is measured:** From the **established grade line** (as defined in Rule I / Rule VII) to the **topmost portion** of the proposed building or structure; separate aviation-height limits from the aviation authority may still apply.[file:17][file:18]

**The 3-meter tolerance rule:** On sloping sites, the height measured from the _lowest_ adjoining ground must not exceed the permitted BHL by more than **3.00 meters**, to avoid penalizing natural topography.[file:17][file:18]

### BHL Exemptions — What Is NOT Counted Against BHL

The following projections above the main roof are permitted and are **excluded** from BHL computation, subject to structural and safety requirements:[file:17][file:18]

- Signage, masts, antennas, telecommunication towers, beacons, and similar appurtenances.

**Towers, spires, steeples and similar elements** that are part of the building and are not used for habitation or storage:[file:17][file:18]

| Material                             | Height Rule                                                                           |
| :----------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Entirely incombustible materials** | May extend beyond BHL, limited by structural and special regulations (e.g., aviation) |
| **Combustible materials**            | Maximum of **6.00 m above the prescribed BHL**                                        |

## Core Concept 2: Floor to Lot Area Ratio (FLAR)

**Definition (Guidelines on Building Bulk, Table VII.G.1):** A development control that limits the maximum allowable **Gross Floor Area (GFA)** that can be generated on a given **Total Lot Area (TLA)**; also referred to as Floor Area Ratio (FAR).[file:17]

**Application:** FLAR is the **primary or initial determinant** of building bulk before BHL and other height-related checks are applied.[file:17]

**The Formula:**

```text
Allowable Maximum GFA = TLA x FLAR
```

**Mental Hook:** _FLAR is your floor area budget; BHL is your height ceiling; they are independent, and FLAR usually bites first on constrained lots._[file:17]

[SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK:ui_0004 component="LawCallout" status="preserved"]

- Text: FLAR links total habitable floor area to lot size and zone capacity, preventing very small lots from carrying tower-like densities that overload surrounding infrastructure and urban form.[file:17]
[/SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK]

## Core Concept 3: Gross Floor Area (GFA)

**Definition (Rule VII, Sec. 705 / Guidelines):** GFA is the total floor space within the perimeter of the permanent external building walls, measured to interior surfaces, inclusive of habitable and usable spaces such as residential units, offices, corridors, lobbies and mezzanines.[file:17][file:18]

**Included in GFA:**  
Building projections that function as integral floor areas directly connected to interior spaces, including **balconies with a floor area of 10.0 sq.m or less** per unit.[file:17][file:18]

### LEART: GFA Exclusions — Do NOT Count These Against FLAR

| Exclusion Category                                   | Examples (non-exhaustive)                                                                                                                                                                            |
| :--------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Parking and driveways**                            | Covered parking areas, covered driveways, loading/unloading bays used exclusively by vehicles                                                                                                        |
| **Vertical penetrations in parking floors**          | Stairways and elevator shafts in floors used _exclusively_ for parking or utilities                                                                                                                  |
| **Uncovered / non-habitable areas**                  | Helipads, A/C cooling towers, ACCU platforms, overhead water tanks, roof decks, laundry drying areas and cages, swimming and wading pools, whirlpools/Jacuzzis, terraces, gardens, courts and plazas |
| **Oversized balconies & external egress structures** | **Balconies exceeding 10.0 sq.m** each, exterior fire escape stairs and similar egress elements                                                                                                      |

[file:17][file:18]

## Core Concept 4: Total Gross Floor Area (TGFA)

**Definition (Guidelines on Building Bulk):** TGFA is the total floor space in the main and all accessory structures, equal to **GFA plus all non-GFA covered/enclosed floor areas**.[file:17]

**The Formula:**

```text
TGFA = GFA + Non-GFA Areas
```

| Component   | Includes                                                                                                                             |
| :---------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **GFA**     | Habitable/sleaable floor space: residential units, office areas, lobbies, corridors, balconies ≤10.0 sq.m per unit                   |
| **Non-GFA** | Parking decks, covered driveways, utility rooms, large balconies (>10.0 sq.m), external fire escape stairs, and other excluded items |

[file:17][file:18]

- Text: TGFA reflects the total built volume that still imposes load on infrastructure (roads, utilities, fire protection) even if the spaces are not habitable, so it is used for impact and compliance checks apart from pure density limits.[file:17]
[/SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK]

## CRITICAL DISTINCTION — TGFA vs. Building Volume (AMVB)

**Read this section twice.** This distinction appears regularly in exam computations.[file:17]

|                       | TGFA                                   | AMVB                                 |
| :-------------------- | :------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------- |
| **Full name**         | Total Gross Floor Area                 | Allowable Maximum Volume of Building |
| **Unit**              | sq.m                                   | cu.m                                 |
| **What it measures**  | Sum of all floor areas (GFA + non-GFA) | Three-dimensional building envelope  |
| **How it is derived** | GFA + non-GFA areas                    | **AMBF x BHL**                       |
| **Governs**           | Floor-area density (with FLAR)         | Bulk/envelope control                |

**The trap formula:**  
`AMBF x BHL = AMVB` — this yields **cubic meters**, not square meters.[file:17]

**Correct source for allowable TGFA:** **Table VII.1** and the related guidelines, which express allowable maximum TGFA as a function of PSO (Percentage of Site Occupancy) and TLA; this is separate from the FLAR-based GFA and from AMVB.[file:17]

**Rule:** When `AMBF x BHL` appears in a problem, the correct dimension is **volume**; any option that interprets this as TGFA in sq.m is wrong for TGFA purposes.[file:17]

## The Master Formula Chain

_This sequence tracks how Rule VII and the Building Bulk Guidelines are typically applied in computations._[file:17]

**Mnemonic — "TaPa-TAFB" (Total, Area, Floors, Building):**

```text
Step 1: TLA   (Total Lot Area) — from survey or title
        |
        v
Step 2: PSO   (Percentage of Site Occupancy) — Rule VIII, Sec. 803 / Table VII.1 / Table VIII.1
        |
        v
Step 3: AMBF  = TLA x PSO              [Allowable Max Building Footprint, sq.m]
        |
        v
Step 4: TOSL  = TLA x (1 - PSO)        [Total Open Space within Lot, sq.m]
        |
        v
Step 5: FLAR  (Floor to Lot Area Ratio) — Guidelines, Table VII.G.1
        |
        v
Step 6: Max GFA = TLA x FLAR           [Floor area budget, sq.m]
        |
        v
Step 7: Max Floors (by area) = Max GFA / AMBF
        |
        v
Step 8: BHL   — Rule VII, Table VII.2  [Height ceiling, storeys and meters]
        |
        v
       USE THE MORE RESTRICTIVE of Step 7 (FLAR-driven floors) and Step 8 (BHL).
```

**Key principle:** Even if BHL allows many storeys, FLAR may only yield enough GFA for fewer full floors at the chosen footprint; conversely, a generous FLAR cannot override a stricter BHL ceiling. Compute both and adopt whichever is more restrictive.[file:17][file:18]

## High-Yield Exam Traps

Your brain looks for shortcuts; these traps exploit that tendency. Internalize each pattern.[file:17][file:18]

---

### TRAP 1 — Confusing TGFA (Area) with AMVB (Volume)

|                    | Wrong             | Correct                                              |
| :----------------- | :---------------- | :--------------------------------------------------- |
| **Formula**        | AMBF x BHL = TGFA | AMBF x BHL = AMVB (volume)                           |
| **Unit of result** | sq.m (incorrect)  | cu.m (correct)                                       |
| **What it gives**  | Mis-labeled area  | Allowable Maximum Volume of Building (bulk envelope) |

**Fix:** TGFA comes from PSO and TLA (Table VII.1 / Guidelines), not from AMBF x BHL. Any option that treats AMBF x BHL as TGFA in sq.m is incorrect.[file:17]

---

### TRAP 2 — Assuming FLAR Implies BHL

Many candidates assume that a very high FLAR automatically means a very tall BHL (and vice versa). The Building Bulk Guidelines explicitly treat FLAR as the _primary determinant of bulk_ and BHL as a separate height constraint.[file:17]

- **Example:** Com-2 has FLAR **3.60 up to 9.00** and may be configured in different height/footprint mixes; but actual BHL still depends on the specific site and BHL table, not just on FLAR.[file:17]

**Why this matters:** You must always check both **Max Floors by FLAR** and **BHL (storeys/meters)**, and then choose the more restrictive.[file:17]

---

### TRAP 3 — C-3 vs. PUD FLAR

|                             | Wrong (typical mix-up) | Correct (Table VII.G.1)                           |
| :-------------------------- | :--------------------- | :------------------------------------------------ |
| **Com-3 FLAR**              | 3.00 to 12.00          | **9.00 up to 34.00**                              |
| **PUD-Inland high density** | “same as C-3”          | **9.00 up to 34.00, at a 60-storey/180.00 m BHL** |

**Why this matters:** Both **Com-3** and some **PUD Inland** entries share the same _maximum_ BHL (60 storeys / 180 m), but practitioners sometimes misremember the FLAR range. Always pull the actual FLAR values from Table VII.G.1 rather than guessing from BHL alone.[file:17]

---

### TRAP 4 — Including Parking in GFA

|                           | Wrong                                  | Correct                              |
| :------------------------ | :------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------- |
| **What was done**         | Basement parking levels counted as GFA | Covered parking is excluded from GFA |
| **Where parking belongs** | Inflates GFA against FLAR              | Included in TGFA but not in GFA      |

**Why this matters:** GFA is used in FLAR compliance. If you mistakenly include parking in GFA, you may think you are over the FLAR limit when you are not, or misjudge how much sleaable area you still have.[file:17][file:18]

---

### TRAP 5 — The Balcony Threshold

| Balcony Area            | Counted in GFA?        |
| :---------------------- | :--------------------- |
| **10.0 sq.m or less**   | YES — included in GFA  |
| **Exceeding 10.0 sq.m** | NO — excluded from GFA |

**Why this matters:** Exam questions often describe balconies slightly above or below 10.0 sq.m. Balconies at or below 10.0 sq.m count against FLAR; balconies larger than 10.0 sq.m do not.[file:17][file:18]
