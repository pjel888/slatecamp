## 1. Hospital Module (High‑Risk Occupancy, Many PWDs)

<LawCallout code="bp344">

Legal Basis: BP 344 (2024 IRR) governs ALL accessible design in the Philippines. PD 1096 (National Building Code) governs fire safety, structural requirements, and occupancy classifications. For hospital design, both apply simultaneously: BP 344 mandates wheelchair turning spaces (1800 mm), accessible routes (1200 mm min width), and curb ramps (900 mm min width, 1:12 max slope). PD 1096 mandates fire-rated exits, stair widths, and occupancy load calculations.

</LawCallout>


### 1.1 Built Features (BP 344 + PD 1096 + Rule VII/VIII Context)

- **Occupancy context (PD 1096):**
  - Hospitals and similar health facilities fall under **Institutional** group (e.g., Group D / Institutional in PD 1096 Rule VII summaries: hospitals, sanitaria, nursing homes, etc.), which justifies stricter **fire and life‑safety** and continuous accessible routes for non‑ambulatory occupants.[web:7][web:11][web:15]

- **Drop‑off zone (BP 344 – Curb ramps / dropped sidewalks):**
  - Provide **dropped sidewalks** or curb ramps at loading/drop‑off points serving hospital entrances.
  - **Curb ramp minimum width:** **900 mm** at PWD drop‑off points.[web:1][web:2][web:4][web:8]
  - **Maximum curb ramp gradient:** not steeper than **1:12**; use gentler slopes (e.g., 1:15–1:20) where possible to accommodate frail users, but do not exceed the 1:12 BP 344 limit.[web:1][web:3][web:8]
  - Ensure the curb ramp does **not reduce** the 1.50 m × 1.50 m level turning area and does not narrow the required sidewalk clear width.[web:1][web:6][web:10]

- **Ramps (BP 344):**

<Warn>

⚠️ EXAM WARNING: Do NOT confuse the 1.00 m minimum ramp width (BP 344) with the 1.20 m minimum sidewalk width (BP 344). The ramp minimum is 1.00 m; the sidewalk minimum is 1.20 m. Practice guides often recommend 1.20 m for ramps as a design improvement, but the CODE MINIMUM is 1.00 m. LEA questions test the code minimum, not the recommendation.

</Warn>

  - **Minimum clear width:** **1.00 m** for accessible ramps (many practice notes use 1.20 m as a design improvement; retain 1.20 m as a _recommended_ width, but code minimum is 1.00 m).[web:3][web:12]
  - **Maximum gradient:** **1:12** for accessible ramps; provide **level landings** (min **1.00 m** deep; many practice guides use **1.50–1.80 m** for easier turning) at top and bottom and at breaks in long runs.[web:3][web:5][web:12][web:13]
  - Provide handrails on both sides at 700 mm and 900 mm heights and 300‑mm extensions at top and bottom, with 100‑mm ramp edge curbs for safety.[web:2][web:3][web:6][web:9]

- **Sidewalks / walkways (BP 344):**
  - **Minimum clear width:** **1.20 m**.[web:1][web:6][web:8][web:10]
  - **Gradient along route:** preferably not steeper than **1:20**; cross slope not more than **1:100**.[web:1][web:6][web:8]
  - Use **1.50 m × 1.50 m** level areas for turning/meeting spaces; designing **1.80 m** width and rest spaces every 10–12 m is a good practice upgrade for hospitals with many wheelchair users (noted in review sheets but above bare minimum).[web:2][web:6][web:8]

- **Accessible routes / corridors (BP 344 + PD 1096 functional logic):**
  - Aim for **at least 1.50 m** clear corridor width in hospital patient areas so two wheelchairs or a bed and wheelchair can pass; provide 1.50 m × 1.50 m (or larger 1.80 m × 1.80 m) turning spaces at changes of direction, dead ends, and major junctions.[web:2][web:6][web:12]
  - Ensure the accessible route is **continuous** from entrance/drop‑off to OPD, wards, diagnostics, and emergency, in line with BP 344’s requirement for barrier‑free routes in public and institutional buildings.[web:1][web:3][web:10]

- **Accessible toilets (BP 344):**
  - Provide at least one **accessible toilet** per floor/major department.
  - Internal clear space should allow wheelchair side transfer; common practice modules use at least **1.50 m clear turning space** and approx **1.80 m × 2.20–2.25 m** overall internal dimension to accommodate fixtures and maneuvering.[web:1][web:6][web:9]
  - **WC seat height:** about **450 mm** from finished floor (within typical BP 344 ranges).
  - Provide side and rear grab bars at appropriate heights and distances, plus an **emergency call device** reachable from the floor (~400–600 mm AFF is common in practice guidance).[web:1][web:3][web:9]
  - Visual and audible alarm indicators should be provided in PWD toilets in hospitals as part of universal design and life‑safety coordination with PD 1096 fire provisions.[web:8][web:12]

- **Elevators (BP 344):**
  - Public hospitals above the ground floor require elevators as part of the accessible route due to non‑ambulatory occupants.
  - Typical **accessible elevator car** minimum internal dimensions are around **1.10–1.40 m (width) × 1.40–1.50 m (depth)**, with **0.90 m** clear door width, based on BP 344 IRR examples and local practice notes.[web:1][web:3][web:9]
  - Provide **1.50 m × 1.50 m** minimum clear turning space in front of elevator doors; larger spaces (e.g., 1.80 m × 1.80 m) are recommended where bed trolleys mix with wheelchairs.[web:1][web:6][web:9]
  - Install car operating panel (COP) controls within **0.90–1.20 m** height range and provide Braille/tactile markings and handrails on car walls.[web:1][web:3][web:9]

- **Stairs (BP 344 + PD 1096):**
  - Provide handrails on both sides of stairs at **700 mm and 900 mm** heights with **300‑mm extensions** at top and bottom.[web:2][web:6][web:9]
  - Use warning tactile blocks before the first and last step of stair flights to assist visually impaired users; many practice documents show around **600 mm depth** placed approx one tread back from the first riser.[web:2][web:6]
  - Provide guards/railings where drop heights exceed about **750 mm**, coordinating with PD 1096 guardrail height and opening limits.[web:7][web:8]

### 1.2 Law Hooks (RA 7277 / RA 9442 / RA 10754)

- **Discounts and VAT exemption:**
  - Public and private hospitals must grant **20% discount** and **12% VAT exemption** on eligible medical services, professional fees in connection with confinement, diagnostic and laboratory fees, and medicines for registered PWDs under RA 10754 amending RA 7277 and RA 9442.[web:29][web:32]

- **Anti‑discrimination and priority:**
  - RA 9442 prohibits ridicule, vilification, and discrimination against PWDs and requires priority in queues and services, which in a hospital translates to priority lanes in ER, OPD, laboratories, and pharmacies.[web:20][web:23][web:29]

- **Incentives for accessible design and employment:**
  - RA 7277 and RA 10754 provide **tax incentives**, such as additional deductions for expenses incurred in improving facilities to enhance the mobility of PWDs and additional deductions on PWD employees’ wages, which hospitals can avail when they implement and maintain accessibility measures and hire PWDs.[web:29][web:32]

## 2. Mall / Mixed‑Use Module (Retail, Recreation, Parking)

### 2.1 Built Features

- **Occupancy context (PD 1096 Rule VII/VIII):**
  - Shopping centers, malls, retail and mixed‑use commercial structures fall under **Commercial / Mercantile** groupings (e.g., Group E/F in some summaries), often with **Assembly** components for cinemas and food courts, which affects required exits, fire ratings, and occupant load calculations while still needing BP 344 accessibility for all public areas.[web:7][web:11][web:15]

- **Accessible parking (BP 344):**
  - BP 344 cites a minimum accessible parking stall size of **3.70 m width × 5.00 m length** for a single bay, plus a **1.20 m** clear walkway at the front connecting to dropped sidewalks/curb ramps.[web:2][web:8]
  - Many local practice notes adopt **4.00 m width** and **1.80 m access aisle** as an _enhanced_ layout (especially when pairing two stalls sharing a central aisle), which is acceptable as an improvement over the minimum so long as the BP 344 minimum 3.70 m × 5.00 m clear stall and complying aisle/walkway are satisfied.[web:2][web:8]
  - Provide vertical clearance of around **2.30 m** in covered accessible parking areas to accommodate vans and high‑roof vehicles used by wheelchair users, as reflected in several BP 344‑based design guides.[web:8]
  - Use International Symbol of Access (ISA) markings and signage for reserved PWD spaces.[web:8]

- **Approach / sidewalks (BP 344):**
  - **Minimum sidewalk width:** **1.20 m**, with dropped sidewalks or curb ramps leading from parking and public streets to mall entrances.[web:1][web:6][web:8][web:10]
  - Maintain gradients preferably not steeper than 1:20, cross slope ≤1:100, and avoid gratings or, where unavoidable, ensure grating openings are perpendicular to the line of travel and not more than about 13 mm spacing.[web:1][web:6][web:8]

- **Ramps / corridors (BP 344):**
  - Corridors used by the public in malls should maintain at least **1.20–1.50 m** clear width, with wider portions where queues are expected.[web:2][web:6][web:12]
  - Any change in level >13 mm requires a ramp with **≥1.00 m** clear width and slope not steeper than 1:12; design to 1:15–1:20 where possible for comfort.[web:3][web:12][web:13]

- **Escalators & lifts:**
  - BP 344 requires that where escalators are provided, an **accessible alternative** (elevator or ramp) must also be provided so PWDs are not forced to use escalators.[web:1][web:3]
  - Escalator design (number of flat steps, angle, etc.) is governed more directly by PD 1096 and equipment standards; tactile warning surfaces at approaches and audible/visual learts are good practice for visually impaired users, consistent with BP 344 principles for detectable warnings.[web:7][web:8]
  - Elevators serving upper‑level shops, cinemas, and food courts must meet BP 344 accessible elevator provisions noted in the hospital module (adequate car size, 0.90 m door, reachable controls, visual and audible indicators).[web:1][web:3][web:9]

- **Washrooms:**
  - Provide at least one **accessible toilet** per floor or per cluster of tenants accessible from public areas, designed in accordance with BP 344 clearances, door widths, grab bars, and emergency call systems, similar to the hospital accessible toilet guidelines above.[web:1][web:3][web:9]

### 2.2 Law Hooks

- **RA 9442 + RA 10754 – discounts in commercial settings:**
  - Mall tenants (cinemas, amusement centers, restaurants, etc.) are required to grant **20% discount** and **12% VAT exemption** on admission charges and applicable meals/refreshments for the exclusive use and enjoyment of the PWD, following RA 7277 as amended by RA 9442 and RA 10754.[web:29][web:32]

- **Express lanes & priority counters:**
  - RA 9442 encourages priority and express lanes for PWDs in establishments such as supermarkets and billing centers, which must be laid out along **accessible routes** that comply with BP 344 widths, slopes, and maneuvering spaces from entrance to counter.[web:20][web:23][web:29]

- **Incentives for employers / improvements:**
  - Mall operators hiring PWDs and investing in accessibility upgrades may claim tax incentives and additional deductions under RA 7277 and RA 10754, similar to hospital incentives.[web:29][web:32]

## 3. School Module (Education + Access)

### 3.1 Built Features

- **Occupancy context (PD 1096 Rule VII):**
  - Schools are typically classified under **Educational / Institutional** groupings (e.g., Group C – Education and Recreation; Group A/B residential‑educational for smaller schools and dormitories), which triggers specific egress, fire protection, and occupant load requirements alongside accessibility.[web:7][web:11][web:15]

- **Approach / entrance (BP 344):**
  - Same BP 344 curb ramp/dropped sidewalk rules as other public buildings: **900‑mm** minimum curb ramp at school drop‑off, maximum 1:12 gradient, and 1.20‑m minimum sidewalk width with 1.50 m × 1.50 m level turning areas.[web:1][web:2][web:6][web:8]

- **Classroom corridors:**
  - Provide accessible corridors with at least **1.20–1.50 m** clear width; maintain continuous, unobstructed routes from entrances to classrooms, library, and laboratories.[web:1][web:6][web:12]
  - Provide turning spaces (1.50 m × 1.50 m or more) near classroom doorways and junctions so a wheelchair user can enter and reverse direction without obstruction.[web:1][web:6][web:9]

- **Washrooms:**
  - At least one **accessible toilet** per floor or per wing in school buildings, detailed similar to earlier accessible toilet requirements.[web:1][web:3][web:9]

- **Vertical circulation:**
  - For multi‑storey school buildings, provide at least one **accessible route** between floors via ramps and/or elevators compliant with BP 344, in coordination with PD 1096 structural and egress requirements for educational occupancies.[web:1][web:3][web:7][web:12]

### 3.2 Law Hooks

- **Right to education and reasonable accommodation:**
  - RA 7277 guarantees the right of PWDs to education and mandates **reasonable accommodation** in schools, which includes accessible physical facilities, assistive devices, and appropriate teaching methods.[web:21][web:29]

- **Discounts and exemptions (case‑dependent):**
  - RA 10754 extends the 20% discount and 12% VAT exemption to certain educational expenses when the services or fees are for the **exclusive use, enjoyment, or availment of the PWD**, but application to tuition and other school fees depends on specific implementing rules and revenue regulations.[web:29][web:32]
