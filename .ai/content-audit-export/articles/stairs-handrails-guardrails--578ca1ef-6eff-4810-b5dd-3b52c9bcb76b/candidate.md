## 1. Big Picture: How BP 344 and PD 1096 Interact

<LawCallout code="pd1096" section="Rule XII - Guardrails">
BP 344 — The Accessibility Law mandates barrier-free access for PWDs in all public buildings.
</LawCallout>

- **PD 1096 IRR** (National Building Code) governs general **stair geometry, exits, and fire safety** (rise/run, landings, enclosures, guardrails, exit capacity, travel distance).[web:45][web:47]
- **BP 344 IRR (with 2013–2024 amendments)** overlays **accessibility refinements**: dual handrail heights, tactile warnings, and coordination with ramps and elevators, particularly for public and institutional buildings.[web:1][web:2][web:9][web:33][web:49]
- Exam mindset: **PD 1096 = life‑safety baseline; BP 344 = accessibility overlay.** Both must be satisfied whenever the building is required to be accessible.

## 2. PD 1096 Stair Basics (You Must Know These First)

From PD 1096 IRR (Rule XII – Means of Egress):[web:45][web:47]

- **Landings:** Dimension in direction of travel at least equal to stair width; for straight runs, landings **need not exceed about 1.20 m** in length, unless design/exits demand more.[web:45]
- **Vertical distance between landings:** Maximum **3.60 m** vertical rise between successive landings.[web:45]
- **Handrail height band (general stairs):** **800–900 mm** above the tread nosing.[web:45]
- **Handrails:** Required on **each side** of stairways; if stair width exceeds about **3.00 m**, provide at least one intermediate handrail, approximately equally spaced.[web:45]
- **Headroom:** Minimum **2.00 m** clear vertical headroom measured from tread nosing line to ceiling or overhead obstruction.[web:45]
- **Exit enclosure rules:** Exit stairways typically enclosed with fire‑rated construction (often 2‑hour walls), protected openings, self‑closing fire doors, and **no usable enclosed space** under exit stairs except as allowed for storage when protected and permitted by the code.[web:45][web:47]

These form the **baseline** for all stairs, whether accessible or not.

## 3. BP 344 Accessibility Overlay on Stairs

The BP 344 IRR extends many **ramp** requirements to **stairs**, then adds tactile-wayfinding rules tied into accessible routes.[web:1][web:2][web:9][web:25][web:33][web:46][web:49]

### 3.1 Handrails (Accessibility Layer)

- Handrails on **both sides** of accessible **ramps and stairs**.[web:1][web:2][web:9]
- **Two heights** for accessible ramps and stairs:
  - **700 mm** and **900 mm** from the finished surface (tread or ramp) to top of handrail.[web:1][web:2][web:9][web:10][web:43][web:48]
- **Extensions:**
  - Provide at least a **300‑mm horizontal extension** at **top and bottom** of both ramps and stairs, returning to the wall, post, or floor with **no open, snag‑prone ends**.[web:2][web:3][web:9][web:34][web:46]
- **Grip size:**
  - Handrails and grab bars for full grip should have outside diameter of about **38–45 mm**, with clearances from wall of **40–50 mm** per BP 344.[web:2][web:3][web:34][web:46]
- **Protective railings (guards):**
  - Where ramps, stairs, balconies, or landings are more than **750 mm** above adjacent grade, install railings at **1100 mm minimum height** from top of rail to finished floor, **in addition to** required accessible handrails.[web:2][web:3][web:9]
  - The **1100‑mm guard** is separate from the **700/900‑mm accessible grip bands**.[web:3][web:45]

**Exam note:**

- PD 1096 general stair: handrail band **800–900 mm** above nosing.[web:45]
- BP 344 accessible overlay: **two handrail heights, 700 and 900 mm**, plus **1100‑mm protective guard** on drops >750 mm.[web:1][web:2][web:3][web:9]

### 3.2 Tactile Warnings at Stairs

From BP 344 IRR on tactile warning surfaces:[web:1][web:2][web:25][web:33][web:46][web:49]

- Tactile floor surfaces required on accessible circulation paths including **stairs** (except fire‑exit stairs used only in emergencies).[web:25][web:49]
- **Warning tactile blocks (domes)** at stairs:
  - **Depth:** **600 mm** tactile strip.[web:25][web:33][web:49]
  - **Setback:** installed **300 mm before** hazardous areas and at **top, bottom, and intermediate landings** of stairs.[web:25][web:33][web:46][web:49]
  - **Width:** extends across **entire clear width of the stair**.[web:25][web:33][web:49]
- On long or direction‑changing landings, ensure the tactile strip is placed so users approaching from all directions encounter warning before stair edge.[web:33][web:49]

Mnemonic: **"600 deep, 300 back, full width at every stair landing."**[web:25][web:33][web:49]

### 3.3 Landing Spacing — BP 344 vs PD 1096

One of the most frequently tested conflicts:

| Law | Max Vertical Rise Before Landing | Min Landing Depth |
|-----|----------------------------------|-------------------|
| **BP 344** | **3,000 mm (3 m)** — accessible stairs/ramps | **1,500 mm** |
| **PD 1096** | **3,600 mm (3.6 m)** — general stairs | Equal to stair width; max ~1,200 mm |

**Exam trap:** A stair with landings at 3,500 mm vertical rise passes PD 1096 but **fails BP 344**. The more restrictive 3 m interval governs in accessible buildings.

## 4. Stairs–Ramps–Elevators Integration Pattern

A fully compliant vertical core in a multi‑storey public building combines PD 1096 life‑safety and BP 344 accessibility:[web:1][web:2][web:3][web:9][web:10][web:25][web:33][web:49]

```text
RAMP  →  STAIR  →  ELEVATOR
```

- **PD 1096** ensures:
  - Correct **rise/run**, landings, headroom, and enclosure ratings.[web:45]
  - Required **number and width** of exits, permissible **travel distance**, and **exit discharge** to public way.[web:45][web:47]

- **BP 344** ensures:
  - **Ramps:** Min **1.00–1.20 m** width, **max 1:12 slope**, **1.50–1.80 m** landings at top/bottom and intervals, dual 700/900‑mm handrails, **300‑mm** extensions, edge curbs, and **tactile warning strips** (600‑mm depth, 300‑mm setback).[web:2][web:3][web:25][web:33][web:49]
  - **Stairs:** 700/900‑mm handrails both sides, **300‑mm** extensions, **continuous** rails around short landings, **1100‑mm protective guard** where drop >750 mm, tactile warning strips (600‑mm depth, 300‑mm before top/bottom and intermediate landings).[web:1][web:2][web:3][web:25][web:33][web:46][web:49]
  - **Elevators:** Accessible car about **1.10–1.40 m** width × **1.40–1.50 m** depth with **0.90‑m** clear door, **1.50–1.80 m** clear turning space in front, **COP around 900 mm**, **Braille on jambs at about 1200 mm**.[web:1][web:3][web:10][web:33]

**Exam tip:** A fully compliant vertical core in an exam sketch shows **all three** (ramp, stair, elevator) sharing landings and lobbies, each meeting both PD 1096 and BP 344.

## 5. Active Recall – Stairs & Handrails

1. PD 1096 max vertical distance between landings? → **3.60 m**.[web:45]
2. PD 1096 general stair handrail height band? → **800–900 mm** above nosing.[web:45]
3. BP 344 accessible handrail heights (stairs/ramps)? → **700 mm** and **900 mm** above surface.[web:1][web:2][web:9][web:10][web:43][web:48]
4. BP 344 ramp/stair handrail extension length? → **300 mm** at top and bottom, returning to wall/post.[web:2][web:3][web:9][web:34][web:46]
5. Protective railing (guard) height when fall > 750 mm? → **1100 mm** minimum, in addition to accessible handrails.[web:2][web:3][web:9]
6. Depth and setback of stair warning tactile? → **600 mm depth**, installed **300 mm** before top, bottom, and intermediate landings, spanning full width.[web:25][web:33][web:46][web:49]
7. BP 344 max vertical rise between landings? → **3,000 mm (3 m)**.

## Common Exam Traps

**TRAP 1 — Confusing BP 344 with PD 1096 handrail requirements:**
PD 1096 allows a single handrail at any height within 800–900 mm. BP 344 requires **dual handrails at exactly 700 mm and 900 mm** for accessible stairs. A single rail at 850 mm passes PD 1096 but **fails BP 344** for accessible routes.

**TRAP 2 — Mixing up ramp vs stair dimensions:**
Ramp slopes (max 1:12, landing 1,500–1,800 mm) differ from stair rise/run (max riser 200 mm PD 1096, 150 mm BP 344). Don't apply ramp landing rules to stairs.

**TRAP 3 — Omitting handrail extensions:**
BP 344 requires **300 mm horizontal extensions** at top and bottom. This is a signature BP 344 requirement — frequently tested. Extensions apply to **both** the 700 mm and 900 mm rails.

**TRAP 4 — Guard height vs handrail height:**
Guard (protective railing) must be **1,100 mm** where drop > 750 mm. This is **separate from** and **in addition to** the 700/900 mm accessible handrails. A single rail at 1,100 mm does NOT fulfill the dual handrail requirement.

## Exam Tip

LEA Trap: When BP 344 and PD 1096 conflict on stairs, BP 344 accessibility requirements PREVAIL for accessible routes, but PD 1096 governs structural and life-safety requirements. Dual handrails (700/900 mm) is the most tested BP 344 stair requirement.
