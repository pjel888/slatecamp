# Core definitions: grounding vs bonding

Grounding and bonding are related, but they are not interchangeable words. Source-safe LEA answers should first separate earth connection, equipment fault-current path, and continuity bonding.

## Definitions

Ground means the earth. Grounded means connected to earth or to a conductive body that extends the ground connection. A grounding conductor connects equipment, circuits, or a wiring system to a grounding electrode or grounding electrodes. An equipment grounding conductor connects non-current-carrying metal parts of equipment, raceways, and enclosures to the system grounded conductor, the grounding electrode conductor, or both.

Bonding is different. The PEC definition treats bonding as the permanent joining of metallic parts to form an electrically conductive path that provides electrical continuity and capacity to conduct safely any current likely to be imposed.

## Grounding electrode system

Where grounding electrodes are present at a building or structure, the checked PEC provision requires them to be bonded together into the grounding electrode system. The checked electrode list includes metal underground water pipe with the required earth contact, metal frame of the building or structure, concrete-encased electrode, ground ring, rod or pipe electrodes, and plate electrodes. Gas piping and aluminum electrodes are not used as grounding electrodes in the checked provision.

Rod and pipe electrodes have source-backed installation requirements, including length and spacing rules. Plate electrodes have a required contact area. Grounding electrode conductors may be copper, aluminum, or copper-clad aluminum, subject to the checked conditions and protection rules.

## Bonding in practice

Bonding keeps metal parts electrically continuous so fault current has a low-impedance path. The checked PEC bonding provisions cover metal water piping, other metal piping, structural metal, lightning protection terminal bonding, and equipment bonding concepts. A bonding jumper is a reliable conductor used to ensure required electrical conductivity between metal parts.

## Exam-safe method

When a question asks grounding versus bonding, ask three questions:

1. Is the answer about connection to earth or an electrode system? That is grounding.
2. Is the answer about joining metallic parts for continuity and fault-current capacity? That is bonding.
3. Is the answer about non-current-carrying equipment parts and enclosures? Look for the equipment grounding conductor and bonding path together.

Avoid treating a bond as an earth electrode, and avoid treating a ground rod as the only fault-current path. The source-safe answer uses the full grounding electrode system plus bonding continuity.
