## PART I: FULL ENFORCEMENT CHAIN — 20 Q&A FLASHCARDS

---

### CARD 1
**Q: What is the primary legal basis for requiring accessibility features in buildings?**

**A:** Batas Pambansa Blg. 344 (BP 344), enacted in 1983, is the *Accessibility Law* — it mandates that public buildings, institutions, establishments, and public utilities be designed for accessibility by persons with physical handicaps. Its 2024 Revised Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) issued by DPWH provides the specific technical standards. BP 344 is implemented together with:
- **RA 7277** (Magna Carta for Persons with Disability, 1992)
- **RA 9442** (2007 amendment — adds anti-discrimination, expanded discounts)
- **RA 10754** (2016 amendment — 20% discount + VAT exemption)

---

### CARD 2
**Q: Who issues building permits, and how does BP 344 become a condition of the permit?**

**A:** The **Building Official (BO)** — an appointee of the LGU — is the sole authority to issue building permits under PD 1096 (National Building Code). Under BP 344 Sec. 8 and Rule 3 of the IRR:
- The BO **shall not issue** a building permit for covered buildings unless the plans comply with accessibility standards.
- Accessibility compliance is a **mandatory checklist item** during plan review.
- Buildings covered: all public buildings, commercial establishments, hotels, shopping centers, hospitals, schools, transport terminals — essentially all buildings open to the public.
- A plan that fails to show accessible routes, accessible toilets, required ramps, or accessible parking is **legally defective** and must be returned for revision.

---

### CARD 3
**Q: Can a Certificate of Occupancy (COO) be issued for a non-compliant building?**

**A:** **No.** Under BP 344 Rule 3 and PD 1096 Section 309, the BO shall not issue a Certificate of Occupancy unless the completed building conforms to all applicable laws, including BP 344. Specifically:
- An on-site inspection for occupancy must verify that all accessibility features shown in the approved plans are actually installed.
- Missing or substandard features (e.g., ramp too steep, parking not marked, accessible toilet not completed) are grounds to **withhold the COO**.
- If a COO is issued in error, the BO can order cessation of operations until compliance is achieved.

---

### CARD 4
**Q: Who can file a complaint for BP 344 noncompliance, and where?**

**A:** Under BP 344 and RA 7277, the following may file complaints:
1. **Any PWD** — directly to the BO, NCDA, DPWH, or courts
2. **Any organization of PWDs** — same channels
3. **NCDA (National Council on Disability Affairs)** — ex officio monitoring; may file administrative and criminal complaints
4. **DPWH** — as promulgator of the IRR; may conduct inspections and file complaints
5. **Any private citizen** — via BO or courts
6. **Barangay officials** — may endorse complaints to the city/municipal BO

**Where to file:**
- **Step 1:** City/Municipal Building Official (administrative)
- **Step 2:** Regional Office of DPWH (if BO fails to act)
- **Step 3:** NCDA (for policy-level and inter-agency escalation)
- **Step 4:** Regular courts (civil/criminal action)

---

### CARD 5
**Q: What is the role of DPWH in BP 344 enforcement?**

**A:** The Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) has three roles:
1. **IRR Promulgation:** DPWH, jointly with DOH, NCDA, and DTI as applicable, issues and updates the IRR of BP 344. The 2024 IRR is the current applicable standard.
2. **Technical Authority:** DPWH provides technical guidance to BOs and resolves interpretation disputes on accessibility standards.
3. **Inspection Authority:** DPWH may conduct independent inspections of covered buildings and file complaints or issue directives to LGUs.

> **Key point:** DPWH does NOT issue building permits — that is the BO/LGU function. DPWH sets the rules; the BO enforces them locally.

---

### CARD 6
**Q: What is the role of NCDA in enforcement?**

**A:** The National Council on Disability Affairs (NCDA) is the coordinating body for all disability-related policies under RA 7277. In enforcement:
- **Monitoring:** Conducts or coordinates accessibility audits of public buildings
- **Advocacy:** Issues advisories to LGUs, building owners, and agencies
- **Complaints:** May formally lodge complaints with BOs, DPWH, or the Office of the Ombudsman if government buildings are noncompliant
- **Inter-agency coordination:** Works with DOH (health facilities), DepEd/CHED (schools), DOTr (transport) to enforce sector-specific accessibility standards
- **Does NOT issue building permits** or have direct authority to impose fines — it escalates to agencies with that authority

---

### CARD 7
**Q: What criminal penalties apply under BP 344 for noncompliance?**

**A:** Under BP 344 Section 10, as amended by RA 10754:
- **Fine:** ₱5,000 to ₱50,000 (original BP 344 penalty — now effectively superseded by RA 10754 higher penalties for related violations)
- Under **RA 10754 Sec. 32** (amending RA 7277): Any person who violates accessibility provisions may face:
  - **Fine:** Up to **₱300,000**
  - **Imprisonment:** Up to **6 years**
  - Or **both**, at the discretion of the court

<Callout variant="lea-watch" title="Trap Leart">

The ₱50,000 fine from original BP 344 is frequently tested as a distractor. The current maximum under RA 10754 is ₱300,000 + 6 years. Always cite the latest amendment.

</Callout>

---

### CARD 8
**Q: Under RA 9442, what anti-discrimination provisions have architectural design implications?**

**A:** RA 9442 (amending RA 7277) adds:
- **Sec. 32 (anti-ridicule / anti-discrimination):** Any person who refuses, withholds, or restricts the rights of PWDs — including physical access to public establishments — commits a violation
- **Sec. 33:** Establishments that deny equal physical access (no ramp, no accessible toilet, no accessible parking) may face administrative sanctions AND criminal liability
- **Priority lanes/counters:** Mandatory in all government offices, hospitals, and commercial establishments — these are physical design requirements (counters, queue management, signage) with direct architectural implications
- **Anti-ridicule:** While not a building design standard per se, designing spaces that humiliate PWDs (e.g., making the only accessible toilet a "service toilet" in a back alley) can be cited as a form of discrimination under RA 9442

---

### CARD 9
**Q: What is the architect's professional liability under RA 9266 for accessibility failures?**

**A:** Under the Architecture Act of 2004 (RA 9266):
- An architect bears **professional responsibility** for the design conformity of projects they sign and seal.
- If accessibility features are absent, deficient, or incorrectly designed in the approved plans, the architect of record may face:
  1. **PRC administrative complaint** — suspension or revocation of Professional Identification Card (PIC)
  2. **Civil liability** — RA 9266 Sec. 39 imposes a **10-year civil liability period** for structural and design defects from completion of the project
  3. **Criminal liability** — if the deficiency resulted in injury or death of a PWD (in conjunction with the Revised Penal Code)
- **The 10-year period** means an architect can be sued up to a decade after the building is completed for design failures — including accessibility failures that result in injury.

---

### CARD 10
**Q: Can the building owner be separately liable from the architect?**

**A:** Yes, and often more directly. The building owner has ongoing obligations:
- **Maintenance:** The owner must maintain all accessibility features in working condition at all times. A broken ramp, missing TGSI strips, or padlocked accessible toilet constitute violations even if the original design was compliant.
- **Alteration duty:** Any renovation, alteration, or fit-out must preserve or improve accessibility compliance.
- **Operational liability:** If a PWD is injured because an accessibility feature was absent, defective, or improperly maintained, the owner faces:
  1. Civil damages under the Civil Code (quasi-delict, Art. 2176)
  2. Criminal liability under BP 344 / RA 10754
  3. Administrative action from the BO (suspension of occupancy permit)

> **Key distinction:** The architect's liability is primarily for **design**. The owner's liability is primarily for **construction, maintenance, and operation**. Both can be liable simultaneously.

---

### CARD 11
**Q: What happens if the Building Official issues a permit despite noncompliant plans?**

**A:** The BO is personally liable for administrative and criminal sanctions:
- **Administrative liability:** BO may be charged with dereliction of duty before the Civil Service Commission (CSC) or Office of the Ombudsman
- **Criminal liability:** Under PD 1096 Sec. 213, a BO who issues a permit for a noncompliant building faces imprisonment and/or fine
- **DPWH oversight:** DPWH may investigate the BO and recommend disciplinary action to the Local Chief Executive (Mayor/Governor)
- The permit, if issued improperly, does NOT provide a legal defense to the building owner — the owner remains liable for the underlying noncompliance

---

### CARD 12
**Q: What is the escalation path if the Building Official refuses to act on a complaint?**

**A:** The escalation path is:

```
Level 1 → City/Municipal Building Official
           (initial complaint, inspection request)
              ↓ (no action within 15 days)
Level 2 → DPWH Regional Office
           (technical review; may issue directives to BO)
              ↓ (no action or if government building)
Level 3 → NCDA
           (inter-agency coordination; may escalate to Ombudsman)
              ↓ (criminal violation alleged)
Level 4 → Office of the Ombudsman / Regular Courts
           (criminal complaint; civil action for damages)
              ↓ (professional misconduct alleged)
Level 5 → PRC / PRBoA
           (administrative case against architect)
```

### CARD 13
**Q: What are the discounts and VAT exemptions under RA 10754, and who must physically accommodate them?**

**A:** RA 10754 grants PWDs:
- **20% discount** on medicines, medical/dental services, transportation, hotel accommodation, restaurant meals, recreation, and burial services
- **12% VAT exemption** on the same transactions
- **Additional 5% discount** on basic necessities (RA 9442 provision retained)

**Architectural design implications:**
- Pharmacies, hospitals, restaurants, and commercial establishments must have **accessible priority counters** (two-level: 750 mm / 950–1100 mm) where PWDs can transact
- **Express lanes** (with ISA signage) for PWDs at government offices, banks, and commercial establishments
- **Cashier counter design** must accommodate wheelchair approach (700 mm knee clearance under counter, 1500 × 1500 mm approach space)
- The right to the discount is meaningless if the PWD cannot physically reach the counter to claim it — **the discount law is simultaneously an accessibility design mandate**

---

### CARD 14
**Q: Does BP 344 apply to privately owned buildings that are not "public buildings"?**

**A:** BP 344 applies to all **buildings and establishments open to the public** — not just government buildings. Specifically:

| Type of Establishment | BP 344 Applies? |
|----------------------|----------------|
| Government offices | YES |
| Commercial establishments (malls, stores) | YES |
| Hotels and lodging houses (6+ rooms) | YES |
| Hospitals and clinics | YES |
| Schools (public and private) | YES |
| Churches and places of worship | YES |
| Purely private single-family residences | Generally NO (unless requesting for a public business permit) |
| Condominiums (common areas) | YES |
| Private offices (employer) | YES (RA 7277 employment provisions) |

<Callout variant="lea-watch" title="Trap Leart">

"Private" does not mean "exempt." If the public can enter the building, BP 344 applies.

</Callout>

### CARD 15
**Q: What is the statute of limitations for filing a criminal complaint under BP 344 / RA 10754?**

**A:** BP 344 violations with penalties of up to 6 years imprisonment fall under the Revised Penal Code's prescriptive periods:
- **Criminal action:** 8 years from discovery of the violation (for offenses punishable by prisión correccional — up to 6 years)
- **Civil action:** 10 years under the Civil Code for written obligations; 4 years for quasi-delicts (from date of injury)
- **Administrative action (PRC):** Subject to PRC's own rules; complaints must generally be filed within a reasonable period from discovery
- **Continuing violations:** A building that remains noncompliant creates a **continuing offense** — the prescriptive period resets each day the violation persists

---

### CARD 16
**Q: What barangay-level role exists in the BP 344 enforcement chain?**

**A:** Barangay officials do not have direct authority to enforce BP 344 or issue building permits. However:
- **Barangay Captains** may receive informal complaints from PWD constituents and refer them to the City/Municipal BO
- **Barangay Health Workers (BHW)** and PWD Affairs Officers may assist PWDs in filing formal complaints
- **Sangguniang Barangay** may pass resolutions urging LGUs to enforce accessibility standards
- Under RA 9442, every LGU (including barangay level) must designate a **PWD Affairs Officer** (PDAO) who serves as a local focal point for accessibility complaints

---

### CARD 17
**Q: What must the architect do when a client instructs them to omit accessibility features to save cost?**

**A:** The architect has a **non-delegable duty** to comply with BP 344:
1. **Inform the client in writing** that BP 344 compliance is mandatory and cannot be waived by either party
2. **Refuse to sign and seal plans** that do not comply with accessibility standards
3. If the client insists, the architect **must withdraw from the project** — continuing to design a noncompliant building exposes the architect to professional and criminal liability
4. The architect's obligation runs to the **public and to PWDs**, not only to the paying client
5. Under RA 9266 Sec. 33, the architect's signature and seal is a **certification of compliance** with all applicable laws — including BP 344

---

### CARD 18
**Q: How does RA 7277 Section 46 interact with BP 344 penalties?**

**A:** RA 7277 Sec. 46 (Penal Clause) provides a general criminal penalty for any violation of the Magna Carta for PWDs, which includes accessibility provisions. RA 10754 (2016) expressly amended and strengthened these penalties:

| Law | Maximum Fine | Maximum Imprisonment |
|-----|-------------|----------------------|
| BP 344 original | ₱50,000 | — (no jail term in original text) |
| RA 7277 Sec. 46 | ₱100,000 | 6 months – 2 years |
| RA 9442 amendment | ₱200,000 | 6 months – 6 years |
| RA 10754 amendment | **₱300,000** | **Up to 6 years** |

> The most recent law (**RA 10754**) controls. Always cite the **₱300,000 fine + 6 years imprisonment** as current maximum.

---

### CARD 19
**Q: What constitutes an accessibility violation that triggers criminal liability (as opposed to a mere administrative violation)?**

**A:** Criminal liability under RA 10754 requires:
1. **Willful non-compliance** — the owner knew of the requirement and failed to comply
2. **Refusal to remedy** — after notice from the BO or DPWH, the owner still did not correct the deficiency
3. **Injury or denial of access** — a PWD was actually denied access or suffered harm due to the missing feature (strengthens the case but not strictly required for criminal filing)

**Administrative violations** (first stage) include:
- Deficient plans submitted for permit
- Minor deviations from approved accessible design during construction
- Failure to maintain features (first notice stage)

**Criminal violations** (escalated stage) include:
- Deliberately omitting accessibility features after BO order
- Padlocking or blocking accessible toilets/routes
- Retaliating against a PWD who complained

---

### CARD 20
**Q: What is the enforcement sequence from plan submission to criminal prosecution?**

**A:**

```
Stage 1 — PLAN REVIEW
  Architect submits plans to BO
  BO checks accessibility compliance (BP 344 checklist)
  Non-compliant → plans returned for revision
  Compliant → Building Permit issued

Stage 2 — CONSTRUCTION INSPECTION
  BO inspects during construction
  Deviations from approved plan → Notice to Correct issued
  Non-compliance → Stop Work Order

Stage 3 — OCCUPANCY INSPECTION
  BO conducts final inspection before issuing COO
  Accessibility features must match approved plans
  Non-compliance → COO withheld
  Continued non-compliance → Referral to LGU legal office

Stage 4 — POST-OCCUPANCY COMPLAINT
  PWD / NCDA / DPWH files complaint
  BO conducts inspection within 15 days
  Violation confirmed → Written Notice of Violation to Owner

Stage 5 — ADMINISTRATIVE ACTION
  BO issues Cease and Desist Order (if continuing violation)
  LGU legal office files administrative case
  BO reports to DPWH Regional Office

Stage 6 — CRIMINAL PROSECUTION
  DPWH or NCDA refers to Office of the City/Provincial Prosecutor
  Prosecutor files Information in court
  Penalties: up to ₱300,000 + 6 years (RA 10754)
  Conviction → civil damages also awarded

Stage 7 — PROFESSIONAL SANCTIONS
  PRC / PRBoA receives referral
  Architect faces suspension or revocation of license
  10-year civil liability period runs from project completion
```

## REASON FOR THE LAW

> **Why is the enforcement chain so elaborate?**
>
<Warn>

The Philippines has approximately 1.57 million registered PWDs (PSA 2020), with the actual number estimated at 3–5 times higher. For decades, accessible design was treated as optional — buildings were approved and occupied with zero accessibility features. The layered enforcement structure (BO → DPWH → NCDA → courts) was designed to create multiple points of accountability. A corrupt or negligent BO is not the end of the road; NCDA and DPWH provide independent oversight, and any PWD can bypass local officials entirely by going directly to the courts. The escalating penalty structure (from administrative warning to ₱300,000 + 6 years) reflects the legislature's recognition that accessibility failures are not mere technical violations — they are acts of exclusion that deny constitutional rights.

</Warn>

## TRAP LEARTS (MASTER LIST)

> **🚨 TRAP 1 — Who Promulgates the IRR**
> Exam questions often test "who issues the IRR of BP 344." Answer: **DPWH** (jointly with relevant agencies like DOH, NCDA, DTI). Not PRC, not HLURB/DHSUD, not LGU.

> **🚨 TRAP 2 — Criminal vs Administrative Penalty Amounts**
> ₱50,000 (original BP 344), ₱100,000 (RA 7277), ₱200,000 (RA 9442), **₱300,000** (RA 10754). Always choose the highest = RA 10754.

> **🚨 TRAP 3 — Who Issues the Building Permit**
> The **Building Official** (LGU appointee) issues building permits — not DPWH, not NCDA, not PRC. DPWH sets the rules; the BO applies them.

> **🚨 TRAP 4 — Architect's Civil Liability Period**
> RA 9266 gives a **10-year civil liability period** for design defects from project completion — NOT the general 4-year quasi-delict period or 3-year criminal prescriptive period. The 10-year period is specific to RA 9266.

> **🚨 TRAP 5 — NCDA Cannot Issue Building Permits or Impose Fines**
> NCDA is a **coordinating and monitoring body**. It cannot issue permits, impose fines, or order building closures directly. It must refer to the BO, DPWH, or courts to enforce.
