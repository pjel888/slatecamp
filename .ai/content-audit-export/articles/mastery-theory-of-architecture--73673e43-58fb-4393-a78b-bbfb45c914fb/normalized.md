# Theory of Architecture — Mastery & Connections

## Learning Goal

Achieve synthesis-level understanding of Theory of Architecture as a coherent intellectual arc from fundamental questions (what is architecture?) through design principles, philosophies, movements, perception, programming, tropical design, and Filipino identity. Connect Theory to History (movement-to-manifesto), Architectural Design (space planning as bridge), Planning (tropical design as site design), and Laws (ethics dimension).

## Ching's Framework as the Unifying Thread

Francis D.K. Ching's vocabulary (form, space, order, proportion, scale, balance, rhythm, emphasis, unity) functions as shared language across nearly every Theory subtopic and extends into other LEA subjects.

When a Philosophies question asks about Le Corbusier's "promenade architecturale," you are applying Ching's concept of movement through space. When a Movements question asks what Deconstructivism challenged, the answer is Ching's classical notions of order and hierarchy.

## Theory ↔ Architectural History

The most important cross-subject connection. Theory and History share enormous territory — Modernism, Postmodernism, Brutalism, Deconstructivism, Critical Regionalism appear in both. The distinction: THEO = "what does this principle mean and how does it apply?" HIST = "when did this happen and who built it?"

## Theory ↔ Architectural Design

Space planning theory is the direct pipeline. Architectural programming — analysis of user needs, functional relationships, adjacencies — is the analytical foundation of every design project. Louis Kahn's "served vs. servant spaces" appears in both Theory and Design.

Design Perception (proxemics, kinesthetics, anthropometrics) directly informs room sizing, furniture layout, corridor widths, and threshold design.

## Theory ↔ Planning: Tropical Design as Site Design

Passive cooling strategies — cross-ventilation, stack effect, shading, elevated floors, orientation relative to sun/wind — are theoretical principles that become site planning tools. The bahay kubo, studied theoretically as vernacular climate intelligence, reappears in Planning for low-income housing and tropical neighborhood design.

## Theory ↔ Laws: The Ethics Dimension

The Nature of Architecture subtopic — what architecture is obligated to do for society — connects to professional ethics in Laws. Social responsibility finds legal expression in RA 9266 and the UAP Code of Ethical Conduct.

## Integration Patterns

**Pattern 1 — Principle + Application:** Gradual transition from public chaos to private calm — which design principle, and which philosopher theorized it?

**Pattern 2 — Movement + Formal Feature:** Floating roof plane, open plan, strong horizontal alignment — which movement?

**Pattern 3 — Perception + Dimension:** Waiting room for strangers — minimum interpersonal distance per proxemics theory?

**Pattern 4 — Filipino Context + Global Theory:** Critical Regionalism applied to a typhoon-prone coastal barangay community center.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Confusing Theory with History.** Know which questions belong to which subject.
- **Memorizing without understanding.** The LEA presents novel scenarios to test real understanding.
- **Neglecting proxemics and anthropometrics.** Specific zone distances and human dimensions are testable.
- **Over-investing in Pritzker list.** Principles and philosophies are weighted more heavily.
