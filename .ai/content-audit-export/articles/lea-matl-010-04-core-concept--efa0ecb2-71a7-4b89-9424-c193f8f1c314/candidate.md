## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Fajardo keeps board-foot estimating practical: board foot is still used for lumber volume because it is convenient in buying and payment. The source-backed definition is one square foot by one inch thick, equal to 144 cubic inches.

## Core Concept

For commercial lumber, thickness and width are expressed in inches while length is expressed in feet. Fajardo states that board foot is found by dividing the product of thickness, width, and length by 12. In formula form: board feet = thickness in inches x width in inches x length in feet / 12.

Fajardo's worked example asks for 5 pieces of 2 in x 6 in x 14 ft lumber. The computation is 5 x 2 x 6 x 14 / 12 = 70 board feet. The key is to keep the mixed units exactly as specified: inches for thickness and width, feet for length.

## How It's Tested

Expect items that mix metric and English dimensions. Fajardo warns that metric measurements should be converted first before solving board feet. If the problem gives a number of pieces, include it in the numerator. If it asks for payment by board foot, compute total board feet and multiply by the agreed unit price.

## Common Mistakes

Do not divide by 144 when the length is already in feet; the /144 version applies when all dimensions are in inches. Do not leave metric length unconverted. Do not forget to multiply by the number of pieces.
