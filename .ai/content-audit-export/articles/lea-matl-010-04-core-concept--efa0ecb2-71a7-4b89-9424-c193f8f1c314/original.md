## What You Need to Know for the LEA
## Learning Goal

Calculate lumber/board foot measure using the formula BF = T(in) × W(in) × L(ft) ÷ 12, convert between dimensional lumber sizes, and estimate total board feet for a given framing plan accounting for spacing, layout, and waste.
## Core Concept

A board foot (BF) represents a fixed volume: 1 inch × 12 inches × 12 inches = 144 cubic inches. When lumber is purchased, it is priced and quoted in board feet, not linear meters or cubic meters, despite the metric system's use in other construction trades. The conversion formula is: _Board feet = (Thickness in inches × Width in inches × Length in feet) ÷ 12_.

Thickness, width, and length must all be measured in the units specified: thickness and width in inches, length in feet. If all three dimensions are in inches, the formula becomes: _Board feet = (Thickness × Width × Length) ÷ 144_. This is equivalent because dividing by 12 (when length is in feet) or by 144 (when length is in inches) accounts for the standard board foot volume.

In the Philippines, lumber is often purchased in nominal sizes (e.g., 2 inches × 4 inches × 8 feet). Actual dressed sizes are slightly smaller due to planing (e.g., actual 1.5 inches × 3.5 inches). For estimation, use nominal dimensions unless otherwise specified. Standard length increments are 6 feet, 8 feet, 10 feet, and 12 feet in the Philippines and global markets.

## Worked Examples

<Example>
**Scenario 1: Formwork for concrete slab using 1×6 boards, 100 linear meters required, standard 8-foot boards**

**Step 1: Determine quantity per board**

Each standard 1×6×8 ft board provides: BF = (1 × 6 × 8) ÷ 12 = 48 ÷ 12 = 4 board feet per board

**Step 2: Convert total length to feet**

100 meters = 100 ÷ 0.3048 = 328 feet (approximately)

**Step 3: Calculate number of boards needed**

Each 8-foot board covers 8 feet of length. Boards needed = 328 ft ÷ 8 ft/board = 41 boards

**Step 4: Calculate total board feet**

Total BF = 41 boards × 4 BF/board = 164 board feet

Alternatively: 100 meters = 328 feet total length. Using 1×6 boards: BF = (1 × 6 × 328) ÷ 12 = 1,968 ÷ 12 = 164 BF

**Step 5: Add waste (10%)**

Total with waste = 164 BF × 1.10 = 180.4 BF ≈ 181 BF

**Answer: 181 board feet of 1×6 lumber, approximately 41–42 boards of 8-foot length**
</Example>

<Example>
**Scenario 2: Framing for a structure requiring multiple sizes: 50 pieces of 2×4×8 ft, 30 pieces of 2×6×10 ft, and 20 pieces of 1×8×12 ft**

**Step 1: Calculate board feet for each size**

2×4×8: BF per board = (2 × 4 × 8) ÷ 12 = 64 ÷ 12 = 5.33 BF/board. Total: 50 boards × 5.33 = 266.7 BF

2×6×10: BF per board = (2 × 6 × 10) ÷ 12 = 120 ÷ 12 = 10 BF/board. Total: 30 boards × 10 = 300 BF

1×8×12: BF per board = (1 × 8 × 12) ÷ 12 = 96 ÷ 12 = 8 BF/board. Total: 20 boards × 8 = 160 BF

**Step 2: Sum total board feet**

Total BF = 266.7 + 300 + 160 = 726.7 BF ≈ 727 BF

**Step 3: Add 5% waste**

Total with waste = 727 BF × 1.05 = 763.4 BF ≈ 764 BF

**Answer: 764 board feet total lumber required**
</Example>

## Philippine Construction Context

In the Philippines, lumber is supplied by major yards including Goodwill, Andal, Pacquiao Lumber, and others. The primary hardwoods used in construction are lauan (mahogany), yakal (hardwood), and narra (premium). Standard sizes follow international dimensions: 1×4, 1×6, 2×4, 2×6, and 2×8 are most common for formwork and structural applications. Prices fluctuate based on timber market conditions and species; hardwoods (yakal, narra) command premium prices compared to lauan.

Formwork (plywoods and timbers) for concrete is a major end-use. Board feet is the universal quotation unit. Some suppliers offer pricing per piece rather than per board foot, so conversion between units is essential.

Climate: Philippines' tropical climate with high humidity requires proper timber storage (covered, ventilated) to prevent excessive moisture absorption and warping. Treated or pressure-treated timber is recommended for structures exposed to weather or moisture.

## How It's Tested

On the LEA, lumber questions focus on:

1. **Single board size**: Given nominal dimensions and length, calculate board feet per board.

2. **Total quantity**: Multiply by number of boards or convert linear meters to total board feet.

3. **Mixed sizes**: Calculate board feet for each size separately, then sum.

4. **Waste factor**: Add 5–15% waste based on project type.

5. **Cost estimation**: Board feet × price per BF = total material cost.

6. **Linear-to-board conversion**: Convert running linear feet/meters to board feet using the formula.

<Tip>
**Memory aid**: The formula BF = (T × W × L) ÷ 12 is simple if you remember: thickness and width in inches, length in feet, divide by 12 (not 144). A 2×4×8 board is always 5.33 BF. A 2×6×10 board is always 10 BF. Memorize one or two common sizes as anchors.
</Tip>
