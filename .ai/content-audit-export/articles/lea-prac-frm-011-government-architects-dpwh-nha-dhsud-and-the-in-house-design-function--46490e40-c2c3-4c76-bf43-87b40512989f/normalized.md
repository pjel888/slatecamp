# Government Architects, In-House Design, and Public Accountability

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to explain what changes when an architect works inside a government agency such as a public works, housing, or human settlements office. The key exam move is not to memorize an agency label. It is to identify which rule controls the professional act: RA 9266 for architectural responsibility, the Civil Code and RA 9266 IRR for architect-of-record liability, RA 9184 for public procurement, RA 11201 for DHSUD's human-settlements mandate, and local public-fund/accountability rules where LGU funds are involved.

## Why This Matters on the LEA

Government practice often appears in Professional Practice questions as a trap. The question may name DPWH, NHA, DHSUD, an in-house design office, or a government consultant. The correct answer usually turns on the architect's responsibility, procurement route, and record duty, not the employer's name.

An architect does not stop being an architect because the client is the government. RA 9266 IRR states that architectural documents include drawings, specifications, cost estimates, and other outputs that only an architect can sign and seal. It also states that the architect-of-record is fully responsible for architectural plans, specifications, and other documents issued under the architect's seal or authorized signature.

## In-House Design Is Not a Shortcut Around RA 9266

An in-house government architect may be involved in programming, design review, standard details, permit coordination, technical evaluation, construction administration, or acceptance documentation. Those tasks happen inside a public agency chain of command, but the professional question remains the same: who prepared the architectural documents, who supervised them, who signed or sealed them, and what law controls the procurement or approval route?

RA 9266 IRR connects the architect's signature, seal, PRC registration number, PTR, and IAPOA information with professional responsibility. The same IRR language states that the architect-of-record rule applies to both government architects and architects employed by private firms. That means a government office cannot use "in-house" status as a substitute for responsible professional control.

## Public Procurement Changes the Delivery Route

RA 9184 governs procurement of government infrastructure projects, goods, and consulting services. It frames public procurement around transparency, competitiveness, streamlined process, accountability, and public monitoring. For an architect, this changes how the commission, consultant package, infrastructure project, or technical service is obtained and documented.

In private practice, the architect-client service agreement is usually the main delivery document. In government practice, the architect must also understand the procurement plan, approved budget, terms of reference, bid or eligibility documents, contract authority, change documentation, acceptance records, and fund-accountability trail. A good LEA answer separates the two: RA 9266 governs the professional practice of architecture; RA 9184 governs how the government procures the project or service.

## Working With Private Consultants

Government agencies may engage private architects or technical consultants. The critical document question is responsibility allocation. The agreement should make clear which party prepares which documents, who signs and seals the architectural set, who reviews contractor submissions, and who has authority to approve design changes.

If a private architect signs documents as architect-of-record, Article 1723 and the RA 9266 IRR architect-of-record provisions become relevant. If the in-house office reviews or administers the project, its approval notes should show what was reviewed, what was returned for correction, and what remained the responsibility of the consultant or contractor. The exam trap is to answer "government architect" or "private architect" automatically. The correct answer follows the signed documents and the contract role.

## Real World Scenario

Suppose a city government is preparing a small public market upgrade. The city has an in-house architect who develops schematic layouts, but later procures a private consultant for final architectural documents. The procurement of the consultant belongs under RA 9184. The final architectural documents must be prepared, signed, and sealed under RA 9266 discipline. If the private consultant becomes architect-of-record, the service agreement should show that role clearly. If the in-house architect reviews the submission, review comments, corrected drawings, approvals, and cost-change notes should be retained so the project record explains why the design was accepted.

## Common Traps

- Do not cite RA 9266 as the procurement law. Procurement of government infrastructure projects and consulting services is RA 9184 territory.
- Do not cite RA 9184 as the law that defines the architect-of-record. That is RA 9266 IRR territory.
- Do not say Civil Code Article 1723 is only a private-practice idea. RA 9266 IRR expressly links the architect-of-record rule to Article 1723 and states that the rule applies to government and private-firm architects.
- Do not overstate agency mandates unless the agency charter or enabling law is actually cited.
