## What You Need to Know for the LEA

This local rewrite narrows the topic from unsupported rebar weight shortcuts to Fajardo's source-backed reinforcing bar takeoff method. The controlling task is to count and schedule reinforcing bars by length, spacing, hooks, bends, splices, commercial lengths, and tie wire.

## Core Concept

Fajardo explains that reinforcing bar estimates must include the additional length for hooks, bends, and splices. For CHB reinforcement, he gives direct counting, area, and unit block methods. In direct counting, the vertical and horizontal reinforcements are counted from the plan, while length is determined from the elevation. Hook, bend, and lapping splice allowances are then added.

A major estimating decision is commercial bar length. Fajardo emphasizes selecting a bar length divisible by the required cut length to reduce unwanted offcuts. Tie wire is a separate estimate: No. 16 galvanized iron tie wire secures bars in position, is ordered by kilogram or roll, and is approximately 53 meters per kilogram.

## How It's Tested

Expect the exam task to ask what must be added or counted, not merely the bar diameter. If the question asks for cut lengths, include cover, hook, bend, and splice instructions from the drawings. If it asks for order quantity, divide the total required length by a chosen commercial length and round up. If it asks about tie wire, convert total tie length to kilograms using the source-backed 53 m per kg relation.

## Common Mistakes

Do not answer with a weight formula unless the prompt provides a checked table or density basis. Do not omit hook, bend, or splice allowances. Do not choose a commercial length that creates avoidable waste when a better divisible length is available.
