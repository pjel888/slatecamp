# Spanish Colonial Bahay Na Bato

## Hybrid House Type

HOA Module 4 P1 describes Bahay na Bato as a housing prototype combining indigenous and Hispanic building traditions to reduce dangers from fire, earthquakes, and cyclones. It also identifies arquitectura mestiza as hybrid construction built partly of wood and partly of stone.

## Physical Characteristics

The checked HOA source lists common characteristics: generally two storeys and sometimes three, cut stone or brick at the ground floor, wood at the upper floor, grillwork at the ground-floor windows, sliding shutters with capiz shells or glass panels at the second floor, and a high hip roof with a 45-degree pitch.

## Plan And Parts

The ground floor includes cochera, zaguan, entresuelo, cuadra, and cocina in the checked source. The second floor includes caida or ante-sala, sala, bano, latrina, cocina, comedor, azotea, cuarto, galeria volada or corredor, oratorio, callado, pasamano, ventanillas, and barandillas. Filipino Architecture adds that Bahay na Bato evolved from the Bahay Kubo through tropical-house traits such as steep hip roof, post-and-lintel construction, light and airy structure, elevated living quarters, economy of materials, and flowing spaces, while adding Spanish, Neo-Classical, Gothic, and Baroque influence through grandeur and solidity.

## LEA Takeaway

Read Bahay na Bato as adaptation, not a simple European import. Its exam value is the combination of indigenous spatial-climatic logic, Hispanic masonry/urban domestic form, and named house parts.
