# Vernacular Architecture

## Source-Safe Scope

Batch 136 grounds vernacular architecture in local source support: trial-and-error building knowledge, available materials, climate, lifestyle, folk-derived patterns, Philippine bahay kubo and bahay na bato vocabulary, and tropical passive-design principles.

The rewrite avoids romanticizing vernacular work as automatically code-compliant, anti-modern, or fixed in the past. The article treats vernacular knowledge as a design intelligence source that still needs current safety and program judgment.

## How to Use the Concept

Use vernacular architecture as a climate-and-culture reasoning tool. Ask what local builders learned about weather, material availability, daily use, and social life before translating those lessons into contemporary requirements.

## Real World Scenario

A Pangasinan barangay needs a small day-care and evacuation support building. A vernacular-informed answer studies local climate, available materials, raised floors, shaded edges, cross-ventilation, and community use patterns, then translates those lessons into a current safe building instead of copying old forms as costume.
