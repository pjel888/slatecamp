# Wire insulation types and ratings

Wire-insulation questions in the LEA are not vocabulary questions only. The correct choice depends on conductor material, insulation type, location condition, temperature rating, ampacity, and marking.

## Definitions to anchor the question

The PEC defines ampacity as the current, in amperes, that a conductor can carry continuously under its conditions of use without exceeding its temperature rating. An insulated conductor is a conductor encased within material recognized by the Code as electrical insulation. The checked PEC source also states that conductors are normally copper unless otherwise provided, and that conductor sizes are expressed in square millimeters.

## Location drives insulation selection

A conductor suitable for dry or damp locations is not automatically suitable for wet locations. The checked PEC conductor provisions list permitted conductor types for wet locations, including listed moisture-impervious metal-sheathed cable, types MTW, RHW, RHW-2, TW, THW, THW-2, THHW, THWN, THWN-2, XHHW, XHHW-2, and other listed wet-location types.

For common LEA choices:

- THHN is a heat-resistant thermoplastic nylon-jacketed conductor and is listed for dry or damp use at its stated temperature rating.
- THWN is a moisture-resistant and heat-resistant thermoplastic nylon-jacketed conductor and is listed for dry and wet use at its stated temperature rating.
- TW is a moisture-resistant thermoplastic conductor and is listed for dry and wet use at its stated temperature rating.
- XHHW is a moisture-resistant thermoset conductor and is listed for dry, damp, and wet use with the temperature ratings given in the PEC table.
- MI cable appears in the PEC conductor application table with dry and wet use and a higher special-application rating.

## Markings and ampacity

The checked PEC marking provisions require conductor identification such as maximum rated voltage, type letter or letters, manufacturer identification, and size. The table and ampacity rules must be read together: the insulation type tells you the applicable use condition and temperature column, but the final ampacity still depends on the installation conditions.

The checked Electrical Layout and Estimate text supports the same design logic: insulation protects conductors from heat and moisture, and conductors must be large enough to carry the connected load with acceptable voltage drop and mechanical strength.

## Exam-safe method

For any wire-insulation question, identify the location first: dry, damp, or wet. Next identify the insulation type and temperature rating from the source table. Then check conductor size, material, ampacity, overcurrent protection, and voltage drop. Do not select a conductor type from memory when the question gives a wet-location or temperature clue.
