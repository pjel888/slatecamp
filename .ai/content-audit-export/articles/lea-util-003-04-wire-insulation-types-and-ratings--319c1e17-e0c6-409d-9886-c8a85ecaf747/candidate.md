## Learning Goal

The Philippine Electrical Code recognizes several standard wire insulation types, each with specific temperature ratings and applications:

### THHN (Thermoplastic High Heat Nylon-coated)
- _Temperature rating: 90°C_ (PEC Table 310-16)
- _Insulation material: Nylon-coated thermoplastic_
- _Applications: General wiring in conduit systems, high-temperature circuits, motor leads_
- _Voltage rating: Up to 600V_
- Single conductor only; must be installed in raceway (conduit)
- Commonly used in industrial and commercial installations throughout the Philippines

### THWN (Thermoplastic Heat and Water Resistant Nylon-coated)
- _Temperature rating: 75°C wet, 90°C dry_ (PEC Table 310-16)
- _Insulation material: Water-resistant nylon coating_
- _Applications: Indoor/outdoor use, wet locations, underground runs_
- _Voltage rating: Up to 600V_
- Most versatile general-purpose wire in Philippine building systems
- Excellent for both exposed and buried conduit applications

### TW (Thermoplastic Wet Location Rated)
- _Temperature rating: 60°C wet, 75°C dry_ (PEC Table 310-16)
- _Insulation material: Basic thermoplastic_
- _Applications: Lighting circuits, general branch circuits, temporary wiring_
- _Voltage rating: Up to 600V_
- Lower cost but limited temperature performance
- Restricted to branch circuits with lower amperage demands

### XHHW (Cross-linked Polyethylene High Heat)
- _Temperature rating: 90°C dry, 75°C wet_ (PEC Table 310-16)
- _Insulation material: Cross-linked polyethylene_
- _Applications: Power distribution, feeder circuits, high-load installations_
- _Voltage rating: Up to 600V_
- Superior insulation durability and thermal stability
- Preferred for main service panels and heavy-load circuits

## Minimum Wire Sizing for Branch Circuits

The Philippine Electrical Code establishes the following minimum branch circuit wire sizes:

| Circuit Type | Minimum Wire Size | AWG equivalent | Ampacity (Cu, 75°C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| General lighting | 2.0 mm² | 14 AWG | 15A |
| Small appliance | 3.5 mm² | 12 AWG | 20A |
| Kitchen counter | 2.0 mm² | 14 AWG | 15A (TW only) |
| Dedicated circuit | 3.5 mm² | 12 AWG | 20A |

_Source: PEC Table 310-16 and Section 210.3_

**Critical requirement:** Branch circuits serving general lighting shall not be smaller than _2.0 mm² (14 AWG)_ for 15A circuits per PEC requirements. 3.5 mm² (12 AWG) is required for 20A circuits., though it may be encountered in older systems.

## Conductor Materials: Copper vs. Aluminum

### Copper Conductors
- _Ampacity: Higher per unit cross-section (baseline for PEC tables)_
- _Conductivity: 1.0 relative conductivity_ (reference standard)
- _Cost: Premium compared to aluminum_
- _Corrosion resistance: Excellent in dry and moderate moisture conditions_
- _Temperature coefficient: ~0.39%/°C increase in resistance_
- Standard choice for all building wiring in the Philippines
- Superior for connections, terminations, and long-term reliability

### Aluminum Conductors
- _Ampacity: 84% of copper at same cross-section_ (PEC Table 310-16 footnotes)
- _Conductivity: 0.61 relative conductivity_
- _Cost: 30–40% less than copper_
- _Corrosion resistance: Fair; requires oxide-inhibiting compound at terminations_
- _Temperature coefficient: ~0.40%/°C increase in resistance_
- Limited use in branch circuits; acceptable only for feeder/service in large buildings
- Requires special termination techniques and larger lug connections

**Practical note:** Most building installations in the Philippines use copper for branch circuits. Aluminum is used selectively in feeder and service conductors only in commercial/industrial projects.

## Wire Ampacity and Sizing Charts

The Philippine Electrical Code establishes ampacity based on insulation temperature rating, conductor material, and ambient conditions. The following chart applies at _30°C ambient temperature_ and _3-conductor cables in raceway_:

### Standard Ampacity Table (Copper, PEC Table 310-16)

| Wire Size (mm²) | AWG | 60°C Insulation | 75°C Insulation | 90°C Insulation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0 | 14 | 15A | 15A | N/A (TW not 90°C rated) |
| 3.5 | 12 | 20A | 20A | 25A |
| 5.5 | 10 | 30A | 30A | 40A |
| 8 | 8 | 40A | 50A | 55A |
| 10 | 6 | 55A | 65A | 75A |
| 16 | 4 | 70A | 85A | 95A |
| 25 | 2 | 95A | 115A | 130A |

_Source: PEC Table 310-16, ambient temperature 30°C, 3 or more current-carrying conductors_

**Derating factors apply when:**
- Ambient temperature exceeds 30°C (common in Philippine installations)
- More than 3 current-carrying conductors are bundled in one raceway
- Conduit fill exceeds 40% of cross-sectional area

## Wire Sizing by Load and Circuit Protection

For common residential and light commercial circuits in the Philippines, wire sizes are matched to circuit breaker ratings as follows:

| Circuit Amperage | Wire Size (mm²) | AWG | Breaker Size | Typical Applications |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15A | 2.0 | 14 | 15A | Lighting, general receptacles |
| 20A | 3.5 | 12 | 20A | Kitchen counter, laundry |
| 30A | 8 | 8 | 30A | Dryer, air conditioner |
| 50A | 16 | 4 | 50A | Electric water heater, oven |
| 60A | 25 | 2 | 60A | Heavy loads, feeders |

_Source: PEC Table 310-16 and Section 240.4(D)_

**Selection principle:** Choose the wire size corresponding to the breaker rating; wire ampacity must _equal or exceed_ the breaker rating at the insulation temperature rating of 75°C.

## Voltage Drop Considerations

Voltage drop in conductors reduces available voltage at the load and causes inefficiency. The Philippine Electrical Code recommends:

- _Maximum voltage drop to point of utilization: 5%_ (branch circuit + feeder combined)
- _Branch circuit maximum: 3%_
- _Feeder maximum: 2%_

_Source: PEC Article 210.19(A)(1) FPN and 215.2(D)(1) FPN_

**Calculation formula:**

Voltage Drop (V) = (2 × R × L × I) / 1000

Where:
- R = conductor resistance (ohms/km at 75°C)
- L = circuit length (meters)
- I = load current (amperes)

**Example:** For a 20A lighting circuit at 30 meters with 3.5 mm² copper (75°C):
- Resistance of 3.5 mm² copper at 75°C = 5.30 Ω/km (PEC Table)
- Voltage drop = (2 × 5.30 × 30 × 20) / 1000 = 6.36V drop (2.9% at 220V)
- _Result: Acceptable; within 3% limit_

For longer runs (above 50 meters), increase wire size to maintain acceptable voltage drop.

## Conduit Types and Selection

### PVC (Polyvinyl Chloride) Conduit
- _Material: Rigid plastic; non-metallic_
- _Temperature limit: 60°C ambient maximum_
- _Installation: Indoor dry locations, underground (encased), above ground_
- _Cost: Lowest; economical for large installations_
- _Advantages: Corrosion-resistant, non-conductive, lightweight, easy termination_
- _Limitations: Softens above 60°C; not suitable for motor circuits in hot areas_
- Most common choice in Philippine residential and light commercial projects

### EMT (Electrical Metallic Tubing)
- _Material: Lightweight steel, zinc-coated_
- _Temperature limit: 50°C ambient (standard); 75°C rated available_
- _Installation: Indoor dry/damp locations, not buried_
- _Cost: Moderate; mid-range material cost_
- _Advantages: Metallic protection, good strength, quick coupling systems_
- _Limitations: Subject to corrosion in wet environments; not suitable for direct burial_
- Common in industrial and high-vibration installations

### IMC (Intermediate Metal Conduit)
- _Material: Stronger steel tubing than EMT; thicker wall_
- _Temperature limit: 75°C ambient_
- _Installation: Outdoor, exposed, areas with potential damage_
- _Cost: Higher than EMT; premium structural protection_
- _Advantages: Superior impact resistance, better UV protection with coating_
- _Limitations: More expensive; overkill for standard indoor use_
- Specified where physical protection is essential

### Rigid Steel Conduit
- _Material: Heavy steel pipe; maximum structural integrity_
- _Temperature limit: 75°C ambient and higher with proper installation_
- _Installation: Outdoor, underground (with concrete encasement), mechanical areas_
- _Cost: Highest material and labor cost_
- _Advantages: Maximum durability, excellent for underground/buried installations, superior mechanical protection_
- _Limitations: Heavy; requires specialized threading tools; labor-intensive installation_
- Used for main service runs, feeder conduits, and mechanical protection in Philippine industrial projects

**Selection matrix for Philippine conditions:**

| Environment | Recommended Conduit | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Indoor dry (residential) | PVC | Cost-effective; corrosion-resistant |
| Outdoor exposed | IMC or Rigid Steel | Withstands UV and moisture |
| Underground direct burial | Rigid Steel or PVC | Structural integrity and corrosion protection |
| High-temperature area (>60°C) | EMT or Rigid Steel | Higher temperature rating |
| Wet/damp location | PVC or IMC | Corrosion resistance; not EMT |
| High-vibration/mechanical area | Rigid Steel or IMC | Impact protection |

## Conduit Fill Limitations

The Philippine Electrical Code restricts the number of conductors in a conduit to prevent overheating and allow for heat dissipation. Fill percentages are based on _conduit internal cross-sectional area_:

| Number of Conductors | Maximum Fill Percentage |
|---|---|
| 1 conductor | 53% |
| 2 conductors | 31% |
| 3+ conductors | 40% |

_Source: PEC Article 300.17 and Annex C_

**Example calculation for 3 conductors:**

For _3.5 mm² (12 AWG) copper wires in 16 mm PVC conduit:
- Conductor area (each): 10.7 mm²; total 3 wires = 32.1 mm²
- 16 mm PVC conduit internal area: 201.1 mm²
- Fill percentage: 32.1 / 201.1 = 15.95% ✓ (well within 40% limit)

**Practical guidance:** Standard installations rarely exceed 40% fill if sized properly. Verify conduit fill when:
- Installing more than 3 conductors in one conduit
- Mixing different wire sizes
- Installing in conduit smaller than 16 mm

## Cable Types and Applications

### NM (Non-Metallic) Sheathed Cable ("Romex" equivalent)
- _Construction: Two or more insulated conductors + ground in plastic sheath_
- _Temperature rating: 60°C or 75°C depending on insulation_
- _Voltage rating: Up to 600V_
- _Applications: Interior branch circuits in residential buildings, dry locations only_
- _Advantages: Quick installation; no separate conduit required_
- _Limitations: Cannot be buried or used in damp locations; limited to branch circuits_
- Not widely available in Philippine market; PVC-sheathed alternatives used instead

### MC (Metal-Clad) Cable
- _Construction: Insulated conductors wrapped in flexible metal armor_
- _Temperature rating: 60°C, 75°C, or 90°C per inner insulation_
- _Voltage rating: Up to 600V_
- _Applications: Interior circuits, some outdoor/damp applications, industrial wiring_
- _Advantages: Metal armor provides mechanical protection and serves as equipment ground_
- _Limitations: More expensive than NM; requires stripping/termination skill_
- Used in commercial and light industrial installations in larger Philippine projects

### AC (Armored Cable)
- _Construction: Insulated conductors in flexible spiral metal armor_
- _Temperature rating: 60°C or 75°C_
- _Voltage rating: Up to 600V_
- _Applications: Commercial interior circuits; industrial machinery connections_
- _Advantages: Lightweight armor; flexible for tight routing_
- _Limitations: Not for wet locations; armor may corrode if moisture enters_
- Limited use in Philippine installations; replaced by MC in most modern projects

### MI (Mineral-Insulated Metal-Sheathed) Cable
- _Construction: Copper or nickel conductors in solid copper/steel tube sheath; mineral insulation (MgO)_
- _Temperature rating: 250°C dry; 150°C wet_
- _Voltage rating: Up to 600V; some rated to 2000V_
- _Applications: High-temperature circuits, fire-rated installations, critical power systems_
- _Advantages: Highest fire resistance; operates at extreme temperatures; longest service life_
- _Limitations: Most expensive; special termination requirements; difficult installation_
- Specified only for specialized applications (data centers, hospitals, fire alarm circuits) in Philippine buildings

**Cable selection summary:**

| Cable Type | Typical Use | Cost Level | Temperature Rating | Wet Location? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NM (Romex) | Residential branch | Low | 60–75°C | No |
| MC | Commercial circuits | Medium | 60–90°C | Limited |
| AC | Industrial machinery | Medium | 60–75°C | No |
| MI | High-temp/fire-rated | High | 150–250°C | Yes (special) |

## Color Coding for Conductors

The Philippine Electrical Code and standard practice establish the following conductor color conventions. **Important note:** The Philippines does not mandate a single universal color code; projects typically specify colors in their electrical plans. The following reflects common practice in Philippine construction:

### Standard Color Assignments (Philippine Practice)

| Color | Function | Voltage Level | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brown or Black | Live/Hot Phase A | 220V | Phase conductor |
| Black or Red | Live/Hot Phase B | 220V | Phase conductor (where 3-phase) |
| Red or Yellow | Live/Hot Phase C | 220V | Phase conductor (where 3-phase) |
| Blue or Light Blue | Neutral | 0V | Return path; not a ground |
| Green/Yellow striped or Green | Ground/Earth | 0V | Safety conductor; equipment ground |
| Bare copper | Ground/Earth | 0V | Equipment ground wire (no insulation) |

_Source: PEC Article 310-12 and standard Philippine electrical practice_

**Critical distinction:**
- _Neutral (Blue):_ Part of the load circuit; carries return current
- _Ground (Green/Yellow):_ Safety conductor; should carry zero current under normal conditions
- These must _never be confused_ in installation

**Practical Philippine context:**
- Some older installations use Red for neutral and Black for ground (non-standard)
- Always verify the specific electrical plans for the project
- Consistent color coding throughout the installation is essential for safety and maintenance
- Motor terminals and control circuits may use different conventions; follow equipment manufacturer specifications

## Insulation Temperature Ratings and Derating

Wire insulation is rated for continuous operation at specific temperatures. Selection of wire insulation type must account for:

1. _Ambient temperature at the installation location_
2. _Heat generated by the wire due to load current_
3. _Heat dissipation capability of the installation method_

### Temperature Rating Definitions

- _60°C (140°F):_ Maximum continuous operating temperature of insulation (basic rating; TW wire)
- _75°C (167°F):_ Higher-capacity insulation; standard for most branch circuits (THWN, TW)
- _90°C (194°F):_ Highest-rated insulation for standard building wiring (THHN, XHHW)

_Source: PEC Table 310-16 header notes_

### Derating Factors for Ambient Temperature

The Philippine Electrical Code reduces ampacity when ambient temperature exceeds 30°C. The following adjustment factors apply:

| Ambient Temperature (°C) | 60°C-Rated | 75°C-Rated | 90°C-Rated |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30°C and below | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| 31–35°C | 91% | 94% | 97% |
| 36–40°C | 82% | 88% | 93% |
| 41–45°C | 71% | 82% | 89% |
| 46–50°C | 58% | 75% | 85% |

_Source: PEC Table 310-16 ambient temperature derating factors_

**Example:** A 20A branch circuit with THWN wire (75°C rated) in a location with 40°C ambient temperature:
- Standard ampacity: 20A
- Derating factor at 40°C: 88%
- Actual allowable ampacity: 20A × 0.88 = 17.6A
- _Result: Wire is undersized; must upgrade to next size (3.5 mm² → 5.5 mm²)_

**Philippine considerations:**
- Most of the Philippines experiences annual ambient temperatures of 25–32°C
- Industrial/mechanical rooms, kitchens, and rooftop installations often exceed 35°C
- Always verify installation location temperature rating before final wire selection

## Installation Best Practices

### Wire Termination
- Use appropriate termination lugs for wire size and material
- Tighten connections with calibrated torque tools per manufacturer specification
- Copper and aluminum conductors must not be directly connected (galvanic corrosion risk); use copper-aluminum lugs or isolating sleeves

### Protecting Against Damage
- Run conductors through conduit when exposed to physical damage
- Elevate above the 2.4-meter line in living spaces to prevent accidental contact
- Use protective covers or boxing where conduit terminates near operating areas

### Future Expansion
- Install conduit with spare capacity (40% fill rule); allows for circuit additions
- Install pull-string in empty conduit for future wire pulls
- Label all conductors and circuits for maintenance and troubleshooting

### Heat Management
- Space conductors in conduit to allow air circulation
- Avoid bundling high-current wires that generate excessive heat
- Provide ventilation in cable trays or conduit in mechanical spaces
- Use 90°C-rated wire in high-temperature installations instead of overdimensioning conduit

## Summary

Proper electrical wiring selection ensures safe, efficient, and code-compliant building systems:

1. **Select insulation type** based on temperature, location, and application (THHN, THWN, TW, or XHHW)
2. **Verify minimum wire size** for branch circuits (never smaller than 3.5 mm² per PEC)
3. **Calculate ampacity** using PEC tables and apply derating factors for ambient temperature
4. **Size conductors** to limit voltage drop to 5% maximum and match circuit breaker ratings
5. **Choose appropriate conduit** type (PVC, EMT, IMC, or rigid steel) for environmental conditions
6. **Verify conduit fill** does not exceed 40% for 3+ conductors
7. **Apply consistent color coding** per project electrical plans
8. **Implement proper termination** with appropriate lugs and torque specifications

All installations must comply with the _Philippine Electrical Code_ and be verified by a licensed electrical engineer or inspector before energization.
