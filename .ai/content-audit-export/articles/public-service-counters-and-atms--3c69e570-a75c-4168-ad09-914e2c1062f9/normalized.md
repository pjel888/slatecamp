# Public Service Counters and ATMs
### BP 344 (2024 IRR) — LEA Review Series

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to:

Apply BP 344 requirements for accessible service counters and ATMs: height range (760-860 mm for counters, max 1,200 mm for writing surfaces), knee clearance (680 mm minimum depth, 680 mm minimum height), forward reach (max 1,200 mm) and side reach (max 1,370 mm) limits; and determine the minimum percentage of counters that must be accessible.

## REAL-WORLD HOOK

> You are at a government service window at 10 AM. The queue is 40 people long. A person in a wheelchair approaches the single counter — its surface is at 950 mm, too high to see over, too high to sign documents. There is no lower section. The ATM across the hall requires a PIN entered on a keypad at 1400 mm — beyond her forward reach. The ATM has no audio output, no Braille. She cannot complete her business without assistance. She leaves. The agency director calls the architect. **You designed this building. Can you defend every dimension?**

## REASON FOR THE LAW

### Why Two-Level Counters? (Sec. 14.2)
A counter at 950–1100 mm is comfortable for standing users but inaccessible for wheelchair users, whose eye level and reach are at approximately 700–900 mm. Providing **only a high counter excludes** wheelchair users from conducting transactions independently. The lower 750 mm section enables eye contact, document signing, and payment without assistance.

### Why 1800×1800 mm at ATMs vs. 1500×1500 mm at Counters? (Sec. 12.1 vs. 14.4)
ATMs involve lateral movement — inserting cards, reaching a keypad, retrieving cash, pocketing a receipt — requiring a **larger maneuvering envelope**. Service counters involve primarily forward approach, so a 1500×1500 mm turning circle is sufficient.

### Why 480 mm Knee Depth? (Sec. 14.3)
A wheelchair footrest extends approximately 400–450 mm from the front of the seat. The 480 mm depth allows the wheelchair to **tuck under** the counter so the user's chest reaches the transaction surface without leaning dangerously forward.

### Why 700 mm Vertical Knee Space? (Sec. 14.3)
Standard wheelchair seat height is ~480–520 mm. The armrests are approximately 700–750 mm high. The 700 mm clearance allows the armrests to slide under the counter without obstruction, enabling the user to get close enough to reach and sign documents.

### Why No Tight Grasping, Pinching, or Twisting? (Sec. 12.5)
This requirement addresses users with arthritis, cerebral palsy, limb differences, or tremors. Controls that require precise motor actions create barriers. All ATM controls must be operable with a **closed fist** or open palm — the lowest common denominator of hand function.

### Why Audio AND Braille AND Tactile at ATMs? (Sec. 12.2)
Different users need different modes:
- **Blind users** → audio output + Braille labels
- **Low-vision users** → tactile labels + audio
- **Deaf users** → visual screen (no change needed)
- **Deaf-Blind users** → Braille is the only option
All three modes must coexist because no single mode serves all users.

## Exam Tip

ALE Swipe: Accessible counter: 760-860 mm high section minimum 900 mm wide. Knee clearance = 680 mm deep × 680 mm high × 760 mm minimum width. Forward reach max = 1,200 mm. Side reach max = 1,370 mm. These four dimensions are the most tested for counters.
