## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Fajardo treats paint estimating as an area-coverage problem. Paint manufacturers give estimated coverage per gallon, and the gallon used in the source has a net content of 4 liters. The exam-safe method is to use the coverage stated in the problem or source table, then divide the paintable area by that coverage.

## Core Concept

For one coat, paint quantity in gallons equals paintable area divided by coverage per gallon. If the problem gives coverage per liter, use the same idea in liters. If the problem gives coverage per gallon and asks for liters, solve gallons first and multiply by 4 liters per gallon.

For more than one coat, compute each coat from the coverage stated for that coat, or multiply the one-coat quantity by the number of equal coats when the same coverage applies. If the item gives a waste factor, apply it after the base paint quantity is computed.

## How It Is Tested

Typical LEA items give a wall area, a paint coverage value, and the required number of coats. A common direct setup is:

Paint quantity = area / coverage

Example: if one gallon covers 30 sq m and the wall area is 360 sq m for one coat, the base quantity is 360 / 30 = 12 gallons. Since the gallon is 4 liters, that is 48 liters before any additional coat or waste factor.

## Common Mistakes

Do not multiply area by coverage; the units point the other way. Do not mix gallon coverage and liter answers without converting. Do not add waste before solving the base quantity unless the problem explicitly defines the waste-adjusted coverage.
