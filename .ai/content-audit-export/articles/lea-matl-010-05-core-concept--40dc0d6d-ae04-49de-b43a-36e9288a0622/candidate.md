## What You Need to Know for the LEA
## Learning Goal

Calculate paint coverage requirements by surface area, number of coats, and paint coverage rate per liter (typically 8-12 m²/L per coat), accounting for substrate porosity and wastage in interior and exterior painting estimates.
## Core Concept

Paint coverage is inversely proportional to the required thickness and surface porosity. The basic formula is: _Paint required (liters) = Total surface area (m²) ÷ Coverage rate (m²/L)_. Coverage rates are printed on paint cans by manufacturers and vary by paint type, color, and surface condition.

Typical coverage rates in the Philippines:

- Interior latex/acrylic: 20–30 m²/L (standard single coat) — typical coverage per manufacturer specifications
- Exterior acrylic enamel: 20–30 m²/L (standard single coat on moderate texture)
- Two-component epoxy: 8–10 m²/L
- Primer: 20–30 m²/L (standard single coat on prepared surface)

Most projects require two coats (primer + finish), which doubles the paint volume. Some surfaces (porous, rough, or previously painted) may require additional coats or a primer, increasing total consumption by 20–50%.

In the Philippines, standard paint cans are 1 liter, 4 liters (1 gallon), and 20 liters (5 gallons). For large projects, contractors often purchase paint by the liter or in bulk containers for cost efficiency.

## Philippine Construction Context

In the Philippines, major paint manufacturers include Boysen, Mapua, Sherwin-Williams, and Safeguard. The market dominates with water-based (latex) and acrylic paints for interior and exterior use. High-humidity coastal environments drive demand for mildew-resistant and moisture-resistant formulations.

Standard coverage rates are stated on paint cans (lata) or product data sheets. For residential projects, a simple interior wall (smooth drywall) typically requires 25–30 m²/L per coat. For exterior applications on rough concrete, coverage drops to 20–25 m²/L due to surface porosity (approximately 15–20% reduction from standard 25–30 m²/L).

Climate considerations: Philippines' high humidity and typhoon exposure mean exterior paints must resist mildew and moisture. Many specifications now require epoxy-based or polyurethane topcoats for durability. Paint costs fluctuate with global resin prices but typically range from 400–1,200 pesos per liter depending on type and quality.

## How It's Tested

On the LEA, paint estimation questions focus on:

1. **Basic coverage calculation**: Given area and coverage rate, calculate liters needed.

2. **Multi-coat systems**: Prime + finish coats; multiply single-coat quantity by number of coats.

3. **Surface condition adjustments**: Reduce coverage rate for rough, porous, or previously painted surfaces.

4. **Waste factor**: Add 5–10% to final quantity.

5. **Can conversion**: Convert liters to can counts (1 L, 4 L, or 20 L cans).

6. **Cost estimation**: Total liters × price per liter = material cost.

7. **Mixed areas**: Different surfaces (interior/exterior, smooth/rough) calculated separately, then summed.
