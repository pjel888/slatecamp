# Proxemics and Personal Space in Architecture

Proxemics studies the symbolic and communicative role of spatial separation between people. The local source map connects it to social and interpersonal situations and to environmental and cultural factors, while the official Hall publisher record identifies proxemics as central to how people use space in architecture, city planning, and cross-cultural interaction.

Personal space is not a fixed number. TOA 3 defines it as the variable and subjective distance at which one person feels comfortable talking to another. That matters in architecture because a bench, counter, corridor, clinic queue, or classroom row can support or frustrate the distance people expect in a given situation.

The local source map gives useful distance bands: intimate distance at 0-18 inches or 0-450 mm, personal distance at 18 inches to 4 feet or 450 mm-1.20 m, and social distance at 4 to 10 feet or 1.20 m-3.00 m. Use these as review anchors, not as automatic design answers for every culture or room.

Proxemics should also be separated from anthropometrics. Anthropometrics asks whether a body can fit and move; proxemics asks how people feel and communicate when they are near or far from one another.

## Real World Scenario

In a review-center waiting area, a row of fixed seats may meet basic body dimensions but still feel uncomfortable if strangers are forced into intimate or close personal distance. A better plan can vary seat spacing, provide side tables or offsets, and give users some choice about whether they wait alone, in pairs, or in a small group.
