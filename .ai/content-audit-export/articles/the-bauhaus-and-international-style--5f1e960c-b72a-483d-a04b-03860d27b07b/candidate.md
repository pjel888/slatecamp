# The Bauhaus And International Style

## Board Exam Quick Check

Keep the Bauhaus and International Style related but distinct. The Bauhaus is the German school founded by Walter Gropius. The International Style is the formal language associated with functional architecture, simple geometry, broad glass areas, untextured surfaces, and steel or reinforced-concrete construction.

## Bauhaus

The checked sources identify the Bauhaus as a school in Germany founded by Walter Gropius. The board-review source defines its program as a synthesis of technology, craft, and design aesthetics with emphasis on functional design. Another local source identifies Gropius as a German architect who founded the Bauhaus, worked under Peter Behrens, and later taught at Harvard.

The Bauhaus Building at Dessau is the key visual anchor. In the checked sources, Gropius's work is connected to simple geometry, steel and glass, smooth surfaces, primary colors, and linear or horizontal elements. TOA 5 adds that Gropius used modern materials and construction methods, with interest in teamwork, standardization, and prefabrication.

## International Style

The International Style is not simply any modern building. The local review source defines it as functional architecture without regional characteristics, using simple geometric forms, large untextured surfaces, often white surfaces, large glass areas, and steel or reinforced-concrete construction.

The broader history source explains that the 1932 Modern Architecture exhibition and related publications codified the style through volume rather than mass, regularity without axial symmetry, and resistance to applied ornament. Those points help separate the International Style from later Post-Modern use of historical allusion, color, and ornament.

## LEA Answer Pattern

When a question asks for Bauhaus, look for Walter Gropius, Dessau, functional design, and synthesis of technology, craft, and design. When it asks for International Style, look for the formal result: simple geometry, glass, steel, reinforced concrete, smooth or white surfaces, and little regional or historical expression.

## Common Exam Traps

- Do not make Bauhaus and International Style exact synonyms. Bauhaus is the school and design-pedagogy anchor; International Style is the codified architectural language.
- Do not confuse International Style with Post-Modernism. The latter reopens historical reference and ornament; the former reduces form toward abstraction and functional expression.
- Remember that Mies is part of the Bauhaus/International Style orbit, but the Bauhaus founding anchor is Gropius.
