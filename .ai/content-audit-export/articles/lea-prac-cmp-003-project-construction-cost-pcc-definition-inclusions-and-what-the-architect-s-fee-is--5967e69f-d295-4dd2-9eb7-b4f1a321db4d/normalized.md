## Board Exam Quick Check

For PCC questions, ask one thing first: is the cost part of the completed building delivered to the Owner, or is it a professional/owner-side cost outside the building work? That distinction is the exam trap.

## Project Construction Cost (PCC): Definition, Inclusions, and Fee Base

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to define Project Construction Cost (PCC), identify the building work included in PCC, identify professional fees excluded from PCC, and explain why PCC matters when an architect's compensation is computed from construction cost.

---

### Source-Grounded Definition

SPP Document 202, Section 7.17 defines Project Construction Cost as the cost of the completed building to the Owner. In board-exam language, PCC is not the Owner's total project budget. It is the construction-cost base for the completed building and the planned building systems/items indicated in the Architect's and consultants' plans and specifications.

The source expressly includes the cost of materials and the labor for their installation. It also lists building and systems items such as the structure, plumbing and sanitary fixtures, electrical fixtures, mechanical equipment, elevators, escalators, air-conditioning system, fire protection system, alarm and clock system, communications and electronic system, architectural interiors, and site development elements when these are planned and designed by the Architect.

### What PCC Excludes

SPP Document 202, Section 7.17 is equally important because it states what PCC does not include. PCC excludes the fees of the Architect, the Engineer, and Specialist Consultants. It also excludes the salaries of construction inspectors. This is the safest exam rule: do not add the architect's own fee into the cost base used to compute that fee.

<ExamTip>
If the item is part of the completed building or its designed building systems, test whether it belongs in PCC. If the item is a professional fee, consultant fee, or construction inspector salary, SPP 202 Section 7.17 excludes it from PCC.
</ExamTip>

### Fair Market Value Rule for Owner-Furnished Items

SPP 202 also handles an Owner-furnished item problem. If the Owner purchases or furnishes labor or material below market cost, the cost used for PCC is still computed on the basis of current and fair market value. The board trap is to use the discounted or donated amount. The source points the candidate back to fair market value because PCC is meant to represent the completed building cost, not merely the Owner's discounted cash outlay.

### Cost Estimate Caution

UAP Document 401-402, Article 9 states that the Architect does not guarantee estimates because the Architect has no control over labor and material costs or competitive bidding. This supports a careful statement: the Architect may prepare a Statement of Probable Project Construction Cost, but the estimate is not a guaranteed final bid or final cost.

Do not memorize unsupported accuracy bands for this topic unless a source is supplied. The source available for this batch supports the non-guarantee principle, not fixed percentage accuracy ranges by phase.

### Real-World Scenario

Suppose an architect in Iloilo is preparing a school building agreement. The Owner gives a total project budget that includes land acquisition, survey work, consultant coordination, and an allowance for future furniture. For LEA purposes, the architect should not treat that entire budget as PCC. The architect first identifies the cost of the completed building and the designed systems: structure, electrical works, plumbing, fire protection, air-conditioning if included, and site development elements shown in the plans. Then the architect removes the professional fees of the Architect, Engineer, and Specialist Consultants from the PCC base. If the Owner says a supplier will donate some fixtures, the architect still checks the current fair market value rule because SPP 202 does not let an artificially low purchase price distort PCC. This is exactly the professional habit the board wants: classify the cost before calculating the fee.

The source-safe exam move is to separate three buckets before computing: PCC, excluded professional or inspector costs, and broader project development cost. PCC is the cost of the completed building and designed systems. Project development cost can be wider because it includes construction plus professional fees, permits, utilities, land, financing, and similar owner-side items. A question that mixes these buckets is testing classification before arithmetic.

### Exam Traps

- Do not include the architect's fee in PCC.
- Do not cite RA 9266 IRR Section 22 for compensation; that section is about refusal to issue registration/PIC, not fee computation.
- Do not use unsupported phase accuracy bands as if they came from SPP 202.
- Do not confuse total project budget with PCC.
