# The distribution hierarchy

Electrical distribution is easiest to audit when it is read from source to load. The checked PEC and Electrical Layout and Estimate sources support the same sequence: service, service equipment or main, feeders, panelboards or load centers, branch circuits, and outlets or equipment.

## Source-to-load sequence

The service is the conductors and equipment used to deliver energy from the serving utility to the wiring system of the premises served. Service equipment is the necessary equipment near the service entrance that acts as the main control and cutoff of the supply.

A feeder runs between the service equipment or other source and the final branch-circuit overcurrent device. A branch circuit is the circuit conductors between the final overcurrent device protecting the circuit and the outlets.

In plan review, that gives this hierarchy:

1. Service drop, service lateral, or service entrance.
2. Service equipment, meter, main disconnect, and main overcurrent protection.
3. Feeders and subfeeders to distribution equipment.
4. Panelboards, load centers, or similar distribution equipment.
5. Branch circuits protected by final overcurrent devices.
6. Outlets, luminaires, appliances, motors, and utilization equipment.

## What drawings must prove

The checked PEC plan provisions require the electrical documents to identify service data, service equipment, wiring methods, grounding, main disconnecting means, overcurrent protection, branch-circuit wiring, complete circuits, load schedule, and motor loads where present. The one-line diagram provisions require feeders and subfeeders to be identified with conductor size, raceway, overcurrent protection, control, and grounding information.

A source-safe distribution answer should therefore ask where the conductor is in the hierarchy. Conductors before the final branch-circuit overcurrent device are feeder or service-side conductors depending on their source and destination. Conductors after the final branch-circuit overcurrent device are branch-circuit conductors.

## Load, voltage drop, and protection

The checked PEC provisions require enough branch circuits to serve the calculated load and require branch circuits to stay within their maximum loads. Feeder ampacity is tied to noncontinuous load plus 125 percent of continuous load, and the checked informational notes discuss voltage drop targets for branch and feeder conductors.

## Exam-safe method

Trace the path from utility or source to the final outlet. Name the conductor set by its endpoints, not by its physical size. Then check overcurrent protection, grounding, load schedule, and voltage drop. This prevents common exam errors, such as calling a feeder a branch circuit just because it is downstream of the meter.
