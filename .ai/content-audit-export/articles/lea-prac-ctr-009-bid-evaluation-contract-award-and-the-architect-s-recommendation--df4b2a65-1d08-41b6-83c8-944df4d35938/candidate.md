## Bid Evaluation, Contract Award, and the Architect's Recommendation

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Describe the bid evaluation process and the architect's recommendation role.
- Explain criteria for contract award: lowest responsive bidder.
- Distinguish bid evaluation from bid shopping.
- Identify the legal effect of contract award on the parties.

### The Bid Evaluation Process

The architect plays a central role in assessing all submitted proposals against the criteria outlined in the tender documents. This evaluation examines not only the financial offer but also the contractor's qualifications, proposed methodology, timeline, and compliance with contract specifications.

<LawCallout code="ra9266" section="UAP Doc 301, SPP Doc 202" title="Bid Evaluation Criteria">
The architect evaluates bids on: (1) Technical compliance — does the contractor understand and can they execute the work? (2) Financial compliance — is the bid calculated correctly and responsive? (3) Qualifications — experience, equipment, workforce, safety record. Non-responsive bids (those failing mandatory requirements) are rejected. The architect must establish the evaluation framework BEFORE bids are opened.
</LawCallout>

### The Architect's Recommendation Authority

Once evaluation is complete, the architect issues a formal recommendation to the owner recommending the most advantageous proposal. This recommendation must be based on the established evaluation criteria and documented in writing. The architect may recommend the lowest bidder, but if circumstances warrant selection of a higher-priced proposal, the architect must justify this decision clearly.

The owner retains final decision-making authority on contract award. The owner may accept the architect's recommendation, request further analysis, or make an independent decision.

<ProcessFlow>
  **Bid Evaluation and Award Process:**
  Step 1: **Pre-evaluation** — Architect confirms evaluation criteria before bids opened
  Step 2: **Bid Opening** — Sealed bids opened at public/private meeting
  Step 3: **Preliminary Review** — Check for compliance with Instructions to Bidders
  Step 4: **Technical Evaluation** — Assess qualifications, methodology, schedule
  Step 5: **Financial Evaluation** — Verify arithmetic, confirm bid bond, check responsiveness
  Step 6: **Architect's Recommendation** — Written report to Owner
  Step 7: **Owner's Decision** — Accept, reject, or request further analysis
  Step 8: **Notice of Award** — Issued to selected contractor
  Step 9: **Contract Execution** — Agreement signed, bonds posted, work commences
</ProcessFlow>

<Warn>
  **Critical Exam Point:** All communications regarding bid evaluation, scoring, and the recommendation must remain CONFIDENTIAL until the contract is formally awarded. Disclosure of bid values, technical scores, or the architect's recommendation before award creates serious legal and professional liability.
</Warn>

### Documentation and Transparency

The bid evaluation process must be documented thoroughly. The architect maintains records of all submitted proposals, the evaluation methodology, scoring or ranking analysis, and the basis for the recommendation. These records protect both the architect and owner by demonstrating compliance with fair procurement practices and providing evidence if any bidder challenges the award decision.

<ExamTip>
  **LEA Priority — Know These Rules:**
  1. Evaluation criteria must be established BEFORE bids are opened (not after)
  2. Non-responsive bids are rejected (not accepted with conditions)
  3. Architect recommends — Owner DECIDES (Owner has final authority)
  4. All evaluation communications are confidential until award
  5. If recommending a non-lowest bidder, the architect must document justification in writing
</ExamTip>
