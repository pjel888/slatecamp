## Bid Evaluation, Contract Award, and the Architect's Recommendation


Evaluating bids and making the contract award recommendation are critical architect functions that require strict adherence to prescribed procedures and transparency throughout the process.


### The Bid Evaluation Process

The architect plays a central role in assessing all submitted proposals against the criteria outlined in the tender documents. This evaluation examines not only the financial offer but also the contractor's qualifications, proposed methodology, timeline, and compliance with contract specifications. The architect must establish a clear evaluation framework before bids are opened, ensuring consistency and fairness across all submissions.

Technical evaluation focuses on whether each proposal demonstrates the contractor's capacity to execute the work as described. The architect reviews the contractor's experience, equipment, workforce, and proposed construction methods. Financial evaluation ensures all bids are calculated correctly and are responsive to the contract requirements. Non-responsive bids—those that fail to meet mandatory requirements—are typically rejected during this phase.

### The Architect's Recommendation Authority

Once evaluation is complete, the architect issues a formal recommendation to the owner recommending the most advantageous proposal. This recommendation must be based on the established evaluation criteria and documented in writing. The architect may recommend the lowest bidder, but if circumstances warrant selection of a higher-priced proposal, the architect must justify this decision clearly to the owner.

The owner retains final decision-making authority on contract award. The owner may accept the architect's recommendation, request further analysis, or make an independent decision. However, once the owner accepts the architect's recommendation, the owner typically issues a notification of award to the selected contractor. This notification formally communicates the owner's intention to execute the contract with the recommended bidder.

### Documentation and Transparency

The bid evaluation process must be documented thoroughly. The architect maintains records of all submitted proposals, the evaluation methodology, scoring or ranking analysis, and the basis for the recommendation. These records protect both the architect and owner by demonstrating compliance with fair procurement practices and providing evidence if any bidder challenges the award decision.

All communications regarding bid evaluation, scoring, and the recommendation must remain confidential until the contract is formally awarded. Disclosure of bid values, technical scores, or the architect's recommendation before award creates serious legal and professional liability. Once award is announced publicly, the architect may discuss the evaluation process with unsuccessful bidders in accordance with professional standards.
