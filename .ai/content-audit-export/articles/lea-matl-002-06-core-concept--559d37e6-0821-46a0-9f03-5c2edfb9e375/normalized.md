## Core Concept

Admixtures may be added to a concrete mix to alter the properties of the fresh mix or the hardened concrete. Salvan lists reasons for using concrete additives: speed up or retard the initial set, improve workability, reduce bleeding, improve watertightness, prevent evaporation from newly placed concrete, and help develop the potential strength of the water-cement paste.

Accelerators hasten setting and early strength development. Retarders delay or extend setting time, which is useful when heat accelerates hydration and reduces the time available for placing, consolidating, and finishing concrete.

Air-entraining agents disperse microscopic air bubbles. The checked sources tie them to workability, freeze-thaw or scaling resistance, reduced bleeding, and capillary interruption.

Water-reducing agents or superplasticizers reduce the amount of mixing water needed for a desired workability. Lowering the water-cement ratio in this way generally increases strength. Salvan explains the reason: excess water can bleed, leave voids after evaporation, decrease strength, increase permeability, and weaken cement paste.

Surface-active agents reduce the surface tension of mixing water, helping wetting, penetration, emulsifying, or dispersion of other additives. Surface sealing agents can prevent evaporation so hydration moisture is retained.

## How It Is Tested

Expect function-matching items: accelerator, retarder, air-entraining agent, water reducer, surface-active agent, and sealing agent. If a question needs a code prohibition, ASTM type letter, dosage, or proprietary product, use the item-supplied source rather than inventing it.

## Common Mistakes

Do not assume every admixture improves every property. Do not use a water reducer as a substitute for curing. Do not answer a source-checked function question with external code language that is not in the checked local source.
