# Inca Architecture

## LEA Point

Inca architecture is best recognized by precise mortarless stonework, terraces integrated with terrain, and infrastructure that organized the Andean empire.

## Source Anchors

- Machu Picchu: UNESCO anchors the site at 2,430 m with walls, terraces, ramps, and a mountain setting integrated into the design.
- Cuzco: source-safe anchor for Inca imperial urban and ceremonial context.
- Qhapaq Nan: UNESCO anchors the Andean road system at about 30,000 km across multiple Andean regions.
- Masonry: polygonal ashlar and tight mortarless fitting remain the exam-safe vocabulary.
- Attribution guardrail: Tiwanaku and Wari built important Andean stone architecture before the Inca, so do not assign every ancient Andean stone site to the Inca.

## Exam Pattern

If the clue emphasizes knife-tight stone joints, polygonal ashlar, seismic movement, terraces, Machu Picchu, Cuzco, or Qhapaq Nan, the answer is usually Inca. If the clue emphasizes corbelled vaults or stelae, it belongs to the Maya cluster instead.

## Guardrails

- Do not rely on a single earthquake anecdote to prove Inca engineering. Use the general principle: interlocking, mortarless stones can move and settle without brittle mortar failure.
- Do not repeat 40,000 km or high-speed relay figures for Qhapaq Nan in this batch. Use about 30,000 km and administrative integration.
- Do not confuse terraces with ceremonial plazas; Inca terraces combine agriculture, slope control, and landscape engineering.

## Real World Scenario

On a site walk, a wall with tight polygonal stones and no mortar tells you about Inca construction technique. A road, tambo, or route network clue tells you about imperial administration and movement across difficult terrain.
