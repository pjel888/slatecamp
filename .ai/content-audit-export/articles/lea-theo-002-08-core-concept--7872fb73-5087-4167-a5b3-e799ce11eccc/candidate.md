# Scale in Architecture by Francis D.K. Ching

## Source-Audited Scope

Batch 055 treats scale as a relative-size and perception concept within the checked theory source families. The checked source states that while length, width, and depth determine proportions, scale is determined by size relative to other forms in context.

## Board Exam Quick Check

When the LEA stem asks about size relative to context, answer scale. When the stem asks about length-width-depth relationships within the form itself, answer proportion.

## Core Concepts

- A form's real dimensions are length, width, and depth.
- Those dimensions determine proportion.
- Scale is determined by size relative to other forms in context.
- Continuity and scale of a path can distinguish major routes from secondary paths.
- The form and scale of circulation space must accommodate movement, pausing, resting, and viewing.
- The checked TOA source lists scale and proportion among the qualities of architectural space, structure, and enclosure.

## LEA Use

Use scale when comparing a form, route, or space to its surroundings or to expected movement. Use proportion when comparing dimensions within the same form.

## Active Recall

1. What determines scale in the checked source? Size relative to other forms in context.
2. What do length, width, and depth determine? Proportion.
3. How can path scale help circulation reading? It distinguishes major and secondary routes.
