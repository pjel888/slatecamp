# Sanitation and Water Supply

For LEA code questions, sanitation and water supply are not separate memory islands. PD 1096 Rule IX ties building occupancy to potable water, plumbing installation, wastewater disposal, storm drainage, vermin control, noise control, and public-health protection.

## Section 901 Baseline

Section 901 of the 2004 Revised IRR requires buildings to have:

- Adequate and potable water supply.
- Sanitary plumbing installation.
- Suitable wastewater treatment or disposal system.
- Storm-water drainage.
- Pest and vermin control.
- Noise abatement.
- Other measures needed for health and safety protection.

This is the cleanest exam frame: a building is not code-ready just because rooms and exits are drawn. The utility and sanitation systems must also be code-compliant.

## Water Supply

Section 902 requires a building intended for human habitation or occupancy to be served by a municipal or city waterworks system when one is available. When water is taken from meteoric, surface, or underground sources, the supply must conform to the National Standards for Drinking Water. Deepwells and natural groundwater extraction remain subject to the Water Code.

The same section routes water piping, wastewater drainage, and proper venting to the Revised National Plumbing Code. This is why LEA questions often connect PD 1096 sanitation rules with RNPC sizing, traps, vents, and inspection requirements.

## Wastewater and Storm Drainage

Section 903 sends wastewater to the nearest sanitary sewer main where one exists. Where no sanitary sewer main exists, wastewater may be handled through an Imhoff tank, septic tank with subsurface absorption, or another suitable treatment plant, subject to the Code on Sanitation and RNPC requirements.

Section 904 keeps storm drainage separate from sanitary sewer discharge. It also requires adequate storm-drainage provisions for low areas, subject to RNPC drainage and sewerage requirements.

## Exam Use

When a question asks what governs a sanitation or water-supply condition, identify the function first. Potable water begins with PD 1096 Section 902 and drinking-water standards. Wastewater begins with Section 903, the Code on Sanitation, DENR rules where applicable, and RNPC. Storm drainage begins with Section 904 and must not be routed into a sanitary sewer.
