# Retention Money: Standard Rates, Purpose, and Release

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- State how retention is deducted under the checked CIAP and UAP contract sources.
- Explain when no further retention is made on later progress billings.
- Separate final payment, retention release, lien protection, and guarantee-bond posting.
- Avoid mixing CIAP Document 102 and UAP Document 301 release wording without naming the source.

## What Retention Is

Retention money is not an architect's fee, a permit charge, or a separate bond. It is an amount withheld from the contractor's progress payment. CIAP Document 102 states that each request for payment is computed from completed work, less a retention of ten percent of the progress payment to the contractor.

UAP Document 301 uses similar progress-payment language: each request for payment is based on completed work, less ten percent retention unless otherwise agreed, and less previous payments.

## When Further Retention Stops

Both checked contract sources use the same practical stopping point. When fifty percent of the contract has been accomplished, no further retention is made on progress billings for the balance of the work. This does not mean the retention already withheld is automatically released; it means additional retention stops being deducted from later progress billings.

## Release Is a Close-Out Control

CIAP Document 102 separates final payment from retention money. It defines final payment as the final progress billing and approved claims, but states that final payment does not include retention money.

For retention release, CIAP Document 102 ties the retained amount to the expiration of the Period of Making Good of Known Defects and the contractor's posting of the guarantee bond. Its time-limit table also summarizes release of retention as not later than sixty days from substantial completion upon posting of the contractor's guarantee bond.

## Lien and Supplier Protection

Do not treat retention release as a purely arithmetic step. CIAP Document 102 makes final payment and release of retention subject to lien protection. If subcontractor or supplier claims remain unpaid, the contractor must provide the required sworn statement, receipts, acknowledgments, or an indemnity bond acceptable to the owner.

## UAP Document 301 Boundary

UAP Document 301 states that retention is released within three months after the date of final payment. That is a different release formulation from the CIAP Document 102 wording. For LEA questions, name the governing document before applying the time rule.

## Real World Scenario

A contractor asks for full retention release after the owner starts using the building, but unpaid supplier claims remain and the guarantee bond has not been posted. The source-safe answer is to keep the request tied to the governing contract: CIAP Document 102 connects release of retention to the Period of Making Good of Known Defects, posting of the guarantee bond, and lien protection. Early occupancy or substantial completion does not by itself erase those close-out conditions.

## Exam Boundary

If the question says CIAP Document 102, use ten percent retention on progress payments, stop further retention after fifty percent accomplishment, and check the guarantee-bond and lien-release conditions before release. If it says UAP Document 301, use the UAP release wording instead of importing the CIAP time-limit table.
