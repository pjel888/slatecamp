# Excavation Foundation and Veneer

## Board Exam Quick Check

For PD 1096 excavation questions, first identify whether the issue is adjoining-property protection, public-property restoration, trench safety, retaining walls, or veneer anchorage. The controlling local source for this article is the 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096, Rule XII Sections 1202 and 1203.

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to apply the Rule XII requirements for excavations, fills, foundations, retaining walls, and veneer attachments without importing unsupported foreign-code or practice claims.

## Source-Grounded Core Rules

### Excavation and adjoining property

Section 1202 makes the person undertaking or causing the excavation responsible for protecting life, property, and adjoining structures. If the excavation affects the lateral or subjacent support of adjoining property or an existing structure, the excavating party must pay for underpinning or for extending the affected foundation or footing.

Before excavation begins, the excavating party must notify adjoining building owners in writing at least 10 days before the work. The adjoining owner must be given access to verify protection, and the excavating party must also be given reasonable access to inspect adjoining property before and during excavation. If the adjoining owner refuses the necessary access, the duty to protect that adjoining building shifts to the refusing party, and the excavating party is not responsible for damage caused by that refusal.

A careful survey of existing cracks in adjoining premises should be made before work begins. Photographs and telltales are source-supported ways to document and monitor movement during demolition or excavation.

### Slope, drainage, lining, and trench safety

Permanent cut slopes and permanent fill slopes must not be steeper than 2 horizontal to 1 vertical. A steeper slope is allowed only when supported by a geotechnical or geological investigation report acceptable to the Building Official.

Excavation disturbances on public property must be restored to their former condition within 48 hours from the start of the excavation or disturbance, unless the Building Official excludes the work from that requirement.

Excavations should be lined with shotcrete, boards, runners, or sheet piles when needed to prevent dangerous movement or cave-in. Linings and framings should be inserted as excavation proceeds and tightened by wedging or jacking.

Every trench 1.50 meters or deeper must have a suitable means of exit or escape at least every 7.50 meters of trench length.

Where water is encountered, the code supports drainage and pumping measures, including intercepting drains and weepholes at the lowest one-third of the excavation wall where groundwater head must be relieved.

### Footings, foundations, and retaining walls

Footings and foundations must be appropriate in type, adequate in size, and adequate in capacity to sustain superimposed loads under seismic or other external forces that affect safety or stability. The architect and/or engineer is responsible for adopting the type and design in accordance with the standards set by the Secretary.

Retaining walls are required where abrupt changes in ground level or foundation level could create soil instability. The retaining wall must be of adequate design and type of construction as prescribed by the Secretary.

No fill or surcharge load may be placed beside an existing building or structure unless that building or structure can withstand the additional load.

### Veneer

Section 1203 defines veneer as a nonstructural facing of brick, concrete, tile, metal, plastic, glass, or similar approved material attached to a backing or structural component for ornamentation, protection, or enclosure.

Veneer must not support any load other than its own weight and the vertical dead load of veneer immediately above it. The backing surface must be designed to support the added vertical and lateral loads from the veneer.

The design must account for differential movement caused by temperature changes, shrinkage, creep, and deflection. Adhered veneer and backing must resist shearing stresses, including seismic effects. Anchored veneer and its attachment must resist horizontal forces equal to twice the weight of the veneer. Anchors, supports, and ties must be non-combustible and corrosion-resistant.

## Real World Scenario

A project team is preparing a small commercial basement excavation beside an older neighboring residence. Before work starts, the architect flags the PD 1096 checks that must be settled before the contractor opens the site: written notice to the adjoining owner at least 10 days before excavation, access for checking protection of the adjoining property, a crack survey with photographs or telltales where needed, and support or underpinning for any affected footings or foundations.

During execution, the same checklist controls site observations. Public-property disturbance must be restored within the required 48-hour window unless excluded by the Building Official, a 1.50 m-deep trench needs suitable exit or escape points at least every 7.50 m, and a steeper-than-2H:1V permanent cut needs an acceptable geotechnical or geological report. When the facade veneer is later reviewed, it is checked as a nonstructural facing: it may carry only its own weight and the vertical dead load immediately above, while the backing and anchors must resist the required vertical, lateral, seismic, and corrosion-resistance demands.

## Exam Values To Memorize

| Requirement | Source-grounded value |
|---|---:|
| Written notice before excavation affecting adjoining buildings | At least 10 days |
| Public-property restoration after excavation disturbance | Within 48 hours from start, unless excluded by Building Official |
| Permanent cut/fill slope limit | Not steeper than 2H:1V unless supported by acceptable geotechnical/geological report |
| Trench depth that triggers exits | 1.50 m or deeper |
| Trench exit spacing | At least every 7.50 m |
| Anchored veneer horizontal design force | Equal to 2 times the weight of the veneer |

## Common Exam Traps

- Do not shift the underpinning cost to the neighbor unless the neighbor refused access after the required written notice and access request.
- Do not treat 2H:1V as merely a suggestion. A steeper permanent slope requires an acceptable geotechnical or geological report.
- Do not confuse adhered veneer with anchored veneer. The explicit 2-times-horizontal-force rule is for anchored veneer and its attachment.
- Do not say veneer carries structural building loads. It supports only its own weight and the vertical dead load of veneer immediately above it.
