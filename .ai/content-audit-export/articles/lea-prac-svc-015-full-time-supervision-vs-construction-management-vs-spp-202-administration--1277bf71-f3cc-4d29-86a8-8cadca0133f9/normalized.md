## Board Exam Quick Check

Do not collapse every construction-period service into one label. The UAP Standards distinguish regular design services under SPP 202, full-time supervision under SPP 204-A, and construction management under SPP 204-B. Each has a different service boundary and compensation logic.

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to:

- Identify the six regular design service phases listed for SPP 202.
- Separate full-time supervision from the construction phase of regular design services.
- Recognize construction management as a separate service category under SPP 204-B.
- Explain why project representatives assist the architect without replacing contractor responsibility.

## SPP 202 Regular Design Services

The UAP Standards list six phases for the architect's regular design services: project definition, schematic design, design development, contract documents, bidding or negotiation, and construction. The construction phase in regular design services is part of the architect's overall design-administration involvement.

That does not mean the architect is automatically providing full-time site supervision or construction management. The exam distinction is service scope. SPP 202 is the regular design service framework. Additional construction-stage services are classified separately when the engagement requires them.

## SPP 204-A Full-Time Supervision

Full-time supervision is treated as a separate construction service. The checked SPP 204-A source describes scope items that include quality control, evaluation of construction work, daily inspection reports, and filing documents. The UAP Standards also list compensation methods for full-time supervision separately from regular design services.

UAP Document 301 helps define the site-administration boundary. Project representatives, resident architects, resident engineers, or construction inspectors may assist the architect and engineers in the general supervision and direction of the work. If disputes arise with the contractor, they are referred to the architect. This assistance does not relieve the contractor from performing the work in accordance with the drawings, specifications, and other Contract Documents.

## SPP 204-B Construction Management

Construction management is also treated as a separate construction service. The UAP Standards explain that project management and construction management roles involve organizing, monitoring, and advising on the project team, cost, time, quality, and methods. When the project manager also performs construction management, the services under SPP 204-A and SPP 204-B apply.

The source also says that when the architect as project manager performs regular design services under SPP 202, compensation for project management services is in addition to the architect's regular compensation for basic services.

## Exam Distinction

Use this comparison:

- SPP 202: regular design services across the listed phases, including the construction phase of design administration.
- SPP 204-A: full-time supervision as a separate construction-stage service, with inspection/reporting and quality-control tasks.
- SPP 204-B: construction management as a separate management service involving broader coordination, cost, schedule, quality, and method concerns.

## How It Is Tested

Expect client-communication questions, service proposal questions, and liability-boundary questions. The safest answer names the service category, checks whether the role is regular design administration, full-time supervision, or construction management, and keeps contractor responsibility intact unless a source says otherwise.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating SPP 202 construction phase as automatic full-time site supervision.
- Treating construction management as merely another name for full-time inspection.
- Forgetting that separate services can require separate compensation.
- Treating resident staff assistance as a transfer of contractor responsibility.

## Real World Scenario

A building owner asks the architect to add daily site presence to a regular design-service proposal. A source-safe answer first names the base SPP 202 construction phase, then checks whether the owner is actually asking for full-time supervision under SPP 204-A or broader construction management under SPP 204-B. If the owner stations project representatives, resident architects, resident engineers, or construction inspectors on site, UAP Document 301 keeps them as assistants to the architect and engineers; it does not transfer the contractor's duty to perform the work according to the drawings, specifications, and other Contract Documents.
