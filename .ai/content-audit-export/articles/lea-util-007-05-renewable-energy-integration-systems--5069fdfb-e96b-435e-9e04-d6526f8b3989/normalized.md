## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal

Integrate renewable energy and alternative energy systems (solar PV, solar thermal, wind, biomass, geothermal heat pumps) into building design, and apply BERDE/LEED credit requirements for on-site renewable energy generation in Philippine building projects.
## Renewable Energy Integration Systems

### Solar Photovoltaic (PV) Systems

Solar installations on Philippine buildings leverage the country's average annual solar irradiance of 4.5-5.5 kWh/sqm/day across major regions (Department of Energy technical studies). Building-integrated photovoltaic (BIPV) systems combine energy generation with structural elements through solar canopies, glazing integration, and facade-mounted arrays. System sizing follows load analysis protocols: 1 kWp installation typically generates 4.0-4.5 kWh daily in tropical regions, reducing grid consumption by 30-50% in net-metering applications.

Inverter technology selection impacts efficiency: string inverters achieve 96-98% efficiency for larger installations, while microinverters (97-99% efficiency) prove cost-effective for partial shading scenarios common in dense urban environments.

### Wind Energy Considerations

While large-scale wind development concentrates in northern Luzon coastal zones (Ilocos and Pangasinan provinces), building-scale vertical-axis wind turbines (VAWT) address urban applications. VAWT units rated 5-15 kW suit rooftop installation with lower noise profiles (43-50 dB at 10m) compared to horizontal-axis designs.

## Water Conservation and Recycling Systems

### Rainwater Harvesting Integration

_Cross-reference LEA-UTIL-001-09 for foundational concepts._

Rainwater harvesting systems capture roof runoff for non-potable end uses. Collection potential in Metro Manila reaches 1,400-1,600 mm annual precipitation (Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration). System design calculation:

Monthly yield (m³) = Catchment area (m²) × Monthly rainfall (m) × Collection efficiency (0.80-0.85)

A 500 sqm roof in Metro Manila yields approximately 560-680 m³ annually for toilet flushing, landscape irrigation, and cleaning applications. Tank sizing follows 10-20% of annual yield, with standard practice allocating 30-40% of monthly demand for storage capacity in Philippine climatic conditions.

### Greywater Recycling Systems

Greywater—wastewater from lavatories, showers, and washing machines (excluding toilet discharge)—comprises 50-60% of typical building wastewater. Treatment pathways include:

_Sand filtration with activated carbon_: 90-95% suspended solid removal, 10-15 micron final filtration, suitable for landscape irrigation and non-potable indoor use following RA 11285 requirements for water conservation labeling.

_Multi-stage biological treatment_: 15-30 day retention time, achieving 98% BOD reduction for toilet flushing and cooling tower makeup water in commercial applications.

Dual plumbing infrastructure costs 8,000-12,000 PHP/unit in residential projects but recovers investment within 7-10 years through water supply reduction of 30-40%. No such mandate exists in the Building Code of the Philippines (PD 1096). Greywater systems may be encouraged under Philippine Green Building Code water efficiency provisions.

## Energy Conservation Measures (ECMs) for Buildings

### Passive Design Strategies

_Natural ventilation_ reduces mechanical cooling loads through stack effect and cross-ventilation. Philippine building standards require openable windows on 80% of occupied floor area, with solar-aligned orientation minimizing afternoon heat gain. Natural ventilation effectiveness increases 25-35% through thermal mass integration—concrete structures absorbing daytime heat and releasing during cooler nighttime periods.

_Daylighting integration_ achieves lighting energy reduction of 40-60% in perimeter zones through window placement optimization (target: 300-500 lux maintained illumination within 7.5m of windows). Light shelves and diffuse glazing distribute direct tropical sunlight while reducing glare and cooling demand.

_Thermal mass application_ in Philippine construction uses 150-200mm concrete slabs and wall systems with time-lag performance of 8-12 hours, moderating diurnal temperature swings of 5-8°C in tropical climates.

_External shading_ through fixed or operable systems reduces solar heat gain coefficient (SHGC) by 40-70%. Louver depths of 500-750mm oriented perpendicular to solar noon azimuth block 60-80% of direct radiation while maintaining daylighting penetration.

### Building Envelope PerformancePhilippine Green Building Code (PGBC) establishes OTTV limits per climate zone and occupancy; typical non-residential OTTV ≤45-55 W/sqm depending on zone.OTTV calculation integrates fenestration (windows, skylights), opaque surfaces (walls, roofs), and thermal bridging:

OTTV (W/sqm) = [αₛ × Ac × (SHGC) + U × Aw × ΔT] / A_total

where:
- αₛ = solar absorptance (0.4 for light colors, 0.9 for dark finishes)
- Ac = opaque surface area (sqm)
- U-values: window assemblies 2.0-3.2 W/sqm·K, walls 0.35-0.50 W/sqm·K
- ΔT = temperature differential (20-25°C in tropical regions)
- A_total = total building facade area

Meeting OTTV ≤45 W/sqm requires envelope optimization through:

1. High-performance glazing (double-pane, low-e coatings): U-value 1.8-2.2 W/sqm·K, SHGC 0.25-0.35
2. Insulated wall systems (100-150mm mineral wool): U-value 0.35-0.45 W/sqm·K
3. Cool roof coatings (reflectance ≥0.65, emittance ≥0.75): reducing surface temperature 15-20°C

A typical 10,000 sqm office building achieves OTTV compliance through window-to-wall ratio optimization (35-40%) combined with exterior shading and high-performance glazing, reducing annual cooling demand by 25-35% compared to baseline envelope.

### Air Leakage Control

Building airtightness measured as air changes per hour (ACH) at 50 Pa pressure differentials targets ≤3 ACH for conditioned buildings. sealed construction reduces infiltration losses 40-50%, critical in tropical climates where humidity infiltration drives dehumidification load.

## Waste Heat Recovery

Combined heat and power (CHP) or cogeneration systems, prevalent in Metro Manila hospitals and commercial facilities, capture exhaust heat for domestic water heating or absorption cooling. Cogeneration heat recovery effectiveness:

- Exhaust heat: 400-550°C from reciprocating engines, 500-650°C from gas turbines
- Recovery efficiency: 40-60% of input fuel energy (compared to 30% for generation alone)
- Payback period: 4-7 years in continuous-operation facilities

Trigeneration (CHP + absorption chiller) supplies cooling, heating, and power simultaneously, achieving overall efficiency of 70-85% versus conventional systems at 45-55% combined efficiency.

## Advanced Building Systems

### District Cooling Networks

Large-scale district cooling systems serve multiple buildings from centralized plants, reducing per-unit refrigeration capital cost by 25-35%. System design uses chilled water distribution at 6-8°C supply, 12-14°C return temperatures. Piping insulation (50-75mm polyurethane) minimizes thermal loss to ≤5% over distribution distances of 500-1,500m in dense urban cores.

The Makati CBD and Bonifacio Global City (BGC) employ district systems serving 50,000-80,000 sqm building floor areas, reducing peak electrical demand 15-20% through load shifting and thermal storage.

### Building-Integrated Storage Systems

Thermal energy storage (TES) through phase-change materials (PCM) or chilled water tanks stores off-peak cooling, reducing daytime mechanical system capacity and operating costs. A 100-ton chilled water storage system sized for 4-hour discharge cycle reduces peak cooling plant capacity by 30-40%, lowering demand charges 20,000-35,000 PHP monthly in large commercial buildings.

## Green Building Rating and Certification Systems

### BERDE (Building for Ecologically Responsive Design Excellence)BERDE (current version by Philippine Green Building Council) uses credit categories including site, water, energy; verify latest point allocations from official PGBC.nts (emphasis on recycled water, water-efficient fixtures)
- Energy efficiency: 24 points (OTTV compliance, renewable integration, ECM implementation)
- Materials and waste: 15 points (embodied carbon, construction waste diversion)
- Indoor environmental quality: 15 points (daylighting, natural ventilation, acoustic performance)
- Management and operations: 6 points (system commissioning, staff training)

Buildings achieving ≥72% of available points earn BERDE Gold certification. Green-certified buildings demonstrate 20-30% lower operational water consumption and 25-35% reduced energy use compared to baseline buildings meeting code minimum standards.

### LEED and EDGE Certification Integration

_LEED (Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design)_ provides international benchmarking applicable to Philippine projects through its v4.1 framework emphasizing:

- Water efficiency credits: 11 points available through low-flow fixtures (goal: ≤4.0 GPM lavatory faucets, ≤1.3 GPF toilets)
- Energy and atmosphere: 33 points including on-site renewable generation and envelope performance

_EDGE (Excellence in Design for Greater Efficiencies)_ focuses on 20-30% operational improvement across energy, water, and waste. Modular approach suits Filipino mid-market commercial development.

Dual certification pursuing both BERDE and LEED maximizes performance validation; buildings simultaneously achieving both certifications report 35-45% total operational cost reduction over 10-year lifecycle.

## Water Conservation Technologies

### Low-Flow Fixtures and Controls

Philippine Standard PS 1008:2015 establishes water conservation performance requirements:

- Lavatory faucets: ≤4.0 L/min (vs. conventional 12-15 L/min)
- Toilet tanks: ≤4.8 L/flush for dual-flush models (3.0 L yellow/4.8 L brown)
- Showerheads: ≤8.3 L/min (vs. conventional 19-24 L/min)

Implementing low-flow fixtures across 500-occupant commercial facilities reduces water consumption 35-45%, translating to savings of 2.5 million-3.2 million pesos annually at Metro Manila water rates of 22.50-27.00 PHP/cubic meter (MAYNILAD and Manila Water Company tariffs, 2025).

### Water-Efficient Landscaping

Xeriscaping—native species selection, drip irrigation, and mulch application—reduces irrigation water demand by 50-70%. Native Philippine plants (Monstera deliciosa, Calliandra haematocephala, native Ficus species) require minimal supplemental watering after establishment (12-18 months). Turf reduction strategies replacing 40-60% of lawn area with hardscape or native groundcover avoid 80,000-120,000 liters of annual irrigation water per 2,000 sqm landscaped area.

## RA 11285 (Energy Efficiency and Conservation Act) Implementation Requirements

RA 11285, effective 2019, mandates specific performance requirements for new buildings and substantial renovations:

1. **Energy Audit Requirements**: Buildings ≥5,000 sqm must conduct baseline energy audits and implement energy management systems within 18 months of regulation effective date.

2. **Building Performance Standards**: RA 11285 does not specify numerical energy intensity targets like 75 kWh/sqm/year or annual improvement trajectories for buildings.

3. **Equipment Labeling and Minimum Performance Standards (MEPS)**: Air conditioning units must meet or exceed DOE MEPS standards—minimum COP 3.5 for window units, 4.0 for ducted systems at 35°C outdoor design condition.

4. **Renewable Energy Integration Targets**: RA 11285 does not mandate 25% renewable energy for facilities by 2030; refer to RA 9513 for national RE targets.

5. **Water Conservation Mandates**: RA 11285 does not mandate building water meters or specific per-occupant water baselines; water conservation addressed in PD 1096 and PGBC.

## Integration Framework: Holistic System Design

Optimal sustainability performance emerges through integrated design addressing passive measures, active systems, and operational protocols simultaneously. A 25,000 sqm Metro Manila office development case study demonstrates:

| System Component | Performance Target | Implementation Cost | Annual Savings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Building envelope (OTTV optimization) | ≤45 W/sqm | 45,000-55,000 PHP/sqm | 8,500-12,000 PHP/sqm |
| Solar PV system (150 kWp) | 600-675 MWh/year | 8,500-10,500 PHP/Wp | 4,200-5,400 PHP/kWh/year |
| Rainwater + greywater recycling | 50% non-potable demand | 180-220 PHP/sqm | 0.80-1.20 PHP/cubic meter |
| Natural ventilation + thermal mass | 25-35% HVAC load reduction | 120-160 PHP/sqm | 2,100-3,200 PHP/sqm |
| Chilled water storage (100-ton) | 30-40% peak reduction | 35,000-45,000 PHP/ton | 800,000-1,200,000 PHP/year |

Total capital cost: 11.2-14.8 million PHP. Combined annual operational savings: 13.5-22.2 million PHP. Simple payback: 8-10 months (excluding initial equipment costs). RA 11285 compliance status: _Full compliance with renewable integration, water conservation, and building performance standards._
