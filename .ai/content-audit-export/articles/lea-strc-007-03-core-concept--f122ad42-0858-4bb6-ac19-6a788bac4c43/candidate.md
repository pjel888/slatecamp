## Board Exam Quick Check

Serviceability questions are not strength questions. First identify whether the item asks for immediate deflection, long-term deflection, nonstructural damage control, or calculation method.

## Learning Goal

By the end of this article, you will be able to:

- Explain what NSCP Section 424 covers for serviceability.
- Use Table 424.2.2 as the source for permissible calculated deflection limits.
- Identify the calculation issues that affect immediate and time-dependent deflection.
- Avoid replacing explicit serviceability checks with memorized span-to-depth shortcuts.

## Core Concept

NSCP 2015 Section 424 is the serviceability section for structural concrete. Section 424.2 focuses on deflections due to service-level gravity loads. The controlling idea is simple: flexural members must have enough stiffness to limit deflections or deformations that adversely affect strength or serviceability.

Section 424.2.2 points designers to Table 424.2.2 for maximum permissible calculated deflections. The readable OCR anchors support these limits and checks:

| Situation | Deflection issue to check |
| --- | --- |
| Floors not supporting or attached to nonstructural elements likely damaged by large deflections | Immediate deflection due to live load, limited to l/360 |
| Roofs or floors supporting or attached to nonstructural elements likely damaged by large deflections | The part of total deflection after attachment, limited to l/480 |
| Roofs or floors supporting or attached to nonstructural elements not likely damaged by large deflections | Sustained-load time-dependent deflection plus additional live-load deflection, limited to l/240 |
| Flat roofs with possible ponding | The table limit is not intended to safeguard against ponding; ponding requires a separate deflection check |

## Key Provisions

Immediate deflections are calculated using elastic deflection methods or formulas while considering cracking and reinforcement effects on stiffness. Two-way slab systems require attention to panel size and shape, support conditions, and edge restraints.

For long-term behavior, Section 424.2.4 says additional time-dependent deflection from creep and shrinkage of non-prestressed flexural members is calculated from the immediate deflection caused by sustained load and a time-dependent factor. Table 424.2.4.1.3 gives factors of 1.0 at 3 months, 1.2 at 6 months, 1.4 at 12 months, and 2.0 at 60 months or more.

## Real World Scenario

An architect asks for brittle ceiling panels below a long-span concrete floor. The engineer cannot stop at a strength check. The serviceability check must consider the deflection that occurs after the ceiling is attached, because that is the movement most likely to crack or distort the nonstructural finish.

## How It's Tested

Expect questions that ask whether a deflection limit protects nonstructural elements, what l/360 applies to, why ponding is a separate check, or how sustained-load duration changes long-term deflection. If an item presents a minimum-thickness ratio, confirm it against the controlling rule instead of relying on memorized shorthand.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating deflection control as the same thing as flexural strength.
- Using old span-to-depth tables when the checked NSCP anchor is Section 424 serviceability.
- Ignoring the timing of nonstructural attachment.
- Forgetting that creep and shrinkage can make sustained-load deflection grow over time.
