# Natural Ventilation

Natural ventilation replaces stale indoor air with fresh air and supports comfort by moving air through occupied zones. In hot-humid settings, air movement is valuable even when the air itself is not much cooler, because moving air can improve the sensation of cooling at the body.

## Wind-Driven Ventilation

TOA 4 identifies seasonal prevailing winds in the Philippines: northeast from October to March and southwest from April to September. Building orientation, windows, vents, and doors should therefore be arranged to make use of local wind rather than blocking it.

Practical moves include aligning openings in a reasonably straight path, reducing internal obstructions, and lifting the building where appropriate to catch breezes.

## Stack Ventilation

Stack ventilation uses convection. Warm air rises and exits high, drawing cooler air in from lower openings. The checked source describes this as more effective when incoming air comes from shaded areas with plants or trees.

## Common Trap

Do not answer natural ventilation questions with isolated windows that cannot create flow. The tested idea is an air path: windward and leeward openings, low and high openings, or a mixed-mode strategy when passive airflow alone is not enough.
