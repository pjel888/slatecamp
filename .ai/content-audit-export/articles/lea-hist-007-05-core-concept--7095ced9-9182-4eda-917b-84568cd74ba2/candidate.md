# Korean Architecture

## LEA Point

Korean architecture questions usually test calibrated synthesis: Chinese-derived palace order, Korean site response, hanok domestic planning, ondol floor-heating culture, and Buddhist temple anchors must stay distinct.

## Source Anchors

- UNESCO Changdeokgung anchors Joseon palace architecture with garden and site-integration significance.
- UNESCO Seokguram Grotto and Bulguksa Temple anchors Silla Buddhist temple/grotto heritage at Gyeongju.
- Korea official palace resources anchor Gyeongbokgung and Joseon palace identity.
- Korea official hanok/ondol resources anchor traditional house and underfloor-heating vocabulary.

## Exam Pattern

If the stem asks for the first major Joseon palace in Seoul, answer Gyeongbokgung. If it asks for a UNESCO Joseon palace known for landscape/site integration, answer Changdeokgung. If it asks for underfloor smoke or heat channels shaping floor-based domestic living, answer ondol. If it asks for Silla Buddhist temple/grotto heritage at Gyeongju, answer Bulguksa and Seokguram.

## Guardrails

Use ondol strongest in hanok and domestic climate-response contexts. Do not claim every Korean palace floor is ondol-heated unless the source packet identifies the specific building. Keep Korean restraint and site response separate from Chinese imperial monumentality and Japanese tatami-module planning.

## Real World Scenario

For a Korean cultural center, a source-safe brief would use courtyard orientation and roofline restraint for public identity, but it would reserve ondol-like floor-warming language for rooms intended for seated or floor-based community use rather than applying it indiscriminately to ceremonial halls.
