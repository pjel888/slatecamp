## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal

By the end of this article, you will be able to:

- Define masonry joint types: bed joint, head joint, collar joint
- Define wythe, cell, and bond in masonry construction
- Identify common bond patterns: running, stack, Flemish, English
- Explain how bond patterns affect lateral resistance

---
Masonry construction relies on precise terminology for joints, unit arrangement, and bonding patterns. Understanding bed joints, head joints, collar joints, wythes, cells, and bond types is essential for specifying, inspecting, and designing masonry structures per NBP/LEA requirements.

## Core Concept

Masonry is built from individual units (bricks, concrete hollow blocks, stone) held together by mortar in organized patterns. The way units are arranged, joined, and stacked determines both structural performance and durability.

_Joints_ are the mortar-filled spaces between units. A **bed joint** runs horizontally between two courses (layers) of units. A **head joint** runs vertically between adjacent units in the same course. A **collar joint** is the vertical mortar space between two parallel wythes (leaf) in cavity wall construction, typically filled with mortar, grout, or left as air space.

A **wythe** (or leaf) is a single continuous vertical section of masonry one unit thick. Single-wythe construction uses one wythe; cavity wall construction uses two parallel wythes separated by an air space. A **cell** is the enclosed void within a concrete hollow block (CHB). Cells may be vertical (running the full height of the unit) or horizontal (running the full length). In grouted masonry, cells are filled with grout (a fluid concrete mixture); in ungrouted masonry, cells remain empty.

**Bond** refers to the pattern and overlap of units to tie wythes together and distribute loads. Common bond types include:
- **Running bond**: Offset courses where each unit spans the head joint of units above and below; simplest pattern, weakest lateral tie.
- **Stack bond**: Units are stacked vertically with head joints aligned; not recommended for seismic zones due to weak lateral resistance.
- **Flemish bond**: Courses alternate between headers (units perpendicular to wall face) and stretchers (units parallel to wall face); strong lateral tie.
- **English bond**: Alternating courses of all headers and all stretchers; provides maximum lateral tie and uniformity.
- **Header course**: A course composed entirely of headers; ties wythes together perpendicular to the wall plane.

## Key Provisions

| _Joint Type_ | _Orientation_ | _Function_ | _Typical Width_ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bed joint | Horizontal | Distributes vertical load, accommodates unit height variation | 10 mm ± 2 mm (typical) |
| Head joint | Vertical, in-plane | Bonds units horizontally, prevents longitudinal cracking | 8–15 mm |
| Collar joint | Vertical, perpendicular | Separates wythes in cavity wall, may be grouted or air space | min 50 mm (cavity) |

| _Bond Pattern_ | _Header Spacing_ | _Lateral Strength_ | _Typical Use_ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Running bond | Metal ties max 600 mm vertically | Low–moderate | Single-wythe walls, veneers |
| Stack bond | Headers omitted | Very low | Architectural, non-seismic only |
| Flemish bond | Every course (alternating units) | High | Multi-wythe masonry |
| English bond | Alternating courses | Very high | Lateral load resistance, seismic zones |
| Header course | Every 4th–6th course | Moderate–high | Cavity wall tie-in |

## Philippine Practice Context

National Building Code (PD 1096) requires consideration of bond patterns for structural integrity and specifies metal ties at max 600 mm vertically in seismic zones (Rule XIX Sec. 1904.2.3.4). Cavity walls typically use a 50 mm minimum air space (common: 75 mm) per NBC Rule XIX Sec. 1904.2.3.2. CHB masonry with grouted cells is the dominant construction method for residential and mid-rise structures in the Philippines due to cost-effectiveness and local unit availability.

## How It's Tested

**Pattern 1 — Joint Definition:**
_Which of the following best describes a collar joint in masonry construction?_
- Correct answer: The vertical mortar space separating two parallel wythes in a cavity wall.
- Test variation: Distinguish collar joint (between wythes) from head joint (within a single wythe).

**Pattern 2 — Bond Pattern Selection:**
_For a six-story reinforced masonry building in Metro Manila subject to seismic Design Category D, which bond pattern provides maximum lateral tie?_
- Correct answer: English bond (or Flemish bond with maximum header spacing of 600 mm).
- Test variation: Recognize that stack bond is NOT allowed in seismic zones.

**Pattern 3 — Cell and Grouting:**
_A concrete hollow block with three cells per unit is selected for a four-story residential building. What must be specified for lateral load transfer?_
- Correct answer: Minimum grouting schedule that fills designated cells at specified intervals (e.g., every 1200 mm horizontally).
- Test variation: Cell dimensions do NOT determine grout requirement; bond type and lateral load do.
