## REASON FOR THE LAW

### Why Pantone 279 (UN Blue)?
Universal recognition. The International Symbol of Access uses this specific blue globally. By standardizing to UN Blue, the Philippine code ensures that the ISA in Manila is **instantly recognized** by international travelers — including tourists, overseas workers returning home, and foreign guests — without translation. Color consistency = global legibility.

### Why Grade 2 Braille? (Not Grade 1)
Grade 1 Braille is one letter per Braille cell — slow and space-inefficient. **Grade 2** uses contractions (like shorthand), the same way skilled Braille readers communicate. Using Grade 1 on signs would be like printing in all capital letters, letter by letter — technically readable but cumbersome. Grade 2 is the literacy standard for Blind Filipinos.

### Why 1 mm Minimum Embossing?
Research on tactile sensitivity shows that Braille dots below **0.6–0.8 mm** are undetectable by many readers, especially older adults with reduced fingertip sensitivity. The 1 mm minimum provides a safety margin above the detection threshold.

### Why Tactile Maps Near Entries?
A building's layout is a spatial problem. A person entering a building for the first time needs to **orient themselves** before they can navigate. Placing tactile maps at the entry gives blind users the same spatial overview that sighted users get from a glance at a directory board — at the same moment, at the same location.

### Why 2200 mm Clearance for Projecting Signs?
A standing adult's eye level is approximately 1600–1700 mm. The head reaches approximately 1900–2000 mm. A person carrying luggage or using a white cane extends their profile upward. The 2200 mm clearance provides **200+ mm of buffer** above a tall person's head to prevent collision with protruding sign structures.

### Why the ISA at Dressing Rooms is at 1800 mm Center? (Sec. 13.4)
This is the conspicuous visual zone — above door height but below ceiling, visible from a standing approach. It differs from the general 1500 mm center spec for lower-mounted signs. The dressing room ISA functions as a **directional beacon** visible from across a retail floor, not a close-reading sign.

### Why Different Textures on Evacuation Plans?
For a blind person, a flat embossed plan is like a photograph — informative only if the textures distinguish rooms, corridors, exits, and hazards. **Different textures** (smooth vs. ribbed vs. dotted) encode semantic information (room type, escape route, danger) into the **tactile vocabulary** of the map. Without textural differentiation, all areas feel the same and the plan becomes useless in an emergency.

## PROTÉGÉ EFFECT CHALLENGE

You are reviewing a new 5-story office building's signage package. The submittal shows:

> *"ISA signs are mounted on poles at 600 × 600 mm in a custom teal color (similar to Pantone 279). Door signs are 150 × 150 mm. Braille is Grade 1, embossed at 0.8 mm depth. A tactile map is placed on Level 2 near the elevator lobby. Tactile map letters are 30 mm tall with 100 mm pictogram borders. A projecting wayfinding sign is mounted with its bottom at 2100 mm AFF. The dressing room ISA is at 1500 mm on center."*

**Find and explain all violations:**

*(Write your analysis before reading the answer key)*

| # | Element | Violation | Correct Standard | Section |
