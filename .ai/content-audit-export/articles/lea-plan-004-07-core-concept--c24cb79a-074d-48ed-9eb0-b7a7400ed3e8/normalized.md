# Regulatory and Zoning Analysis

## Board Exam Quick Check

Zoning analysis applies the adopted zoning ordinance to a specific site. The article is not asking for a generic design preference; it is asking whether the proposal fits the legal controls that implement the CLUP.

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Regulatory and zoning analysis connects the CLUP system to project review. RA 7160 authorizes LGUs to prepare CLUPs enacted through zoning ordinances, and gives city and municipal Sanggunians authority to adopt CLUPs and enact integrated zoning ordinances in consonance with the approved plan. EO 72 links this to the review and implementation path, including local responsibility for locational clearances where the plan has been reviewed and approved under the Order.

At site level, the designer checks four things in order. First, identify the applicable zoning map and district. Second, verify whether the proposed use is permitted, conditional, or prohibited. Third, apply the ordinance's dimensional controls, such as setbacks, height, lot coverage, FAR, open space, parking, and access. Fourth, identify whether any local procedure is required, such as variance, conditional use, rezoning, or plan amendment.

Use the ordinance numbers supplied in the problem. If the question states that a zone allows 40 percent lot coverage on a 1,000 square meter lot, the maximum covered area is 400 square meters. If it states that a site requires 25 percent open space on 3 hectares, required open space is 0.75 hectare or 7,500 square meters. Those computations are zoning applications, not independent national defaults.

RPS 2008 and the HLURB CLUP Guidebook explain why consistency matters. The CLUP gives the long-term spatial policy; the zoning ordinance makes that policy enforceable. The technical planning component supplies data and analysis, but the Sanggunian enacts the ordinance. DHSUD and the land-use review structure provide standards, technical assistance, review participation, and monitoring.

Do not import unrelated code numbers unless the item names that law. Water easements, building-code yards, subdivision open-space percentages, fire limits, or accessibility standards may matter in real practice, but they must be anchored to the controlling law or explicitly stated facts in the exam item. For this zoning-analysis cluster, answer from the adopted zoning ordinance unless the item gives an additional source.

## Exam Focus

- Verify district and permitted use first.
- Apply the ordinance's stated dimensional controls.
- Treat zoning ordinance as legally enforceable, not advisory.
- Treat CLUP as the policy source that zoning implements.
- Identify who has authority: technical offices review and recommend; the Sanggunian enacts local zoning law; DHSUD participates through standards/review/monitoring functions.

## Practice Cue

If a site proposal exceeds a stated height, FAR, coverage, use, or open-space limit, the safe answer is noncompliance unless the ordinance provides a variance, conditional-use, rezoning, or amendment path.
