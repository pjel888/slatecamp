## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal
Apply zoning ordinance constraints to specific development proposals, verify permitted land uses, calculate FAR and lot coverage, identify variances or exceptions required, and prepare zoning compliance documentation for building permit applications.

Zoning analysis at the site level applies regulatory constraints to a specific development proposal. The designer must verify that proposed land uses comply with the adopted zoning ordinance, understand permitted uses and their restrictions, and identify any variances or exceptions required.

## Core Concept

Regulatory and zoning analysis bridges planning law (CLUPs and zoning ordinances established by local government) with site-specific development. While LEA-PLAN-001-05 teaches the CLUP process and how zoning ordinances are adopted by the Sanggunian (municipal/city legislative body), this article applies zoning constraints to real development scenarios.

A zoning ordinance partitions a municipality into districts (residential, commercial, industrial, agricultural, etc.) and specifies what uses are permitted, permitted with conditions, or prohibited in each zone. Setback requirements, height limits, lot coverage ratios, and open space percentages are zone-specific. During site planning, the designer must answer: Is my proposed use permitted in this zone? What setbacks apply? What height limits? What open space must I provide?

The zoning ordinance is the legal implementation tool of the CLUP, adopted by the Sanggunian and signed by the Mayor. It carries legal force; violations create grounds for denial of building permits. Understanding zoning is not optional—it is foundational to any site proposal.

## Philippine Context

In Philippine municipal zoning ordinances, agricultural zones remain extensive in the periphery; commercial zones cluster along major corridors and nodes; residential zones are mixed by income (socialized per BP 220 standards, economic, market). Critical to Philippine zoning is the Sanggunian's role—the municipal or city council adopts the ordinance through legislative action; the Mayor signs it. This is not an executive decision alone. Additionally, zoning analysis must respect **PD 1067 (Water Code)** easements: 3 meters from drainage canals/esteros with constructed banks in urban areas, 20 meters from natural riverbanks/creeks in agricultural areas, 40 meters in forest areas [citation:1][citation:6][citation:9]. Post-Yolanda coastal constraints and other hazard overlays may impose additional restrictions [citation:8]. Many LGUs are updating their zoning ordinances post-2010 to integrate climate resilience and disaster risk reduction per RA 10121.

## How It's Tested

**Pattern 1 — Permitted use verification:** "A residential subdivision is proposed in a zone marked R-2 (medium-density residential). Are residential uses permitted in this zone? What setback applies?" → Consult ordinance; confirm use is permitted; identify setback distance.

**Pattern 2 — Non-compliance identification:** "A proposed 12-story commercial building is in a zone with a 6-story height limit. What is required?" → Building exceeds zone height; conditional use permit or zoning variance required.

**Pattern 3 — Open space calculation:** "A 2-hectare site requires 25% open space per the zoning ordinance. How much area must be dedicated?" → 2 hectares × 0.25 = 0.5 hectares = 5,000 sq.m.
