## Private Bidding — Competitive Bidding, Negotiated, and Invited Bid Procedures



Private construction contracts may employ different bidding procedures: public competitive bidding (if required by law), private competitive bidding, negotiated contracts, and invited bid procedures. Each has distinct steps and legal implications.



### Competitive Bidding Process

In a competitive bidding process, the Owner publicly announces the project through advertisements or invitations and invites multiple contractors to submit sealed bids. The Architect prepares the bid documents (drawings, specifications, general conditions, instructions to bidders) and distributes them to prospective bidders. Bidders are given a specified deadline to prepare proposals and submit sealed bids. Bids are typically opened at a public meeting where the Owner reads each bid amount aloud.

The Owner is not obligated to accept the lowest bid in a private competitive bidding process, though public projects must typically award to the lowest responsible bidder. In private bidding, the Owner may consider qualifications, past performance, financial stability, and other factors in addition to price. However, the Owner's selection should be based on rational criteria established before bidding; arbitrary selection or selection based on undisclosed preferences may constitute breach of the implied obligation to conduct fair procurement.

Each bidder must submit a bid bond accompanying the proposal, guaranteeing that the bidder will enter into the contract if the bid is accepted. If the selected bidder refuses to sign the contract, the bid bond is forfeited. The bid bond amount is typically 1% to 5% of the bid amount. Bid bonds protect the Owner against frivolous bidding and ensure that the selected bidder will commit to the project.

### Advertisement and Invitation to Bid

The Owner advertises the project through appropriate media (newspapers, architectural journals, owner websites, contractor associations) to attract qualified bidders. The advertisement specifies the project description, bid deadline, bid opening time and location, and point of contact for obtaining bid documents. The Owner should attract a sufficient number of bidders (typically at least three) to ensure competitive pricing.

The Architect prepares Instructions to Bidders, which specify the bidding procedure, bid format, content requirements, deadline, and conditions for bid submission. Instructions to Bidders are part of the contract documents and establish binding rules for bidding. Failure to comply with Instructions to Bidders may result in bid rejection. The Instructions clarify whether bids must be sealed, whether oral bids are acceptable, and what documentation must accompany the bid (bid bond, qualifications statement, proposed subcontractors list).

### Negotiated Contracts

In a negotiated procurement, the Owner selects a contractor without competitive bidding and negotiates contract terms, pricing, and schedule directly. Negotiated contracts are used when time constraints prohibit competitive bidding, when a specialized contractor with unique qualifications is required, or when the Owner has an established relationship with a preferred contractor. Negotiated contracts are not "sole source" unless the Owner has legitimate reasons to select only one contractor and cannot conduct even limited competitive bidding.

In negotiated contracts, the Architect prepares drawings and specifications; the Owner and Contractor discuss design, pricing, and schedule; and the parties execute an agreement reflecting the negotiated terms. Negotiated contracts typically require submission of cost breakdowns, value engineering reviews, and price justifications to ensure the Owner is receiving fair value. The Owner may also require that costs not exceed a specified limit or that savings beyond a target cost are shared with the Owner.

### Invited Bid Procedures

Invited bidding is a hybrid approach where the Owner invites a selected group of pre-qualified contractors to submit bids without general public advertisement. This procedure reduces the number of bidders but ensures quality; only contractors meeting specified qualifications are invited. Invited bidding is common in private construction where the Owner has established relationships with reliable contractors.

The Owner may pre-qualify contractors by requesting submission of past project references, financial statements, insurance verification, and safety records. Only contractors meeting the pre-qualification criteria are invited to bid. The bid documents are provided only to invited bidders, not published publicly. Rejected bidders are not given a detailed explanation of why they were not invited; the Owner has discretion in pre-qualification decisions unless discrimination or other illegal factors are involved.

### Bid Preparation and Submission

Bidders must prepare proposals in strict compliance with Instructions to Bidders. The proposal identifies the Contractor's name, organization, and authorized representative; states the total bid amount; includes itemized costs if the bidding procedure requires line-item detail; and confirms that the bidder has reviewed all Contract Documents and understands the scope and conditions.

Each bid must be accompanied by a bid bond (or bid security) guaranteeing that the bidder will enter into the contract if selected. The bid is sealed in an envelope marked with the project name and bid deadline. Bids received after the deadline are rejected, even if only slightly late. Oral bids (if permitted) must be recorded and read back to the bidder for confirmation. Email or electronic bids are permitted only if the Instructions specify electronic submission as acceptable.

### Evaluation and Award

After the bid deadline, bids are opened at a public meeting (or private opening, as specified in Instructions). Bids are reviewed for compliance with Instructions to Bidders; non-compliant bids may be rejected. The Owner evaluates bids based on criteria established before bidding, such as price, qualifications, schedule, value engineering proposals, and safety record. If the lowest bidder fails to meet qualifications or proposes an unrealistic schedule, the Owner may reject that bid and award to the next-lowest qualified bidder.

The Owner issues a Notice of Award to the selected bidder, notifying the contractor that the bid has been accepted and the contractor is awarded the contract. The Award is conditional on the contractor signing the Agreement within a specified period (typically 7 to 14 days). If the contractor refuses to sign after Award, the contractor has breached the bid bond guarantee, and the Owner may claim the bid bond amount and award the contract to the next-lowest bidder.

### Bid Clarification and Withdrawal

Bidders may submit written questions regarding the bid documents before the bid deadline. The Owner and Architect respond to clarification requests and may issue an addendum to the bid documents if the clarification affects the bid. Addenda are issued to all potential bidders to ensure equal information.

Before the bid deadline, a bidder may withdraw a bid by written notice without penalty. After the deadline, bid withdrawal is not permitted unless the Owner consents. If a bidder discovers an error in the bid after submission and before Award, the bidder may request withdrawal on the basis of error. The Owner may grant or deny the withdrawal request; if denied, the bidder is bound to the submitted bid price.

### Competitive Fairness and Prebid Meetings

The Owner and Architect conduct a prebid meeting (typically one week before the bid deadline) to allow bidders to clarify questions, tour the site, and ensure equal understanding of the project scope. The prebid meeting agenda is provided to all bidders, and minutes are recorded and distributed. Information disclosed at the prebid meeting is shared with all bidders to prevent any bidder from gaining unfair advantage.

All bidders must have access to the same Contract Documents, specifications, and site information. The Owner should not provide confidential or preferential information to selected bidders. If information is provided to one bidder, it must be disclosed to all bidders. This obligation to fair dealing is implied in the bidding process, even in private (non-public) competitive procurement.

### Documentation and Award Procedures

After Award, the Owner and selected Contractor execute the Agreement, incorporating the Contract Documents by reference. The Agreement identifies the parties, the contract price (the bid amount), the Completion Date, and key terms. The Agreement is signed by authorized representatives of both Owner and Contractor. The Contractor then submits the Performance Bond, Payment Bond, and Guarantee Bond, which are approved by the Owner before work commences.

All bid documents, including rejected bids, are retained by the Owner as project records. Bids are confidential until Award; rejected bidders may request an explanation of why their bid was not selected, and the Owner should provide factual information about the evaluation criteria and why their bid did not meet the Owner's selection criteria.
