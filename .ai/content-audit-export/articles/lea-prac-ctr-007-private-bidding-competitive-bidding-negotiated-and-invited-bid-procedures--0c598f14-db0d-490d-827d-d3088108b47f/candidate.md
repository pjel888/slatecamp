## Private Bidding — Competitive Bidding, Negotiated, and Invited Bid Procedures

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Distinguish private bidding, competitive public bidding, negotiated contract, and invited bid.
- Explain when each procurement method is appropriate.
- Describe the architect's role in the bidding process.
- Apply procurement method rules to exam scenarios.

### Competitive Bidding Process

In a competitive bidding process, the Owner publicly announces the project through advertisements or invitations and invites multiple contractors to submit sealed bids. The Architect prepares the bid documents and distributes them to prospective bidders. Bids are typically opened at a public meeting where the Owner reads each bid amount aloud.

### Competitive vs. Negotiated vs. Invited

| Feature | Competitive Bidding | Negotiated Contract | Invited Bidding |
|---------|-------------------|--------------------|-----------------|
| Public advertisement | Yes | No | No |
| Bid bond required | Yes | No (or reduced) | Yes |
| Number of bidders | Unlimited | Single | Pre-qualified group |
| Lowest bid obligated? | Public: Yes; Private: No | N/A | Depends on Owner policy |
| Best for | Standard projects | Specialized/complex projects | Quality-focused projects |

## Common Mistakes

<WikiMistakeList
  mistakes={[
    "Assuming the Owner must accept the lowest bid in private projects — in private bidding, the Owner may consider qualifications and other factors",
    "Believing negotiated contracts don't require documentation — cost breakdowns and price justifications are still required",
    "Confusing invited bidding with sole-source procurement — invited bidding still involves multiple pre-qualified bidders",
    "Thinking bid bonds are optional in competitive bidding — bid bonds (1-5%) are required to guarantee the bidder will enter into the contract",
    "Assuming late bids can be accepted with a penalty — bids received after the deadline are rejected even if only slightly late",
