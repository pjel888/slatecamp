## Private Bidding: Competitive, Negotiated, and Invited Procedures

## Board Exam Quick Check

For this local source audit, keep the article inside private construction-document evidence. UAP Document 301 and CIAP Document 102 define bid-related terms such as Advertisement or Invitation to Bid, Bid, Bid Documents, Bid Bulletin, Instruction to Bidders, Proposal Bond or Bid Bond, and Contract Documents. They do not, by themselves, prove every public-procurement rule or every private owner selection policy.

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Identify source-backed bid terms used in private construction contracts.
- Distinguish an Advertisement or Invitation to Bid from Instructions to Bidders and Bid Bulletins.
- Explain the limited role of a Proposal Bond or Bid Bond.
- Use the private-bidding documents for private award and bid-process questions; do not import public-procurement award rules without the governing source.

## Bid Terms in the Local Sources

UAP Document 301 defines Advertisement or Invitation to Bid as the notice or invitation issued by the Owner to prospective bidders, giving information about the nature of the proposed project, conditions for issuing contract documents, bidding date, estimated cost, or other information giving the contractor a general idea of the magnitude and extent of the project.

CIAP Document 102 defines Bid as the tender, proposal, quotation, or offer of a bidder to perform the Work described in the Contract, in form and substance complying with the Instruction to Bidders. It defines Bid Documents as all documents provided or made available to prospective bidders, including the Invitation to Bid and a copy of the Contract the winning bidder would sign with the Owner.

## Instructions, Bulletins, and Bonds

UAP Document 301 defines Instructions to Bidders as the list of instructions stipulating how bids are prepared and the conditions for award of contract. CIAP Document 102 defines Instruction to Bidders similarly, as instructions regarding the manner bids are prepared and conditions for award.

UAP Document 301 defines a Bid Bulletin as additional information on contract documents issued to bidders before the date of bidding. CIAP Document 102 defines Bid Bulletin as a document containing additional information on Bid Documents issued before the date of bidding.

UAP Document 301 defines Proposal Bond as a cashier's check or surety bond accompanying the proposal, guaranteeing that the bidder will enter into the contract if awarded. CIAP Document 102 defines Bid Bond similarly as acceptable security accompanying the Bid to guarantee that the bidder will enter into the Contract if the Contract is awarded.

## Competitive, Negotiated, and Invited Language

The checked sources support a careful distinction: a private Owner may issue an advertisement or invitation, provide Instructions to Bidders, issue Bid Bulletins before bidding, and require a Proposal or Bid Bond. They do not support a blanket statement that every private project must be publicly advertised or awarded only to the lowest bidder. When a question asks about public procurement, use the public procurement source supplied by the question, not private construction documents alone.

## Real World Scenario

A private owner invites three contractors to price a small commercial project and issues instructions, drawings, specifications, and a bid bond requirement. On the LEA, the source-safe answer should identify the documents and definitions first: invitation or advertisement, bid, bid documents, bid bulletin, instructions to bidders, and bid or proposal bond. Do not convert the private owner's invited process into a public-procurement rule unless the question expressly shifts to RA 9184 or another public source.

## Exam Boundary

If the option says "lowest bidder automatically wins," slow down. The local private-contract sources define bidding documents and bid security, but they do not by themselves create an automatic award rule. The answer should stay with the governing document named in the item.
