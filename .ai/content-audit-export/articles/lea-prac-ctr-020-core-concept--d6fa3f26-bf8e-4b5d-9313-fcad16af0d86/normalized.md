# Construction Claims

## Board Exam Quick Check

A construction claim is not proven by complaint alone. The source-safe workflow is notice, source-supported entitlement, cost or time proof, contract decision path, and arbitration if the contract dispute remains unresolved.

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Identify when extra work or delay must be raised by written notice.
- Separate extra-work value from unsupported estimates.
- Explain why vouchers and supporting documents matter.
- Keep unresolved construction contract disputes inside the CIAP/CIAC arbitration path when the contract and law require it.

## Core Rule

CIAP Document 102 requires written notice for a claim for extra cost within fifteen days after receipt of an instruction, drawing, or specification involving a change. The notice should be given as much as possible before the contractor proceeds, except in an emergency endangering life or property.

The same article gives a parallel notice rule for delay attributable to the owner. If mobilization or progress is delayed by owner-supplied materials, owner work that interferes with the contractor, delayed owner decisions, or similar owner-attributable matters, the contractor must give written notice within fifteen days after recognition of the delay.

## Cost Support

Extra work value may be set by lump sum, unit prices within the source limit, or actual direct cost plus value-added tax if any plus fifteen percent for the contractor's profit and overhead. When actual direct cost is used, the contractor must keep and present a correct account of direct cost with vouchers and supporting documents.

That is the exam anchor. A claim that says only "costs were significant" is not yet source-supported. The architect or owner needs the written notice, the contract basis, and the cost record.

## Decision and Dispute Path

UAP Document 301 keeps the architect involved in claims and decisions under the contract. CIAP Document 102 sends unresolved construction contract disputes to arbitration under the construction arbitration rules, and the party entering the contract is deemed to submit to CIAC jurisdiction for disputes arising out of or connected with that contract.

Final payment also matters. Under CIAP Document 102, acceptance of final payment waives claims against the owner except claims previously noticed and reserved, claims pending and unresolved, disputes already referred to arbitration, or claims the owner acknowledges are not covered by the payment.

## Real World Scenario

An owner instructs the contractor to upgrade lobby finishes after bidding. The contractor starts the next day and submits a large extra-cost claim two months later with no vouchers. The source-safe review asks first whether written notice was given within fifteen days, whether an emergency justified proceeding first, whether the instruction changed the work, and whether actual direct cost is supported by vouchers and other documents. If entitlement and amount remain disputed under the contract, the dispute path points to CIAC arbitration rather than a purely informal negotiation.

## Exam Traps

- Using a generic 30-day deadline when the checked CIAP/UAP provisions use fifteen days.
- Treating oral approval as enough without written notice and cost records.
- Awarding actual direct cost without vouchers and supporting documents.
- Treating final payment as harmless when the claim was not reserved, pending, arbitrated, or acknowledged.
