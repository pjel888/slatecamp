# Per Diem, Retainer, and Honorarium in Architectural Practice

Per diem, retainer, and honorarium are not interchangeable labels for one fee. In the UAP professional-practice materials, they are different compensation methods used when the service pattern is not best handled by a single percentage or fixed project fee.

## Per diem and honorarium

SPP 202 recognizes per diem or honorarium plus reimbursable expenses for project-related meetings, trips, ocular inspections, and consultations about investments or ventures. The source ties these charges to the architect's time and attendance, with out-of-pocket expenses such as travel, accommodations, and subsistence treated as reimbursable project costs.

For professional consulting architecture, the UAP standards also describe per diem or hourly compensation for intermittent personal service. The compensable time includes time spent on the assignment and travel time. The amount is expected to reflect the complexity of the work and the architect's experience or specialization, while travel, subsistence, and other out-of-pocket expenses are reimbursed separately.

## Retainer

A retainer fits a different pattern: services expected at intervals over a period of time. The UAP standards describe it as a stipulated amount paid regularly for a specified service or a specified amount of time, primarily to ensure the consultant's availability. Services outside that agreed retainer scope are paid separately, and reimbursable costs remain separate.

## Exam boundary

A source-safe contract answer should identify which service is being compensated, which method applies, what expenses are reimbursable, and when additional services require separate payment. Avoid invented daily rates, automatic rollovers, unlimited-service assumptions, or claims that the source does not state.
