# Per Diem, Retainer, and Honorarium in Architectural Practice

Per diem, retainer, and honorarium are not interchangeable labels for one fee. The checked UAP professional-practice sources use them for different service patterns: intermittent personal time, reserved availability over time, and specific time-based professional participation. They should not be used to replace a percentage-of-PCC or fixed-fee agreement for full regular design services unless the source facts support that scope.

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to identify per diem, retainer, and honorarium from the service pattern described in a board-exam item.

## Per Diem and Honorarium

SPP 202 recognizes per diem or honorarium plus reimbursable expenses when a client asks the architect to spend personal time on project-related meetings, conferences, trips, ocular inspections, or consultations about prospective investments or ventures. The source keeps out-of-pocket expenses such as travel, accommodation, and subsistence separate from the professional-time charge.

For professional architectural consulting services, per diem or hourly compensation fits intermittent personal service. The compensable time includes the consultant's work time and travel time. The rate should reflect the complexity of the work and the architect's experience or specialization, but this Batch 106 article does not invent a peso amount.

## Retainer

A retainer fits a continuing availability pattern. The UAP source describes it as a stipulated amount paid at regular intervals so the Professional Consulting Architect will be available when required. The retainer covers a defined service or a defined amount of time. Additional services are paid separately, and travel, subsistence, and other out-of-pocket expenses may still be reimbursed.

## Contract Clarity

When more than one method appears in a single engagement, the service agreement should separate the services, trigger conditions, billing basis, and reimbursables. A design fee, site-visit per diem, and monthly retainer can coexist only if each covers a distinct service. Do not let a retainer become an unlimited-service promise.

## Real World Scenario

A developer asks a senior architect to attend monthly concept-review meetings for one year, remain available for occasional phone consultation, and travel twice to inspect possible expansion sites. The monthly availability is a retainer if the agreement states the service and time covered. The travel days can be per diem or honorarium plus reimbursable expenses. If the developer later commissions full design services, that design scope should be contracted separately instead of hidden inside the retainer.

## Exam Traps

- Do not call every one-time payment an honorarium; look for the service pattern.
- Do not treat a retainer as unlimited work.
- Do not merge reimbursables into the professional fee without contract/source support.
- Do not invent daily or monthly amounts unless the problem supplies them.
