## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Define per diem, retainer, and honorarium as compensation types.
- Distinguish each method by scope and billing basis.
- Explain when each method is used in Philippine practice.
- Apply fee method selection to specific service scenarios.

### Per Diem Compensation

Per Diem (Latin for "per day") establishes a daily fee for the architect's time and expertise. The architect charges a fixed daily rate for each day spent on project-related work, regardless of how many hours are worked that day or what specific deliverables result. Per Diem is appropriate for site observation during construction (Construction Administration), site visits for feasibility analysis, expert consultation on specialized projects, or limited architectural services.

Per Diem rates should be established by mutual agreement between architect and client per UAP-APOA SPP Documents. Rates vary based on the architect's experience level, specialization, and the work's nature. The engagement contract must specify the agreed daily rate and what constitutes a billable day. The contract should specify what activities constitute a billable day (a full day on-site, multiple site visits in one day, or partial-day visits) and whether travel days are billable.

Per Diem works well for Construction Administration when the architect's on-site presence is intermittent or variable in intensity. Rather than estimating total hours (which is uncertain during construction), the architect and client agree to specific on-site visit frequency (twice weekly, for example) at a negotiated Per Diem rate. The contract specifies whether the rate includes travel time or whether travel is separate.

Per Diem is also appropriate for specialized consultation where the architect provides expert analysis or recommendations. A facade consultant providing limited analysis, a sustainable design expert advising on building performance, or a heritage conservation specialist providing periodic guidance might charge Per Diem rates for their specialized time.

### Retainer Compensation

A Retainer is a fixed periodic fee (typically monthly) that reserves the architect's availability for project-related services. Retainer fees provide advance payment that covers the architect's cost of maintaining capacity to serve the client; the architect then provides services on an as-needed basis within the retainer amount. Unused retainer typically does not roll forward; if the client doesn't use all reserved capacity in a month, that allowance expires.

Retainers are appropriate for ongoing advisory relationships, such as an architect retained by a developer for ongoing project consultation, space planning assistance for a corporation planning facility expansion, or strategic facilities planning for an institution. The retainer establishes a predictable revenue stream for the architect while giving the client flexible access to architectural expertise.

Retainer fees are established by mutual agreement between architect and client per UAP-APOA SPP Documents, based on the client's anticipated demand and the architect's level of involvement. The contract should specify: (1) the monthly retainer amount, (2) what services are included in the retainer (advisory meetings, drawing revisions, consultation on design issues), (3) whether additional services beyond the retainer are billed separately, and (4) billing timing (when invoices are submitted and payment is due).

A client retaining an architect for ongoing facilities consultation might pay a 75,000 peso monthly retainer covering up to 30 hours of professional consultation, design review, and space planning. If the client uses fewer than 30 hours in a month, the retainer still applies. If services exceed 30 hours, additional hours are billed at negotiated hourly rates.

### Honorarium Compensation

An Honorarium is a fee for specialized professional contribution—typically a one-time or occasional fee for providing design review, serving on a jury, presenting professional expertise, or contributing to a specialized project role. Honorariums recognize professional value without creating ongoing compensation obligation. They are used when the architect provides valuable expertise but the nature of the engagement (project jury member, design consultant for a specific issue, peer reviewer) doesn't fit ongoing project fee structures.

Honorariums are established by mutual agreement between architect and client per UAP-APOA SPP Documents, based on the nature of the expertise, time commitment, and client organization's resources. The engagement letter should specify the honorarium amount and scope of contribution.

Honorariums are typically one-time payments; the contract (or email engagement letter) should specify the scope of the honorarium engagement, what deliverables or contributions are expected, and the payment amount. Unlike retainers, honorariums do not establish ongoing availability; they represent specific professional contribution.

### Applying Per Diem, Retainer, and Honorarium in Philippine Projects

In Philippine architecture practice, these methods are used for specialized roles or services not fitting standard design fees. An architect serving as Construction Administration representative typically charges hourly or Per Diem; a developer retaining an architect for ongoing facilities planning might establish a monthly retainer; a design competition might offer honorariums to jurors and selected consultants.

These methods work well in combination with primary project fees. An architect might charge percentage-of-PCC for design services (Schematic through Contract Documents) and Per Diem or hourly rates for Construction Administration site observation. A client might contract for design services under one agreement and establish a separate retainer for ongoing strategic consultation.

### Documentation and Clarity

Regardless of which method applies, the contract must clearly document:

1. The compensation method and rate (Per Diem amount, retainer amount, or honorarium amount).
2. The scope of services included (what activities trigger billing, what is covered, what is excluded).
3. Billing and payment procedures (when invoices are submitted, payment terms, frequency).
4. Duration of the engagement (one-time honorarium, monthly retainer, or estimated number of Per Diem days).
5. Any additional terms (travel reimbursement, meal provisions for Per Diem engagements, conditions for extending retainers).

Clear documentation prevents misunderstandings regarding compensation and service expectations.

### Comparison with Standard Design Fees

Per Diem, Retainer, and Honorarium should complement—not replace—appropriate compensation for primary design services. An architect providing design through Contract Documents should charge percentage-of-Project-Construction-Cost or Multiple-of-Direct-Personnel-Expense per UAP-APOA SPP Doc. 202; additional Per Diem or hourly rates should apply only to services beyond this primary engagement. Clients sometimes attempt to use retainers or Per Diem rates to avoid paying standard design fees; the architect should clearly document what services are covered under each compensation method.
