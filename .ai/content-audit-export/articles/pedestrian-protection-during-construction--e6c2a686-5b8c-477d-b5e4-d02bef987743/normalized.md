# Pedestrian Protection During Construction

## LEA Focus

PD 1096 treats public-way construction as a life-safety condition, not as a site convenience. When construction or demolition affects a street, alley, public sidewalk, or other route used by pedestrians, the work must protect pedestrians for the full period in which they may be endangered.

## Source-Backed Rules

- Work on or next to a public way must follow Rule XI. A street, alley, or public sidewalk cannot be used for permit work unless the Rule XI conditions are met.
- Pedestrians must be protected when work is performed on a building or structure adjacent to a public way in general pedestrian use.
- Temporary structures occupying public property, including fences, canopies, and walkways, must be adequately lighted between sunset and sunrise.
- Materials and equipment stored on public property must not obstruct fire hydrants, fire or police alarm boxes, utility boxes, catch basins, manholes, drainage, or the safe movement of vehicles and pedestrians.
- Mixing mortar, concrete, or similar materials on public streets is not allowed.
- Public and private utilities above or below grade must be protected from damage while work proceeds, and the protection must not obstruct normal utility function.
- If the Building Official authorizes sidewalk closure, or if there is no sidewalk in front of the site, a temporary walkway not less than 1.20 meters wide must be provided. The walkway must support a uniform live load of 650 kilograms per square meter and keep a durable safe wearing surface.
- Where only partial sidewalk occupancy is needed, a temporary walkway is not required if at least 600 millimeters of sidewalk remains open with a protective railing on the road side.
- If a walkway occupies part of the roadway or is adjacent to an excavation, protective railings are required; required railings must be at least 1.00 meter high.
- Construction or demolition fences must enclose the site on the building side of sidewalks or walkways and must be at least 2.40 meters above the curb line.
- Where a canopy is required, it must provide a 2.40 meter clear unobstructed height above the walkway. If used for work or storage, it must have substantial railings at least 1.00 meter high and toe boards at least 300 millimeters high.
- Warning signs must be conspicuously posted and lighted at night, entrances and exits must be closed except during passage, and signs and lights must be maintained even when operations are not in progress.
- Protective devices must stay in place and in good order for as long as pedestrians may be endangered.

## Exam Traps

- Do not treat sidewalk closure as permission to omit pedestrian passage. Rule XI requires a temporary walkway unless a qualifying partial sidewalk opening remains available.
- The 1.00 meter pedestrian railing, 2.40 meter fence, and 2.40 meter canopy clear height are different requirements.
- Public-property storage cannot interfere with fire, police, utility, drainage, or traffic functions.
- Protective devices remain required while pedestrians may be endangered, even if a visible construction milestone has already been completed.

## Real World Scenario

A contractor for a mid-rise renovation needs to fence the full sidewalk along a busy street. Under Rule XI, the review should not stop at the fence permit. Check whether the Building Official-authorized closure triggers a temporary walkway at least 1.20 m wide, whether any roadway-side walkway has protective railings, whether the fence and canopy clearances are distinct, and whether warning signs and night lighting stay maintained while pedestrians remain exposed. If the contractor proposes storing materials on the public way, reject any layout that blocks hydrants, alarms, utility boxes, drainage, or safe pedestrian and vehicle movement.
