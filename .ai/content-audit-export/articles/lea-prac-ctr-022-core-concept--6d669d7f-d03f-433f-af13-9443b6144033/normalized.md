# Liquidated Damages for Delay in Construction Contracts

## Board Exam Quick Check

Liquidated damages are a contract amount for delay, not a universal daily rate supplied by memory. Under CIAP Document 102, they accrue when the contractor fails to complete within the completion time and continue until substantial completion as determined under the contract.

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Identify the contract trigger for liquidated damages.
- Explain why substantial completion stops delay-liquidated-damages accrual under CIAP Document 102.
- Separate delay damages from corrective-work damages after the known-defects period.
- Use Civil Code penal-clause principles without inventing a rate.

## Contract Rule

CIAP Document 102 states that time is an essential feature of the contract. If the contractor fails to complete the work within the completion time, the contractor pays liquidated damages in the amount stipulated in the contract as indemnity and not by way of penalty. The owner may deduct the accrued amount from sums due to the contractor.

The amount is therefore contract-based. An LEA question should not invent a percentage, daily amount, or maximum cap unless the problem gives it or the cited contract source supplies it.

## Substantial Completion Cutoff

The same source defines substantial completion at 95 percent of the work when the remaining work and the work necessary to complete the project do not prevent normal use of the completed portion. It also states that no liquidated damages for delay beyond the completion time accrue after the date of substantial completion.

That is the main exam trap. Minor punch-list or corrective work may remain, but delay-liquidated-damages accrual stops at substantial completion under the checked CIAP clause. Separate damages may apply to uncompleted corrective work in the period for making good known defects, based on the value of that corrective work.

## Civil Code Frame

The Civil Code provisions on penal clauses explain why a stipulated penalty can substitute for damages and interest if there is no contrary stipulation, why proof of actual damages is not necessary when the penalty is demandable, and why courts may equitably reduce a penalty when there has been partial or irregular performance or when the penalty is iniquitous or unconscionable.

Use that Civil Code frame carefully. It supports the penal-clause logic, but the specific construction delay amount still comes from the contract.

## Real World Scenario

A contractor finishes late but reaches substantial completion on June 10. The owner accepts normal use of the completed portion and issues a punch list for minor corrective work. Under the checked CIAP rule, liquidated damages for delay can accrue from the first day of delay until June 10, the substantial completion date. The owner should not keep charging delay-liquidated-damages for ordinary punch-list work after substantial completion, though separate corrective-work rules may still matter.

## Exam Traps

- Memorizing an invented daily rate instead of reading the contract amount.
- Continuing delay-liquidated-damages accrual after substantial completion.
- Confusing delay damages with damages for uncompleted corrective work.
- Ignoring Civil Code equitable reduction language when the issue asks about penal clauses.
