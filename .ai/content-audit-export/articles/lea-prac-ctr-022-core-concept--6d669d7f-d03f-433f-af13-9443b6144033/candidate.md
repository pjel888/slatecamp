# Liquidated Damages for Delay in Construction Contracts

## Learning Goal

Apply CIAP Document 102 and Civil Code penal-clause rules to delay damages without inventing rates, dates, or remedies that the audited sources do not state.

## Contract Rule

CIAP Document 102 treats failure to complete within the contract time, as adjusted by approved changes, as the event that can trigger the stipulated liquidated damages for delay. The document describes the amount as indemnity, not as a penalty, and allows the owner to deduct the amount from sums due to the contractor.

The damages accrue from the first day of delay until the date of substantial completion under Article 20.11. That is the source-safe time frame for this article.

## Substantial Completion Limit

CIAP Document 102 defines substantial completion as the point when at least 95 percent of the work has been completed and the remaining work does not prevent normal use of the completed portion. It also states that liquidated damages for delay do not accrue after the date of substantial completion.

For exam answers, do not continue liquidated damages through final payment or punch-list completion unless another audited contract provision supports that result.

## Civil Code Frame

The Civil Code provisions on obligations with a penal clause explain how stipulated damages operate in a broader legal setting. Article 1226 provides that the penalty substitutes for indemnity for damages and interest unless otherwise stipulated. Article 1228 states that proof of actual damages is not necessary before the penalty may be demanded. Article 1229 allows equitable reduction when there has been partial or irregular performance, or when the penalty is iniquitous or unconscionable.

## Practice Cue

In a delay problem, identify the contractual completion time, approved extensions or changes, the date delay began, and the substantial completion date. Then apply the stipulated method in the contract. Do not supply a daily rate, grace period, or automatic waiver unless it appears in the audited contract source.
