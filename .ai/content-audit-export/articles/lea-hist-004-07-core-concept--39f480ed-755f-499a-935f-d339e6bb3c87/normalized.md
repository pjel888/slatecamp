# Mughal architecture

## LEA Point
Mughal architecture is Indo-Islamic architecture in northern India. For the LEA, identify it by garden-tomb composition, chahar bagh planning, white marble and red sandstone, pietra dura or parchin kari inlay, domed chambers, iwan-like portals, chajja shading, and synthesis of Persian, Central Asian, Islamic, and Indian forms.

## Source Anchors
UNESCO source-maps the Taj Mahal as the safest Shah Jahan anchor: a Mughal mausoleum for Mumtaz Mahal, set in a garden, using white marble and refined decorative work. The local HOA module adds the exam formula: chahar bagh, canals, marble facade, and cut-fitted polished colored-stone inlay.

UNESCO Humayun Tomb is the precursor anchor. Use it when the item asks for the garden-tomb lineage that leads toward the Taj Mahal.

UNESCO Fatehpur Sikri is the Akbar anchor. Use it for planned capital, red sandstone, court halls, palace hierarchy, and Hindu-Islamic design synthesis.

## Exam Pattern
If the item mentions Taj Mahal, Shah Jahan, Mumtaz Mahal, white marble, garden-tomb planning, chahar bagh, or pietra dura/parchin kari, answer Mughal and usually Taj Mahal.

If it mentions Akbar, red sandstone, Diwan-i-Am, Diwan-i-Khas, palace courts, or a planned capital, answer Fatehpur Sikri.

## Guardrails
Do not call Mughal architecture simply Persian architecture in India. The source-safe answer is synthesis: Persian and Central Asian planning language adapted through Indian material, craft, climate, and court culture.

Do not use unsupported exact workshop, labor, or module-number claims unless a later source capture records them.

## Real World Scenario
On an image item, a marble tomb framed by axial water channels and a four-part garden points to Mughal garden-tomb architecture. A red sandstone court city with audience halls points to Akbar and Fatehpur Sikri.
