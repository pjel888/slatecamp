# Rule I General Provisions

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to use Rule I of the 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096 as the legal framework for code questions: title, policy, scope, general building requirements, site requirements, and key definitions.

## Controlling Source

This article is source-audited against the DPWH official PDF of the 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096, Rule I Sections 101 through 106.

## What Rule I Does

Rule I does not contain every technical dimension in the National Building Code IRR. Its function is more foundational. It tells you what the IRR is called, what public policy it serves, what buildings and professional disciplines it covers, what general safety obligations apply to buildings and sites, and how important code terms are defined.

For LEA work, use Rule I to decide whether a project is inside the PD 1096 framework and how to classify the action being done to a building. Then use the later rule that controls the specific technical issue.

## Section 101: Title

Section 101 states that the rules are known and cited as the Revised Implementing Rules and Regulations of the National Building Code of the Philippines, P.D. 1096. They are referred to as the IRR.

This title matters because LEA questions often distinguish the PD 1096 basic law from the 2004 Revised IRR. When a question asks for the IRR rule, answer from the implementing rules, not from a generic code summary.

## Section 102: Declaration of Policy

Section 102 declares the State policy to safeguard life, health, property, and public welfare, consistent with sound environmental management and control.

The purpose of the Code is to provide a framework of minimum standards and requirements for buildings and structures. Those standards regulate and control:

- Location.
- Site.
- Design.
- Quality of materials.
- Construction.
- Use.
- Occupancy.
- Maintenance.

The word "minimum" is important. PD 1096 sets regulatory minimums. A project, contract, local ordinance, or referral code may require more, but the Code establishes the baseline.

## Section 103: Scope and Application

Section 103 states that the IRR covers the following disciplines:

- Architectural.
- Civil/structural.
- Electrical.
- Mechanical.
- Sanitary.
- Plumbing.
- Electronics.

It applies to the design, location, siting, construction, alteration, repair, conversion, use, occupancy, maintenance, moving, demolition, and addition to public and private buildings and structures.

The stated exceptions are traditional indigenous family dwellings and those covered by Batas Pambansa Bilang 220, the law on economic and socialized housing projects.

Section 103 also allows existing buildings or structures without appropriate building permits or certificates of occupancy to be legalized and issued the necessary permits and certificates if they are made to conform to the rules and regulations. Penalties, surcharges, fines, and other appropriate measures may still apply.

Finally, applicable and consistent provisions of allied professional codes and other government-agency codes approved by the DPWH Secretary serve as referral codes of PD 1096 and its IRR.

## Section 104: General Building Requirements

Section 104 requires all buildings and structures, including accessory facilities, to conform to the principles of safe construction and to be suited to the purpose for which they are designed.

It also requires buildings used for manufacturing or production to observe adequate environmental safeguards. Buildings, parts of buildings, and facilities must be maintained in safe, sanitary, and good working condition.

The continuing maintenance duty is a common exam trap. Code compliance is not only a one-time permit issue. The building must remain safe, sanitary, and functional.

## Section 105: Site Requirements

Section 105 requires the land or site for a building or ancillary or auxiliary facility to be sanitary, hygienic, or safe.

For sites or buildings intended for human habitation or abode, the site must be at a safe distance, as determined by competent authorities, from polluted streams, bodies of water, or air sources; from a volcano or volcanic site; and from any other building considered to be a potential source of fire or explosion.

This is the framework for site-safety questions. The exact technical clearance may come from another law, hazard map, local ordinance, or competent authority, but Section 105 supplies the minimum safety concept.

## Section 106: Definitions That Drive Exam Classification

Section 106 defines terms that often decide the correct answer.

| Term | Exam use |
| :-- | :-- |
| Addition | New construction that increases the height or floor area of an existing building or structure. |
| Alteration | Work involving changes in materials, partitioning, openings, structural parts, utilities, or equipment, without increasing building height or floor area. |
| Building Permit | The document issued by the Building Official authorizing the permittee to proceed with construction, installation, addition, alteration, renovation, conversion, repair, moving, demolition, or other work after plans and documents are found compliant and fees are paid. |
| Construction | All on-site work from site preparation, excavation, foundation, assembly, installation, erection, alteration, repair, or similar work. |
| Conversion | A change in use or occupancy, or a change of an existing building to another use or occupancy. |
| Demolition | Systematic dismantling or destruction, in whole or in part, of a building or structure. |
| Moving | Transfer of a building or structure from one location or position to another. |
| OBO | Office of the Building Official. |
| Referral Codes | Applicable and consistent allied professional or agency codes approved by the DPWH Secretary that supplement PD 1096. |
| Renovation | Physical change to increase value, quality, or improve appearance. |
| Repair | Remedial work on damaged or deteriorated portions to restore original condition. |

## How to Use Rule I in Linked Questions

Rule I supplies the governing vocabulary. If a question asks whether work is an addition, alteration, conversion, renovation, repair, moving, or demolition, start with Section 106. If a question asks about safe construction, maintenance, or site safety, start with Sections 104 and 105. If a question asks for a dimensional rule, use Rule I only for framework and then go to the later section that gives the technical value.

## LEA Recall Anchors

- Rule I is the framework rule: title, policy, scope, safety obligations, site requirements, and definitions.
- Section 102: minimum standards for location, site, design, materials, construction, use, occupancy, and maintenance.
- Section 103: applies across major design and engineering disciplines and across the full building lifecycle.
- Section 104: safe construction, suitability to purpose, environmental safeguards, and maintenance in safe, sanitary, good working condition.
- Section 105: sanitary, hygienic, and safe site, with human-habitation distance concerns from polluted sources, volcano/volcanic sites, and potential fire or explosion sources.
- Section 106 definitions often decide whether a project is an addition, alteration, conversion, renovation, repair, moving, or demolition.
