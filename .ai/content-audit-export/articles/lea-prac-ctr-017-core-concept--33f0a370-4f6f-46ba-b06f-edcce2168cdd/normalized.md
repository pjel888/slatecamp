# Substantial Completion

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Apply the CIAP Document 102 substantial-completion test.
- Separate substantial completion from final completion.
- Explain why certificates and owner use do not automatically waive defective or incomplete work.
- Connect substantial completion to punch lists, known-defect correction, and liquidated-damages limits.

## CIAP Document 102 Test

CIAP Document 102 defines substantial completion by both percentage and usability. There is substantial completion when the contractor completes ninety-five percent of the work, provided the remaining work and the performance needed to complete the work do not prevent normal use of the completed portion.

Owner use of a completed or partially completed portion is not the same as acceptance of nonconforming work. CIAP Document 102 states that prior possession or use does not waive the owner's right to damages for delay or accept work that is not complete under the contract.

## Certificate and Date Boundary

The owner may issue a written acknowledgement of substantial completion, such as a Certificate of Substantial Completion or equivalent document. CIAP Document 102 cautions that the date of that document is not controlling if substantial completion is shown to have been made earlier, unless the contractor accepts the certificate without written exceptions within fifteen days from receipt.

## What Substantial Completion Triggers

Substantial completion starts close-out controls. The owner issues the punch list or lists within the source-stated period. The Period of Making Good of Known Defects is then counted from the contractor's receipt of the last punch-list item submitted within the allowed period.

Substantial completion also sets the liquidated-damages boundary. CIAP Document 102 states that liquidated damages for delay accrue until the date of substantial completion as determined under the substantial-completion article, and no liquidated damages for delay beyond completion time accrue after that date.

## UAP Document 301 Boundary

UAP Document 301 uses a different substantial-completion threshold in the checked source: not less than ninety-eight percent of the contract amount, with remaining unfinished work minor in nature, and with required equipment, fixtures, and utilities tested where the contract requires testing. Do not mix this UAP threshold with the CIAP ninety-five percent rule unless the question names both sources.

## Real World Scenario

A project is 95% complete and usable, but the owner argues that liquidated damages should keep running while minor punch-list work continues. Under the checked CIAP source, substantial completion stops liquidated damages for delay after the substantial-completion date, while punch-list and defect-correction controls continue separately.

## Exam Boundary

Substantial completion is not final completion and not a Certificate of Occupancy. It is a contract close-out milestone. Keep the source named, apply its percentage and usability test, then handle punch-list corrections, known defects, retention, and final payment as separate steps.
