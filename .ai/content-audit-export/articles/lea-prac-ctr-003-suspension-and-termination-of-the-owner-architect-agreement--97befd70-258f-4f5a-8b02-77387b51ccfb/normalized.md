# Suspension and Termination of the Owner-Architect Agreement

The first exam move is to identify which agreement is being tested. SPP 202 and the 401-402 proforma address the Owner-Architect service and fee relationship. UAP Document 301 and CIAP Document 102 address the Owner-Contractor construction contract. Do not transfer one document's notice periods or remedies into the other.

## Owner-Architect Service Consequences

For regular design services, SPP 202 states that when the Architect's work is abandoned or suspended in whole or in part, the Owner pays the Architect for services rendered corresponding to the amount due at the stage of suspension or abandonment. The same source treats prepared plans, designs, specifications, and construction documents as the Detailed Design and Contract Documents phase, equivalent to ninety percent of the Architect's work. If the Owner does not implement the prepared plans and documents for construction, the Architect is entitled to compensation corresponding to ninety percent of the Architect's fee.

The 401-402 proforma gives a related owner-architect agreement version: if the Architect's work is abandoned or suspended, the Architect is paid commensurate with services rendered at the component fee due at the stage reached. The proforma also records an older eighty-five-percent contract-documents value and separates the remaining fifteen percent into civil-liability/signing-sealing and construction-phase service components.

## Construction Suspension and Termination Are Separate

UAP Document 301 and CIAP Document 102 deal with the construction contract. Under UAP Document 301, the Contractor may suspend work or terminate the contract upon fifteen days' written notice to the Owner and Architect for listed causes, including a court or public-authority stoppage for ninety days, payment-action failures, or failure to pay an arbitral award. UAP Document 301 also allows the Owner to terminate the construction contract after fifteen days' written notice for listed contractor defaults.

CIAP Document 102 has a similar but not identical private construction-contract framework. It allows contractor suspension or termination upon fifteen days' written notice to the Owner for listed causes; permits Owner suspension without cause for not more than an aggregate fifteen days with adjustment rules; and provides termination with cause, other termination grounds, and termination without cause.

## Real World Scenario

An owner stops a clinic project after approving contract documents, then asks whether the Architect receives nothing because construction never began. The owner-architect answer comes from SPP 202: pay for services rendered at the stage reached, with the non-implementation rule for prepared plans and documents. Do not import CIAP contractor-termination remedies unless the question is about the Owner-Contractor construction contract.

## Exam Traps

- Do not claim SPP 202 creates a ten-to-thirty-day cure period for terminating the Architect; that period was not found in the checked local sources for this batch.
- Do not say the Architect may independently terminate the construction contract. UAP Document 301 describes the Architect as the Owner's representative during construction and gives authority only to the extent provided in the Contract Documents or in writing.
- Do not mix the SPP 202 ninety-percent non-implementation rule with the older 401-402 eighty-five-percent proforma wording unless the question tells you which document governs.

## Source Anchors

- UAP Document Standards/SPP 202: lines 493-505 for owner-ordered changes, suspended/abandoned work, non-implementation compensation, and six-month remaining-work rule.
- UAP Document 401-402: lines 119-126 for the proforma abandoned/suspended work clause and eighty-five-percent contract-documents formulation.
- UAP Document 301: lines 1062-1086 for construction-contract time limits, contractor suspension/termination, owner termination, and disputes.
- CIAP Document 102: lines 712-761 for private construction-contract suspension and termination rules.
