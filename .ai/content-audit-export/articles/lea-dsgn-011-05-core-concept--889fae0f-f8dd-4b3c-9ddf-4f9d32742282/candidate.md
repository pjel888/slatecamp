## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal
Identify and interpret standard architectural symbols for materials (section, elevation), fixtures, doors, windows, and mechanical/electrical elements, read dimension and annotation systems, and apply consistent notation across plan, elevation, and section views.

Architectural symbols and notations are the standardized graphical language used on drawings to communicate building components, materials, and design intent to builders and regulators. The LEA expects you to recognize and interpret these symbols accurately and to understand their use across plan, elevation, and section views.

## Core Concept

Architectural drawings communicate through two primary means: **dimensions and annotations** (text) and **symbols and notation** (graphical marks). Symbols allow complex information to be conveyed quickly and consistently across large drawings. Standardized symbols ensure that anyone—builder, inspector, engineer—can interpret the drawing without ambiguity.

**Categories of architectural symbols:**

1. **Plan symbols** (viewed from above):
   - **Doors**: Single-swing, double-swing, pocket, bifold, accordion. Shown with an arc indicating swing direction. Symbol includes a solid line representing the door leaf and a dotted line showing the swing arc.
   - **Windows**: Shown as an opening in the wall with a break or mark indicating the window type (double-hung, casement, fixed, etc.). Size and type are noted in a schedule or on the drawing.
   - **Stairs**: Shown as a series of parallel lines representing treads, with an arrow indicating the "UP" direction. Number of risers is often noted.
   - **Elevators**: Shown as a rectangle with "ELV" or "E" designation. **Minimum inside cab dimensions per NBC Rule XVIII (Mechanical Regulations) Section 1801: 1,100 mm width x 1,400 mm depth.**
   - **Walls**: Shown as lines of varying thickness. Exterior walls are typically thicker lines (150–200 mm drawn width); interior walls are thinner. Fire-rated walls may be shown with a dotted or dashed line and labeled "1-HR" or "2-HR".

2. **Section/elevation symbols**:
   - **Material hatching**: Standardized patterns indicate material type:
     - **Concrete**: Dotted or speckled pattern.
     - **Masonry**: Diagonal hatching (45°) with regular spacing.
     - **Earth**: Organic, irregular speckled pattern.
     - **Wood**: Diagonal lines (typically 45° at a steeper angle than masonry).
     - **Steel**: Cross-hatching in perpendicular directions.
     - **Insulation**: Wavy or diagonal lines indicating thermal break.
   - **Section cut symbols**: A heavy line on a plan with endpoints and arrows indicating viewing direction (e.g., "Section A-A"). The corresponding section elevation is drawn and labeled with the same letter pair.

3. **Detail markers**: Small symbols placed on plan or elevation indicating where a detail drawing exists at a larger scale. Typically shown as a circle with a number or letter (e.g., "Detail 3" or "Detail A").

4. **Electrical & Plumbing symbols**: Outlets, switches, water lines, and drains. These are typically shown in a dedicated legend on their respective specialty plans.

**Notations and annotations:**

- **Dimension lines**: Thin lines with arrows or tick marks at endpoints, showing distances. Critical dimensions include floor-to-floor heights, room widths, and setbacks.
- **Leader lines**: Thin lines with arrows or dots pointing to a feature, with text annotation (e.g., "FFL +0.00 m").
- **Title blocks**: Located at the bottom right of each sheet, showing project name, scale, and date.
- **Legends and schedules**: Lists defining abbreviations, symbols, materials, and specifications.

## Key Provisions

| Symbol Type              | Representation                                            | Standard Use                                                                              |
| :----------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Doors (single-swing)** | Arc from hinge, solid line at leaf                        | Plan view; indicates swing direction and clearance                                        |
| **Doors (double-swing)** | Two arcs, one in each direction                           | Barn doors, accordion; allows swing either way                                            |
| **Windows**              | Break in wall line; type noted in schedule                | All views; detailed in elevation and section                                              |
| **Stairs**               | Parallel lines with up arrow; number of risers noted      | Plan; direction of ascent; NBC minimum tread/riser noted                                  |
| **Elevators**            | Rectangle labeled "ELV"; **min 1.1 m × 1.4 m inside cab** | All views; **required for buildings 4 storeys and above per NBC Rule XVIII Section 1801** |
| **Concrete hatching**    | Dotted or fine speckled pattern                           | Section/detail views                                                                      |
| **Masonry hatching**     | Diagonal 45° lines                                        | Section/detail views; clarifies unit assembly                                             |
| **Wood hatching**        | Diagonal lines (steeper or finer than masonry)            | Section/detail views; interior finishes                                                   |
| **Steel hatching**       | Cross-hatching (perpendicular lines)                      | Section/detail views; columns, beams                                                      |
| **Insulation**           | Wavy or diagonal lines                                    | Section/detail views; energy code requirements                                            |
| **Fire-rated wall**      | Dotted/dashed line or special notation ("1-HR", "2-HR")   | Plan and section; specifies duration of fire resistance                                   |
| **Section cut**          | Heavy line with arrows and letter labels (e.g., "A-A")    | Plan; references corresponding section elevation                                          |
| **Detail marker**        | Circle with number (e.g., ⊙ 3)                            | Plan or elevation; references enlarged detail drawing                                     |

**Philippine-specific notations:**

- Dimensions in millimeters (mm) or meters (m), with metric units standard.
- Fire ratings noted per **RA 9514** (Fire Code) and **PD 1096** (NBC).
- **Accessible** symbols for ramps, parking, and facilities per **BP 344**.

## Philippine Practice Context

In the Philippines, architectural drawings follow NBC standards. The use of symbols is essential for multi-disciplinary coordination between architects and engineers. Material hatching conventions help builders quickly identify concrete, masonry, or timber—crucial for cost estimation and constructability in a tropical climate.

## How It's Tested

**Pattern 1: Symbol identification and interpretation**
A floor plan section is shown with various symbols. The candidate must identify each correctly (e.g., distinguishing between a casement and a sliding window symbol).

**Pattern 2: Section cut and detail reference**
A plan shows a section cut line. The candidate must determine the viewing direction based on the arrow orientation and locate the correct corresponding elevation.

**Pattern 3: Code-Mandated Symbols**
The exam may ask for the minimum interior dimensions of an elevator cab or the threshold at which an elevator is required.

- **Answer:** Required at **4 storeys and above**; minimum inside cab is **1.10 m x 1.40 m** (Rule XVIII Sec 1801).

<Tip>
  **Memory aid:** "Door-Direction-Arc Rule" — The arc always shows the path of
  the door. If a door swings into a required egress corridor and reduces the
  width, it is a code violation!
</Tip>

**Exam Tip:** Material symbols: diagonal lines = concrete/brick/masonry (spacing indicates material), random squiggles = earth/gravel, dots = sand/cement. Section marks show cut location + view direction.
