## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal

Explain how thermal insulation controls heat flow and classify source-supported insulation materials and placement strategies.

## Core Concept

Thermal insulation controls the transfer of heat through exterior assemblies so less heating or cooling energy is needed to maintain comfort. Salvan frames the concept through conduction, radiation, and convection. BCI adds the assembly-level rule: insulation works best when continuity is maintained at foundation, floor, wall, and roof junctions so the envelope remains unbroken against heat transmission.

R-value is a measure of thermal resistance. Rt is the sum of the individual R-values of the components in a construction assembly. U-value measures thermal transmittance, and BCI defines it as the reciprocal of R-value for the component or assembly. This means higher R generally indicates more resistance to heat flow, while lower U generally indicates less heat transmission.

BCI groups insulating materials into batt or blanket insulation, rigid foam insulation, foamed-in-place insulation, loose-fill insulation, and reflective insulation. Reflective insulation uses high reflectivity and low emissivity with a dead-air space to reduce radiant heat transfer. Salvan similarly lists loose fill, blanket, batts, structural insulation board, rigid slabs, foamed plastics, and reflective insulation.

Moisture coordination matters. BCI warns that condensation can reduce insulation effectiveness and damage finishes. Roof insulation may be placed below the roof deck with a ventilated airspace, between deck and membrane, or above the membrane in a protected membrane system, depending on the assembly.

## How It's Tested

Expect calculation and concept questions: identify R, Rt, and U; choose an insulation family for a wall or roof condition; and recognize why vapor retarders, ventilation, and continuity protect performance. Use product data or the named source when exact R-values are required.

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing R-value with U-value.
- Adding insulation in one bay while leaving junctions discontinuous.
- Ignoring condensation, vapor migration, and ventilation.
- Inventing local energy-code R-values without a primary source.
