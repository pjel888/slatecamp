# Ownership of Architectural Plans and Client Use Rights

## LEA Point

RA 9266 treats architectural drawings, specifications, and contract documents as instruments of professional service and as intellectual property of the architect. Payment for a commissioned project does not automatically give the client unlimited rights to repeat, license, or reuse the design for another project.

## Source Anchors

- RA 9266 IRR Section 3(27): copyright ownership refers to intellectual proprietary rights retained by the architect over architectural documents or work unless there is a written stipulation to the contrary.
- RA 9266 IRR Section 3(32): ownership in a commissioned architectural work is confined to execution of the original project and does not include repetitions or subsequent projects.
- RA 9266 IRR Section 20(4): drawings and specifications are instruments of service and intellectual property of the architect.
- RA 9266 IRR Section 33: drawings and specifications duly signed, stamped, or sealed as instruments of service are the architect's intellectual property and may not be copied or used for repetition or other projects without the architect's written consent.

## Client Use vs Ownership

The client may use the documents for the original commissioned project. That is not the same as owning the copyright or having a general reuse license. Unless the architect gives written consent or a written stipulation transfers broader rights, the client should not repeat the design on another lot, license it to another developer, or use it for a subsequent project.

## Exam Guardrails

Payment does not automatically transfer copyright ownership. Written consent matters for repetition or other projects. A written stipulation can change the default retained-ownership rule, so read the facts before choosing an absolute answer.
