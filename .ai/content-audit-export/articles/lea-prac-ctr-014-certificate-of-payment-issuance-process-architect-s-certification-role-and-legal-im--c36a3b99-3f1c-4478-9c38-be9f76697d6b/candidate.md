## Certificate of Payment: Architect Certification and Payment Control

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Explain the architect's action on a contractor request for payment under UAP Document 301.
- Separate the contractor request, architect certificate, and owner payment action.
- Connect payment certificates to measured work, retention, and written reasons for withholding or amended payment.

## Architect Action on a Payment Request

A certificate of payment is the architect's source-based response to a contractor's payment request under UAP Document 301. The contractor asks for payment; the architect does not simply pass the request through. Within fifteen days after receipt of a request for payment, the architect either issues a certificate of payment or withholds the request. If the request is withheld, the architect informs the contractor in writing of the reasons.

When the contractor and architect cannot agree on a revised amount, UAP Document 301 states that the architect issues a certificate for the amount for which the architect can make representations to the owner. The certificate includes the value of work accomplished during the covered period and a recommendation to the owner for the amount the architect decides is properly due.

## Payment Context

CIAP Document 102 gives the payment-request context: the contractor may submit requests periodically, but not more than once each month, based on work completed in the Breakdown of Work and Corresponding Value. The certificate or payment action should therefore be read as a payment-control step tied to measured work, contract deductions, and written reasons, not as an unlimited approval of every amount requested.

## Real World Scenario

A contractor submits a monthly progress billing for stored materials and partially completed work, but the architect can only support a lower value from the approved breakdown and site progress. Under UAP Document 301, the architect should issue or withhold within the stated time and, if agreement on a revised amount is not reached, certify only the amount for which the architect can make representations to the owner. CIAP Document 102 keeps the payment request tied to completed work, retention, and the breakdown of work and corresponding value.

## Exam Boundary

For LEA purposes, separate these roles: the contractor requests, the architect certifies or withholds with written reasons under UAP Document 301, and the owner acts on the certified or otherwise determined amount under the payment provisions.
