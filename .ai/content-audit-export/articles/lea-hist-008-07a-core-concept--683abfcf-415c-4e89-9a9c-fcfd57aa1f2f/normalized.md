# Deconstructivism

## LEA Point
Deconstructivism is tested as late-20th-century architecture of instability, fragmentation, non-orthogonal geometry, and visual contradiction. The safe exam frame is that it destabilizes geometric order; it is not simply Postmodern ornament, nor a literal application of Derrida's texts to buildings.

## Source Anchors
- MoMA's 1988 Deconstructivist Architecture exhibition is the source-safe institutional marker that crystallized the movement in architectural discourse.
- Zaha Hadid's Vitra Fire Station is source-safe as a 1993 angular concrete work and Hadid's first complete building.
- Peter Eisenman's House VI is source-safe as a 1972-1975 process-based house where conceptual transformations challenge conventional domestic order.
- Frank Gehry's Guggenheim Museum Bilbao is source-safe for titanium skin, complex curved form, and CATIA-enabled construction coordination.
- Daniel Libeskind's Jewish Museum Berlin is source-safe for the zigzag titanium-zinc building, angled walls, and voids tied to absence and loss.

## Exam Pattern
The usual correct answers are MoMA 1988, Zaha Hadid for Vitra Fire Station, Peter Eisenman for House VI, Frank Gehry for Guggenheim Bilbao, and Daniel Libeskind for Jewish Museum Berlin or the Denver Art Museum Hamilton Building.

## Guardrails
Do not say Deconstructivism is just random chaos. Its forms are intentionally composed to challenge stable, rational, orthogonal order. Do not confuse it with Postmodernism's applied historical pastiche or Parametricism's systematic algorithmic variation.

## Real World Scenario
If a museum addition uses shards, tilted planes, titanium cladding, and disorienting angular circulation, the exam-safe reading is Deconstructivist fragmentation unless the question instead points to historical quotation or parametric continuity.

## Related Articles
This topic links backward to Postmodernism and forward to Parametricism because all three challenge modernist universals, but each uses a different formal logic.
