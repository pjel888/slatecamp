# Conflict Matrix Stairs Guards Elevators
## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to:

Apply the conflict-resolution hierarchy when PD 1096, BP 344, and other codes impose conflicting dimensional requirements on stairs, guards, and elevators; identify which code governs for each specific element (landing depth, handrail height, guard spacing, elevator cab size); and apply the strictest requirement principle consistently.
---

## REAL-WORLD HOOK

You are reviewing permit drawings for a mid-rise office building. The structural engineer has dimensioned the main stair at 1,200 mm clear width with a single handrail at 850 mm. The architect is citing PD 1096. The building official stamps it. Then BP 344 applies — and suddenly the stair needs to be 1,500 mm wide with *dual* handrails at 700 mm *and* 900 mm. The drawings go back. The slab is already poured.

This is not a theoretical problem. In the LEA, the examiner will hand you exactly this conflict and ask: *which law governs?* This module gives you the answer — every time.

## CORE MNEMONIC: **"DUAL GRIP — 700 PLUS 900"**

For BP 344 stairs and ramps, remember:
- **D**ual handrails — always two
- **G**rip diameter — 30–40 mm
- **7** — 700 mm lower rail (seated eye level)
- **9** — 900 mm upper rail (standard ambulatory height)
- **3** — 300 mm horizontal extension at top and bottom
- **5-0** — 50 mm clearance from wall to grip surface

*PD 1096 uses ONE rail in the 800–900 mm band. The exam will try to make you confuse these.*

## CRITICAL CONFLICTS DECODED

### Conflict 1: The Handrail Height Battle (Most Tested)

```
PD 1096 STAIRCASE:
            ───────────  ← 800–900 mm above nosing (ONE rail, any height in this band)
           /
          /
         /

BP 344 STAIRCASE:
            ───────────  ← 900 mm above nosing (UPPER rail)
           /
          /
    ──────  ← 700 mm above nosing (LOWER rail)
         /

If a building is subject to BP 344 (i.e., public or private used by public):
→ BOTH rails required at EXACTLY 700 mm and 900 mm
→ PD 1096 allows 850 mm as sole handrail; BP 344 does NOT accept this
```

**The trap question:** *"A staircase has one handrail at 850 mm above the nosing. The building official approved it under PD 1096. Is it accessible?"*

**Answer:** **No.** BP 344 requires dual handrails at 700 mm AND 900 mm. A single rail at 850 mm — while compliant with PD 1096 — fails BP 344.

---

### Conflict 2: Landing Interval — 3 m vs. 3.6 m

| Law | Max vertical rise before landing |
|-----|----------------------------------|
| **BP 344** | **3,000 mm (3 m)** |
| **PD 1096** | **3,600 mm (3.6 m)** |
| **Governing** | **BP 344 — 3 m** |

A staircase in a public building may have been designed under PD 1096 with a landing at 3,500 mm vertical rise. This is **non-compliant** with BP 344. The more restrictive 3 m interval governs.

---

### Conflict 3: Landing Depth — Apparently Contradictory

| Law | Landing depth |
|-----|--------------|
| **BP 344** | Min **1,500 mm** |
| **PD 1096** | Equal to stair width, **max 1,200 mm** for straight stairs |
| **Governing** | **BP 344 — 1,500 mm** |

### Conflict 4: Stair Clear Width

| Law | Minimum clear width |
|-----|-------------------|
| **BP 344** | **1,500 mm** (accessible staircases) |
| **PD 1096** | ~1,100 mm typical (varies by occupancy and floor area) |
| **Governing** | **BP 344 — 1,500 mm** for any stair on an accessible route |

---

### Conflict 5: Guards — The Non-Conflict

| Law | Guard height |
|-----|------------|
| **BP 344** | **1,100 mm** where drop > 750 mm |
| **PD 1096** | **1,100 mm** where drop > 750 mm |
| **Result** | **Perfect agreement.** |

> This is the one area of zero conflict. Both laws agree: **1,100 mm guards where drop exceeds 750 mm**. Examiners sometimes present this as a trap asking "which law governs?" — the answer is: **both specify 1,100 mm; no conflict exists**.

## > TRAP LEART #1 — Upper Handrail: 900 mm or 800–900 mm?

**PD 1096** says 800–900 mm (a range). **BP 344** says 900 mm (a fixed value). If asked what height an accessible stair upper handrail must be, the answer is **900 mm exactly** (BP 344 governs on accessible routes).

A drawing showing 850 mm is PD 1096-compliant but **BP 344-non-compliant** for accessible routes.

---

## > TRAP LEART #2 — Extensions Beyond the Stair

**BP 344** requires **300 mm horizontal extensions** at top and bottom of each handrail run. PD 1096 is silent. These 300 mm extensions are a signature BP 344 requirement that appears regularly on the LEA. Both the upper (900 mm) and lower (700 mm) rails need extensions.

---

## > TRAP LEART #3 — "3 m" Not "3,000 mm" Confusion

The landing interval under BP 344 is commonly written as "3 m vertical rise." This is exactly 3,000 mm. PD 1096's 3,600 mm is exactly 3.6 m. Examiners write these as decimals, fractions, or mixed units. Convert everything to mm for comparison.

---

## > TRAP LEART #4 — Elevator Handrails on 3 Walls

A common wrong answer is handrails on 1 wall (the back wall) or 2 walls. BP 344 requires the inside of the elevator cab to have handrails at 900 mm on **three walls** — typically the back wall and both side walls. The door wall has no handrail (it's the door).

---

## > TRAP LEART #5 — Area of Refuge vs. Wheelchair Parking vs. Elevator Lobby

All three have different clear spaces:

| Element | Clear Space |
|---------|------------|
| Area of Refuge | **900 × 1,200 mm** |
| Assembly wheelchair space | **900 × 1,200 mm** |
| Elevator lobby | **1,800 × 1,800 mm** |
| ATM / Phone / Fountain clear | **1,800 × 1,800 mm** |

Don't mix 900 × 1,200 mm (refuge/wheelchair) with 1,800 × 1,800 mm (elevator lobby/fixture approach).

---

## > TRAP LEART #6 — Raised Edge on Stair Sides

BP 344 requires a **100 mm raised edge** (curb) on both sides of accessible staircases. This is distinct from the handrail. Its purpose is to prevent a white-cane or foot from slipping off the stair edge. PD 1096 does not specify this. It is tested as a "detail frequently omitted from drawings."

---

## > TRAP LEART #7 — Stair Clear Width 1,500 mm vs. Corridor Clear Width

BP 344 stair clear width is **1,500 mm**. Corridor clear width is typically **1,200–1,500 mm** depending on the section. Do not conflate stair width with corridor width. The 1,500 mm stair standard is for accessible staircases; some corridors may be 1,200 mm minimum.

## AREAS OF REFUGE — INTEGRATION WITH STAIRS

### Legal Basis: BP 344 (2024 IRR) Section 5

| Element | Specification |
|---------|--------------|
| **Clear footprint per wheelchair** | **900 × 1,200 mm** |
| **Location** | At stair landings used as evacuation refuge |
| **Emergency communications** | Required at **700–900 mm** AFF |
| **Illumination** | **200 lux** |
| **Identification** | On tactile/Braille evacuation plan |
| **Access** | Must not require passing through another wheelchair space |

> Areas of refuge are mandatory because elevators are typically shut down during fire emergencies. PWDs who cannot use stairs must have a safe, protected space to wait for rescue. The 900 × 1,200 mm footprint matches one wheelchair parking space.

## QUICK SUMMARY: BP 344 WINS LIST

*The following are all cases where BP 344 is stricter than PD 1096 and therefore governs:*

1. **Dual handrails** (BP 344) vs. single handrail (PD 1096)
2. **700 mm lower handrail** (BP 344) — no equivalent in PD 1096
3. **900 mm upper handrail** (BP 344) vs. 800–900 mm band (PD 1096)
4. **300 mm extensions** (BP 344) — not in PD 1096
5. **Landing interval: 3 m** (BP 344) vs. 3.6 m (PD 1096)
6. **Landing depth: 1,500 mm** (BP 344) vs. max 1,200 mm typical (PD 1096)
7. **Stair clear width: 1,500 mm** (BP 344) vs. ~1,100 mm typical (PD 1096)
8. **Max riser 150 mm** (BP 344) vs. 200 mm typical (PD 1096)
9. **Min tread 300 mm** (BP 344) vs. 250 mm typical (PD 1096)
10. **Nosing luminance contrast** (BP 344) — not in PD 1096
11. **Raised stair edge 100 mm** (BP 344) — not in PD 1096
12. **Elevator car dimensions** (BP 344) — not addressed in PD 1096

### Where they agree:
- **Guards: 1,100 mm where drop > 750 mm** — same in both

## Cross-Referencing

### Connection to BP 344 Accessible Washrooms
The 1,800 × 1,800 mm elevator lobby clear space matches the BP 344 requirement for accessible washroom turning space. This is not a coincidence — BP 344 uses this universal turning radius for all fixture approach clearances.

### Connection to Commercial Clearances
The 1,500 mm stair clear width under BP 344 matches the dining circulation path minimum. Both derive from the minimum space needed for a wheelchair user and an ambulatory person to pass.

### Connection to Widths & Slopes
The 1:15 maximum ramp slope and 1,800 mm landing dimensions in the ramp article coordinate with the stair landing requirements here. A ramp and stair sharing a landing in a vertical core must both satisfy BP 344 minimums — the largest dimension governs.

### Connection to PD 1096 Rule XII (Means of Egress)
PD 1096 governs exit stair capacity (number of exits, occupant load, travel distance). BP 344 overlays accessibility dimensions on top of these life-safety requirements. Neither law can be satisfied in isolation — a compliant building must meet both simultaneously.
