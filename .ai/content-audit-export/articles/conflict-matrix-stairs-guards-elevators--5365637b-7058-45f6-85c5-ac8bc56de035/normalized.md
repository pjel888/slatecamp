# Conflict Matrix Stairs Guards Elevators

## Source Boundary

This article resolves common accessibility conflicts using the 2024 Revised IRR of BP 344. It is limited to the checked provisions for ramps, handrails, stairs, doors, tactile guidance, and accessible routes.

## Stairs Need an Accessible Companion

A stair can be code-compliant as a stair and still fail the accessible-route requirement. Where staircases are present along an accessible route, complementary ramps, lifts, or escalators must be provided. Accessible stairs also need 150 mm maximum risers, 300 mm minimum treads, continuous handrails on both sides, 1.50 m clear width, and landings not less than 1500 mm.

## Guards Are Not Handrails

Protection railings and handrails solve different problems. Protection railings are installed at a minimum height of 1100 mm where the listed stairs, ramps, balconies, landings, or porches are more than 750 mm above adjacent grade. Accessible handrails are installed at 900 mm and 700 mm and require 300 mm horizontal extensions.

## Doors Can Break a Route

Door clear width and door swing matter. All doors require 900 mm minimum clear width. A clear level space of 1800 mm by 1800 mm is required before and extending beyond a door, with the stated corridor-side exception. Protection is required from doors that swing into corridors.

## Tactile and Elevator Coordination

Tactile paths prioritize elevators as the preferred means of vertical access for persons with disabilities. The path terminates in front of the Braille-equipped control panel at the waiting lobby, with warning tactile blocks 300 mm before the control panel.

## Exam Use

When a problem mixes stairs, guards, elevators, and doors, separate the issue: accessible route continuity, stair geometry, handrail heights, protective railing height, door clear width, door clear space, and tactile/elevator wayfinding.
