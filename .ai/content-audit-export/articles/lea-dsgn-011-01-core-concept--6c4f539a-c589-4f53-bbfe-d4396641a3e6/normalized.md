## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal
Read and produce architectural floor plans using standard conventions — wall types, door and window symbols, dimensioning, room labels, and scale — at appropriate drawing scales, and verify plan compliance with PD 1096 for building permit submission.

Architectural floor plans are the primary communication medium in building design and regulation. The LEA tests your fluency in plan conventions—how walls, doors, windows, and spaces are drawn—so you can both interpret existing plans and draft compliant new ones.

## Core Concept

A floor plan is a horizontal slice (orthogonal view from above) through a building at a fixed height, typically 1.2–1.5m above floor level to capture door/window openings. Plans show the spatial organization, circulation, and relationships of rooms, and they govern permitting, construction, and maintenance.

**Key Functions of Floor Plans:**

- **Space identification:** Names, dimensions, and areas of each room.
- **Egress verification:** Confirm that occupancy, fire exits, and travel distances meet code.
- **Accessibility:** Check doorway widths, ramp locations, and accessible toilet placement.
- **Utilities:** Locate mechanical, electrical, and plumbing fixtures shown symbolically.
- **Coordination:** Reference with elevations, sections, and details for 3D understanding.
- **Construction:** Contractors use plans to lay out walls, finishes, and rough-ins.

### Plan Drawing Hierarchy

**Walls:**

- **Exterior walls:** Thicker lines (typically 200–300mm).
- **Interior partitions:** Thinner lines (typically 100–150mm).
- **Fire-rated walls:** Identified by hatching or dashed lines to denote specific ratings (e.g., 2-hour).
- **Structural columns:** Solid or hollow shapes labeled with specific sizes.

**Doors & Windows:**

- **Standard Doors:** Swing arc indicates direction; opening width is specified (e.g., 0.9m).
- **Double Doors:** Two swing arcs indicating active and inactive leaves.
- **Sliding Doors:** Thick line with an arrow showing sliding direction.
- **Windows:** Simple lines dimensioned horizontally.

**Spatial Elements:**

- **Stairs:** Tread lines with a direction arrow (UP/DN) and landing dimensions.
- **Ramps:** Sloped lines with a gradient (1:12 max for ramps; 1:20 for sloped walks).
- **Fixtures:** Standard symbols for toilets (WC), sinks, and kitchen appliances.

## Key Provisions

### Standard Floor Plan Hierarchy

| Element | Scale | Line Weight | Representation |
|:------------------- |:----------- |:------------------ |:---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Walls (Exterior)** | 1:100 / 1:50 | Heavy (0.5–0.7 mm) | Thick solid lines; hatching optional |
| **Walls (Interior)** | 1:100 / 1:50 | Medium (0.3–0.4 mm) | Thin solid lines; ratings shown by hatch |
| **Doors** | 1:100 / 1:50 | Medium | Swing arc 90° from hinge |
| **Stairs** | 1:100 / 1:50 | Medium | Tread lines + direction arrow |
| **Dimensions** | 1:100 / 1:50 | Light (0.2 mm) | Thin extension and dimension lines |

### Doorway & Passage Conventions

| Door Type | Standard Dimension | Code Requirement | Plan Symbol |
|:-------------------- |:----------------- |:---------------------------------------------------- |:------------------- |
| **Residential Entry** | 0.90 m | Min 0.80 m clear width (NBC) | Single swing arc |
| **Accessible Entry** | 1.00 m | **Min 0.90 m leaf (0.80 m clear)** | Swing arc + "ACC" |
| **Double Entry** | 1.20–1.50 m | Min 0.90 m per active leaf | Two swing arcs |
| **Fire-Rated Exit** | 0.90–1.00 m | Must match wall rating; self-closing | Door in hatched wall |

### Egress & Accessibility Notation (BP 344 2024 IRR)

- **Exit Route:** Bold dashed line from occupied space to exit.
- **Travel Distance:** Measured along the line of travel (e.g., Max 45m).
- **Accessible Route:** Continuous **1.50 m clear width** (1.20 m allowed only in constrained existing conditions).
- **Slopes:** Gradient $\le$ 1:20 (5%); surfaces must be firm and slip-resistant.
- **Turning Space:** Clear 1.50 m diameter circle for wheelchairs at dead-ends and changes in direction.

## Philippine Practice Context

Permitting drawings for building permits must be sealed and signed by a licensed architect. All new plans must explicitly identify accessible facilities and egress paths. Room area calculations typically use clear interior dimensions (C-C), though structural walls may be included in specific commercial GFA calculations.

## How It's Tested

**Pattern 1: Egress Path Verification**
Question: "A building has a maximum travel distance of 45m. On the plan, the measured path is 50m. Is it compliant?"

- **Answer:** No. Travel distance must be within code limits based on occupancy class.

**Pattern 2: Door Width & Accessibility**
Scenario: A floor plan shows an accessible entry door with a 0.80 m leaf width. Is this compliant with BP 344 2024?

- **Answer:** **No.** While the clear opening must be 0.80 m, the door leaf itself should typically be **0.90 m** to ensure that hardware and stops do not reduce the clear width below the 0.80 m threshold.

**Pattern 3: Accessible Route Compliance**
Question: "What is the standard width for an accessible corridor?"

- **Answer:** 1.50 m. Using 1.20 m is a common mistake unless it is a "constrained existing condition".

**Exam Tip:** Floor plans at 1:100 or 1:50 scale for building permit. Verify that all rooms are labeled, dimensions are complete, door swings are shown, and wall types are indicated. Missing room labels = permit rejection.
