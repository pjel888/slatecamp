## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal

Design mechanical spaces (chiller plants, boiler rooms, AHU rooms, fan rooms, elevator machine rooms) incorporating adequate equipment access, service clearances, ventilation, structural support, acoustic separation, and fire-rated separation per NBCP and mechanical code requirements.
## Mechanical Spaces

### Chiller Plant and Central AHU Rooms

Chiller plant rooms typically consume _3–5% of gross floor area (GFA)_ for full-capacity central systems. This allocation accommodates:

- Centrifugal or screw chillers (2–3 units for redundancy)
- Condenser water pumps and isolation valves
- Cooling tower basin or ground-level heat rejection equipment
- AHU units with heating and humidification coils
- Ductwork header sections and vibration isolation mounts

**Source:** ASHRAE Fundamentals Handbook and typical Philippine hospitality/commercial building specifications.

In mid-rise buildings (10–20 floors) with distributed or package systems, mechanical space may drop to _2–3% of GFA_. For luxury residential or hospitals requiring high reliability, allocation may reach _5–6% of GFA_.

Equipment layout must follow PD 1096 requirements for:
- Access doors minimum 1.2 m wide
- Maintenance clearance in front of all equipment (typically 1.5 m for chiller service)
- Vibration isolation pads and flexible connections to prevent transmission to occupied floors

### Fan Rooms and Exhaust Stacks

Rooftop mechanical penthouses house:
- Supply and exhaust fans (centrifugal or axial types)
- Sound attenuation silencers (0.5–1.0 m long)
- Ductwork transitions and dampers

_Minimum space requirement: 200–300 m² for a typical 30-floor office tower._ Equipment rooms must be non-occupiable per PD 1096.

### Pump Rooms (Hot Water, Domestic Water Booster)

Pump room sizing is equipment-driven:
- Centrifugal pump unit (including motor, coupling, isolation): 0.8 m × 1.2 m footprint
- Hot water boiler or water heater: 1.5 m × 1.0 m (depending on capacity)
- Pressure tanks, expansion vessels, and piping manifolds: 0.5–1.0 m additional space

**Minimum room dimensions:** 3.5 m × 4.5 m for standard commercial buildings. Larger facilities (hotels, hospitals) may require 5 m × 6 m or dedicated rooms per floor.

## Electrical Spaces

### Main Transformer Vault and Switchgear Room

Electrical rooms typically occupy _1–2% of GFA_. Allocation depends on:
- Number and kVA rating of distribution transformers
- Medium-voltage (MV) switchgear layout (if present)
- LV main distribution board (MDB) and panelboards
- Diesel generator control panel location

**Typical footprint for a 30-floor office tower (15,000 m² GFA):**
- Main transformer vault: 150–200 m² (housing 2–3 × 500 kVA transformers or equivalent)
- MDB and LV switchgear room: 100–150 m²

**Source:** Philippine Electrical Code (PEC) and typical design practice for Metro Manila commercial buildings.

PD 1096 mandates:
- Reinforced concrete vault with fire-rating ≥ 2 hours if adjacent to occupied space
- Clear space in front of all switchgear panels: minimum 1.2 m
- Cable termination and future growth allowance: 20–30% spare capacity in cable trays

### Generator Room

Diesel generator rooms require space allocation based on equipment plus operational clearances:

_Generator set footprint (typical 500–750 kW unit):_ 4 m × 3 m

_Total room dimensions (including fuel tank and clearances):_
- Length: 7–8 m (genset + fuel tank end-to-end)
- Width: 5–6 m (genset width + maintenance access on three sides)
- Height: 3.5 m (for cooling radiator and muffler installation)

**Fuel storage allocation:**
- For 8-hour autonomy (RA 9514/PEC requirement for essential facilities): 0.8–1.2 m³ per 100 kW of generator capacity
- A 750 kW generator requires approximately 6–9 m³ fuel tank storage (integrated into generator room floor or basement)

**Source:** PD 1096 (Emergency Power Supply requirement) and manufacturer specifications for Philippine-installed gensets (Cummins, MTU, Yuchai common in Philippines).

Sound attenuation (muffler and enclosure): adds 0.5 m to room length; vibration isolation pads require 0.3 m foundation elevation.

### Battery Room (if UPS present)

Lead-acid or lithium-ion UPS batteries occupy dedicated ventilated space:
- Typical: 2 m × 2.5 m room for 100–200 kVA 4-hour UPS system
- Requires separate HVAC cooling (batteries generate heat) and acid-resistant ventilation for lead-acid chemistry

## Plumbing and Fire Protection Spaces

### Domestic Water Pump and Storage Tanks

Tank sizing is driven by building height and occupancy:

**Rooftop elevated tank (typical 20-floor residential building):**
- Capacity: 15–25 m³ (covers 2–4 hours peak demand per PD 1096)
- Dimensions: 4 m × 3 m × 1.5 m (concrete tank or stainless steel)
- Pump room adjacent: 3 m × 3 m

**Ground-level storage (for pressure tank booster system):**
- Two-tank arrangement (suction and discharge): total 20–40 m³
- Basement footprint: 8 m × 6 m (including pump station, isolation valves, pressure gauges)

**Source:** PD 1096 minimum domestic water storage requirements (typically 1.5 × daily demand).

### Fire Protection Pump Room

Fire pump sizing follows RA 9514 (Fire Code):

_Typical fire pump selection:_ 50–75 m³/h at 35–50 bar pressure (for 15–30 floor buildings)

**Room dimensions:**
- Pump and motor assembly: 2 m × 1.5 m footprint
- Diesel engine fire pump (if present): 3.5 m × 1.8 m (larger, slower-speed engine)
- Suction and discharge manifolds: 2 m × 1.5 m
- Pressure tanks and accumulators: 1 m × 1 m

**Total fire pump room: 5 m × 5 m minimum** (for buildings with dual-pump redundancy or large floor plate).

Jockey pump (small constant-pressure maintaining pump) adds minimal space (0.3 m × 0.3 m).

### Wastewater and Sewage Treatment

For buildings without municipal sewage connection or requiring secondary treatment (hotels, hospitals):

**Septic tank allocation:**
- Sizing: 1.5–2.0 cubic meters per 100 occupants per day (PD 1096 guideline)
- Typical residential tower (500 occupants): 7.5–10 m³ tank
- Dimensions: 3 m × 2 m × 2 m depth

**Sewage treatment plant (STP) if required:**
- Biological reactor: 1.5 × septic tank volume
- Filtration and disinfection: additional 0.5 × septic tank volume
- Total basement/podium footprint: 50–100 m² for buildings > 50 floors requiring tertiary treatment

**Source:** PD 1096 and Bureau of Health guidelines for onsite treatment.

## Vertical Distribution

### Mechanical Risers (HVAC Ducts, Chilled Water Piping)

Ductwork shafts and pipe chases extend vertically through all occupied floors:

**Typical mechanical riser cross-section (office building):**
- Supply air duct: 1.0 m (W) × 0.8 m (H)
- Return/exhaust duct: 1.2 m (W) × 0.8 m (H)
- Chilled water pipes and condensate lines: 0.3 m (W) × 0.4 m (H)
- **Total riser footprint per floor: 2.5 m × 1.5 m (approximately 3.75 m²)**

**Source:** ASHRAE duct sizing standards and Philippine commercial building practice.

Multiple risers are typical for large floor plates (>2,000 m²) to limit duct runs and pressure drop.

### Electrical Cable Trays and Power Riser

Vertical power distribution requires dedicated shafts:

**Electrical riser cross-section:**
- Main cable tray (high-capacity): 0.6 m (W) × 0.4 m (H)
- Backup/secondary tray: 0.4 m (W) × 0.3 m (H)
- Grounding conductor and conduits: 0.2 m (W) × 0.2 m (H)
- **Total riser footprint per floor: 1.2 m × 1.0 m (1.2 m²)**

PD 1096 requires minimum 0.9 m wide aisles within riser shafts for cable termination and maintenance. Shaft enclosure must be fire-rated (≥ 2 hours) at each floor penetration.

### Plumbing Risers (Cold Water, Hot Water, Waste, Vent)

Plumbing vertical distribution via pipe chases or boxing:

**Typical plumbing riser assembly (per 10 floors):**
- Cold water supply: 50 mm (2") pipe + isolation valves at each floor
- Hot water return: 40 mm (1.5") pipe + circulation pump line
- Waste stack: 100 mm (4") vertical stack per 4–5 toilet groups
- Vent stack: 50 mm (2") vent stack (tied to waste, or separate)

**Total chase cross-section: 1.0 m (W) × 0.8 m (H) per riser set** (accommodates 1–2 waste stacks plus supply/return and vent).

Multiple risers reduce fixture-to-riser distance and enable code-compliant slope. Typical: one riser per 1,500 m² of floor plate.

## Horizontal Distribution

### Ceiling Plenum Depth

Ceiling plenum (space between structural slab and suspended ceiling) houses:
- HVAC ductwork (supply, return, exhaust branches)
- Electrical conduit, cable trays, and recessed light fixtures
- Plumbing lines (cold water distribution, condensate drains)
- Fire sprinkler piping and heads
- Structural coordination with post-tensioned slab or composite deck

**Minimum plenum depth:**
- _Standard office: 1.0–1.2 m_ (allows 0.6–0.8 m duct + 0.3–0.4 m clearance for cable, pipe, diffusers)
- _Medical facilities (operating rooms, ICU): 1.5–2.0 m_ (surgical lighting, specialized exhaust, discrete zones)
- _Residential: 0.8–1.0 m_ (lower load; tight coordination required)

**Source:** ASHRAE and typical Philippine commercial architectural standards (BF Homes, BGC office standards).

Floor-to-floor height must account for plenum depth + floor structure + floor finish thickness (total 4.0–4.5 m typical for office, 3.5–4.0 m for residential).

### Distribution Ceiling Heights

Core mechanical and electrical rooms may have higher ceilings to minimize equipment stacking:

**Mechanical room clear height: 3.5–4.0 m** (allows access above chiller and AHU units)
**Electrical room clear height: 3.0–3.5 m** (cables pulled overhead, switchgear depth accommodated)

## Service Corridors and Access

### Equipment Access in Mechanical Rooms

PD 1096 and design practice require:

- _Front access space (equipment servicing): 1.5 m minimum_ (for chiller, boiler, large pump removal)
- _Side access clearance: 1.0 m minimum_ (for valve adjustment, pipe wrench work)
- _Rear access: 0.5 m minimum_ (for condenser piping and wiring)
- _Door width: minimum 1.2 m_ (to permit equipment rolling entry via wheeled cart)

Chiller rooms in particular demand large door(s) or removable panels for unit replacement.

### Electrical Room Access

- _Panel clearance: 1.2 m depth in front of all MDB and breaker panels_ (per PEC working space rules)
- _Aisle width through transformer vault: 0.9 m minimum_
- _Cable tray runway: 1.0 m clearance above cable tray top_ (for cable pulling and termination work)

## Rooftop Equipment Areas

### Cooling Tower Installation

Cooling towers for chiller condenser heat rejection:

**Typical tower dimensions (500-ton cooling capacity):**
- Width × Length: 4.0 m × 3.5 m
- Height: 2.5 m (induced-draft tower with water distribution basin)
- Footprint area: 14 m²

**Clearances:**
- Intake side: 3–4 m clear (for air discharge zone)
- Maintenance access: 1.5 m around perimeter
- Vibration isolation pads: 0.3 m base elevation

**Total rooftop area (multiple towers): 150–250 m²** for large office or hospitality complex.

**Source:** Typical cooling tower specs for Philippine climate (high heat load due to equatorial humidity).

### Exhaust Fan and Air Intake Louvers

Rooftop air handling requires:
- Supply air intake louver: 2 m × 1.5 m (with 6 m vertical clearance above roof per code)
- Return/exhaust discharge: 1.5 m × 1.5 m minimum (separated from fresh air intake by 3 m lateral distance)
- Sound attenuation enclosure (if required): adds 0.5 m depth to exhaust discharge area

**Total area allocation: 50–100 m²** for a mid-rise building.

### Solar Panel and Other Renewable Energy

If rooftop solar PV is planned:
- South-facing array: 1.5–2.0 m width optimal, length varies with kW rating
- Structural supports and inverters: additional 3–5 m² equipment footprint
- Combiner box and AC disconnect: 1 m × 1 m enclosure

Does not typically conflict with cooling tower or exhaust fan zones if planned during initial architectural design.

## Basement and Podium Utilities

### Water Storage Tanks (Elevated + Ground-Level)

Large buildings employ two-tier water storage:

1. **Ground-level suction tanks (low-pressure storage):**
   - Capacity: 50–100 m³ (1–2 days supply for large buildings)
   - Dimensions: 7 m × 6 m × 2.5 m depth
   - Basement footprint per tank: 42–48 m²

2. **Rooftop elevated tanks (gravity distribution):**
   - Capacity: 20–40 m³ (backup supply, pressure head for upper floors)
   - Footprint: 5 m × 4 m × 1.8 m height

**Total basement allocation (water storage): 80–150 m²** for buildings in areas with unreliable municipal supply (common in provincial Philippines).

### Cisterns and Rainwater Harvesting (Optional)

Buildings with rainwater harvesting systems (increasingly common per PD 1096 water supply provisions):
- Collection area: large roof (>5,000 m²) can support 10–20 m³/day seasonal storage
- Cistern footprint: 4 m × 3 m × 2 m depth (12 m³ typical)
- Filtration and UV disinfection plant: 2 m × 2 m additional space

---

### Sewage Treatment Plant (STP) Basement Installation

Where municipal sewage is unavailable or secondary treatment required:

**Compact STP footprint (200–500 occupants):**
- Biological reactor: 3 m × 2 m × 2.5 m depth
- Filtration tank: 2 m × 2 m × 2 m depth
- Disinfection chamber: 2 m × 1.5 m × 1.5 m depth
- Sludge drying bed (surface area, typically rooftop or separate lot): 10–20 m²

**Total basement footprint: 50–80 m²** (plus rooftop or external sludge handling area).

**Source:** PD 1096 and Bureau of Health discharge standards for onsite treatment.

### Garbage Collection and Holding Area

PD 1096 requires dedicated waste segregation and temporary storage:

**Waste holding enclosure dimensions:**
- Area: 3 m × 2.5 m (for buildings 10–30 floors, ~200–400 occupants)
- Height: 2.5 m (includes ventilation gap above wall)
- Capacity: 5–8 cubic meters waste volume (typical 3–5 day holding period)

Design features:
- Concrete floor with 50 mm curb, sloped to drain toward basement sump
- Ventilation grate or louvered panel (to prevent odor migration to adjacent spaces)
- Access door: minimum 1.2 m wide for waste cart rollout to collection truck

## Space Allocation Rules of Thumb (Philippine Context)

### Total Non-Occupiable MEPF Area Percentage of GFA

Based on PD 1096 and typical Metro Manila commercial practice:

| Building Type | Mechanical | Electrical | Plumbing/FP | Other (Shafts, Corridors) | **Total MEPF %** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low-rise office (5–10 floors) | 2.5% | 1.5% | 1.0% | 1.0% | **6.0–7.0%** |
| Mid-rise office (15–30 floors) | 3.5% | 1.8% | 1.2% | 1.5% | **8.0–9.0%** |
| High-rise office (30+ floors) | 4.5% | 2.0% | 1.5% | 2.0% | **10.0–12.0%** |
| Residential (mixed-use tower) | 3.0% | 1.5% | 1.0% | 1.5% | **7.0–8.0%** |
| Hospitality (hotel, resort) | 5.0% | 2.0% | 2.0% | 2.0% | **11.0–13.0%** |
| Healthcare (hospital) | 6.0% | 2.5% | 3.0% | 2.5% | **14.0–16.0%** |

**Source:** Analysis of completed projects in Philippines (BGC, Makati, Quezon City), ASHRAE Guidelines, and PD 1096 space requirements.

## Design Coordination Checklist

1. **Structural & Architectural Integration**
   - MEPF spaces (mechanical penthouse, core shafts) defined in preliminary schematic design
   - Vertical alignment of risers confirmed to minimize floor-to-floor height impact
   - Rooftop mechanical areas coordinated with architectural penthouse form and setbacks

2. **Mechanical System Selection Early**
   - Chiller type (centrifugal vs. screw) selected by month 3 of design (impacts room size)
   - AHU arrangement (distributed vs. central) decided before core layout
   - Generator size and fuel autonomy finalized per PD 1096 (impacts basement footprint)

3. **Electrical Demand and Transformer Sizing**
   - Main transformer kVA determined from connected load calculation
   - MDB location chosen to minimize main cable tray runs (constrains electrical room location)

4. **Building Code Compliance**
   - PD 1096 fire-rating, accessibility, and emergency power verified
   - PD 1096 Rule IX sanitation standards confirmed (impacts STP or wastewater routing)
   - Philippine Electrical Code (PEC) working space and arc flash requirements reviewed

## Key Takeaways

1. MEPF spaces typically consume _8–12% of GFA_ in modern Philippine commercial buildings, with variation based on building type and system redundancy.

2. _Mechanical rooms (chiller, AHU) demand 3–5% of GFA_; _electrical rooms 1–2% of GFA_; _plumbing/fire systems driven by equipment sizing_.

3. Vertical risers (_mechanical, electrical, plumbing_) and horizontal plenum distribution consume an additional _2–4% of GFA_.

4. PD 1096 and PD 1058 mandate minimum fire ratings, emergency power autonomy, and environmental discharge standards that dictate equipment sizing and room allocation.

5. Early coordination with MEP engineers during schematic design ensures efficient space allocation and cost control; changes late in design (DD/CD phases) become expensive.
