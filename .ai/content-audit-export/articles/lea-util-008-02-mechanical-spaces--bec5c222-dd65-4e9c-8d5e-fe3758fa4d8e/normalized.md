# MEPF mechanical spaces

MEPF space questions are coordination and access questions. The source-safe answer is rarely a fixed percentage of gross floor area. It is whether equipment can be installed, inspected, operated, maintained, ventilated, protected from fire or water hazards, and coordinated with other utilities under the controlling code.

## Electrical Rooms And Closets

The Philippine Electrical Code requires sufficient access and working space around electrical equipment so it can be operated and maintained safely. For equipment at 600 V nominal or less that may require work while energized, the minimum working-space depth depends on voltage and condition, and the width in front of equipment is the equipment width or 750 mm, whichever is greater. The work space must allow at least 90 degree opening of equipment doors or hinged panels. Headroom about service equipment, switchboards, panelboards, or motor control centers is at least 2000 mm unless a listed exception applies.

Electrical rooms also need illumination. In electrical equipment rooms, illumination is not controlled by automatic means only. Switchboards, panelboards, distribution boards, and motor control centers are located in dedicated spaces and protected from damage. Pipes or ducts foreign to the electrical installation and requiring periodic maintenance, or whose malfunction would endanger the electrical system, are not located near service equipment, metal-enclosed power switchgear, or industrial control assemblies.

Salvan's architectural utilities text reinforces the planning point: panels, motor control centers, busduct, switchboards, transformers, and distribution centers are large, bulky, noisy, and sensitive to tampering. Space allocation must consider maintenance, ventilation, expandability, centrality, access limitation, noise, and basic adequacy. Electric closets should be stacked where practical, kept clear of other utilities, and sized for present and future panels, switches, transformers, cabinets, risers, and safe working floor area.

## HVAC And Mechanical Plant Spaces

The DOE Guidelines require cooling system design loads to be calculated using ASHRAE Handbook of Fundamentals procedures or equivalent publications. Air-conditioning equipment is sized as close as possible to the calculated space and system loads. Selection accounts for application, building construction, indoor and outdoor conditions, internal load patterns, control methods for efficient energy use, and economics.

For chilled-water systems, the DOE Guidelines expect multiple chiller units as capacity increases, good unloading characteristics, centralized monitoring and control, variable-speed or staged pumping where appropriate, and controls for temperature, zone operation, and ventilation shutoff or volume reduction when ventilation is not required. Mechanical rooms therefore need space for equipment, controls, pumps, piping, access, and maintenance, not just the equipment footprint.

Salvan also notes code-like mechanical-room concerns: elevator machine rooms or penthouses need sufficient room for repair and inspection and adequate ventilation; boiler rooms are not used for another purpose, provide clearance for maintenance, and have separated exits where required; ducts need access doors at automatic dampers, fire dampers, thermostats, and other serviceable apparatus.

## Plumbing And Fire Protection Coordination

The Revised National Plumbing Code requires drainage piping to run with practical alignment and uniform slope, protects water supply from contamination, and separates rainwater from excreta drainage unless approval is given. Fire-protection spaces must also respect RA 9514 documentation for water supplies, fire pumps, capacities, and hydraulic calculations. For MEPF coordination, this means pump rooms, risers, shafts, and closets must preserve code function, access, and separation.

## How It Is Tested

LEA questions often give a congested room and ask what should be corrected. Choose the option that restores access, clearance, ventilation, maintenance access, utility separation, fire protection, or code documentation. Avoid answers that rely on outside percentages, market-standard room sizes, or a single discipline's equipment footprint without checking the other utilities crossing the same space.
