# Design Principles: Proportion, Balance, Rhythm, Contrast, Scale, and Character

## Source-Audited Scope

Use this topic for theory questions about design principles rather than building types. The checked local sources support proportion, scale, contrast, balance, symmetry, rhythm, and related source-safe exam cues.

## Board Exam Quick Check

Before answering, identify the visual cue in the stem. Repeated units suggest rhythm. Part-to-whole ratios suggest proportion. Opposing or interacting elements suggest balance. Difference in form, color, orientation, or material suggests contrast. Human body relation suggests human scale.

## Core Concepts

- Proportion is the harmonious relation of one part to another or to the whole.
- Proportion systems in the checked source include Golden Section, regulating lines, classical orders, Renaissance theories, Modulor, Ken, and anthropometry.
- Scale is a relationship to standards. Visual scale concerns apparent size; human scale concerns dimensions and proportions of the human body.
- Contrast differentiates elements by line, form, character, mass, color, material, texture, geometry, or orientation.
- Balance is an apparent state of equilibrium. Symmetry is exact correspondence around a dividing line, plane, center, or axis.
- Rhythm is patterned repetition or alternation of formal elements or motifs.
- Character should be answered from the source-backed clue in the stem, not from personal taste.

## Real World Scenario

A municipal building has repeated columns, a larger central entry, stone walls, and human-scaled doors. The repeated columns support rhythm. The larger entry establishes hierarchy and contrast. The bay spacing and door dimensions support proportion and scale. A source-safe answer names the principle tested by the clue, not every principle visible in the building.

## LEA Use

The exam often gives one visual condition and asks for the principle. Do not choose proportion when the clue is repetition, or rhythm when the clue is equilibrium. If the stem uses human body dimensions, favor human scale or anthropometry.

## Active Recall

1. What is proportion? Harmonious relation of parts to each other or to the whole.
2. What is rhythm? Patterned repetition or alternation.
3. What is symmetry? Exact correspondence around an axis, plane, or center.
4. What is the exam trap? Answering from a familiar style label instead of the stated design cue.
