## What You Need to Know for the LEA

The architect's primary obligation to the public transcends individual projects—it encompasses a duty to maintain public health and safety, represent qualifications honestly, and report violations of professional standards that endanger the community.

## Core Concept

The Code of Ethical Conduct establishes that architects carry **civic responsibilities to the public** regardless of whether these arise from good citizenship or professional practice. This foundational obligation precedes client interests and contractual relationships. The architect must advance the safety, health, and well-being of people and communities through competent practice and honest representation.

The obligation has three critical dimensions. First, architects must maintain uncompromising standards for health, safety, and welfare in all work—this is absolute and non-negotiable. Second, architects cannot misrepresent their qualifications, experience, or expertise in any communication. Third, architects must actively report when they encounter illegal practice, unlicensed individuals posing as architects, or professional misconduct that endangers the public.

Section 1-a of the Code requires architects to "advance the safety, health and well-being of the people and the community" and contribute to preservation of architectural heritage. This establishes the public obligation as the broadest category—it supersedes all commercial interests. An architect cannot prioritize profit, convenience, or client demands if doing so compromises public safety.

Section 1-c requires compliance with government laws and regulations as a good citizen. This creates dual accountability: both to professional standards and to civil law. An architect who becomes aware of building code violations, illegal construction, or unlicensed practitioners must report these matters, even if the client requests silence.

## Key Provisions

| Provision | Source | LEA Relevance |
|-----------|--------|---------------|
| Advance safety, health, well-being | Code §1-a, RA 9266 §3 | Primary public obligation—absolute priority |
| Do not misrepresent qualifications | Code §1-f, RA 9266 §29 | Cannot claim specializations without professional consensus/years of experience |
| Abide by government laws/regulations | Code §1-c | Dual obligation to laws AND professional code |
| No paid advertisements or endorsements | Code §1-d, §1-e | Cannot solicit ads; cannot endorse building materials for compensation |
| Report illegal practice | Implicit in §1-c, §1-a | Affirmative duty when aware of violations |
| Factual materials permitted | Code §1-d | Can present factual aims/standards/progress through literature/work quality |
| Preserve architectural heritage | Code §1-a | Obligation to protect historic examples |

## Philippine Practice Context

Under RA 9266, architects registered in the Philippines assume specific public duties. Architects must report to the Board of Architecture any unlicensed individuals claiming to practice architecture—failure to report constitutes complicity in unauthorized practice. The professional identification card and signature/seal signify that the architect has personally verified compliance with safety and code requirements. In government practice, architects cannot permit non-architects to perform architectural work under the architect's seal; this protects the public from incompetent design.

The Board has emphasized that architects cannot shield themselves behind "following client instructions" when public safety is compromised. An architect who signs selead plans containing unsafe design bears full responsibility and may face suspension or revocation under RA 9266 §23.

## How It's Tested

**Pattern 1: Misrepresentation Scenarios**
- Question: An architect includes "30 years hospital design experience" when he has 5 years. This violates § 1-f because specializations must be "well known facts or sanctioned by professional consensus and years of experience."
- Correct answer focuses on the requirement for documented, widely recognized expertise.

**Pattern 2: Safety vs. Profit Conflict**
- Question: Client demands cost reduction that compromises building safety. Architect's obligation?
- Correct answer: Code §1-a and §2-c require the architect to advise the client against proceeding; this overrides commission loss.

**Pattern 3: Reporting Obligation**
- Question: Architect discovers unlicensed person preparing architectural drawings. Obligation?
- Correct answer: Must report under Code §1-c (abide by laws) and RA 9266 §29 (prohibition on unauthorized practice). Silence makes architect complicit.

**Pattern 4: Paid Endorsements**
- Question: Building material manufacturer offers fee for architect to endorse product. Permitted?
- Correct answer: Code §1-e prohibits this. Code §4-b allows only "marker discount which shall be credited to the Client"—no commissions.

<Tip>**Memory Aid: "PSR" = Public Safety Reporting.** Remember the three core elements: (P) Public safety above all else, (S) Specializations require credentials, (R) Report violations. When an LEA question involves a conflict between profit and public welfare, public welfare always wins.</Tip>
