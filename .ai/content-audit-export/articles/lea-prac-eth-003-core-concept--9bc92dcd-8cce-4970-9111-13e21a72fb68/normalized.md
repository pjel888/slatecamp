## What You Need to Know for the LEA
## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to:

- Explain the architect's primary obligation to public health, safety, and welfare as the foundational ethical duty.
- Identify the prohibition on misrepresenting qualifications, experience, or expertise.
- Describe the affirmative duty to report illegal practice, unlicensed individuals, and professional misconduct.
- Apply public-obligation ethical standards to LEA scenarios involving code violations, misrepresentation, and reporting duties.

The architect's primary obligation to the public transcends individual projects — it encompasses a duty to maintain public health and safety, represent qualifications honestly, and report violations of professional standards that endanger the community.

## Core Concept

The Code of Ethical Conduct establishes that architects carry **civic responsibilities to the public** regardless of whether these arise from good citizenship or professional practice. This foundational obligation precedes client interests and contractual relationships. The architect must advance the safety, health, and well-being of people and communities through competent practice and honest representation.

The obligation has three critical dimensions. First, architects must maintain uncompromising standards for health, safety, and welfare in all work — this is absolute and non-negotiable. Second, architects cannot misrepresent their qualifications, experience, or expertise in any communication. Third, architects must actively report when they encounter illegal practice, unlicensed individuals posing as architects, or professional misconduct that endangers the public.

Section 1-a of the Code establishes the public obligation as the broadest category — it supersedes all commercial interests. An architect cannot prioritize profit, convenience, or client demands if doing so compromises public safety.

Section 1-c requires compliance with government laws and regulations as a good citizen. This creates dual accountability: both to professional standards and to civil law. An architect who becomes aware of building code violations, illegal construction, or unlicensed practitioners must report these matters, even if the client requests silence.

## Philippine Practice Context

Under RA 9266, architects registered in the Philippines assume specific public duties. Architects must report to the Board of Architecture any unlicensed individuals claiming to practice architecture — failure to report constitutes complicity in unauthorized practice. The professional identification card and signature/seal signify that the architect has personally verified compliance with safety and code requirements. In government practice, architects cannot permit non-architects to perform architectural work under the architect's seal; this protects the public from incompetent design.

The Board has emphasized that architects cannot shield themselves behind "following client instructions" when public safety is compromised. An architect who signs sealed plans containing unsafe design bears full responsibility and may face suspension or revocation under RA 9266 §23.

## How It's Tested

**Pattern 1: Misrepresentation Scenarios**
An architect includes "30 years hospital design experience" when he has 5 years. This violates §1-f because specializations must be "well known facts or sanctioned by professional consensus and years of experience."

**Pattern 2: Safety vs. Profit Conflict**
Client demands cost reduction that compromises building safety. Architect's obligation? Code §1-a and §2-c require the architect to advise the client against proceeding; this overrides commission loss.

**Pattern 3: Reporting Obligation**
Architect discovers unlicensed person preparing architectural drawings. Must report under Code §1-c (abide by laws) and RA 9266 §29 (prohibition on unauthorized practice). Silence makes architect complicit.

**Pattern 4: Paid Endorsements**
Building material manufacturer offers fee for architect to endorse product. Code §1-e prohibits this. Code §4-b allows only "marker discount which shall be credited to the Client" — no commissions.
