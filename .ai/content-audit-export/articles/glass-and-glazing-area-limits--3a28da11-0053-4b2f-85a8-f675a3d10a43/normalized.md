## 2. Core Concepts with Mnemonic

Rule XVIII of PD 1096 governs all glass and glazing installed in buildings subject to the National Building Code. The 2004 Revised IRR refines its scope, area limitations, glazing methods, louvered window standards, and impact requirements. Five foundational principles organize this entire rule.

### Principle 1 — Scope of Application (Sec. 1801)

Per the 2004 Revised IRR, Rule XVIII, Sec. 1801, the glass and glazing provisions apply to:

1. **Exterior glass and glazing** in all occupancies **except** Groups A (residential), B (dormitories/hotels not over 3 storeys), and J (accessories/miscellaneous) that are **not over 3 storeys in height**.
2. **Interior and exterior glass** subject to **human impact** in **all occupancies without exception**.

The practical implication: a two-storey single-family residence (Group A) is exempt from exterior glazing area limits, but any interior glass panel that poses an impact hazard in that same house is still governed by Sec. 1805.

**Labeling requirements (Sec. 1801):**
- Every glass panel (each "light") must bear the **manufacturer's label** showing glass **type and thickness**, visible and legible prior to installation.
- Special performance glass — including **laminated, heat-strengthened, tempered, and insulated** units — must carry a **permanent identification** (typically etching or ceramic-fired marking) that remains **visible after the glass is installed and glazed**.

**Fixed glass deterrence (Sec. 1801):** Where a fixed glass panel is not adjacent to a wall opening and the finished floor level is the same on both sides, the building must incorporate measures to prevent people from walking into the panel. Acceptable measures per the 2004 Revised IRR include:
- Horizontal bars or railings at guardrail height
- A 450 mm opaque bulkhead at the base
- Etched, sand-blasted, or translucent glass
- Fixed planter boxes or flower bins in front of the panel

### Principle 2 — Area Limitations for Exterior Glass (Sec. 1802)

Exterior glass must safely resist both **inward and outward wind loads** for its height zone. The individual light area must not exceed the **maximum allowable area** (from the table) multiplied by the applicable **wind load adjustment factor** for the building's height zone and location.

**Glass Area Limitation Table** (per 2004 Revised IRR, Sec. 1802 — applicable to shopping malls, commercial, theaters, offices, institutional, and factory occupancies; NOT Groups A, B, or J):

| Glass Thickness (mm) | Maximum Width (m) | Maximum Length (m) |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | 1.10 and below | 1.10 and below |
| 10 | 2.25 and below | 2.25 and below |
| 12 | 3.00 and below | 3.00 and below |
| 15 | Over 3.00 | Over 3.00 |

**Safety glass trigger (Sec. 1802):** Glass panels that are **more than 600 mm wide AND at least 180 mm high**, installed adjacent to wall openings, require **safety glass** — unless a **450 mm opaque bulkhead** is provided at the base.

> **Spotting the violation in the opening scenario:** The 10 mm glass panels nearly 3.00 m wide exceed the maximum allowable width of 2.25 m for 10 mm glass. Either thicker glass (12 mm for widths up to 3.00 m, or 15 mm for over 3.00 m) must be specified, or the panel must be subdivided.

### Principle 3 — Glazing Methods (Sec. 1803)

- Glass supported on **all four edges** must be glazed with the **minimum laps and edge clearances** prescribed in Sec. 1801(2) of the 2004 Revised IRR.
- Edge clearance in fixed openings must be **at least as large as required** to accommodate wind-induced deflection and seismic drift — a critical concern given the Philippines' position in the Pacific seismic belt.
- Glass **not supported on all four edges** (e.g., point-fixed or channel-only systems): a full design must be **submitted to and approved by the Building Official** before installation.
- Glass supports are considered **"firm"** when deflection under design load does not exceed **1/175 of the span** — a benchmark shared with IBC 2021.

### Principle 4 — Louvered (Jalousie) Windows (Sec. 1804)

Per the 2004 Revised IRR, Rule XVIII, Sec. 1804:

- Regular plate, sheet, or patterned glass used in **jalousie (louvered) windows** must be:
  - Minimum **5.6 mm thick**
  - Maximum **1.20 m long**
- All **exposed glass edges** in jalousies must be **smooth** (ground or polished) to prevent laceration of hands during operation.

> **Spotting the second violation in the opening scenario:** The jalousie blades specified at 5.0 mm thick (below the 5.6 mm minimum) and 1.50 m long (exceeding the 1.20 m maximum) violate Sec. 1804 on both counts.

### Principle 5 — Impact Requirements (Sec. 1805)

The following glass installations must comply with the **impact load provisions** of Sec. 1802:
- **Frameless glass doors** (the entire panel acts as a door)
- **Glass panels installed within door assemblies**
- **Fixed glass panels** adjacent to or in the path of human circulation where impact is likely

**Exceptions — the following do NOT require impact compliance under Sec. 1805:**
1. **Bathtub and shower enclosures**, provided the glass used is one of:
   - Wire-reinforced glass, minimum **5.6 mm** thick
   - Fully tempered glass, minimum **4.8 mm** thick
   - Laminated safety glass, minimum **6.4 mm** thick
2. Glass lights with the **bottom edge 450 mm or more above finished floor level**
3. Glass lights whose **least dimension (width or height) is 450 mm or less**
4. Glass lights with a total area **≤ 1.50 sq.m**

## 4. In-Depth Technical Analysis

### Section 1801 — General Requirements

The section establishes the regulatory framework for all subsequent provisions. The exemption for Groups A, B, and J not over 3 storeys acknowledges the reality of the Philippine residential building stock — requiring engineering-grade glazing analysis for every barangay home would be administratively unworkable. However, the human impact provisions apply universally because the risk of laceration injury from broken glass is indifferent to occupancy classification.

The **labeling requirement** serves a verification function: Building Inspectors and Owners' Representatives can confirm at the point of installation that the specified glass type and thickness was actually delivered and installed. This is particularly important in the Philippines, where substitution of specified materials for cheaper alternatives during construction is a known risk.

**Permanent identification** for special performance glass addresses a deeper problem: once heat-strengthened or laminated glass is installed within a frame and sealed with sealant, it is visually indistinguishable from ordinary annelead glass. A permanent etch or ceramic marking — typically applied during manufacturing at the corner of the lite — ensures that future renovators, inspectors, or emergency responders know what they are dealing with.

The **fixed glass deterrence provisions** respond to a documented pattern of injuries in commercial buildings, particularly in shopping centers and hotel lobbies, where large unframed glass panels at corridor junctions have caused pedestrian injuries. The 450 mm opaque bulkhead option is the most architecturally neutral solution; it also provides a natural surface for interior landscaping, consistent with tropical architectural practice in the Philippines.

### Section 1802 — Area Limitations

The prescriptive dimension table in Sec. 1802 is unique to the Philippine code and reflects a simplified approach to wind load resistance appropriate for standard commercial construction. The table correlates glass thickness directly to maximum panel dimensions, eliminating the need for a full wind pressure calculation for straightforward applications.

The wind load adjustment factor, however, requires the designer to account for the building's height zone. The Philippines experiences some of the world's highest typhoon wind speeds — particularly in Areas 1 and 2 under the Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (PAGASA) wind maps, encompassing the Visayas and eastern Mindanao. Buildings in these high-wind zones must apply a reduction factor to the tabulated maximum area, effectively requiring thicker glass for the same panel size.

The **600 mm × 180 mm trigger for safety glass** is an important detail. Both dimensions must be met simultaneously: a panel 700 mm wide but only 100 mm tall does not trigger the safety glass requirement. This recognizes that very shallow glass panels (transom lights, for instance) present minimal hazard even at widths exceeding 600 mm. Importantly, the 450 mm opaque bulkhead alternative provides an architectural solution that avoids the cost premium of safety glass for standard commercial applications.

### Section 1803 — Glazing

The four-edge support requirement links glazing practice to the structural behavior of glass. Glass loaded in two-way bending (supported on all four sides) has significantly different deflection and stress characteristics than a plate supported only on two edges. The 2004 Revised IRR implicitly recognizes this by prescribing a straightforward method for four-edge systems while requiring Building Official review for all others.

The seismic drift consideration is particularly relevant in the Philippines. During an earthquake, building frames rack — columns and beams move relative to each other in a racking pattern. If glazing is too tightly fixed within the structural frame, the glass will be crushed or shattered by the frame deformation before the frame itself yields. Adequate edge clearance allows the frame to drift without loading the glass. This is a life-safety concern, as shattered glass poses a secondary hazard during and immediately after a seismic event.

The **L/175 deflection limit** for glass supports defines "firm" framing. A support element (mullion, frame member, sill) that deflects more than 1/175 of its span under design wind or seismic load will induce bending stresses in the glass it supports, as the glass follows the support deformation. Beyond L/175, glass breakage risk increases substantially. This limit aligns with IBC 2021 practice, providing a consistent benchmark for glazing systems sourced from international manufacturers.

### Section 1804 — Louvered Windows

The jalousie window remains a dominant window type in Philippine residential and commercial construction. Its popularity stems from its effectiveness in tropical climates: louvered blades can be opened to capture cross-ventilation breezes without admitting rain, a critical performance characteristic in a country receiving 2,000–4,000 mm of annual rainfall in many regions.

The **5.6 mm minimum thickness** balances structural adequacy with thermal performance. Glass blades in jalousies are supported only at their short ends (the operator mechanism clips), making them structurally equivalent to a simply supported beam under wind pressure. Thinner glass would deflect excessively or snap under the wind loads generated by typhoons passing over or near most Philippine provinces.

The **1.20 m maximum length** corresponds to the maximum unsupported span for 5.6 mm glass under the wind load conditions prevalent in the Philippines. Longer blades would require thicker glass or intermediate support — both of which compromise the operation of the jalousie mechanism.

The **smooth edge requirement** is a direct response to a common maintenance injury: jalousie blades are handled directly by building occupants when opening and closing the window, and exposed raw-cut glass edges can cause lacerations. Factory-ground or polished edges eliminate this hazard.

### Section 1805 — Impact Requirements

The impact provisions target the intersection of glass and human movement. Frameless glass doors are particularly dangerous because, when closed, they are nearly invisible in many lighting conditions; a person walking toward what appears to be an open corridor may strike the closed door at full walking speed.

The **bathtub and shower enclosure exceptions** reflect the specialized hazard environment of wet areas: occupants are barefoot, wet, and in an enclosed space with no stable footing. The three acceptable glass types (wire-reinforced, tempered, laminated) all share the characteristic that when broken, they do not produce large, sharp-edged shards. Wire-reinforced glass holds fragments in place; tempered glass dices into small, relatively harmless cubes; laminated glass holds fragments on the interlayer.

The **height exception (450 mm above floor)** is premised on the observation that below this height, a person falling — or a child running — is likely to strike the glass with body mass. Above 450 mm, the primary contact point shifts to limbs or extremities rather than torso or head, reducing the severity of potential injury.

The **dimension exception (least dimension ≤ 450 mm)** similarly recognizes that a very narrow panel cannot admit a person's torso even if broken, limiting the injury potential to lacerations rather than penetration injuries.

The **area exception (≤ 1.50 sq.m)** provides a practical threshold for small fixed panels — such as sidelights adjacent to entry doors — that present a limited hazard surface.

## 5. PH vs. USA Comparative Analysis

### Prescriptive vs. Performance-Based Approach

The most fundamental divergence between the Philippine and US approaches is in methodology. The 2004 Revised IRR adopts a **prescriptive table** tying glass thickness directly to maximum panel dimensions. This approach is administratively simple and accessible to Building Officials who may not have specialized glazing engineering backgrounds.

IBC 2021, by contrast, prescribes **no dimension limits** for exterior glass — instead requiring that glass be designed to resist wind pressures calculated per ASCE 7 (or equivalent structural analysis), with the glass type, thickness, and frame system selected to keep stress within allowable limits for the glass type. This performance-based approach allows for much larger glass panels (curtain wall systems exceeding 5.0 m per lite are common in US high-rise construction) but requires specialized glazing engineering documentation.

**Implication for Philippine practice:** Architects and engineers specifying glazing systems from international manufacturers — particularly curtain wall systems designed to IBC/ASCE 7 criteria — must verify compliance with the PH prescriptive table OR seek Building Official approval for an engineered alternative per Sec. 1803.

### Hazardous Location Categories (IBC 2406)

IBC 2021 Sec. 2406 identifies **seven specific hazardous locations** requiring safety glazing, with precise dimensional triggers:

1. Glazing in doors (IBC 2406.4.1)
2. Glazing adjacent to doors within **610 mm** (24 in) of the door opening (IBC 2406.4.2)
3. Windows with glazing area > **0.84 sq.m** (9 sq.ft) whose bottom edge is < **457 mm** (18 in) above floor (IBC 2406.4.3)
4. Glazing in guards and railings (IBC 2406.4.4)
5. Glazing in wet areas with bottom edge < **1,524 mm** (60 in) above floor (IBC 2406.4.5)
6. Glazing adjacent to stairways, ramps, and their landings within specific dimensions (IBC 2406.4.6)
7. Glazing at the bottom of stairway landings (IBC 2406.4.7)

The Philippine code in Sec. 1805 addresses categories 1, 3, and 5 in a generalized manner but does not enumerate all seven IBC hazardous location types. In particular, **glazing adjacent to stairways and ramps** (IBC 2406.4.6 and 2406.4.7) has no explicit parallel in Rule XVIII, though RA 9514 (Fire Code) and Rule XII (Exits) may impose requirements in those locations by reference.

### CPSC Impact Categories

The US Consumer Product Safety Commission standard **16 CFR Part 1201** classifies safety glazing into two categories based on both panel area and impact energy:
- **Category I:** Panels < 0.84 sq.m — must resist 150 ft-lbs (≈ 203 J) impact
- **Category II:** Panels ≥ 0.84 sq.m — must resist 400 ft-lbs (≈ 542 J) impact

The Philippine code does not reference CPSC categories or establish equivalent energy thresholds. This creates a gap for projects specifying safety glass: Philippine architects must ensure that imported or locally manufactured safety glass meets the PH thickness minimums but cannot directly verify CPSC compliance from PH code alone without reverting to manufacturer test data.

### Wire Glass

In US practice, **wire-reinforced glass has been largely phased out** of new hazardous location applications following studies showing that wire glass can cause severe lacerations despite the wire holding fragments together. IBC 2021 permits wire glass only in limited legacy fire-rated applications. The Philippine code, by contrast, still explicitly accepts wire glass in bathtub/shower enclosures (Sec. 1805, Exception 1) at a minimum 5.6 mm thickness. Architects specifying wire glass for shower enclosures in joint PH-US projects should note this divergence.

### Glass Handrails and Guards

IBC 2021 Sec. 2407 establishes specific allowable stress limits for glass used structurally in handrails and guards:
- Maximum tensile stress of **20.7 MPa** for heat-strengthened glass
- Maximum tensile stress of **41.4 MPa** for fully tempered glass

Rule XVIII of PD 1096 does not address glass handrails as a distinct category; structural design of glass guards in Philippine buildings would fall under NSCP (National Structural Code of the Philippines) provisions for structural glass or require Building Official approval as a non-standard system under Sec. 1803.

### Athletic Facilities

IBC 2021 Sec. 2408 requires all glass in **athletic facilities** — regardless of size — to meet Category II impact resistance (400 ft-lbs). This recognizes the elevated risk of high-speed ball and body impacts in sports environments. Rule XVIII has no equivalent provision; Philippine architects designing gymnasiums, squash courts, or multipurpose sports facilities with glass installations must rely on the general impact provisions of Sec. 1805 and, where applicable, manufacturer-specified sport glazing systems.

## 6. Exam Traps

### Trap 1 — The "Group A Exemption" Misread

**Wrong answer:** "The glass and glazing provisions of Rule XVIII do not apply to any glass installed in a single-family residence."

**Why it's wrong:** The exemption for Group A occupancies applies only to **exterior** glass and glazing in buildings **not over 3 storeys**. The impact provisions of Sec. 1805 apply to all occupancies without exception, including Group A. A frameless glass shower door in a one-storey house is still governed by Sec. 1805.

**Correct answer:** Groups A, B (≤3 storeys), and J (≤3 storeys) are exempt from the **area limitation** provisions but remain subject to **all human impact provisions** of Rule XVIII per the 2004 Revised IRR, Sec. 1801.

---

### Trap 2 — Confusing the Louvered Window Dimensions

**Wrong answer:** "Jalousie blades may be up to 1.20 m long and 5.0 mm thick per Rule XVIII."

**Why it's wrong:** The 2004 Revised IRR, Sec. 1804 sets the minimum glass thickness at **5.6 mm** — not 5.0 mm. The 1.20 m maximum length is correct. Choosing 5.0 mm because it is "close enough" to 5.6 mm is a common error; the board exam has historically tested this exact threshold.

**Correct answer:** Jalousie glass must be **at least 5.6 mm thick** and **no more than 1.20 m long**, per Sec. 1804 of the 2004 Revised IRR.

---

### Trap 3 — Misapplying the Impact Exceptions

**Wrong answer:** "A 0.90 m × 1.80 m fixed glass panel next to a door entrance does not require safety glass because its bottom edge is 500 mm above finished floor level."

**Why it's wrong:** The height exception in Sec. 1805 (bottom edge ≥ 450 mm above floor) applies **only when that is the sole reason the panel would otherwise be subject to impact requirements**. A 0.90 × 1.80 m panel has an area of 1.62 sq.m — **exceeding 1.50 sq.m** — so the area exception does not apply. More critically, this panel is **adjacent to a door**, placing it squarely within the scope of Sec. 1805 for panels subject to human impact in a circulation path. The height exception does not override the adjacency-to-door trigger.

**Correct answer:** The exceptions in Sec. 1805 must each be evaluated on their own merits; satisfying one exception does not override the primary applicability trigger. A panel adjacent to an entrance door at any height above floor requires safety glass unless it meets **all applicable exception criteria** simultaneously.

## 9. Cross-Referencing Section

### Rule VIII — Light and Ventilation (Window-to-Floor Area Ratios)

Rule VIII of PD 1096 establishes minimum window openings for habitable spaces: **10% of floor area for natural light** and **5% for natural ventilation**. Rule XVIII governs what kind of glass fills those openings. A design that meets the window-to-floor area ratio of Rule VIII but uses non-compliant glass thickness (Rule XVIII, Sec. 1802) violates both rules simultaneously — the code compliance review must address both. Architects should note that glazed but fixed windows may satisfy the light ratio but cannot satisfy the ventilation ratio.

### Rule XII, Section 1207 — Exits and Egress Corridors

Glass panels installed in **exit door assemblies** and in the walls of **exit corridors** are subject to Sec. 1805 impact requirements by the nature of their location in circulation paths. Additionally, RA 9514 (Fire Code of the Philippines) imposes **fire-rated glazing requirements** for glass in fire barriers and fire door assemblies — wire glass in steel frames remains the legacy fire-rated glazing solution, though ceramic glazing products now offer higher fire-resistance ratings. Rule XVIII and the Fire Code must be applied concurrently for corridor and exit door glazing.

### Rule XVI — Plastics

Rule XVI addresses the use of plastic glazing materials as an **alternative to glass** in skylights, translucent roofing, and certain partition applications. Where plastic is specified instead of glass, Rule XVIII does not directly apply, but the plastic must comply with Rule XVI's own thickness, area, and fire spread limitations. The choice between glass (Rule XVIII) and plastic (Rule XVI) carries material implications for durability, impact resistance, and maintenance in the Philippine climate.

### RA 9514 — Fire Code of the Philippines

Fire-rated glazing — glass or glass-ceramic products used in fire barriers, fire doors, and smoke compartments — must comply with fire testing standards established under RA 9514 and its IRR. The fire resistance rating (typically 45 minutes, 60 minutes, or 90 minutes) governs the selection of fire-rated glass products. Standard tempered or laminated glass does not constitute fire-rated glass; dedicated ceramic glazing or specifically tested and labeled fire-rated glass products are required. Rule XVIII's impact and area provisions still apply to fire-rated glazing panels, adding a dual compliance obligation.

### BP 344 — Accessibility Law

BP 344 (the Accessibility Law) and its implementing standards require that **glass doors on accessible routes** be readily identifiable by persons with visual impairments. This typically requires contrasting door hardware, visible frame elements, or applied markings. A frameless glass door that satisfies Rule XVIII's impact requirements may still violate BP 344 if it lacks the visual contrast markings required for accessible routes. Glass doors at main building entries serving persons with disabilities must be evaluated under both standards.

### NSCP — National Structural Code of the Philippines

The NSCP governs the **wind pressure design** that underlies the area limitation framework of Sec. 1802. The wind load adjustment factor referenced in Rule XVIII is derived from NSCP wind zone maps. For buildings in high-wind zones — particularly in the eastern Philippines — NSCP wind pressure calculations may reduce allowable panel areas below the prescriptive table values, effectively requiring thicker glass even for panels within the stated dimension limits.
