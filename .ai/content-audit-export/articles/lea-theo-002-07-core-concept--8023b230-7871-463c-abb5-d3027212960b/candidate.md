# Architectural Elements and Articulation

## Source-Audited Scope

Use this topic for theory questions about architectural elements, planes, openings, and articulation. The checked local sources support linear structural elements, roof/wall/base planes, beams, columns, form articulation, and entrance/opening treatment.

## Board Exam Quick Check

Classify the element by what it does in the stem. Columns and beams are linear support/frame cues. Wall, roof, ceiling, floor, and ground are plane cues. Openings and portals are enclosure interruptions or entry cues. Articulation is the way parts are made clear in relation to one another.

## Core Concepts

- Linear members with sufficient material strength can support structure, express movement, provide support for overhead planes, or form three-dimensional frames.
- Beams and girders span between supports and carry transverse loads.
- Columns can read as point or linear elements depending on plan and elevation, and repeated columns can establish rhythm and spatial order.
- Planes in architectural design include overhead roof/ceiling planes, wall planes, and base ground/floor planes.
- A roof plane shelters the interior from climatic elements. A wall plane can become a facade, enclosure, boundary, or public-space edge.
- Articulation is the method of joining or expressing parts clearly. It can use material, color, texture, pattern, corners, openings, and light.
- Openings can preserve wall continuity, mark an entrance, project outward, recess inward, provide shelter, or create transition.

## Real World Scenario

A public hall uses a concrete column grid, long beams, a high roof plane, masonry side walls, and a recessed glass entry. Read the columns and beams as linear support/frame elements, the walls and roof as planes of enclosure, and the recessed entry as an articulated opening that marks transition. A good answer names the element function being tested.

## LEA Use

Avoid treating every element as either structure or decoration only. The source-safe answer often recognizes multiple simultaneous roles: support, enclosure, light, entry, transition, and visual expression.

## Active Recall

1. What do beams and girders do? Span between supports and carry transverse loads.
2. What are the three generic plane types? Overhead plane, wall plane, and base plane.
3. What is articulation? A way of making joined parts clear, distinct, and precise in relation to each other.
4. What is the exam trap? Confusing articulation with mere ornament, or openings with decoration only.
