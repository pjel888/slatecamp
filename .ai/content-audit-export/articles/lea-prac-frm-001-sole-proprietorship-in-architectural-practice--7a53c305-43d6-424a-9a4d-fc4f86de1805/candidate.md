## Sole Proprietorship in Architectural Practice

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Define a sole proprietorship as an individual architectural practice form.
- Identify the source-backed registrations tied to that form.
- Separate RA 9266 professional authorization from broader business-risk claims.
- Compare the sole proprietor's individual registration posture with group practice forms.

### Definition and Source Basis

RA 9266 IRR defines a sole proprietorship as an individual Architect practicing and delivering architectural services, duly registered with the DTI, BOA, and PRC. The UAP Standards of Professional Practice likewise recognize DTI registration as the registration route for individual architectural practice by a sole proprietor.

This means the exam-safe core is registration and professional authorization, not a general business-law essay. A DTI business-name registration does not replace the architect's PRC/BOA authority. RA 9266 Section 25 separately requires a person to hold the required Board registration and Professional Identification Card before practicing architecture or using the title Architect in the Philippines.

### What the Sole Proprietor Controls

The source-backed distinction is that the practice is centered on one registered and licensed architect rather than a partnership, corporation, firm, company, or association. The sole proprietor may organize office work and engage staff, but the professional authority to practice architecture remains tied to the registered architect.

For LEA purposes, avoid treating staff employment, office size, or a trade name as proof that a separate professional entity has been formed. The safer analysis is: identify the registered architect, identify the DTI registration for the individual practice form, and confirm BOA/PRC authority for the practice of architecture.

### Claims That Need Separate Sources

Common review materials often attach broader business characteristics to sole proprietorship, such as corporate-veil language, personal-creditor exposure, insurance requirements, investment capacity, or succession mechanics. Those may be business-risk topics, but they are not established by the RA 9266 and UAP SPP excerpts used for this article.

When a question asks about RA 9266, answer from the law: individual Architect, DTI/BOA/PRC registration, and personal professional qualification. If a question asks about liability, insurance, tax, or estate continuity, require a separate source before treating the claim as exam-safe.

### Exam Use

Use this sequence:

1. Is the practice form an individual Architect? If yes, consider sole proprietorship.
2. Is the registration route DTI for the individual practice plus BOA/PRC authority? If yes, the source-backed requirement is satisfied.
3. Is the item asking for personal liability, insurance, or succession? If yes, do not answer from RA 9266 alone unless the item provides a separate business-law source.
