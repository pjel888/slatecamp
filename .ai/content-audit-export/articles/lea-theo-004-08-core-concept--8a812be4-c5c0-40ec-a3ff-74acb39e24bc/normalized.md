# Solar Geometry Fundamentals

Solar geometry turns sun movement into design decisions. In TOA 4, orientation is tied to two tasks: reducing solar heat gain and preserving airflow. Sun-path diagrams support that judgment by locating the sun through azimuth, altitude, and date lines.

## Sun-Path Reading

- Azimuth describes the sun direction around the edge of the diagram.
- Altitude describes the sun height using concentric circular lines.
- Date lines trace the sun path for a particular date from east to west.
- The designer reads the diagram to decide where shade, eaves, awnings, or louvers must respond to sun angle.

## Orientation Logic

For minimum heat gain, the checked source emphasizes shading walls from the sun as much as possible. It also notes that some northern sun may be desirable during cooler months, which is controlled through eave and awning width.

For maximum airflow, the same orientation decision must be coordinated with prevailing winds. In the Philippines, the source identifies northeast winds from October to March and southwest winds from April to September.

## Common Trap

Do not treat solar geometry as a drawing exercise only. In LEA questions, the diagram is evidence for a design choice: which face needs protection, which device fits the sun angle, and how orientation affects heat gain.
