## What You Need to Know for the LEA
## Learning Goal

Explain formwork removal timing (stripping time) requirements per NSCP 2015 based on concrete strength gain, temperature, and member type, and identify minimum curing periods before form removal for slabs, beams, walls, and columns.
## Core Concept

_Formwork_ temporarily supports fresh concrete until it achieves sufficient strength for self-support. _Scaffolding_ provides elevated access for workers and supports loads during construction. Both are temporary; they are removed after their purpose is served, but they directly impact construction quality, safety, and schedule.

### Formwork Types

_Conventional Formwork_ — Timber (coco lumber in Philippines) beams and joists supporting plywood decking. Props (vertical supports) hold the system at height. Advantages: flexible design, adaptable to custom shapes, low capital cost, reusable (~5–10 times). Disadvantages: labor-intensive assembly/disassembly, slower than system formwork, quality dependent on carpenter skill. Standard in low-rise and custom Philippine projects.

_System Formwork_ — Standardized aluminum or steel panels with integrated bracing. Rapid assembly via documented connections (minimal training required). Panels reuse 50+ times, amortizing capital cost over high-volume projects. Advantages: speed, consistency, reduced labor per floor. Disadvantages: high capital cost (~PHP 1–2 million), economical only for projects with 15+ identical floors. Standard in Philippine high-rise towers.

_Slip-Form_ — Continuously moving forms support concrete placement as the structure rises. Used for tall repetitive structures (silos, towers, cores). Specialized jacking and guide systems maintain form position as concrete hardens. Advantages: continuous progress (days/floor), minimal setup/removal. Disadvantages: high cost, specialized labor, used only for tall/repetitive geometry.

### Formwork Design

Formwork must support: (1) self-weight of fresh concrete, (2) reinforcement, (3) construction live load (workers, tools, impact from bucket placement), (4) form weight. ACI 347R specifies design loads:

- Vertical pressure from concrete: ρg×h (density × height), increased by impact
- Lateral pressure (on vertical forms): higher at depth (hydrostatic-like)
- Live load: ~2.4 kPa total design pressure (includes construction personnel, tools, and impact)
- Impact: 15–25% additional on vertical loads

Form pressure increases with concrete temperature (warmer = faster setting, lower pressure) and rate of placement.

### Stripping Time (ACI 347)

Forms supporting horizontal spans must not be struck until concrete achieves 70% of design 28-day strength per ACI 347. Typical timeline: 7–14 days for normal Portland cement at ambient temperature. Accelerated curing (heat, steam, special admixtures) reduces timeline to 1–3 days. Failure to observe minimum strength before stripping risks deflection, cracking, and structural failure.

### Scaffolding Systems

_Tubular Scaffolding_ — Steel or aluminum tubes (40–50 mm diameter) connected by clamps/welds. Highly flexible; assembled to custom configurations. Advantages: adaptable to irregular structures, reusable. Disadvantages: labor-intensive assembly, slower than frame scaffolding.

_Frame (or Cuplock) Scaffolding_ — Pre-fabricated frames with integrated connections. Vertical and horizontal tubes slot into frames. Fast assembly (training-free) compared to tube-and-clamp. Advances in "Ringlock" (newer system) allow vertical/horizontal tubes to engage at any height. Advantages: speed, consistency, safe design. Disadvantages: higher capital cost, less flexible than tube-and-clamp.

_Suspended Scaffolding_ — Hangs from roof structures or cables. Used for exterior work on facades, bridge undersides. Advantages: access to areas difficult for standing scaffolds (overhangs, suspended construction). Disadvantages: anchor points must be extremely strong, inspection critical, high safety risk if anchor fails.

_Ladder Scaffolding_ — Lightweight portable frames for low-height work (&lt;5 m). Common in small repairs and light maintenance. Limited load capacity.

### Philippine Construction Context

Philippine construction predominantly uses tube-and-clamp scaffolding (locally fabricated steel tubes ~3–4 cm diameter, Manila clamps or locally-made fasteners). Coco lumber is the formwork standard for economic reasons, though system formwork appears in high-rise projects. Safety practices vary significantly; proper OSHA-equivalent training (per NSCP 2015 Chapter 34) is not universal—common deficiency requiring enforcement.

OSHA Construction Standards 1926.500 (Scaffolding General Requirements) and 1926.501 (Duty to Have Fall Protection) specify scaffolding design, fall protection, inspection intervals, and maintenance. Philippine construction authority (DOLE) enforces similar requirements but enforcement varies by LGU.

| System (Aluminum) | Aluminum panels | 50+ | Fast (documented) | High | High-rise repetitive floors |

| Frame (Ringlock) | Steel frames | High | Fast | Medium | Moderate–High |
| Suspended | Steel cables, anchors | Medium | Slow (anchor critical) | Low | High |

|---|---|---|---|
| Horizontal spans (beams, slabs) | 70% of f'c | 7–14 days | Heat, steam, admixtures: 1–3 days |
| Vertical members (columns) | 50% of f'c | 3–7 days (normal Portland cement, ambient temp) | Accelerated: 24–48 hours |
| Supporting posts under beams | 100% of f'c | 14–28 days | Maintain until above loads fully supported |

## How It's Tested

The LEA evaluates formwork/scaffolding through:

1. _Formwork selection_ — Match type (conventional, system, slip-form) to project scale and geometry.
2. _Design loads_ — Calculate concrete pressure, live loads, impact for formwork design verification.
3. _Stripping criteria_ — Apply ACI 347 strength thresholds (70% for horizontal, 50% for vertical) to determine removal timing.
4. _Scaffolding safety_ — Identify proper system (tube-clamp vs. frame), inspection intervals, fall protection per OSHA/NSCP 2015.
5. _Philippine practices_ — Recognize coco lumber, tube-clamp assembly, and enforcement gaps in local practice.
