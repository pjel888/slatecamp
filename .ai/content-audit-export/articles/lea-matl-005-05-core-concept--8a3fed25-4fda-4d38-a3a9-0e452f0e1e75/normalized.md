# Formwork temporarily supports fresh concrete until it can hold its shape

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Formwork, scaffolding, and shoring are temporary works, but they do different jobs. Formwork shapes and supports fresh concrete until it sets. Scaffolding provides safe elevated access for workers. Shoring provides temporary support to avoid collapse or unwanted movement.

## Core Concept

- Building Construction Illustrated states that formwork for columns and walls may be custom-built or made from reusable panels.
- The formwork framework and bracing must maintain the position and shape of the forms until the concrete sets.
- Wall forms use spreaders, plywood sheathing, wood studs, horizontal walers, strongbacks where needed, sill plates, and bracing.
- Form ties keep wall forms from spreading under the fluid pressure of newly placed concrete.
- Chudley/Greeno describes scaffolds as temporary working platforms erected around a building or structure to provide safe work at height, and shoring as temporary support for existing buildings or structures.

## How It Is Tested

- Separate the purpose of formwork from scaffolding and shoring.
- Identify walers, strongbacks, spreaders, sheathing, and form ties in a formwork detail.
- Explain why wall forms need ties and bracing when concrete is newly placed.
- Avoid unsupported stripping-day percentages unless the exact standard and curing evidence are provided.

## Common Mistakes

- Do not use scaffolding and formwork as interchangeable terms.
- Do not strip forms from a source-audited answer based only on a memorized day count.
- Do not ignore form ties when the question describes wall forms spreading under fresh concrete pressure.
