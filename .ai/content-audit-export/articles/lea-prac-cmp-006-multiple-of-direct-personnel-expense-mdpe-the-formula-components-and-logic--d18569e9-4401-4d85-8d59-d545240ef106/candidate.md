## Multiple of Direct Personnel Expense (MDPE) — The Formula, Components, and Logic

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Define Multiple of Direct Personnel Expense (MDPE) and its formula.
- Identify the components of MDPE: DPE × Multiplier.
- Calculate MDPE for a given project scenario.
- Distinguish MDPE from the percentage-of-PCC method.

---
### Definition and Purpose of MDPE

MDPE calculates compensation by identifying all personnel directly engaged in project design and delivery, calculating their total cost (salaries plus labor-related benefits), and multiplying by a factor that covers the architect's overhead, profit, and non-technical project requirements. The formula is: MDPE Fee = Direct Personnel Expense × Multiplier. This approach aligns fee with effort invested, making it particularly suitable for projects with uncertain scopes, innovative solutions, or limited construction costs relative to design complexity.

### Components of Direct Personnel Expense

Direct Personnel Expense (DPE) includes only salaries and benefits of architects, engineers, and technical staff directly engaged in the project's design and delivery. Included costs are: senior architect or principal time, architect time, design architect time, junior architect or architectural technologist time, and any specialist consultants employed directly by the architect (structural engineer, MEP consultant, cost estimator, specifier). DPE includes salary costs plus labor-related benefits such as PAGIBIG contributions, PhilHealth, SSS, and other mandatory benefits as a percentage of salary.

DPE does not include: owner or partner time allocated to firm management, marketing, or business development; administrative or clerical staff not directly assigned to the project; facility costs, utilities, or equipment depreciation; insurance or licensing fees; or consultant fees for specialized consultants retained as independent contractors. These costs are covered by the multiplier.

### Determining Direct Personnel Cost

To calculate DPE, the architect must track or estimate hours spent by each team member and multiply by their fully-loaded hourly cost. Fully-loaded hourly cost = (annual salary + benefits) ÷ billable hours per year. If an architect earns 600,000 pesos annually plus 80,000 pesos in benefits (total 680,000 pesos) and works 1,800 billable hours per year, the fully-loaded hourly rate is 680,000 ÷ 1,800 = 377.78 pesos per hour.

Project personnel assignment determines DPE. If the senior architect allocates 200 hours to the project at 1,500 pesos per hour, the cost is 300,000 pesos. If the design architect allocates 400 hours at 900 pesos per hour, the cost is 360,000 pesos. If junior staff allocates 800 hours at 450 pesos per hour, the cost is 360,000 pesos. Total DPE = 1,020,000 pesos. This amount becomes the base for the multiplier calculation.

### The MDPE Multiplier Logic

The multiplier reflects costs beyond direct personnel: overhead (office rent, utilities, insurance, equipment, administrative staff), profit margin, and non-technical project costs (client meetings not involving design, administrative correspondence, invoicing, project management, coordination overhead). Multipliers in Philippine architecture practice typically range from 2.0 to 3.5, depending on the firm's overhead structure and profit objectives.

A multiplier of 2.0 assumes that overhead and profit equal the direct personnel cost. A multiplier of 3.0 assumes overhead and profit are twice the direct personnel cost. The specific multiplier depends on the firm's historical overhead rates, target profit margins, and project risk profile.

### Multiplier Components Breakdown

The MDPE multiplier can be decomposed into three components: base multiplier covering overhead costs (typically 1.5–2.0), profit component (0.3–0.7), and a risk/non-technical factor (0.2–0.5). A multiplier of 2.5 might reflect 1.8 (overhead) + 0.5 (profit) + 0.2 (non-technical factors). This breakdown helps the architect understand what the multiplier covers and adjust for project-specific characteristics.

### Selecting the Multiplier for the Project

Different project types and risk profiles justify different multipliers. Straightforward projects with clearly defined scope, experienced clients, and uncomplicated delivery may justify multipliers at the lower end (2.0–2.3). Projects with innovative design, uncertain scope, or demanding clients may justify multipliers in the mid-range (2.3–2.8). Highly specialized or exceptionally complex projects may justify higher multipliers (2.8–3.5). The architect should negotiate multipliers appropriate to the project's characteristics rather than applying the same multiplier to all projects.

### MDPE vs. Percentage-of-PCC Fee Comparison

MDPE and percentage-of-PCC produce different results depending on project characteristics. For projects with well-defined scope and reliable cost estimates, percentage-of-PCC provides simplicity and transparency. For projects with uncertain scope, limited construction cost, or design complexity disproportionate to construction cost, MDPE provides fairer compensation tied to actual effort. The architect should calculate both methods and compare results to understand which is more favorable for the specific project.

### Documentation and Transparency in MDPE Proposals

When proposing MDPE compensation, the architect should document: (1) the hourly rate structure for different staff levels, (2) the estimated hours for each staff level (or the method for determining hours if estimated during project execution), (3) the total DPE calculation, (4) the multiplier and its justification based on overhead structure and project characteristics, and (5) the resulting total fee. This documentation demonstrates transparency and allows the client to understand what the fee covers.

### MDPE Invoicing and Project Management

When using MDPE compensation on a time-and-materials basis, the architect must track actual hours carefully and invoice based on actual DPE plus the negotiated multiplier. Some MDPE contracts establish a capped fee based on estimated DPE times the multiplier; others allow true billing based on actual hours. The contract should clearly specify whether MDPE is estimated and capped or actual and variable.
