## Board Exam Quick Check

For MDPE, do not start with percentage of PCC. Start with technical hours and rates, then apply the multiplier, then add reimbursables.

## Multiple of Direct Personnel Expense (MDPE): Formula Components and Logic

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to identify when MDPE is used, state the formula components from SPP 202, and distinguish source-backed MDPE computation from unsupported payroll-benefit formulas.

---

### What MDPE Is For

SPP Document 202, Section 5.2 lists Multiple of Direct Personnel Expenses as a compensation method for Regular Design Services. The source limits its use to non-creative work such as accounting, secretarial work, research, data gathering, preparation of reports, and similar work. It explains the reason: technical hours can be measured, but the value of creative design cannot be measured merely by time spent.

That sentence is important for the board. MDPE is not simply a universal replacement for percentage-of-PCC compensation. It is a cost-based method used when the work can be reasonably tracked through technical personnel time.

### Formula Components

SPP 202 gives the variables. A is the Architect's rate per hour. C is the Consultant's rate per hour. T is the rate per hour of Technical Staff, Researchers, and others involved in the project. AN, CN, and TN are the number of hours spent by the Architect, Consultants, and Technical Staff. M is the multiplier to account for overhead and reasonable profit. R is reimbursable expenses.

The source-backed computation is:

- Direct cost = AN + CN + TN, understood in context as the cost of the relevant hours and rates.
- Fee = Direct Cost × M.
- Total Cost of Service charged to Client = Fee + R.

<ExamTip>
MDPE computation follows this order: compute direct technical personnel cost, multiply by M, then add reimbursables. Do not add reimbursables before applying the multiplier unless the problem expressly defines a different contract formula.
</ExamTip>

### Reimbursables

SPP 202 lists reimbursable items such as transportation, housing and living allowance, per diem, extra printing beyond seven copies, overseas and long-distance calls, technical and laboratory tests, licenses, fees, taxes, and similar project cost items. These are added as R after the fee is computed.

### Unsupported Expansions

The source for this batch does not define Direct Personnel Expense as salary plus SSS, PhilHealth, PAG-IBIG, and other statutory benefits. It also does not provide annual billable-hour formulas or fully loaded payroll calculations. Those may be business-accounting methods in practice, but they are not source-supported for this article unless a verified source is added.

### Real-World Scenario

Imagine an architect in Cebu is asked to prepare a research report for a campus planning study. The work involves data gathering, meetings, analysis, and report preparation rather than a full creative building design. MDPE may be appropriate because the work is measurable by technical hours. The architect records the hours of the Architect, consultants, and technical staff, applies the agreed hourly rates, obtains the direct cost, multiplies by the agreed multiplier for overhead and reasonable profit, and then adds reimbursables such as travel or extra printing. If a board question gives hours, rates, multiplier, and reimbursables, the candidate should compute in that sequence. If the question tries to make the candidate use PCC, the topic has shifted to a different compensation method.

The source-safe red flag is whether the work is creative design or measurable non-creative support work. SPP 202 allows MDPE for non-creative work such as accounting, secretarial work, research, data gathering, preparation of reports, and similar work. A full building design problem should not be forced into MDPE unless the question or agreement expressly frames the service that way.
