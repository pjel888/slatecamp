# Choosing MDPE vs Percentage of PCC: The Decision Matrix

SPP 202 does not make MDPE and percentage of Project Construction Cost interchangeable. Percentage of PCC is the method tied to a construction-cost base and the Architect's Guidelines. MDPE is a time-and-rate method for non-creative work such as accounting, secretarial work, research, data gathering, and report preparation. The safe board-exam move is to match the service pattern to the source-backed method, then avoid any rate, percentage, or multiplier that the question does not give.

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to choose between percentage of PCC, MDPE, and another listed compensation method without inventing unsupported fee rules.

## Source-Safe Decision Matrix

| Project fact in the problem | Source-safe compensation direction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regular design service with a reliable PCC and a supplied or verified fee table | Percentage of PCC | SPP 202 lists percentage of PCC for regular design services, and the fee based on PCC is detailed in the Architect's Guidelines. |
| Research, data gathering, reports, accounting, secretarial, or other non-creative technical work | MDPE | The source limits MDPE to non-creative work and computes it from direct technical cost, a multiplier, and reimbursables. |
| Continuing relationship over a series of projects | Professional fee plus expenses | The source describes a fixed amount over technical time and overhead reimbursement for continuing relationships. |
| Clearly defined government paperwork/time-heavy commission | Lump sum or fixed fee | The source specifically recognizes lump sum or fixed fee for government projects because of paperwork and time-consuming effort. |
| Meetings, trips, ocular inspections, or consultation time | Per diem or honorarium plus reimbursables | The source lists these personal-time services and keeps out-of-pocket costs separate. |
| Mixed service pattern | Mixed methods | SPP says each project should be examined to determine the most appropriate method or combination. |

## Percentage of PCC Boundary

Percentage of PCC is not a blank check to use any remembered percentage. Use it only when the problem supplies the needed fee table or enough source-backed facts to apply the method. PCC itself is the construction-cost base for the completed building and related systems, while professional fees and construction inspector salaries stay outside the PCC base.

## MDPE Boundary

MDPE starts with direct personnel cost: architect, consultant, technical staff, and researcher hours multiplied by their rates. The fee is direct cost times the multiplier, and the total cost charged to the client is that fee plus reimbursable expenses. The multiplier range in the checked source is 1.5 to 2.5. Do not use MDPE to price creative design value simply because a project feels complex; the source says creative design value cannot be measured merely by time spent.

## Real World Scenario

An owner asks an architect to run a short site-selection and feasibility study before deciding whether to proceed with a regular building design. The study phase involves research, data gathering, interviews, and a written report, so MDPE or a professional-fee-plus-expenses structure can be source-safe for that limited non-creative work. If the owner later commissions regular design services with a reliable PCC and a verified fee basis, percentage of PCC can become the better source-backed method for that later phase. The contract should keep those phases and compensation methods separate.

## Exam Traps

- Do not choose MDPE just because the project is innovative; the source limitation is non-creative technical work.
- Do not calculate percentage of PCC from a remembered rate table unless the audited source or exam item supplies the rate.
- Do not add reimbursables before applying the MDPE multiplier; the source sequence is direct cost times multiplier, then plus reimbursables.
- Do not force a mixed commission into one method when the source permits mixed methods.
