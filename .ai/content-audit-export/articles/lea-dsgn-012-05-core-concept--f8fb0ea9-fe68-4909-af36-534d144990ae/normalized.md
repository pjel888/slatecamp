# Utility Infrastructure Systems

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal
Coordinate water, sanitary, storm drainage, electrical, gas, communications, fire protection, and service-access requirements with site planning, building placement, easements, grading, and MEP rooms.

Utility infrastructure is part of site planning from the start. The checked site-analysis source requires designers to ascertain the availability of water mains, sanitary and storm sewers, gas lines, electrical power lines, telephone and cable lines, and fire hydrants. It also requires possible access points, circulation paths, drainage patterns, water features, easements, rights-of-way, adjacent streets, and municipal services to be studied.

PD 1096 site development plan requirements point in the same direction: show existing public utilities or services, existing or proposed access roads and driveways, and the proposed building position in relation to the lot and surrounding structures.

Storm drainage is not optional coordination. Surface drainage is grading and surfacing that diverts rain and surface water into natural drainage patterns or a municipal storm sewer system. Subsurface drainage uses underground piping to convey groundwater to disposal. If runoff exceeds storm sewer capacity, a holding pond may be needed.

Mechanical and electrical references warn that site and building service spaces compete with other planning needs. Parking below buildings can compete with boilers, chillers, tanks, switchgear, and vertical transportation, and parking ventilation requires fans and energy. Information, electrical, and piping distribution spaces may be smaller than air ducts, but they still need access and adaptability.

Electrical service planning also has spatial consequences. A transformer vault houses a transformer and auxiliary equipment. A switchgear room contains service equipment. One-line or riser diagrams describe the major electrical components and their spatial relationships. These are not merely engineering drawings; they affect service entry, room placement, access, and fire/life-safety coordination.

## Key Takeaways

Map utility availability before fixing the site plan.

Coordinate easements, rights-of-way, service rooms, drainage, access, and grading as one system.

Do not use unsupported water-pressure, sewer-slope, road-width, river-easement, or green-rating calculations unless the problem supplies the values or the checked source packet supports them.
