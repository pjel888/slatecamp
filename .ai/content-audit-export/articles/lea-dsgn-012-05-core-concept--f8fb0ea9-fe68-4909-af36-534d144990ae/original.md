## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal
Coordinate water supply, sewage, stormwater, electrical, telecommunications, and gas utility systems within site plans, apply easement requirements per PD 1096 and utility standards, and design utility routing that minimizes conflicts with buildings and landscape.

Site utilities and infrastructure form the hidden backbone of every development. Water supply, sewage, stormwater, electrical power, telecommunications, and gas service must be coordinated into efficient, resilient networks that serve the building while respecting public rights-of-way and environmental standards. Understanding utility routing, easement requirements, and integration logistics is essential to feasible site planning.

## Core Concept

Utility infrastructure systems move resources (water, power, data) to and from buildings while managing waste (sewage, stormwater) away from sensitive areas. Site planning must integrate these systems into a coherent network that minimizes cost, environmental impact, and conflicts with buildings and landscape.

**Water Supply System:**

Public water distribution typically reaches a site through a main line in the street or via an easement. The designer must:
- Determine supply pressure and flow rate from the municipal water authority
- Size the site's distribution network (pipes, valves, meters)
- Locate the main service connection (typically near the site entry for accessibility)
- Provide adequate pressure for fire suppression (minimum 20 psi to upper floors, though boosters may be required)
- Design storage (elevated tanks or pressure tanks) if site elevation exceeds municipal supply pressure or demand is highly variable

**Sewage (Sanitary) System:**

Wastewater from toilets, sinks, and drains must flow to a municipal sewer line (if available) or an onsite treatment system (septic tank, treatment plant). Design requirements:
- Minimum 2% slope (20 mm/m) per Revised National Plumbing Code Section 302 (Grade of Horizontal Drainage Piping); see also PD 1096 Rule IX Section 903 (Wastewater Disposal System) which mandates compliance with Revised National Plumbing Code
- No flat or inverted pipes (causes blockage)
- Separate sewage from stormwater in combined systems (where possible)
- Coordinate main connection point and easement with municipal authorities
- Verify capacity of municipal system; upgrades may be required for large projects

**Stormwater (Drainage) System:**

Rainwater is typically managed via surface drainage (slopes, swleas, channels) or underground storm pipes. Design must:
- Provide positive drainage (minimum 2% slope) away from buildings
- Route water to safe discharge point (stream, detention pond, municipal storm system)
- Size storm pipes using intensity-frequency-duration (IFD) rainfall data
- Incorporate retention/detention basins if groundwater table is high or discharge capacity is limited
- Integrate green infrastructure (bioretention, permeable paving) to reduce runoff

**Electrical System:**

Power service to the site typically arrives via overhead or underground lines. Site design must:
- Locate transformer and main electrical panel in an accessible, ventilated location
- Size distribution circuits for site demand (HVAC, lighting, equipment, EV charging)
- Coordinate with utility authority on service size and connection point
- Provide separate backup generator circuits if critical equipment (hospital, data center) requires uninterrupted power
- Ensure electrical conduit routing avoids utility conflicts

**Telecommunications:**

Data and voice services increasingly arrive via fiber optic, 5G wireless, or traditional copper lines. Design must:
- Reserve conduit space for telephone, internet, and broadband providers
- Coordinate with multiple providers (some cities require open-access conduit)
- Locate external connection points away from building entries and landscape features
- Future-proof: oversized conduits (now and future tenants' needs)

**Gas Service (if applicable):**

Natural gas arrives via a pressure-regulated main. Design must:
- Locate the main connection with pressure regulator on the site entry
- Establish a meter location accessible for reading and maintenance
- Size piping based on appliance demand (HVAC, hot water, kitchen equipment)
- Maintain safe distance from electrical lines and other utilities

**Utility Easements and Right-of-Way:**

Easements are legal rights to cross private land for public infrastructure. Key easement standards:
- **Street/Road:** 6–10m width for local roads; includes drainage, utilities, landscaping
- **Utility corridors:** 3–5m width for bundled water, sewer, electrical, telecommunications
- **River setback (PD 1067):** 3m (all waterways), 20m (rivers >20m wide), 40m (rivers >60m wide or national waterway)
- **Stormwater retention:** May require 5–10m buffer if detention pond or bioretention basin
- Easements cannot be built upon; they reduce net developable area

**Green Building and Utility Integration (BERDE Framework):**

Sustainable utilities practice reduces environmental footprint:
- **Water:** Rainwater harvesting, reclaimed water for irrigation, greywater systems reduce demand
- **Sewage:** Wastewater treatment for reuse, onsite constructed wetlands improve water quality
- **Stormwater:** Green infrastructure (swleas, bioretention) reduces runoff and pollutant load
- **Electrical:** Renewable energy (solar, wind), efficient equipment reduce grid demand
- **Telecommunications:** Shared infrastructure reduces redundancy and cost

**Utility Routing Coordination:**

Utilities must be bundled efficiently in common trenches (5–10m width) to minimize site disturbance. Typical layout (top to bottom):
1. Electrical conduit (above all wet utilities for safety)
2. Telecommunications conduit
3. Water main
4. Sewage line
5. Stormwater pipes (if underground)
6. Gas line

**Service Connection Checklist:**
- Water: Main shutoff valve accessible outside building
- Sewage: Cleanout (access point) at each change of direction
- Stormwater: Inlet grates on low-elevation walkways, overflow spillway if basin is present
- Electrical: Main disconnect switch accessible and labeled
- Telecommunications: Demarcation point (entry) accessible for provider service

## Philippine Practice Context

In the Philippines, water supply is often intermittent or pressurized only during off-peak hours, requiring onsite storage (elevated tanks common in Manila). Sewage treatment capacity is limited in many municipalities; some developments operate proprietary treatment plants. Typhoons and monsoon flooding challenge stormwater design; municipal systems are often overwhelmed, making onsite retention essential. Natural gas penetration is limited outside major cities; many sites use liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) or electrical cooking. Fiber optic and 5G infrastructure rapidly expand in urban areas but are absent in rural zones. Coordination with DPWH, MWSS (Manila Water/Maynilad), PLDT, Meralco, and local government agencies is lengthy and requires advance planning. Informal settlements often occupy utility easements, complicating rightway enforcement. Green infrastructure (bioretention, permeable paving) is increasingly mandated by environmental compliance offices, particularly in flood-prone areas.

## How It's Tested

**Pattern 1: Utility Easement and Space Allocation**
A site plan shows a street frontage with 6m road width (local road per BP 220), a utility easement, and a building setback. Identify where utilities are located, what easement area is required, and how it reduces net developable area.

**Pattern 2: Sewage System Slope and Functionality**
A site is 60m long with elevation change of 0.6m from entry to exit. Calculate the average slope and determine if gravity-fed sewage (minimum 1:100 or 1%) is feasible. If not, specify a pump station location.

**Pattern 3: Stormwater Detention and Sizing**
A 5-hectare site requires stormwater management. Given rainfall intensity (50mm/hour, typical for monsoon) and soil infiltration (slow), size a retention basin and specify discharge capacity to prevent flooding.

**Pattern 4: Water Supply Pressure and Storage**
A 12-story building requires 20 psi minimum at the top floor (12 × 4.34 psi/story = 52 psi needed at base). If municipal pressure is only 30 psi, design a pressure tank or booster pump system.

**Pattern 5: Utility Coordination and Conflict Resolution**
A site plan shows water main, sewage line, electrical conduit, and gas line all crossing in narrow trenches. Recommend rerouting or stacking to eliminate conflicts and ensure safety.
