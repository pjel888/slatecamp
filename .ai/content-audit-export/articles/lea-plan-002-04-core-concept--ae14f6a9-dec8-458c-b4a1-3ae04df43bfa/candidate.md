## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal
Apply land use zoning classifications (residential, commercial, industrial, agricultural, institutional), identify zoning ordinance as the CLUP implementation tool, apply density and intensity regulations, and distinguish zoning from other land use controls.

Land use planning categorizes how cities regulate human activity across space through zoning. The Zoning Ordinance is the legal tool that implements land use decisions made in the CLUP.


## Core Concept

Land use planning assigns legal categories to parcels of land—residential, commercial, industrial, agricultural, institutional—and establishes rules for each. The **Zoning Ordinance** is the Sanggunian-adopted law that translates Comprehensive Land Use Plan (CLUP) decisions into enforceable regulations. Unlike the CLUP (a 10-year vision document prepared by planners), the zoning ordinance is a **legislative enactment** that grants or restricts specific activities on specific sites.

A zoning regulation typically specifies: permitted uses, prohibited uses, building coverage ratios (floor-to-lot), setbacks, building height limits, and parking requirements. These are measured and enforceable. Zoning simultaneously enables development (by identifying where it is allowed) and restrains it (by banning it elsewhere). In the Philippines, **RA 7160 (Local Government Code, 1991)** mandates that every LGU prepare a CLUP, and the Zoning Ordinance is its implementation mechanism. The Sanggunian (city or municipal council) has the power to adopt the ordinance; the Planning Office drafts the technical content.

Land use categories vary by context. Residential zones typically separate single-family neighborhoods from apartment districts, each with different density allowances. Mixed-use zones permit residential and commercial on the same lot, supporting the "eyes on the street" principle of urban vitality. Industrial zones buffer manufacturing from housing. Institutional zones protect government offices, schools, and hospitals from conflicting uses. In many Philippine municipalities, agricultural zoning protects farmland at the periphery but often conflicts with sprawl pressure. The key challenge: zoning rigidity. Once categories are legally set, changing them requires political approval and often triggers property value shifts.

The Zoning Ordinance must be internally consistent—use designations must align with infrastructure capacity (roads, water, sewage) and with the CLUP's vision. A zoning ordinance that permits high-density residential in an area with no planned water system is both unenforceable and legally vulnerable to challenge.

## Philippine Context

In Philippine municipalities, zoning is often the first tool deployed to manage uncontrolled sprawl. Many CLUPs designate 60–70% of municipality as "Agricultural" at the outset, then face pressure to convert to residential as population grows. Metro Manila cities use finer gradations—"Residential 1" (low-density) vs. "Residential 3" (high-rise)—to calibrate development intensity. The Sanggunian adopts zoning ordinances by majority vote; mayoral veto exists but is rare. DHSUD (formerly HLURB) reviews the ordinance for conformity with the CLUP and national standards before it takes effect. Enforcement varies widely: well-resourced cities conduct regular zoning inspections; rural municipalities often lack the capacity to track violations.

## How It's Tested

- "A developer wants to build a 20-story residential condominium in a zone designated for 6-story maximum height. What must happen?" → The zoning ordinance must be amended by the Sanggunian, or a variance granted (requires public hearing).
- "Which authority adopts the Zoning Ordinance?" → The Sanggunian (Sangguniang Bayan or Panlungsod, depending on LGU level). The Mayor signs it, but does not initiate it.
- "The CLUP designates an area for residential use, but the Zoning Ordinance permits commercial. Is this permitted?" → No. Zoning ordinance must be consistent with the CLUP. If inconsistent, the ordinance is vulnerable to legal challenge.

<Tip>Zoning adoption = Sanggunian legislative function (not Planning Office, not Mayor alone). Zoning ordinance must align with CLUP. Use designations are enforceable rules with numeric limits (height, setback, lot size).</Tip>


**Exam Tip:** The Zoning Ordinance is a LEGISLATIVE enactment by the Sanggunian, not the CLUP. The CLUP is a planning document; the zoning ordinance is the enforceable law. This distinction is tested frequently.
