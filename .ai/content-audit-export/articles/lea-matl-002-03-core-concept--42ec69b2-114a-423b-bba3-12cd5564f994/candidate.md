## Core Concept

Masonry is built-up construction using units such as clay, concrete, or stone laid in mortar or plain concrete. A bed is the horizontal layer of mortar on which units are laid. A course is a horizontal layer of masonry units. A wythe is a vertical section of wall one unit thick, and bond describes the arrangement that ties units together.

Brick orientation is a common LEA trap. A stretcher shows the long face of the unit lengthwise in the wall. A header is laid so the short end is exposed and the unit extends through the thickness of the wall. Common bond uses repeated stretcher courses with a header course at intervals. English bond alternates stretcher and header courses. Flemish bond alternates headers and stretchers in the same course.

Concrete hollow blocks are made from water, Portland cement, and aggregate. The checked Masonry Lecture gives standard CHB sizes as 100 mm, 150 mm, and 200 mm thicknesses, with 200 mm height and 400 mm length. It also notes that 100 mm CHB is for interior partitions where weather tightness is not required. CHB should be laid on a full bed of mortar with about 10 mm joints.

## How It Is Tested

Expect identification items: bed, course, wythe, bond, stretcher, header, brick bond, and CHB size. If a question asks for compressive strength, ASTM grade, or code capacity and that exact source is not provided, do not invent the value.

## Common Mistakes

Do not confuse bed and course. Do not call a stretcher a header. Do not use unsupported compressive-strength values for this Batch 082 cluster. Do not turn a lecture-based material identification item into a code design item.
