## What You Need to Know for the LEA
## Learning Goal

Classify masonry units (concrete hollow blocks, clay bricks, solid concrete blocks) by size, compressive strength, and application, and apply NSCP 2015 minimum strength requirements for load-bearing and non-load-bearing masonry walls.
## Core Concept

Masonry units (concrete hollow blocks, clay bricks, solid concrete blocks) are the fundamental components of masonry construction.
Each unit type has specific geometric, strength, and absorption properties governed by ASTM standards and NSCP 2015. Units must meet minimum compressive strength to carry structural loads;
oversized units reduce mortar cost but may sacrifice workability and bonding.

## Concrete Hollow Block (CHB): Overview

Concrete hollow blocks are the most common masonry unit in Philippine construction.
They are made from hydraulic cement, water, and mineral aggregates (crushed stone, sand) cast into molds.
The blocks are hollow (containing vertical and horizontal voids) to reduce weight while maintaining strength and allowing for reinforcement and grout.

## ASTM C90: Loadbearing Concrete Masonry Units

ASTM C90 covers hollow and solid concrete masonry units for loadbearing applications.
The critical requirement is minimum net area compressive strength: 2,000 psi (13.8 MPa).

**Key provisions from ASTM C90:**

- Minimum net area compressive strength: 2,000 psi (13.8 MPa)
- Weight classifications: Light Weight (&lt;105 lb/ft³), Medium Weight (105—125 lb/ft³), Normal Weight (>125 lb/ft³)
- Face shell and web thickness requirements per ASTM C90 should be verified against the current ASTM C90 standard specification document.
- Absorption: Maximum 10% by weight for normal weight concrete;
  higher for light weight per standard
- Maximum linear drying shrinkage: 0.065% for all types

NSCP 2015 Chapter 7 (Masonry) references ASTM C90 for material standards. While ASTM C90 specifies a minimum of 13.8 MPa (2,000 psi) net area compressive strength, load-bearing requirements in the Philippines must always be verified against Chapter 7 and the specific project specifications.

## ASTM C129: Non-Loadbearing Concrete Masonry Units

ASTM C129 covers hollow and solid concrete masonry units intended for non-loadbearing applications only (partition walls, non-structural infill, veneer backup).
The compressive strength requirement is lower: 600 psi (4.1 MPa) minimum net area.
Non-loadbearing units are lighter, have thinner webs, and are more economical but cannot be used in structural walls or where vertical load is applied.

## Solid Concrete Blocks and Solid Grouted Construction

Solid concrete blocks have no internal voids and can be fully grouted (grout-filled) for enhanced strength and stiffness.
A solid grouted cell develops much higher compressive strength than a hollow unit with air voids because grout fills the space.
In reinforced concrete masonry, vertical reinforcement is placed in grouted cells, transferring tension loads through the reinforcement and compression loads through the grout and unit combination.
Grouted cells must meet minimum size: typically 1.5 inches (38 mm) net width for placement of reinforcement.

## Philippine CHB Manufacturing and Market Standards

Philippine concrete hollow block manufacturers typically produce blocks conforming to ASTM C90 or equivalent.
Common compressive strengths in the local market are:

- 4-inch blocks: 8—12 MPa (mainly for non-loadbearing use)
- 6-inch blocks: 12—16 MPa (for light to medium loads)
- 8-inch blocks: 15—22 MPa (for heavy loadbearing and reinforced structures)

Most Philippine CHB is produced from Class A concrete (fine aggregates) and Class B concrete (recycled aggregates or lower quality).
The quality and strength depend on cement type, water-cement ratio, curing duration, and aggregate source.

## Clay Bricks: ASTM C62

ASTM C62 covers clay brick (solid or cored) for loadbearing construction.
Clay bricks are kiln-fired at high temperature (1,900—2,200°F), resulting in excellent durability and weather resistance.
**Compressive strength requirement:** Minimum 3,000 psi (20.7 MPa) average, with individual units not less than 2,500 psi (17.2 MPa).
**Advantages of clay brick over CHB:**

- Higher compressive strength (20.7 MPa typical vs. 13.8 MPa for CHB)
- Excellent weather and freeze-thaw resistance
- Superior fire resistance and durability (50+ years service life)
- Aesthetic variety and color options

**Disadvantages:**

- Higher cost (2—3 times CHB in the Philippines)
- Heavier (approximately 8 lbs/unit vs. 3—4 lbs for CHB)
- Less availability in the Philippines;
  primarily used for heritage restoration and high-end projects

Clay brick is not common in typical Philippine construction due to cost;
CHB dominates residential and commercial markets.

## Concrete Masonry Units: Physical Properties and Defects

### Common Defects and Quality Issues

**Spalling and Surface Deterioration:** Frost damage (freeze-thaw cycles) causes surface layers to chip and flake.
In the Philippines, spalling is less common due to warm climate but occurs in coastal areas exposed to salt spray.
**Efflorescence:** White mineral deposits (calcium carbonate, sodium sulfate) appear on block surfaces when moisture carrying dissolved salts migrates through the unit and evaporates on the surface.
Efflorescence does not affect structural integrity but indicates excessive moisture; it is controlled through proper drainage and mortar joint sealing.
**Cracking:** Diagonal cracks across blocks indicate overstress or settlement. Vertical cracks in aligned units suggest differential movement or tension.
Horizontal cracks near mid-height suggest shear failure. Cracks wider than 0.3 mm (0.012 inch) are visible and require investigation.
**Moisture Absorption:** High absorption (above 10%) increases weight and can lead to efflorescence and mold in damp environments.
The Philippines' high humidity favors lower-absorption units with good drainage.

## Compressive Strength: Net vs. Gross Area

Compressive strength is reported in two ways:

- **Gross area strength:** Measured on the entire unit (including voids).
  Lower value, not used for design.
- **Net area strength:** Measured on solid portions only.
  This is the design value used in NSCP 2015.

If a unit has 40% void area, net area compressive strength is approximately 1.67 times gross area strength.
This distinction is critical: a unit failing on net area strength is unsuitable for loadbearing, even if it appears sound.

## Philippine Construction Context

In the Philippines, 8 × 8 × 16 inch (200 × 200 × 400 mm) concrete hollow blocks are standard.
Most residential and commercial buildings use CHB with compressive strength in the 12—16 MPa range.
Reinforced concrete frames with CHB infill are the most common structural system.
NSCP 2015 Chapter 7 requires:

- Minimum net area compressive strength for loadbearing CHB conforming to standards cited in Section 703.
- Minimum f′m (specified compressive strength of masonry) determined by unit strength and mortar type.
- Joint reinforcement and detailing per NSCP 2015 Chapter 7 structural requirements.

In load-bearing masonry design, f′m (masonry compressive strength) is determined from unit strength and mortar type using tabular values or unit test data.
A typical f′m for 8 × 8 × 16 CHB with Type N mortar is 3.5—4.5 MPa, significantly lower than individual unit strength due to mortar joint weakness and unit-to-mortar bonding variability.

## How It's Tested

The LEA expects you to:

1. Identify unit types and nominal dimensions from descriptions or images
2. Distinguish loadbearing (ASTM C90) from non-loadbearing (ASTM C129) units
3. Apply compressive strength requirements from ASTM standards and NSCP 2015
4. Calculate or estimate unit weight based on size and material
5. Recognize common defects and quality issues in masonry units
6. Understand why net area compressive strength is the controlling criterion for design
7. Match unit type and strength to structural applications (partition wall vs. load-bearing column)

Typical questions present a wall detail with a specified CHB size and ask whether it meets code requirements for a
given load. Some questions show defective units and ask whether they are acceptable.
