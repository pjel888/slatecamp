# Top 50 Traps 2024 IRR
### LEA Review | BP 344 2024 IRR | PD 1096 | RA 7277/9442/10754
---

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to:

Master the 50 most frequently tested exam traps in the 2024 Revised IRR of PD 1096: distinguish the correct code reference from plausible distractors; apply the strictest requirement rule across conflicting provisions; and identify hidden conditions — exceptions, footnotes, and 'unless otherwise specified' clauses — that reverse the general rule.
---

## CATEGORY A: RAMPS & SLOPES (Traps 1–10)

---

**TRAP #1: The Slope Confusion**
- **WRONG:** 1:12 slope is the Philippine standard for accessible ramps
- **CORRECT:** **1:20** is the preferred/ideal slope; **1:15** is acceptable; **1:12** is the *maximum* allowed (BP 344 2024 IRR App. A, Ramps)
- **WHY it's tricky:** "1:12" appears in U.S. ADA standards and is the Philippine maximum — examinees confuse the maximum with the standard. The IRR uses 1:20 as preferred and 1:15 as acceptable, only allowing 1:12 where space constraints apply.

---

**TRAP #2: Maximum Ramp Run — Philippines vs. ADA**
- **WRONG:** Maximum ramp run between landings is 9,000 mm (9 m) — from U.S. ADA
- **CORRECT:** Maximum ramp run in the **Philippines (BP 344)** is **10,500 mm (10.5 m)** (BP 344 IRR Ramps)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Philippine reviewers often cite ADA figures. The PH standard is 10.5 m before a rest landing is required.

---

**TRAP #3: Landing Length — 1500 vs. 1800 mm**
- **WRONG:** Ramp landings are 1500 mm long (confusing with stair landings)
- **CORRECT:** Ramp landings (top, bottom, intermediate) are **1800 mm** in length (BP 344 IRR Ramps)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Stair landings and approach clear floors are 1500 mm; ramp landings are 1800 mm. The LEA often mixes these in the same question.

---

**TRAP #4: Ramp Clear Width — 1000 vs. 1200 mm**
- **WRONG:** Minimum ramp clear width is 1000 mm
- **CORRECT:** Minimum ramp clear width is **1200 mm** (BP 344 IRR Ramps)
- **WHY it's tricky:** 1000 mm is the minimum for limited access routes; ramps require the wider 1200 mm to allow a wheelchair plus a guide.

---

**TRAP #5: Edge Protection — Height**
- **WRONG:** Ramp edge protection (kerb/barrier) is 50 mm high
- **CORRECT:** Edge protection on ramps is **100 mm minimum** on both open sides (BP 344 IRR Ramps)
- **WHY it's tricky:** 50 mm is seen in some older publications. The 2024 IRR and standard Philippine practice require 100 mm.

---

**TRAP #6: Mixing Handrail Heights on Ramps**
- **WRONG:** Ramp handrails are at 900 mm only (one rail)
- **CORRECT:** Ramps require **dual handrails at 700 mm and 900 mm** (BP 344 IRR Ramps)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Single-rail answers appear frequently. Always: ramps = two rails = 700 mm (lower) + 900 mm (upper), both continuous.

---

**TRAP #7: Handrail Extension — Length at Top and Bottom**
- **WRONG:** Handrail extends 300 mm horizontally past the bottom riser only
- **CORRECT:** At the **top** of a ramp: extends **300 mm** horizontal; at the **bottom**: extends **300 mm** *plus the slope run beyond the ramp* (BP 344 IRR Ramps/Stairs)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Top and bottom extensions are different. The bottom extension follows the slope before going horizontal, which adds more than a flat 300 mm.

---

**TRAP #8: Slope Exception for Renovation**
- **WRONG:** All existing buildings must be retrofitted to 1:20 slope
- **CORRECT:** For **alterations/renovations** to existing buildings where space is limited, the slope may be **steeper than 1:20** but still **must not exceed 1:12** (BP 344 IRR General Provisions)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Examinees apply new-construction standards to renovation questions. The IRR provides relief for retrofit conditions — but 1:12 remains the hard maximum in all cases.

---

**TRAP #9: Ramp vs. Curb Ramp Width**
- **WRONG:** Curb ramps must also be 1200 mm wide
- **CORRECT:** Curb ramp minimum width is **900 mm** (exclusive of flared sides) (BP 344 IRR Site/Curb Ramps)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Ramps in buildings = 1200 mm; curb ramps at sidewalks = 900 mm. Different elements, different minimums.

---

**TRAP #10: Ramp Landing Width**
- **WRONG:** Landing width may differ from ramp width
- **CORRECT:** Landing width must be **at least equal to the ramp width** (minimum 1200 mm) and length is **1800 mm** (BP 344 IRR Ramps)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Students memorize only one dimension (1800 mm length) and forget that width must equal or exceed ramp width.

---

## CATEGORY B: STAIRS & HANDRAILS (Traps 11–18)

---

**TRAP #11: Stair Handrail Height — BP 344 vs. PD 1096**
- **WRONG:** Stair handrails are at 800–900 mm (citing PD 1096)
- **CORRECT:** For **accessible** stairs under BP 344: **dual rails at 700 mm and 900 mm** (BP 344 IRR Stairs); PD 1096 standard stairs use 800–900 mm
- **WHY it's tricky:** This is the most-tested conflict. BP 344 governs when accessibility is in question. For any LEA question about an "accessible staircase," choose 700 mm and 900 mm.

---

**TRAP #12: Maximum Stair Riser Height**
- **WRONG:** Maximum riser height is 200 mm (citing PD 1096 general)
- **CORRECT:** For **accessible** stairs under BP 344: maximum riser is **150 mm** (BP 344 IRR Stairs)
- **WHY it's tricky:** PD 1096 allows risers up to 200 mm for general occupancies; BP 344 accessible stairs cap at 150 mm.

---

**TRAP #13: Minimum Stair Tread Depth**
- **WRONG:** Minimum tread is 250 mm
- **CORRECT:** Minimum tread for accessible stairs is **300 mm** (BP 344 IRR Stairs)
- **WHY it's tricky:** 250 mm appears in some NBC-era tables. BP 344 accessible standard is 300 mm.

---

**TRAP #14: Nosing Luminance Contrast**
- **WRONG:** Nosing contrast requirement is 50% luminance contrast (same as tactile strips)
- **CORRECT:** Stair nosing requires **30% luminance contrast** from the adjacent surface (BP 344 IRR Stairs)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Tactile strips require 50% contrast; nosings only require 30%. Easy to swap on a multi-choice exam.

---

**TRAP #15: Stair Clear Width**
- **WRONG:** Accessible stair clear width is 1200 mm (same as ramp)
- **CORRECT:** Accessible stair clear width is **1500 mm** (BP 344 IRR Stairs)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Ramps = 1200 mm; stairs = 1500 mm. Memorize the pair: ramp 1200 / stair 1500.

---

**TRAP #16: Stair Landing Interval — Accessible vs. PD 1096**
- **WRONG:** Stair landings are required every 3600 mm (3.6 m) vertical rise (citing PD 1096)
- **CORRECT:** Accessible staircases under BP 344 require a landing at every **3 m** vertical rise (BP 344 IRR Stairs); PD 1096 general stairs allow 3600 mm
- **WHY it's tricky:** BP 344 is more restrictive (3 m vs. 3.6 m). For any accessible stair question, 3 m is correct.

---

**TRAP #17: Clear Space Before/After Staircase**
- **WRONG:** No specific clearance needed at stair entry/exit
- **CORRECT:** Minimum **1200 mm clear space** before and after an accessible staircase (BP 344 IRR Stairs)
- **WHY it's tricky:** This dimension is rarely mentioned in review books but appears in the IRR. The 1200 mm allows a wheelchair user or person with mobility aid to position before the stair.

---

**TRAP #18: Raised Edge on Stairs**
- **WRONG:** Only one side of a stair needs edge protection
- **CORRECT:** Raised edge **100 mm minimum on both sides** of accessible stairs (BP 344 IRR Stairs)
- **WHY it's tricky:** "Both sides" is the key phrase. Similar to ramp edge protection — same 100 mm dimension.

---

## CATEGORY C: ELEVATORS (Traps 19–25)

---

**TRAP #19: Elevator Car Dimensions — Standard vs. Transport**
- **WRONG:** All accessible elevators are 1200 × 1400 mm (W × D)
- **CORRECT:** Standard accessible elevator car: **1200 mm (W) × 1400 mm (D)**; elevators in **transport facilities** (terminals, transit): **1500 mm (W) × 1400 mm (D)** (BP 344 IRR Sec. 5.6)
- **WHY it's tricky:** The transport facility elevator is wider to accommodate larger luggage and power wheelchairs. Both have the same 1400 mm depth.

---

**TRAP #20: Old IRR Elevator Width**
- **WRONG:** Accessible elevator car width is 1100 mm (old IRR)
- **CORRECT:** Under the **2024 IRR**, minimum is **1200 mm** wide (BP 344 2024 IRR Sec. 5)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Pre-2024 IRR specified 1100 mm; updated 2024 IRR increased this to 1200 mm. Older review materials still show 1100 mm.

---

**TRAP #21: Where the Wheelchair Turns**
- **WRONG:** The wheelchair user turns inside the elevator car
- **CORRECT:** The wheelchair turn is made in the **elevator lobby** (1800 × 1800 mm), not inside the car (BP 344 IRR Sec. 5.3)
- **WHY it's tricky:** The car is only 1200 × 1400 mm — not large enough for a 1800 mm turning radius. The lobby provides the turning space.

---

**TRAP #22: Braille on Elevator Door Jambs**
- **WRONG:** Braille floor identification is placed on one (either) side of the elevator door jamb
- **CORRECT:** Braille must be placed on **both sides** of the elevator door jamb, at **1200 mm AFF** (BP 344 IRR Elevators)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Questions say "on the jamb" — the key trap is whether it's one or both jambs. Always both.

---

**TRAP #23: COP (Car Operating Panel) Height**
- **WRONG:** COP controls may be anywhere from 900–1200 mm AFF
- **CORRECT:** COP height is fixed at **900 mm AFF** — this is the specified mounting height (BP 344 IRR Elevators)
- **WHY it's tricky:** The 900–1200 mm band applies to ATM controls; the COP is fixed at 900 mm.

---

**TRAP #24: Elevator Handrail Placement**
- **WRONG:** Elevator car handrails are on two walls (side walls only)
- **CORRECT:** Accessible elevator car handrails are on **three walls** — both side walls and the rear wall (BP 344 IRR Elevators)
- **WHY it's tricky:** "Two walls" sounds reasonable. The IRR requires three walls for stability.

---

**TRAP #25: Visual Emergency Communications**
- **WRONG:** Audio emergency communication (intercom) is sufficient in an elevator
- **CORRECT:** The 2024 IRR requires **visual emergency communications** in addition to audio, to serve deaf and hard-of-hearing users (BP 344 2024 IRR Elevators)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Visual communication (flashing indicator, text display) is a 2024 IRR addition that older materials omit.

---

## CATEGORY D: TOILETS & WASHROOMS (Traps 26–32)

---

**TRAP #26: Grab Bar Height — Above Seat vs. AFF**
- **WRONG:** Grab bar is mounted at 800 mm AFF (i.e., measuring from floor)
- **CORRECT:** Grab bar is mounted **300 mm above the toilet seat** (measured from top of seat to top of bar); this typically results in approximately 750 mm AFF, **not** 800 mm (BP 344 IRR Sec. 6)
- **WHY it's tricky:** The reference dimension in the IRR is relative to the seat top, not the floor. Knowing the seat is 450 mm AFF, add 300 mm = approximately 750 mm AFF — a common distractor is 800 mm.

---

**TRAP #27: Accessible WC Cubicle Shape**
- **WRONG:** Accessible toilet cubicle is 1800 × 1800 mm (a square)
- **CORRECT:** Accessible WC cubicle is **1800 mm wide × 2250 mm deep** (BP 344 IRR Sec. 6)
- **WHY it's tricky:** 1800 mm is the turning circle dimension everywhere else; the WC cubicle is NOT a square — it is 1800 × **2250**.

---

**TRAP #28: Shower Curb / Threshold Maximum**
- **WRONG:** Shower threshold may be up to 25 mm or 20 mm
- **CORRECT:** Accessible shower threshold/curb maximum is **12 mm** (same as door threshold) (BP 344 IRR Sec. 6)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Standard bathroom thresholds and shower curbs are often much higher. The accessibility standard caps both doors and showers at 12 mm.

---

**TRAP #29: Emergency Call Button Location**
- **WRONG:** Emergency call button in toilet must be placed at 900–1200 mm AFF (operable range)
- **CORRECT:** Emergency call button must be placed at **400–600 mm AFF** so it is reachable from the floor by a fallen user (BP 344 IRR Sec. 6)
- **WHY it's tricky:** 900–1200 mm is the standard operable controls range; the emergency button defies this rule because it must be reachable when the person is on the floor.

---

**TRAP #30: Urinal Lip Height**
- **WRONG:** Urinal front lip is at 450 mm AFF (same as toilet seat)
- **CORRECT:** Urinal front lip height is **350–400 mm AFF** (BP 344 IRR Sec. 6)
- **WHY it's tricky:** 450 mm is the toilet seat height. The urinal lip is lower (350–400 mm) to allow wheelchair approach.

---

**TRAP #31: Lavatory Rim vs. Grab Bar Height**
- **WRONG:** Lavatory rim height is the same as grab bar height
- **CORRECT:** Lavatory rim maximum = **800 mm AFF**; grab bar height ≈ **750 mm AFF** (300 mm above 450 mm seat) — different fixtures, different heights (BP 344 IRR Sec. 6)
- **WHY it's tricky:** 800 mm and 750 mm are easily swapped. Lavatory rim ≤ 800 mm; grab bar ~ 750 mm.

---

**TRAP #32: Mirror Bottom Edge**
- **WRONG:** Mirror bottom edge should be at 900 mm AFF (eye level standing)
- **CORRECT:** Mirror bottom edge maximum is **1000 mm AFF** — low enough for a seated wheelchair user to see their reflection (BP 344 IRR Sec. 6)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Standing eye level = ~1500 mm. The mirror must serve both seated and standing users, requiring the bottom edge at ≤ 1000 mm.

---

## CATEGORY E: DOORS & HARDWARE (Traps 33–37)

---

**TRAP #33: Door Clear Width — Clear vs. Nominal**
- **WRONG:** Specifying a 900 mm door is sufficient for accessibility
- **CORRECT:** **900 mm is the minimum CLEAR width** — measured from the face of the fully-open door to the stop. A nominal 900 mm door provides only ~875 mm clear. Specify **1000–1100 mm nominal** to achieve 900 mm clear (BP 344 IRR Doors)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Architects often specify the nominal frame size; the code requires clear (net) width.

---

**TRAP #34: Door Threshold Height**
- **WRONG:** Thresholds up to 25 mm are acceptable if beveled
- **CORRECT:** Maximum threshold height is **12 mm**; if between 6–12 mm it must be **beveled at 1:2** or less steep (BP 344 IRR Doors)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Many reviewers cite 25 mm from older standards. BP 344 2024 IRR sets a hard maximum of 12 mm.

---

**TRAP #35: Kick Plate Coverage**
- **WRONG:** Kick plates cover the full height of the door bottom
- **CORRECT:** Kick plates are **300–400 mm in height** (a band, not full height), made of durable material, on doors along major accessible circulation routes (BP 344 IRR Doors)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Full-height kick plates seem more protective but are not specified. The exam tests the specific range (300–400 mm).

---

**TRAP #36: Vision Panel Minimum Width**
- **WRONG:** Vision panels (glass lights in doors) have no minimum width in the code
- **CORRECT:** Vision panel width: **not less than 100 mm** (BP 344 IRR Doors)
- **WHY it's tricky:** This small detail is rarely emphasized in review sessions but appears in the IRR and can be a differentiating question.

---

**TRAP #37: Frameless Glass Door Markings**
- **WRONG:** Frameless glass doors only need markings at eye level (1500 mm AFF)
- **CORRECT:** Frameless glass doors require safety markings between **900 mm and 1500 mm AFF** — a band that covers both seated and standing users (BP 344 IRR Doors)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Single-height answers are wrong; the code requires a band that is visible at wheelchair-seat height (900 mm) up to standing eye level (1500 mm).

---

## CATEGORY F: SIGNAGE & TACTILE (Traps 38–43)

---

**TRAP #38: Tactile vs. Nosing Contrast Percentage**
- **WRONG:** All contrast requirements in BP 344 are 30% luminance contrast
- **CORRECT:** **Tactile strips (floor, wall) require 50% luminance contrast**; **stair nosings require 30% luminance contrast** (BP 344 IRR App. B / Stairs)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Two different percentages for two different elements. Tactile = 50%; Nosings = 30%.

---

**TRAP #39: Tactile Surface Type Confusion**
- **WRONG:** All tactile tiles are the same (just different colors)
- **CORRECT:** The IRR specifies **distinct patterns by function**:
  - **Domes (circular bumps) = Warning/Danger** — stop, hazard ahead
  - **Bars (elongated ridges) = Directional** — follow this path
  - **Staggered domes = Positional** — landmark, decision point
- **WHY it's tricky:** Questions will describe a tactile surface by its shape and ask its meaning — or ask which type to use at a stair top. At stair tops = warning domes.

---

**TRAP #40: Tactile Warning Strip Depth and Setback**
- **WRONG:** Tactile warning strip depth is 300 mm
- **CORRECT:** Tactile warning strip is **600 mm deep** and placed **300 mm back** (setback) from the hazard edge (BP 344 IRR App. B)
- **WHY it's tricky:** 300 mm is the setback distance, not the depth. The strip itself is 600 mm deep.

---

**TRAP #41: Signage Mounting Height**
- **WRONG:** Signs must be mounted at 1800 mm center AFF (eye level for standing adult)
- **CORRECT:** General signage maximum mounting height is **1500 mm center AFF** (BP 344 IRR Signage / Sec. 5)
- **WHY it's tricky:** 1800 mm is the mounting height for the **dressing room ISA symbol** only — a specific exception. The general rule for signs is 1500 mm.

---

**TRAP #42: Braille on Elevator Jambs — Height**
- **WRONG:** Braille floor identification is at 900 mm AFF (same as COP)
- **CORRECT:** Braille on elevator door jambs is at **1200 mm AFF** on **both sides** (BP 344 IRR Elevators)
- **WHY it's tricky:** 900 mm = COP; 1200 mm = Braille jamb. Two different heights in the same elevator.

---

**TRAP #43: Projecting Sign Clearance**
- **WRONG:** Signs projecting from walls must clear 2000 mm (headroom standard)
- **CORRECT:** Projecting signs and overhanging objects must provide **2200 mm minimum vertical clearance** from the floor (BP 344 IRR Signage)
- **WHY it's tricky:** 2000 mm is stair/ramp headroom; projecting signs must clear 2200 mm to allow safe passage by a person using a cane or who cannot see the sign.

---

## CATEGORY G: PARKING & SITE (Traps 44–47)

---

**TRAP #44: Accessible Parking Stall Dimensions**
- **WRONG:** Accessible parking stalls are 2500 × 5000 mm (standard car stall)
- **CORRECT:** Accessible parking stall is **4000 mm wide × 5000 mm long** (BP 344 IRR Parking)
- **WHY it's tricky:** The stall is significantly wider than a standard car space because it includes an access aisle or embeds the aisle in the wider stall dimension.

---

**TRAP #45: Shared Aisle Width**
- **WRONG:** No specific aisle is required if the stall is wide enough
- **CORRECT:** Accessible parking requires a **minimum 1800 mm access aisle** beside the stall (BP 344 IRR Parking)
- **WHY it's tricky:** The 1800 mm aisle allows a ramp-equipped van or a wheelchair user to deploy and maneuver beside the vehicle.

---

**TRAP #46: Vertical Clearance**
- **WRONG:** Standard 2100 mm parking structure clearance is sufficient
- **CORRECT:** Accessible parking must provide **2300 mm minimum vertical clearance** for raised-roof vans (BP 344 IRR Parking)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Standard parking clearance is often 2100–2200 mm. The 2300 mm requirement is exclusive to accessible spaces.

---

**TRAP #47: Required Number of Accessible Parking Spaces**
- **WRONG:** One accessible space per facility regardless of total count
- **CORRECT:** The count follows the BP 344/DPWH standard ratio table; typically **1 accessible space per 25 regular spaces** for commercial facilities; exact thresholds specified in IRR Parking provisions (BP 344 IRR Parking)
- **WHY it's tricky:** Examinees often cite "one per facility" or use ADA tables. Use the Philippine ratio.

---

## CATEGORY H: LODGING & ASSEMBLY (Traps 48–50)

---

**TRAP #48: Accessible Guest Room Percentage — Old vs. New**
- **WRONG:** Accessible guest rooms = 1 per 50 units up to 150; +1 per 100 thereafter (OLD IRR formula)
- **CORRECT:** Under the **2024 IRR**: **2% of total guest rooms** shall be accessible (BP 344 2024 IRR Sec. 17)
- **WHY it's tricky:** The 1-per-50 formula was the pre-2024 standard. The 2024 update simplifies this to a flat 2%. Both formulas are tested — know which exam cycle applies.
- **BONUS:** Government-owned facilities must provide **10% accessible units**.

---

**TRAP #49: Assembly Wheelchair Seating Percentage — Old vs. New**
- **WRONG:** Wheelchair spaces follow a tiered table: 4–50 seats → 2 spaces; 51–300 → 4 spaces; 301–500 → 6 spaces; >500 → +1 per 100
- **CORRECT:** Under the **2024 IRR**: **3% of total seating capacity** shall be wheelchair spaces (BP 344 2024 IRR Sec. 4 App. D)
- **WHY it's tricky:** The old tiered table is still commonly cited. The 2024 IRR replaces it with a simple 3% rule.
- **BONUS:** 5% of general waiting area seating must be designated priority seats for PWDs.

---

**TRAP #50: Wheelchair Space Dimensions — Assembly**
- **WRONG:** Wheelchair spaces in cinemas/assembly areas are the turning circle size (1800 × 1800 mm)
- **CORRECT:** Each wheelchair viewing position is a **clear, firm, level footprint of 900 mm wide × 1200 mm long** for front/rear entry (BP 344 2024 IRR Sec. 4.5)
- **WHY it's tricky:** The 1800 × 1800 mm turning circle is for circulation; the individual viewing slot is 900 × 1200 mm. The 1800 mm space is needed to access it but is not the space itself.

## QUICK-FIRE TRAP ELIMINATION TABLE

| If the question mentions… | Watch for this trap |
|--------------------------|---------------------|
| Ramp slope "standard" | 1:20 preferred, 1:12 is MAX — not the standard |
| Stair handrail height | BP 344 = 700/900 dual; PD 1096 = 800–900 single |
| WC cubicle dimensions | 1800 × **2250** — not a square |
| Emergency button in toilet | 400–600 mm — not 900–1200 mm |
| Ramp landing vs. stair landing | Ramp = 1800 mm; Stair = 1500 mm |
| "All accessible elevators" | Transport elevators are wider (1500 mm) |
| Elevator Braille location | **Both** jambs at 1200 mm |
| Sign mounting height | 1500 mm general; 1800 mm only for dressing room ISA |
| Tactile contrast | Tactile strip = 50%; nosing = 30% |
| Guest room count | 2% (2024 IRR); old = 1/50 formula |
| Assembly WC seating | 3% (2024 IRR); old = tiered table |
| Wheelchair "space" in assembly | 900 × 1200 mm footprint |
| Curb ramp vs. building ramp width | Curb = 900 mm; building = 1200 mm |
| Projecting sign vs. headroom | Sign = 2200 mm; headroom = 2000 mm |

<LawCallout code="pd1096" section="2024 Revised IRR of PD 1096">


The BP 344 2024 IRR introduced several changes that differ from earlier versions and from US ADA standards. Critical updates: preferred ramp slope is 1:20 (not 1:12), maximum ramp run is 10,500 mm (not 9,000 mm), and ramp landing length is 1800 mm (not 1500 mm). The LEA favors questions that test whether you know the current IRR value versus the commonly cited older or US value.


</LawCallout>

<Warn >

TRAP: When a question states 'per the Philippine accessibility code' and gives a ramp slope of 1:12, check if they're testing the MAXIMUM vs. the PREFERRED. 1:12 is the maximum allowed, 1:20 is the preferred, 1:15 is acceptable. Many examinees incorrectly state 1:12 as the standard because it matches US ADA.

</Warn>

<ExamTip >

The LEA loves close numerical distractors. For parking: is it 3700 mm or 4000 mm stall width? (4000 mm is correct). For corridor: is it 1500 mm or 1800 mm? (1500 mm is correct). For landing: is it 1500 mm or 1800 mm? (1800 mm is correct). Memorize the exact values — the difference of 300 mm is the trap.

</ExamTip>

<Tip >

Mnemonic: '20-15-12' = Ramp slopes from best to max. '18-15-12' = Landing, Corridor, Ramp widths in hundreds of mm. '10.5' = max ramp run in meters. Say: 'Twenty preferred, fifteen okay, twelve if you must.'

</Tip>
