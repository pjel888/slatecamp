# The Triple Nature of Architecture

## Source-Audited Scope

Use this topic as the source-safe foundation for architecture as art, science, and purposeful service. The checked local sources support architecture as the art and science of designing buildings, design as a combination of practical and aesthetic concerns, and the Vitruvian requirements of durability, convenience, and beauty.

## Board Exam Quick Check

When a question asks what makes architecture different from isolated art or isolated technical work, choose the option that integrates use, structure or performance, and aesthetic judgment. Do not choose an answer that says beauty alone can compensate for failure of function or durability.

## Core Concepts

- Architecture is source-backed as the art and science of designing buildings.
- Design must combine practical and aesthetic concerns to be satisfactory to an individual or community.
- The checked Vitruvius source names durability, convenience, and beauty as required references for building.
- The local theory source gives the Vitruvian triad as firmitas, utilitas, and venustas.
- Architectural space should be conditioned to suit human needs, not treated only as solids in space.

## LEA Use

Triad questions usually test integrated judgment. The safe answer avoids one-sided thinking: a building should stand, serve its purpose, and provide an appropriate aesthetic/spatial experience. If a stem describes a beautiful but unsafe or unusable building, it fails the integrated idea of architecture.

## Active Recall

1. What is architecture in the checked theory source? The art and science of designing buildings.
2. What three Vitruvian qualities are source-backed? Durability/convenience/beauty, also taught as firmitas/utilitas/venustas.
3. What is the exam trap? Letting one dimension, usually beauty, excuse failure in the others.
