Biophilic design is pattern-based connection to nature. The checked source set supports visual and non-visual nature, thermal and airflow variability, water, dynamic light, and biomorphic forms, while local tropical references keep those patterns tied to shade, ventilation, humidity control, material culture, and maintenance.

## Board Exam Quick Check
Use nature patterns for comfort, perception, and use; reject plant-only decoration and unsupported quantified health or productivity claims.

## Core Concept
Biophilic design is not a styling layer. In tropical architecture it becomes credible when nature-related patterns improve orientation, shade, airflow, daylight quality, sensory richness, and daily use without creating moisture, safety, or maintenance risks.

## Real World Scenario
A school courtyard can use planting, shaded edges, filtered daylight, water only where maintenance and mosquitoes are controlled, and airflow variability. The result is biophilic because nature supports comfort and orientation, not because plants were added as decoration.

## Key Facts
- Terrapin patterns include visual and non-visual connection with nature.
- Thermal and airflow variability is a recognized biophilic pattern and fits tropical passive design when controlled.
- Presence of water can be sensory, but maintenance and mosquito risk matter in local projects.
- Dynamic and diffuse light can connect users to nature without glare or heat gain.
- Biomorphic forms and patterns are source-safe as a design pattern, not as an unsupported exact numerical claim.

## Common Mistakes
- Exact fractal-dimension ranges, quantified benefit percentages, and unsupported architect quotes were removed.
- Decorative plant-only claims were replaced with pattern-based, climate-aware biophilic design guidance.
