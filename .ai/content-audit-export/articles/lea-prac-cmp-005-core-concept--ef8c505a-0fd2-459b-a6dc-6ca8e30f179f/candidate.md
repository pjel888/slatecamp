# Percentage of PCC Method and Regular Design Service Compensation

For Regular Design Services, the safer exam anchor is not a memorized shortcut. Start with the service scope, the project classification, the Project Construction Cost (PCC), and the agreed compensation method.

SPP 202 organizes Regular Design Services into six phases: Project Definition, Schematic Design, Design Development, Contract Document, Bidding or Negotiation, and Construction. The compiled UAP Standards give the working detail: schematic design includes a Statement of Probable Project Construction Cost; design development updates that statement; contract documents set out detailed drawings and specifications; bidding or negotiation includes bid documents and contractor procurement assistance; and the construction phase includes interpretation of contract documents, change orders, site visits, and certificates for payment.

The percentage-of-PCC method is one allowed compensation method. The compiled SPP says that the architect's fee may be based on a percentage of PCC, with details supplied by the Architect's Guidelines. The same source also lists other methods: multiple of direct personnel expenses, professional fee plus expenses, lump sum or fixed fee, per diem or honorarium plus reimbursable expenses, and mixed methods.

PCC means the cost of the completed building to the owner, including building systems and items shown in the plans, designs, drawings, and specifications prepared by the architect and consultants. PCC does not include the architect's fee, engineer's fee, specialist consultant fees, or salaries of construction inspectors.

Project classification still matters. The compiled SPP groups work by complexity and creative skill, with Groups 1 through 10 covering simple industrial buildings, moderate-complexity buildings, exceptional-complexity buildings, residences, monumental buildings, repetitive work, housing projects, extensive detail/interiors/development planning, alteration or renovation work, and technical consultation.

The ethical gate is the service agreement. The Code of Ethics requires the architect to ascertain the exact nature and scope of services and the corresponding professional charges at the start of the client relationship, and to charge a professional fee commensurate with the work involved and professional standing. RA 9266 also treats forced architectural services without a written contract or service agreement as prohibited conduct.

Use this order in a fee problem: identify the service scope, classify the project, define PCC correctly, select the compensation method, then apply only rates and arithmetic that are actually supplied by the exam fact pattern or the audited source.
