# Spanish Colonial Laws Of The Indies

## Urban Instruments

Spanish colonial urbanism in the Philippine history source is presented through reduccion, encomienda, a hierarchy of cities and towns, cuadricula, and colonial infrastructure. Reduccion consolidated scattered barangays into larger compact communities to support religious conversion and cultural change. Encomienda divided the colony into parcels assigned to an encomendero.

## Settlement Pattern

The system of cities and towns placed the cabecera or poblacion as the municipal core, with barrios as adjacent barangays. Intramuros is described as a walled city patterned after European fortresses and reserved for nobility and clergy, while extramuros refers to life beyond the walls, including pueblos, Parian, and Dilao.

## Laws Of The Indies And The Plaza

Cuadricula is the grid pattern of streets and blocks. The Laws of the Indies source lists an elevated location, orderly grid, central plaza, defensive wall, and zones for churches, shops, government buildings, hospitals, and slaughterhouses. The plaza complex places the main plaza at the center, surrounded by the church, tribunal, other government buildings, and marketplace.

## LEA Takeaway

For exam items, connect the Laws of the Indies to settlement control: grid, plaza, civic-religious center, defensive order, and functional zoning. Do not reduce it to a decorative style; it is an urban planning framework.
