## Hook Scenario

You are the architect of record for a new 800-seat performing arts center in Quezon City — a complex with a full fly gallery, gridiron, and a dedicated motion picture projection room for recorded performances. The building official's technical staff returns your plans with two major comment items.

First: the stage ventilator area you specified is 8 sq.m, but the stage floor area within the proscenium walls is 180 sq.m. Your reviewer pulls out a calculator and shows you that 5% of 180 sq.m is 9.00 sq.m — your ventilator is undersized by 1 sq.m. The comment also flags that your proscenium wall terminates flush with the auditorium roof, when the code requires it to extend at least 1.20 m above the roof line.

Second: the projection room is 6.00 sq.m — fine for one projector, except you specified two projectors in the equipment schedule. The minimum is 7.00 sq.m for the first machine plus 3.50 sq.m for each additional — meaning you need at least 10.50 sq.m. The room has only one door. The code requires two exits, separated by at least one-third of the room's perimeter.

Both comments arise directly from PD 1096, Rule XII, Sections 1213 and 1214. Getting these numbers wrong on the exam — or in practice — is the kind of error that delays a Certificate of Occupancy by months.

## Core Concepts

### Concept 1 — Stage Ventilators (Sec. 1213.1)

Every stage must have **one or more ventilators** made of metal or incombustible material, positioned **near the center above the highest part of the working stage**. The total ventilation area must equal at least **5% of the stage floor area within the stage walls**.

Additional requirements:
- All glass within the ventilator must be **protected against falling** (wire glass or protective screen)
- **Manually controlled ventilators** are operated by hand winches — the wire must be accessible from both the stage floor and a position outside the stage
- The ventilator serves as a **smoke exhaust** during a fire event, protecting evacuation routes by allowing hot gases and smoke to escape upward rather than banking down into the auditorium

**The 5% Rule is the single most-tested metric in Sec. 1213.** If the exam gives you a stage floor of 200 sq.m, the minimum ventilator area is 10.00 sq.m — nothing less.

### Concept 2 — Gridirons (Sec. 1213.2)

Gridirons are the structural framing systems above the stage from which scenery, lighting, and rigging are suspended. Per PD 1096:
- **Materials:** Incombustible only (steel, iron)
- **Fire protection of steel/iron may be omitted** — the code recognizes that steel in this location does not require a cementitious or intumescent coating, as the stage ventilator provides smoke/heat relief
- **Live load:** Must be designed for a minimum of **367 kg/sq.m**

This 367 kg/sq.m is a high live load requirement — it accounts for the dynamic, concentrated loads of rigged counterweight systems, full scenery drops, and lighting battens. For comparison, typical floor live loads for offices are 240–480 kg/sq.m; the gridiron is in the heavy range precisely because the consequences of failure during a live performance are catastrophic.

### Concept 3 — Proscenium Wall (Sec. 1213.4)

The proscenium wall is the fire-separation barrier between the stage (where the highest fire risk exists due to scenery, curtains, and pyrotechnics) and the auditorium (where the audience is seated). Its requirements are among the strictest in the NBC:

- **Fire rating:** Minimum **2-hour incombustible construction**
- **Extension above roof:** Must extend **≥ 1.20 m above the auditorium roof** — this prevents fire from spreading over the top of the wall by rolling across the roof
- **Permitted openings:**
  - The main proscenium opening (the stage aperture)
  - Maximum **1 opening** at orchestra pit level
  - Maximum **2 openings** at stage floor level, each **≤ 2.00 sq.m**
- **All openings:** Protected by **1.5-hour fire assemblies** (fire doors/shutters)
- **Main proscenium opening:** Must have a **self-closing fire-resistive curtain** (the "iron curtain") — this is an automatic drop curtain that descends in the event of a fire to seal the opening

The self-closing curtain is triggered by a fusible link or automatic fire detection system; it must descend by gravity without power, making it a fail-safe device.

### Concept 4 — Stage Floor Live Load and Platforms (Sec. 1213.5–1213.6)

**Stage floor:**
- Construction per the building's type of construction and occupancy requirements
- **Live load:** Minimum **620 kg/sq.m**
- **Trap doors:** Permitted; must be of wood with minimum **50 mm nominal thickness**

This 620 kg/sq.m live load is among the highest in the NBC — it accommodates rolling loads from heavy set pieces, motorized stage lifts, and the possibility of large cast or chorus members concentrated in a small area during performance.

**Platforms (Sec. 1213.6):**
A platform is a raised performance area that does **not** have a fly gallery, gridiron, or rigging systems — it is architecturally simpler than a full stage. Per the code:
- Platforms are **subject to the same ventilator requirements** (5% of floor area) as stages — with one critical exemption:
- **Enclosed platforms ≤ 45.00 sq.m** are **exempt** from the ventilator requirement

This 45 sq.m threshold is a critical exam data point. A small platform in a school auditorium (e.g., 6 m × 7 m = 42 sq.m) does not need a stage ventilator. A platform that is 7 m × 7 m (49 sq.m) does.

### Concept 5 — Motion Picture Projection Rooms (Sec. 1214)

Section 1214 applies to **ribbon-type film wider than 22 mm** and to **electric arc projection equipment** — essentially, commercial cinema projection booths. The code recognizes the severe fire hazard of combustible film stock and electrical arc equipment in an enclosed space.

**Construction requirements:**
- Minimum **1-hour fire-resistive construction** throughout
- Walls and ceiling: **incombustible materials**
- Minimum ceiling height: **2.40 m**
- Minimum floor area: **7.00 sq.m** for the first projector, plus **3.50 sq.m** for each additional machine

**Exit requirements:**
- Minimum **2 doorways**, separated by a distance of **≥ 1/3 of the room's perimeter**
- Each doorway: minimum **750 mm wide × 2.00 m high**
- Doors: **self-closing 3/4-hour fire assembly**; must swing **outward**; maximum width **750 mm**; **no latches** (panic-free egress)

**Port sizes:**
| Port Type | Maximum Size |
|---|---|
| Projection port | 750 sq.cm |
| Observation port | 1,300 sq.cm |
| Combination port (max 3 allowed) | 750 mm × 600 mm each |
| Projection port (acetate safety film only) | Up to 4,500 sq.cm |

**Ventilation:**
- **Fresh-air inlet:** ≥ 900 sq.cm area, located **within 50 mm of the floor level** (draws cool air from floor level to exhaust heat upward)
- Exhaust ducts: incombustible material; clearance of **25 mm** from combustible material or covered with **10 mm insulation**

**Shutters:**
- Minimum **2.4 mm sheet metal**
- Overlap each other by **≥ 25 mm**
- **Fusible link** required over each projector magazine

## Cross-Referencing

**1. PD 1096, Rule XII, Sec. 1212 — Fire-Extinguishing Systems**
Stages present among the highest fire risks of any occupancy type. The fire-extinguishing system requirements of Sec. 1212 interact directly with stage design: stages with occupant loads exceeding 500, or where exits are > 30.00 m from dispersal areas, require sprinkler or equivalent systems. In the IBC, stages > 93 sq.m (1,000 sq.ft) trigger a Class III wet standpipe requirement per IBC 905.3.4. The proscenium curtain in Sec. 1213.4 is the passive counterpart to the active fire suppression required by Sec. 1212.

**2. PD 1096, Rule VII — Occupancy Classification**
Stages and assembly halls are classified under **Group H (Public Assembly)**:
- Group H, Division 1: Theaters with stage (fixed seating, stage, fly gallery) — the most regulated
- Group H, Division 2: Theaters without stage
- Group H, Division 3: Other assembly occupancies with fixed seating
- Group H, Division 4: Assembly without fixed seating

Platforms in school auditoria may fall under Group H, Division 4. Motion picture projection rooms within a theater are typically considered ancillary to Group H, Division 1. Understanding the division governs which supplemental fire, egress, and structural requirements apply.

**3. PD 1096, Sec. 1207 — Exits and Travel Distance**
Stage exit requirements (Sec. 1213.7) mandate at least one 900 mm exit from each side of the stage directly to a passageway leading to the street. This is separate from, and cumulative with, the audience egress requirements of Sec. 1207. An architect must design two parallel egress systems: one for the audience and one specifically for stage performers and crew.

**4. PD 1096, Rule XIII, Sec. 1302 — Mechanical Regulations**
Fly galleries and gridirons use motorized rigging. Mechanical equipment in the stage tower (counterweight arbors, motorized flying systems) must comply with Sec. 1302 regarding hoists and cranes — specifically the requirement for automatic stops at dead ends and electric brakes on power cutoff.

**5. RA 9514 — Fire Code of the Philippines**
The Fire Code supplements Section 1213 with requirements for the fire safety management plan of assembly occupancies. Any theater or performing arts venue must have a designated Fire Safety Officer, fire safety inspection compliance, and a fire emergency plan — all prerequisites for the BFP to issue the Fire Safety Inspection Certificate (FSIC) required before the building official can issue the Certificate of Occupancy.

## Active Learning Techniques

### Memory Aid 1 — "5% Vent, 367 Grid, 620 Floor"
Three critical load/area numbers for stages in ascending order:
- **5%** of floor area → stage ventilator
- **367 kg/sq.m** → gridiron live load
- **620 kg/sq.m** → stage floor live load

Think of it as "5, 367, 620" — the ventilator percentage is the smallest, the gridiron is moderate, and the stage floor must take the most because it carries actual performers and sets.

### Memory Aid 2 — "1.20 Above, 2-Hour Wall, 1.5-Hour Door"
Proscenium wall in three numbers:
- Extension: **1.20 m** above auditorium roof
- Wall rating: **2-hour** incombustible
- Opening assembly: **1.5-hour** fire-rated

These three numbers increase in reverse order of the physical position: the top extension (1.20 m) → the wall itself (2-hour) → the assembly at the openings (1.5-hour).

### Memory Aid 3 — "7 + 3.5 per Machine"
Projection room size: start with **7.00 sq.m** (one projector), then add **3.50 sq.m** for every additional projector. Two projectors = 7.00 + 3.50 = **10.50 sq.m** minimum.

### Memory Aid 4 — "45 Exempt, 45 Travel"
- Platform **≤ 45 sq.m** → exempt from ventilator requirement
- Travel distance **45 m** → maximum without sprinklers (from Article 22)

Both involve the number 45 in different contexts. Keeping them paired prevents confusion while reinforcing both articles simultaneously.

### Memory Aid 5 — "Port Sizes: 750–1,300–4,500"
Projection room port maximum areas:
- **Projection port: 750 sq.cm**
- **Observation port: 1,300 sq.cm** (almost double the projection port)
- **Safety film projection port: 4,500 sq.cm** (six times the standard)

The progression 750 → 1,300 → 4,500 reflects increasing openness: you see through the observation port more than the projection beam needs, and safety film allows a much larger aperture because it is non-flammable.

### If/Then Scenarios

**If** the exam states a stage with a floor area of 150 sq.m:
**Then** the minimum required stage ventilator area is **7.50 sq.m** (5% × 150 sq.m).

**If** the exam describes a platform that is 40 sq.m and fully enclosed:
**Then** no stage ventilator is required — the platform is below the 45.00 sq.m exemption threshold.

**If** the exam describes a platform that is exactly 45.00 sq.m:
**Then** the ventilator is **not required** — the threshold for exemption is **≤ 45.00 sq.m**, so 45 sq.m is exactly at the exempt boundary.

**If** the exam asks about a projection room with 3 projectors:
**Then** minimum floor area = 7.00 + (2 × 3.50) = **14.00 sq.m**.

**If** the exam states the projection room has only 1 exit:
**Then** it is a code violation — **2 doorways** are required, separated by ≥ 1/3 of the room perimeter (Sec. 1214.3).

## Exam Traps

**Trap 1 — Gridiron vs. Stage Floor Live Loads**
The exam will offer both 367 and 620 kg/sq.m as answer options in a question about "stage" live load. The critical distinction:
- **Gridiron (overhead rigging structure): 367 kg/sq.m**
- **Stage floor (where performers stand): 620 kg/sq.m**

The stage floor must support greater live loads because it carries direct concentrated loads from heavy rolling scenery and cast members. The gridiron carries distributed rigging loads that are high but distributed over a wider framing structure. Confusing these two is the most common error in Sec. 1213 questions.

**Trap 2 — Platform Ventilator Exemption: "Enclosed" AND "≤ 45 sq.m"**
The ventilator exemption requires **both** conditions: (1) the platform must be **enclosed** and (2) it must be **≤ 45.00 sq.m**. A 40 sq.m platform that is open on one side to the audience does not qualify as "enclosed" and may require a ventilator depending on interpretation. An exam question phrasing "an open platform of 40 sq.m" should prompt careful reading — the "enclosed" qualifier matters.

**Trap 3 — Projection Room Door Direction and Latch Rules**
Section 1214.3 requires projection room exit doors to swing **outward**, be a maximum of **750 mm wide**, and have **no latches**. The exam will offer "inward swing" or "latched" as plausible-looking distractors. The no-latch rule is critical for life safety: in an emergency, a panicked occupant should not need to manipulate a latch to exit. The outward swing maximizes egress speed. These are non-negotiable provisions and the exam specifically targets them.
