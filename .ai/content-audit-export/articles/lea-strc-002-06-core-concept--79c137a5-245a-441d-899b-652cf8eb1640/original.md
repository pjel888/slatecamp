Understanding their definitions, characteristics, and typical applications is important for recognizing system types on drawings and in building descriptions. A **space frame** (or 3D truss) is a three-dimensional skeletal structure of members meeting at nodes, typically forming triangulated or pyramidal patterns. Unlike 2D trusses, space frames extend load-carrying capacity in all three dimensions, allowing efficient coverage of large areas with minimal depth. Members are primarily in axial stress (tension or compression).

**Long-span systems** are structural forms designed to bridge distances of 30 meters or more without intermediate supports. These include:
- **Cable-stayed structures**: Cables run from towers or masts to the deck, directly supporting it. Cables are in tension; towers are in compression.
- **Suspension structures**: Main cables hang in a catenary shape, supporting the deck from above. Economical for very long spans (100+ m).
- **Barrel vault or arch systems**: Curved forms that distribute loads through compression along the curve, reducing bending moments.
- **Hybrid systems**: Combinations of trusses, cables, and arches optimized for specific spans and loads.

**Advanced structural forms** include:
- **Geodesic domes**: Spherical or hemispherical frames of great-circle chords, highly efficient for large enclosed areas.
- **Tensile structures**: Fabric or membrane elements held under tension by cables or rigid frames; used in roofs and facades.
- **Pneumatic structures**: Air-pressurized membranes that resist loads through internal pressure; lightweight and fast to deploy.
- **Folded plates**: Planar elements bent or folded to increase bending stiffness; used in roofs and bridge decks.

These systems are chosen for their ability to span large distances, minimize self-weight, and achieve architectural expression. Choice depends on span, load, material availability, climate, and aesthetics.

## Key Provisions

| System Type | Typical Span Range | Load Path Logic | Primary Stress Type | Material Common |
|----------|-------------------|-----------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Space Frame (3D Truss)** | 20–60 m | Triangulated load distribution in 3D | Axial (tension/compression) | Steel, aluminum |
| **Cable-Stayed** | 100–500 m | Cables in tension support deck; tower in compression | Tension (cables), compression (tower) | Steel cable + concrete/steel tower |
| **Suspension** | 200–2000+ m | Main cables hang; vertical suspenders support deck | Tension (cables), compression (towers) | Steel cable + steel/concrete deck |

| **Folded Plate** | 30–80 m | Bent plates act as interconnected beams | Bending in plates; shear transfer between folds | Concrete, steel |

## Philippine Practice Context

Space frames are used in large warehouse roofs, exhibition halls, and airport terminals. Long-span cable-stayed systems are rare in the Philippines but may be encountered in major bridge projects. Suspension bridges exist but are limited to specific high-impact projects. Geodesic domes and tensile structures appear in high-profile architectural projects and specialty buildings (stadiums, museum roofs). Arch and folded-plate systems are used in bridges and civic structures. Design of these systems requires specialized analysis and often involves non-linear, large-displacement modeling.
