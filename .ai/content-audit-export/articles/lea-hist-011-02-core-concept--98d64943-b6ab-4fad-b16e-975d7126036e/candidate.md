# Colonial Architecture in Africa

## LEA Point

Colonial and postcolonial African architecture should be read as a set of urban, political, climate, and identity responses. The exam risk is treating Africa as one architectural category or making unsupported claims about individual buildings.

## Source Anchors

- Asmara: UNESCO anchors it as a modernist African city shaped by Italian colonial planning and architecture. It is the source-safe example for colonial modernist urban form in this batch.
- Rabat: UNESCO supports the pairing of a modern capital with historic urban fabric under a protectorate-era planning context.
- Gando Primary School: Francis Kere's office and Pritzker source material support the climate response: clay/cement hybrid bricks, an overhanging roof, passive ventilation, and local participation.
- Makoko Floating School: NLE identifies Kunle Adeyemi/NLE as the designer of a prototype floating structure for the Makoko waterfront community in Lagos.
- Monument of the African Renaissance: official Senegalese source material supports the Dakar monument as a state identity/pan-African symbol.

## Exam Pattern

Questions usually ask for one of three contrasts: colonial planning versus local traditions, early postcolonial modernist symbolism versus climate-responsive local material work, or contemporary African design responses to heat, water, urbanization, and identity.

## Guardrails

- Avoid saying colonial architecture never adapted to climate or culture. Some cities combined imported planning with local conditions, but often within unequal colonial power structures.
- Do not use weak building anecdotes when a UNESCO city record can answer the question more safely.
- Do not merge Kere and Adeyemi into one approach: Gando is a climate/material/community school case; Makoko is a water-urbanism prototype.

## Real World Scenario

A school in rural Burkina Faso and a floating community prototype in Lagos both answer climate stress, but the design constraints differ. Gando's problem is heat, light, cost, and local material skill; Makoko's problem is waterfront settlement, flood exposure, and urban informality.
