## Board Exam Quick Check

Before choosing a seismic coefficient, identify the NSCP seismic zone named by the problem. Do not rename the zones or import older training shorthand.

## Learning Goal

By the end of this article, you will be able to:

- State the NSCP 2015 seismic zones used for Philippine site classification.
- Match Zone 2 and Zone 4 to their seismic zone factor Z values.
- Identify the Zone 2 provinces and the default Zone 4 assignment for the rest of the country.
- Explain why Z directly scales a base-shear calculation when all other factors are unchanged.

## Core Concept

NSCP 2015 Section 208.4.4.1 says the Philippine archipelago is divided into two seismic zones only. Zone 2 covers Palawan, except Busuanga, plus Sulu and Tawi-Tawi. The rest of the country is Zone 4. Each structure is assigned a seismic zone factor Z from Table 208-3.

The source-safe values for this article are:

| NSCP seismic zone | Location rule | Z factor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zone 2 | Palawan except Busuanga, Sulu, and Tawi-Tawi | 0.20 |
| Zone 4 | The rest of the Philippines | 0.40 |

Use the NSCP 2015 zone names: Zone 2 and Zone 4, not Zone 1 and Zone 2. For exam arithmetic, if the base-shear form is presented as V = ZICW, doubling Z doubles V when I, C, and W stay the same.

## Key Provisions

1. Site seismic hazard characteristics are established from seismic zone, proximity to active seismic sources, site soil profile, and importance factor.
2. The Philippines has two seismic zones only under Section 208.4.4.1.
3. Zone 2 is the limited exception: Palawan except Busuanga, Sulu, and Tawi-Tawi.
4. Zone 4 is the default for all other Philippine locations, including Metro Manila, Cebu, Davao, and most exam examples.
5. Table 208-3 assigns Z = 0.20 to Zone 2 and Z = 0.40 to Zone 4.

## Real World Scenario

A project team preparing a school design in Davao should not treat Davao as part of the lower zone simply because the site is outside Luzon. Under Section 208.4.4.1, Davao is in the rest-of-country category, so the Zone 4 factor Z = 0.40 controls the seismic coefficient input.

## How It's Tested

Common exam items ask for the lower-zone provinces, the correct Z factor for Manila or Davao, or the ratio between Zone 4 and Zone 2 base shear. The safe shortcut is: Zone 2 is the named exception list; Zone 4 is everywhere else; 0.40 is twice 0.20.

## Common Mistakes

- Calling the NSCP 2015 zones Zone 1 and Zone 2. The OCR-supported NSCP text uses Zone 2 and Zone 4.
- Assigning Batanes or major cities to the lower zone by intuition. The code text assigns the rest of the country to Zone 4.
- Explaining the province list with an assumed geological story instead of the code assignment.
- Treating the Z factor as a small lookup note. In base-shear arithmetic, Z scales the lateral design force directly.
