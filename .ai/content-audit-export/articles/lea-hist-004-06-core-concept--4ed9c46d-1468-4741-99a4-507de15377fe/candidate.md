# Ottoman architecture (15th-19th centuries)

## LEA Point
Ottoman architecture is tested through the centralized domed mosque and the kulliye. For the LEA, connect Mimar Sinan, Selimiye, Suleymaniye, Hagia Sophia influence, half-domes, pendentives, controlled light, and the social complex around the mosque.

## Source Anchors
UNESCO source-maps the Selimiye Mosque and its Social Complex in Edirne as an Ottoman mosque complex associated with Mimar Sinan. The local HOA module states the same exam anchor: Selimiye is the ultimate architectural expression of the Ottoman kulliye by Sinan.

Hagia Sophia is the safe background source for the dome-and-half-dome problem. Ottoman architecture does not simply copy Hagia Sophia; Sinan and later Ottoman builders refine centralized prayer space, dome hierarchy, and light.

Fletcher and the local module support the mosque vocabulary: minaret, mihrab, qibla, minbar, courtyard, dome, inscription, marble/tile finish, and the relation between mosque and founder/tomb/social setting.

## Exam Pattern
If the item names Sinan, Selimiye, Suleymaniye, Edirne, Istanbul, a large central dome, half-domes, pendentive transitions, or a mosque social complex, answer Ottoman.

If the question contrasts Ottoman with Andalusian or Mughal work, choose the option that emphasizes unified interior dome space rather than intimate water courts or garden-mausoleum composition.

## Guardrails
Do not overstate Ottoman decoration as absent. The safer statement is that structure, light, proportion, calligraphy, tile, and marble work together, while the central dome remains the dominant spatial fact.

Do not use exact measurements unless the local source map or the question stem supplies them.

## Real World Scenario
On an LEA image item, a mosque with a single dominant dome, subordinate half-domes, tall minarets, and a surrounding social complex should trigger the Ottoman/Sinan pattern rather than the Cordoba hypostyle or Mughal garden-tomb pattern.
