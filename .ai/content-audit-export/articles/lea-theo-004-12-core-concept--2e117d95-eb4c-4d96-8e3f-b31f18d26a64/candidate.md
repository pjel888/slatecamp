# Thermal Comfort in Hot-Humid Design

Thermal comfort in the checked TOA 4 scope is tied to reducing heat gain and increasing useful air movement. In hot-humid localities, air movement is vital because moving air over the body supports evaporation of sweat and creates a cooling sensation.

## Comfort Moves

- Minimize heat gain before it enters the building.
- Shade walls and windows, especially where thermal mass or glass would store or admit heat.
- Orient the building and windows toward prevailing winds.
- Provide operable windows and ceiling vents for natural ventilation.
- Direct airflow into occupied zones, including areas far from windows.
- Use convection paths so warm air exits high and cooler air is drawn in low.

## Exam Use

If the question asks for comfort in a hot-humid setting, the strongest source-supported answer is not heat retention. It is shade, low heat gain, airflow, and directed movement through occupied zones.

## Common Trap

Do not confuse thermal comfort with air temperature alone. The source explicitly values air movement because the body can feel cooler when moving air promotes evaporation.
