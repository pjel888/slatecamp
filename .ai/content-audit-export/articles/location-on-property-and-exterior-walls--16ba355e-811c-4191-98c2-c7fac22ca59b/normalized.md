# Location on Property and Exterior Walls

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to apply PD 1096 Rule VII Section 704 to building location, property-line measurement, eaves, exterior wall openings, projections, and multiple buildings on one property.

## Controlling Source

This article is source-audited against the DPWH official PDF of the 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096, especially Rule VII Section 704, with Rule VI Sections 601 to 604 used only for the general fire-resistance vocabulary.

## The Basic Location Rule

Rule VII Section 704 provides that no building may be constructed unless it adjoins or has direct access to a public space, yard, or street on at least one side.

This is the first filter for location questions. Before calculating openings or projections, confirm that the building has the required access or frontage relationship.

## Measuring from Streets, Alleys, and Property Lines

Section 704 treats the centerline of an adjoining street or alley as the adjacent property line for the specific exterior-wall rules in that section.

When an exam item gives a wall near a street or alley, do not automatically measure only from the legal lot boundary. Use the measurement convention stated by Section 704 for exterior wall and opening requirements.

## Eaves Over Required Windows

Eaves over required windows must be at least 750 millimeters from side and rear property lines.

This rule is narrow. It is about eaves over required windows and the side or rear property line. Do not convert it into a universal eave rule for every projection or every facade condition.

## Exterior Wall Openings

Section 704 ties exterior wall fire resistance and opening protection to the Secretary's prescribed standards. It also gives a limit for protected openings: protected openings in an exterior wall in any storey must not exceed 50 percent of the total wall area in that storey.

For LEA questions, track whether the item asks about:

- Whether openings require protection.
- The maximum amount of protected exterior wall openings.
- The distance from the property line or assumed vertical plane.
- The wall area of one storey, not the whole building.

The 50 percent limit is not a free permission to open any wall. It applies when the opening is protected and when the other location and fire-resistance conditions are satisfied.

## Projections Beyond the Exterior Wall

Section 704 limits projections beyond the exterior wall to one-third of the distance from the assumed vertical plane where opening protection first becomes required to the property line, whichever is least restrictive under the rule.

The distance is measured at right angles from the property line. This measurement direction matters. Do not measure diagonally or along the wall.

## Multiple Buildings on the Same Property

For buildings on the same property, Section 704 applies an assumed-property-line concept to courts and walls facing another building. Two or more buildings on the same property may be treated as one building if their aggregate area is within the allowable floor area for a single building.

If the buildings have different occupancy groups or construction types, apply the most restrictive occupancy and construction requirements when treating them as one building.

## Fire Resistance Vocabulary

Rule VI defines fire-resistive rating and fire-resistive time period rating. Section 704 depends on that vocabulary when it refers to fire resistance, opening protection, and exterior wall requirements. However, Section 704 itself is the primary location-on-property rule for this article.

## Real World Scenario

During permit review for two residential buildings on one lot, first confirm that each building adjoins or directly accesses a public space, yard, or street on at least one side. If their walls face each other, apply Section 704's assumed-property-line rule before checking exterior wall opening protection, the 50 percent protected-opening cap per storey, and any projection beyond the exterior wall.

## LEA Recall Anchors

Use this checklist:

1. Does the building adjoin or directly access a public space, yard, or street on at least one side?
2. Is the relevant line a property line, or does Section 704 treat a street or alley centerline as the adjacent property line?
3. Are eaves over required windows at least 750 millimeters from side and rear property lines?
4. Are exterior wall openings protected, and are protected openings limited to 50 percent of the wall area of that storey?
5. Are projections measured at right angles and limited by the one-third distance rule?
6. Are multiple buildings being treated separately or as one aggregate building under the most restrictive applicable requirements?
