# Plastics in Construction

## Exam scope

This lesson covers PD 1096 Sections 1601 to 1609 on plastics: approved plastics, installation, glazing of openings, skylights, light-transmitting panels, light diffusers, partitions, exterior veneer, and awnings/canopies.

<LawCallout code="pd1096" section="Sections 1601-1609 - Plastics">
PD 1096 treats approved plastics as a fire-performance category: flame-spread rating of 225 or less, smoke density not greater than untreated wood, and combustion products no more toxic than untreated wood. Passing Section 1601 is only the first gate; Sections 1602 to 1609 still control where and how approved plastics may be installed.
</LawCallout>

## Approved plastics and installation data

Section 1601 defines approved plastic materials by three fire-performance criteria. The flame-spread rating must be 225 or less. Smoke density must not be greater than that obtained from burning untreated wood under similar conditions. Products of combustion must be no more toxic than burning untreated wood under similar conditions.

Section 1602 then adds installation discipline. Plastic materials must have adequate strength and durability for prescribed design loads. Sufficient technical data must establish stresses, maximum unsupported spans, and other necessary information for the thicknesses and forms used. Fastenings must also withstand design loads and internal and external stresses required of the assembly.

## Glazing, doors, and openings

The 2004 DPWH Revised IRR states that doors, sashes, and framed openings glazed or equipped with approved plastics at exterior walls must be arranged so occupants can use such openings to escape in case of fire. Openings at the ground floor must open directly to a street or exit court, while upper-floor openings must be horizontally separated from enclosed stairs, outside stairs, or exit passages leading to a street or exit court.

The same source allows plastic doors, sashes, and framings for Group A to I occupancies except for entrance doors and exit doors, which should be materials other than plastics permitted by the Code.

## Skylights

Approved plastics may be used in skylights on roofs of Types I, II, or III constructions with the stated fire-extinguishing system context and exceptions. Except in Group A occupancies, no skylight may be installed within 3.00 meters of a property line. Plastic skylights must be separated from one another by at least 2.50 meters laterally and 3.00 meters along the roof slope.

The individual area of a plastic skylight must not exceed 10.00 square meters. The total aggregate area of plastics used in skylights, monitors, and sawtooth glazing must not exceed 20 percent of the floor area of the room or occupancy sheltered. Plastic skylights on roofs with slope less than 1 in 3 require a 100 mm high curb unless the specified wire screen alternative is provided.

## Monitors, sawtooth roofs, and ceiling diffusers

For light-transmitting panels in monitors and sawtooth roofs, individual plastic glazing must not exceed 15.00 square meters. The same aggregate 20 percent room or occupancy floor-area limit applies. Panels must be separated by incombustible material or roofing material at least 1.50 meters long, and the lower edge of the plastic material must be at least 150 mm above the adjoining roof surface.

Ceiling light diffusers with an area greater than 10 percent of any 10.00 square meters of room area must be approved plastics. Plastic light diffusers must be installed so they will not readily become detached at room temperature of 80 C for 15 minutes, subject to the exceptions stated in the Code and IRR.

## Partitions, exterior veneer, awnings, and canopies

Approved plastics may be used in partitions where partitions are not required to be fire-resistive or incombustible construction. Exterior plastic veneer is limited to the first storey as the general rule. The stated exception allows use above the first storey outside highly restrictive fire zones, provided the veneer height is not more than 10.00 meters above adjacent grade.

Sections of plastic veneer must not exceed 15.00 square meters, except that less restrictive fire zones may increase the area by 50 percent. Veneer sections must be separated by at least 1.20 meters vertically and 600 mm horizontally.

For awnings and canopies, plastic materials appropriate for use according to flame-spread characteristics may be used if the awnings and canopies are constructed under the Code provisions governing projections and appendages. Approved plastics may also be used where untreated canvas is permitted and in lieu of plain glass in greenhouses in less restrictive fire zones.
