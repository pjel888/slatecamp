# Plastics in Construction
**LEA Review | Area 2: Materials & Construction | PD 1096 Rule XVI, Sections 1601–1609**

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to:

Apply the fire performance and use limitations for plastics in buildings under PD 1096 and relevant ASTM/NSCP standards: distinguish foam plastic, fiber-reinforced plastic, and thermoplastic by fire behavior; calculate maximum permitted plastic area in interior and exterior applications; and identify where thermal barrier or automatic sprinkler protection is required.

## 1. Hook / Opening Scenario

A plan reviewer flags a proposed mixed-use commercial building in a less restrictive Fire Zone: the architect has specified polycarbonate skylights covering 25% of the ground-floor retail area, plastic light diffusers in all corridors, and a translucent plastic veneer extending up to the third floor. Without knowing the precise limits under Rule XVI of PD 1096, an architect risks costly redesigns, failed inspections, and — in the worst case — a building that becomes a rapidly spreading fire hazard when its skylights detach and let flames vent unchecked through the structure.

---

## 2. Core Concepts with Mnemonic

Rule XVI of PD 1096 (Sections 1601–1609), as amplified by the **2004 Revised Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR)**, governs every application of plastic materials in building construction — from exterior cladding to overhead skylights. The overarching philosophy is **fire-hazard limitation**: plastics are permitted only when their combustion behavior is demonstrably no worse than untreated wood, and only within defined area and spacing limits that prevent fire spread.

### Five Governing Principles of Plastics in Construction

**Principle 1 — Approved Plastics Are Defined by Fire Performance (Sec. 1601)**
A plastic is "approved" under PD 1096 only if it meets all three criteria simultaneously:
1. Flame-spread rating ≤ **225**
2. Smoke density **no greater than** that of untreated wood burning under similar conditions
3. Combustion products **no more toxic** than those from burning untreated wood

**Principle 2 — Structural Adequacy Is Non-Negotiable (Sec. 1602)**
Plastic components must be supported by technical data demonstrating adequate strength and durability for design loads (dead, live, wind, seismic). Per the 2004 Revised IRR, fastenings must account for both internal and external stresses, and proper allowances must be made when plastics bear against or are in contact with dissimilar materials (thermal expansion differentials are significant in the Philippine tropical climate).

**Principle 3 — Location and Area Limits Control Fire-Zone Risk (Secs. 1603–1607)**
Every plastic application — veneer, glazing, awnings, diffusers, skylights — has a **maximum area**, **minimum separation**, and **height or distance** restriction that scales with Fire Zone restrictiveness. The less restrictive the Fire Zone, the more plastic area is permitted.

**Principle 4 — Overhead Plastics Require Active Fire Suppression or Proven Fire Venting (Sec. 1607)**
Plastic skylights in occupied Groups A, B, C, E, F, H-I, and J-I must be protected by automatic fire-extinguishing systems — unless a qualifying exception applies (approved fire venting, Type IV 1-hr construction, or 1-storey without a required fire-resistive ceiling).

**Principle 5 — Aggregate Area Cap Ties Skylights, Monitors, and Sawtooth Roofs Together (Secs. 1607–1608)**
The total combined area of plastic skylights, plastic panels in monitors, and plastic panels in sawtooth roofs shall **not exceed 20% of the floor area** served. This is a shared cap — not independent limits per element type.

### Master Mnemonic: **"FAVSA"**
> **F**lame-spread ≤ 225 | **A**rea limits per zone | **V**ertical height capped | **S**eparation distances apply | **A**ggregate 20% roof cap

## 4. In-Depth Technical Analysis

### Section 1601 — Approved Plastics: What Makes a Plastic "Approved"

Per PD 1096 Section 1601 (as clarified by the 2004 Revised IRR), three fire-performance criteria must **all** be satisfied:

1. **Flame-spread rating ≤ 225.** This is a relatively permissive threshold compared to the IBC's CC2 ceiling of 75. The Philippine standard aligns with the recognition that many functional plastics (acrylics, polycarbonates, PVC) fall in the 25–225 range. A plastic with a flame-spread rating above 225 is categorically disallowed, regardless of other merits.

2. **Smoke density ≤ untreated wood.** Rather than assigning a numeric index, the PH code uses untreated wood as a comparative benchmark — a practical standard given the prevleance of wood construction in the Philippines. In practice, manufacturers must provide test data showing smoke density does not exceed wood under the same test conditions.

3. **Combustion toxicity ≤ untreated wood.** Again, wood is the comparative benchmark. This provision is critical in occupied spaces (retail malls, schools, offices) where smoke inhalation is a primary life-safety concern.

**Practical implication:** An architect specifying polycarbonate multiwall sheets, acrylic dome skylights, or UPVC diffusers must obtain manufacturer test certificates confirming all three thresholds are met — not just flame-spread alone.

### Section 1602 — Structural Requirements

Per PD 1096 Section 1602 and the 2004 Revised IRR, plastic components must be supported by **technical data** addressing:
- Allowable stresses for the specific plastic type and thickness
- Maximum unsupported spans under design loads (particularly critical for skylights subject to Philippine typhoon wind loads)
- Fastenings adequate for design loads and accounting for the differential thermal expansion between plastics and their supporting frames (aluminum, steel, or concrete)
- Proper allowances when plastics are in contact with dissimilar materials to prevent stress concentrations

In the Philippine context, **NSCP (National Structural Code of the Philippines)** provisions for wind load (particularly relevant along typhoon corridors in the Visayas, Luzon eastern seaboard) govern the structural design of plastic skylight panels. A thermoplastic acrylic dome that is adequate under normal gravity loads may fail under suction uplift from a Signal No. 3 typhoon if span-to-thickness ratios are not properly calculated.

### Section 1603 — Exterior Plastic Veneer

Per the 2004 Revised IRR, Section 1603 establishes:

**Height Restriction:**
- Standard rule: plastic veneer may **not** be attached above the first storey.
- Exception: Outside highly restrictive Fire Zones, plastic veneer may extend up to **10.00 m above grade**. This exception directly recognizes that highly restrictive Fire Zones (typically dense urban commercial and industrial cores) present unacceptable fire-spread risk if plastic cladding extends to upper floors.

**Area Restriction:**
- Maximum individual section area: **15.00 sq.m**
- In less restrictive Fire Zones: area may increase by **50%** = maximum **22.50 sq.m** per section

**Separation Restriction:**
- Between sections: minimum **1.20 m vertically**, minimum **600 mm horizontally**
- These separations function as fire breaks — non-combustible wall or frame material between plastic sections limits vertical fire travel along the facade.

**Design note:** The combination of section-area limits and mandatory separation means that even on a large facade, continuous sheets of plastic cladding are prohibited. The 600 mm horizontal gap and 1.20 m vertical gap must be filled with incombustible cladding material.

### Section 1604 — Glazing of Openings

Per PD 1096 Section 1604 and the 2004 Revised IRR:

**Applicability:** Approved plastics may be used in doors, sashes, and framed openings at **exterior walls**. The intent is to allow plastic glazing as a functional substitute for glass in window openings — not as structural wall panels.

**Travel Distance Rule:**
- To a plastic-glazed opening: ≤ **45.00 m** for unprotected assembly areas
- Where fire suppression systems are installed: ≤ **30.00 m**
- Note the counter-intuitive logic: suppressed buildings have a *shorter* travel distance to plastic openings because the code is concerned with fire-fighter access and egress integrity, not just occupant evacuation time.

**Ground Floor Rule:** Ground-floor plastic-glazed openings must open **directly to a street or exit court** — they cannot open into internal circulation spaces.

**Upper Floor Rule:** Upper-floor plastic-glazed openings must be at least **3.00 m horizontal distance** from enclosed or outside stairways and exit passageways. This prevents fire in an upper-floor plastic window from directly threatening stairway egress paths.

**Occupancy Groups:**
- Groups A through G: plastic doors and sashes are permitted in these openings
- Critical exception: **entrance and exit doors must NOT be plastic** in any group. This reflects life-safety logic — the primary egress doors of any occupied building must be non-combustible.

**Dimensional Minimums:**
- Minimum opening size: **600 mm square** — sufficient for one-person passage
- Minimum spacing between openings: **2.00 m** for all spans — ensuring non-combustible wall material separates adjacent plastic-glazed openings

### Section 1605 — Awnings and Canopies

Per PD 1096 Section 1605:
- Approved plastics may be used in awnings and canopies wherever **untreated canvas** is permitted by the Code. This provision does not independently authorize awnings beyond what the projections and appendages rules allow — it simply permits plastic as a material substitute for canvas in qualifying applications.
- Plastic awnings must conform to all projections and appendages provisions under PD 1096 (typically Rule XV and related local ordinances governing sidewalk clearance, projection limits over property lines, and structural attachment).
- Special permission: approved plastics may replace **plain glass** in greenhouses located in **less restrictive Fire Zones** — acknowledging the agricultural/horticultural use case where large-area light-transmitting coverage is essential.

### Section 1606 — Plastic Light Diffusers in Ceilings

Per PD 1096 Section 1606 and the 2004 Revised IRR:

**When Approved Plastics Are Required:**
If the total area of plastic light diffusers in a ceiling exceeds **10% of any 10.00 sq.m room area** (i.e., more than 1.00 sq.m of diffuser per 10.00 sq.m of room), only **approved plastics** per the Code may be used. Below this threshold, the Code does not mandate approved plastic classification — though good practice recommends it.

**Thermal Stability Test:**
Diffuser panels must **not detach** from their supporting frames at a room temperature of **65°C sustained for 15 minutes**. In a pre-fire or fire condition, diffuser panels that detach create fire pathways to plenum spaces, spread burning material, and can injure occupants below. The 65°C threshold is calibrated to conditions reachable in a hot, poorly-ventilated Philippine interior during a smoldering fire.

**Exceptions (per the 2004 Revised IRR):**
1. First floor, Group I occupancies (institutional — hospitals, care facilities) with **direct exterior egress** — where the primary life-safety consideration is rapid occupant evacuation to the exterior.
2. Between an approved sprinkler head and the protected area below (not in public corridors) for Groups A, B, C, E, F, H, and I — provided testing confirms the diffuser does not interfere with sprinkler distribution patterns.

**Design implication:** In a Philippine office building or school (Groups B or E), large-format linear diffuser panels common in commercial LED troffer systems must be of approved plastic classification if they cover more than 10% of any given room area, AND must pass the 65°C/15-minute retention test.

### Section 1607 — Skylights

Per PD 1096 Section 1607 and the 2004 Revised IRR, this is the most detailed plastic provision and the most frequently tested on the LEA.

**Permitted Construction Types:**
Plastic skylights are permitted in **Types III, IV, and V** constructions. They are NOT permitted in Types I and II (fully fire-resistive construction). This aligns with the fire-resistance philosophy: the most fire-resistive building types must maintain their protective shell integrity.

**Fire Suppression Requirement:**
In Groups A (residential), B (educational), C (institutional), E (business/commercial), F (mercantile), H-I, and J-I, buildings with plastic skylights must have **automatic fire-extinguishing systems**.

**Exceptions to Fire Suppression Requirement:**
- **(a)** A fire venting system has been approved by the Building Official
- **(b)** Type IV, 1-hour fire-resistive construction with skylight curb edge **≥ 400 mm above the ceiling flange**
- **(c)** 1-storey buildings that do not have a required fire-resistive ceiling

**Setback from Property Line:**
Plastic skylights must be set back at least **3.00 m from any property line**, except for Group A (residential) occupancies — recognizing that residential buildings typically have lower floor-to-property-line setbacks.

**Dome Construction:**
Edges of plastic skylight domes must be **properly flashed** — a critical waterproofing and fire-stop detail. Unfailed skylight edges are a common source of both water infiltration and fire bypass in Philippine construction.

**Spacing Between Skylights:**
- Lateral separation: ≥ **2.50 m**
- Along roof slope: ≥ **3.00 m**
- These spacings prevent fire from leaping from one plastic skylight to the next across the roof plane.

**Maximum Individual Area:** 10.00 sq.m per skylight unit.

**Aggregate Area Cap:** Total combined plastic area from skylights + monitors + sawtooth roofs ≤ **20% of floor area served.**

**Curb and Screen Requirements:**
- **Curb option:** For roofs with a slope less than 1:4 (i.e., relatively flat roofs), a **100 mm high curb** is required at skylight perimeter — this prevents ponded rainwater (a significant issue in Philippine typhoon rains) from infiltrating the skylight frame.
- **Wire screen option:** As an alternative to the curb, install **No. 12 gauge wire mesh with maximum 25 mm openings** directly below the skylight — this prevents burning material from falling through an open or failed skylight frame onto building contents below.

### Section 1608 — Light-Transmitting Panels in Monitors and Sawtooth Roofs

Per PD 1096 Section 1608:

**Applicability:** Roofs **without a required fire-resistive rating**, or buildings equipped with **sprinkler systems**.

**Type IV Special Requirement:** In Type IV construction (Heavy Timber), plastic panels must be of a **flame-spread rating appropriate** to the construction type — the code implicitly requires a higher standard of flame-spread performance given the combustible structural system.

**Area Limits:**
- Individual panel: ≤ **15.00 sq.m**
- Aggregate (shared with skylights): ≤ **20% of floor area**

**Separation Between Panels:**
- ≥ **1.50 m** of incombustible construction between adjacent panels — functioning as a fire break to prevent flame travel across the entire monitor or sawtooth glazing run.
- Lower edge of each panel must be ≥ **150 mm above the adjacent roof surface** — preventing moisture accumulation and ensuring the incombustible base detail is maintained.

### Section 1602 (IRR) — Plastic Partitions

Per the 2004 Revised IRR:
Where partitions are **not required to be fire-resistive** and **not required to be incombustible**, approved plastics conforming to Code requirements may be used. This provision enables the use of translucent plastic partition systems in open-plan offices, school corridors, and commercial interiors — provided the partition function does not require fire-resistance rating. Where a 1-hour or 2-hour fire-resistive partition is required (e.g., between occupancies, at fire corridors), plastic partitions are categorically prohibited regardless of their flame-spread rating.

## 5. PH vs. USA Comparative Analysis

### Where the Philippine Code Is **More Permissive** Than the IBC

| Provision | PD 1096 (PH) | IBC 2021 (USA) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flame-spread threshold (light-transmitting) | ≤ 225 | CC2: ≤ 75 | PH significantly more permissive |
| Aggregate skylight area | ≤ 20% of floor area | ≤ 33.33% of floor area | PH more restrictive |
| Max height of plastic veneer | 10.00 m (exception) | 12.2 m (40 ft) | PH more restrictive |
| Light diffuser panel size | No single panel size limit; area-ratio trigger | > 0.93 sq.m triggers compliance | PH more permissive (no size limit) |

### Where the Philippine Code Is **More Restrictive** Than the IBC

| Provision | PD 1096 (PH) | IBC 2021 (USA) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aggregate skylight area | ≤ 20% of floor area | ≤ 33.33% of floor area | PH **more restrictive** |
| Max veneer height | 10.00 m (exception) | 12.2 m (40 ft) | PH **more restrictive** |
| Skylight setback from property line | 3.00 m min | Varies (generally less) | PH **more restrictive** |

### Key Analytical Observations

1. **Flame-Spread Philosophy:** The IBC's CC1/CC2 classification system (0–25 and 26–75) is far more rigorous than PD 1096's single threshold of 225. An acrylic plastic with a flame-spread index of 200 is "approved" under PH law but would fail IBC requirements for light-transmitting plastics. This gap reflects the IBC's life-safety-first orientation versus PD 1096's material-availability realism for the Philippine construction market.

2. **Aggregate Area Cap:** The IBC's 33.33% is one-third of floor area, while PH code caps at 20%. Larger skylight coverage is permitted in the USA — partly because IBC requires stricter flame-spread performance, creating a compensating trade-off.

3. **Height Limits for Veneer:** The IBC allows plastic veneer to extend higher (12.2 m vs. 10.00 m in the PH exception), but the PH code offers a less complex rule structure with clear Fire Zone-based modifications.

4. **Fire Classification Systems:** The IBC's explicit CC1/CC2 classification enables predictable product selection. PD 1096's qualitative "no worse than untreated wood" smoke and toxicity benchmarks require test data but do not define product classes. Philippine practitioners rely on imported product test certificates, which may use IBC/ASTM protocols — these are generally accepted as evidence of PH Code compliance where they demonstrate flame-spread ≤ 225.

## 6. Exam Traps

### Trap 1 — The Aggregate Skylight Area

**Wrong Answer:** "The maximum area of plastic skylights is 10.00 sq.m per skylight, so a building can have as many skylights as needed as long as each is ≤ 10.00 sq.m."

**Why It's Wrong:** The 10.00 sq.m figure is the **individual** skylight maximum. The **aggregate** total of all plastic skylights + monitor panels + sawtooth roof panels combined must not exceed **20% of the floor area** served (per PD 1096 Section 1607, 2004 IRR). A 500 sq.m floor can have a maximum of 100.00 sq.m of combined plastic roof glazing — regardless of individual unit size.

**Correct Answer:** Maximum individual area = 10.00 sq.m; maximum aggregate (skylights + monitors + sawtooth) = 20% of floor area.

---

### Trap 2 — The Suppression/Travel Distance Inversion

**Wrong Answer:** "Buildings with automatic fire suppression systems allow a greater travel distance to plastic-glazed openings because the sprinklers control fire spread."

**Why It's Wrong:** Per PD 1096 Section 1604, the opposite applies: suppressed buildings have a **shorter** maximum travel distance (≤ 30.00 m) compared to unprotected assemblies (≤ 45.00 m). The provision is not about egress travel time — it is about limiting the extent to which plastic-glazed openings are part of the suppressed fire envelope. Suppressed buildings have stricter proximity rules for plastic-glazed openings.

**Correct Answer:** Unprotected assemblies → ≤ 45.00 m; fire-suppressed buildings → ≤ 30.00 m.

---

### Trap 3 — Veneer Height Exception vs. Standard Rule

**Wrong Answer:** "Plastic exterior veneer may be used up to 10.00 m above grade in all Fire Zones."

**Why It's Wrong:** The 10.00 m height is an **exception** available only **outside highly restrictive Fire Zones** (per the 2004 Revised IRR, Section 1603). In highly restrictive Fire Zones, the standard rule applies: plastic veneer shall NOT be attached above the **first storey**. The full 10.00 m allowance is a zone-dependent privilege, not a universal standard.

**Correct Answer:** Standard rule: first storey only. Exception (outside highly restrictive Fire Zones only): up to 10.00 m above grade.

## 7. Board-Prep Mnemonics

### Mnemonic 1 — Skylight Numbers: **"10-20-25-3-3"**

For plastic skylights under Rule XVI:
- **10** sq.m = max individual skylight area
- **20%** = max aggregate plastic roof area
- **2.5 m** = min lateral separation between skylights
- **3 m** = min separation along roof slope
- **3 m** = min setback from property line

*Visualization:* Imagine ten (10) skylights on a roof, together covering only twenty percent (20%) of it, each two-and-a-half meters (2.5 m) apart side-to-side, three meters (3 m) apart slope-wise, three meters (3 m) back from the edge.

### Mnemonic 2 — Veneer Rule: **"1st STOREY — unless OUTSIDE, then 10"**

> "You can veneer the **1st storey** of any building. But if you're **outside** a highly restrictive Fire Zone, you can go all the way to **10** meters."

For section areas: "15 standard, plus 50% = 22.5 in less restrictive zones."

### Mnemonic 3 — Diffuser Trigger: **"10% of 10 = 1 square meter rule"**

> If plastic ceiling diffusers cover more than **10%** of a **10 sq.m** room area = that's more than **1.00 sq.m** of diffuser = approved plastics required AND 65°C/15-min retention test applies.

## 9. Cross-Referencing Section

### Rule XVI × Rule IV (Types of Construction)
The permitted use of plastic skylights is **explicitly conditioned** on construction type. Types I and II (fully fire-resistive) prohibit plastic skylights entirely (per Section 1607). Types III, IV, V permit them — but with escalating fire suppression requirements. When reviewing plans, always establish the Type of Construction before applying Rule XVI skylight provisions.

### Rule XVI × Rule V (Fire Zones)
Fire Zone restrictiveness directly modifies three Rule XVI provisions:
- Exterior veneer height exception (Sec. 1603): available only outside highly restrictive zones
- Exterior veneer area increase (Sec. 1603): +50% in less restrictive zones
- Greenhouse plastics replacing glass (Sec. 1605): permitted only in less restrictive zones

Architects must map the project's Fire Zone before finalizing any plastic element — a building straddling zone boundaries requires zone-specific analysis for each facade.

### Rule XVI × Rule XVIII (Glass and Glazing)
Rule XVIII governs glass glazing; Rule XVI governs plastic glazing. The two rules function as parallel systems for different materials. Where a project switches from glass to plastic (e.g., replacing a glass skylight with an acrylic dome), the governing rule shifts from Rule XVIII to Rule XVI — with different area limits, fire suppression requirements, and construction type restrictions. A common LEA exam strategy is to test whether candidates correctly identify which rule governs a given material.

### Rule XVI × RA 9514 (Fire Code of the Philippines)
The sprinkler interaction provisions in Sections 1606 and 1607 directly engage RA 9514. The Fire Code governs when sprinkler systems are **required** (by occupancy, height, and area); Rule XVI then uses the presence or absence of sprinklers as a modifier for plastic element permissions. A building that is not sprinklered under RA 9514 cannot claim the sprinkler-based exceptions in Sections 1606 or 1607 — the two systems must be analyzed together.

### Rule XVI × Rule VIII (Light and Ventilation)
Skylights under Rule XVI serve a dual function: they provide natural light (Rule VIII compliance) and — when openable — natural ventilation. However, Rule VIII's skylight area computations for light and ventilation purposes are calculated on the glass or transparent area, and plastic skylights must meet the same geometric requirements as glass skylights for light and ventilation compliance. Rule XVI adds the fire-safety overlay; Rule VIII adds the environmental performance overlay — both must be satisfied simultaneously.

### Rule XVI × NSCP (National Structural Code of the Philippines)
Structural adequacy of plastic elements (per Section 1602) is governed by NSCP load combinations. In typhoon-prone regions (Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration [PAGASA] Zone IV), wind uplift on plastic skylights can be determinative. NSCP wind load maps and fastening requirements must inform span-to-thickness calculations for all plastic roof elements.

### Rule XVI × BP 344 (Accessibility Law)
Plastic-glazed openings and doors under Section 1604 must simultaneously comply with BP 344 accessibility requirements where applicable. Accessible entrance doors must be non-plastic (per Rule XVI, entrance/exit doors excluded from plastic allowance) — this provision aligns with BP 344's requirement for accessible entrances to be reliably operable (plastic doors that warp or deform under heat may compromise accessibility).
