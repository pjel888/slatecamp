# Outside Returns, Discounts, and Kickbacks

The source-safe rule is simple: the architect's compensation should come from the client as a professional fee, not from hidden returns supplied by interested parties. The UAP Document Code of Ethics also protects client trust by barring contractor and supplier benefits that create moral obligation or impair professional opinion.

## Professional Fee And Outside Returns

The Code says the architect shall be compensated solely through the professional fee charged directly to the client. It also says the architect shall not accept or ask for other returns, in whatever form, from an interested source other than the client. This is the controlling rule for kickback questions. If the payment comes from a contractor, supplier, agent, or other interested party and is kept by the architect, it is not source-safe.

## Contractor And Supplier Benefits

The contractor provision bars free engineering services, substantial aid, gifts, commissions, or favors from contractors or subcontractors when the benefit places the architect under moral obligation. The manufacturer and supplier provision bars technical services that come with obligations detrimental to the client or that adversely affect professional opinion.

The source gives one narrow exception: a market discount may be accepted when credited to the client. The benefit cannot become the architect's private return.

## LEA Study Anchor

In exam questions, track where the benefit goes. Direct professional fee from client is proper. Hidden commission, gift, free service, or return from an interested source is unsafe. A market discount is different only when the client receives the credit.
