## The Contractor's Obligations, Scope Compliance, Standards, and Guarantee Period

## Board Exam Quick Check

The Contractor is tested through three recurring ideas: execute the Work according to the Contract Documents, remain responsible for safety and defective work, and furnish the required insurance and bonds. Keep the one-year contractual guarantee separate from broader statutory-liability questions unless the question supplies a separate governing source.

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Define the Contractor under UAP Document 301 and CIAP Document 102.
- Explain the Contractor's obligation to conform to Drawings, Specifications, and other Contract Documents.
- Identify the role of Performance, Payment, and Guarantee Bonds.
- State the one-year guarantee/warranty obligations found in UAP Document 301 and CIAP Document 102.

## Scope and Conformity

UAP Document 301 defines the Contractor as the person or firm whose proposal has been accepted and to whom the Contract was awarded. CIAP Document 102 similarly defines the Contractor as the person or firm registered and licensed by PCAB whose proposal has been accepted and to whom the Contract was awarded to execute the Work.

Both sources treat the Contractor's Work as tied to the Contract Documents. UAP Document 301 says all work shall conform to the Contract Documents and that discrepancies in Drawings, Specifications, or site conditions must be reported to the Architect or Engineer for correction. CIAP Document 102 states that the Work shall be executed in accordance with the Contract, and that unreported discrepancies may leave the Contractor working at the Contractor's risk.

## Safety, Defective Work, and Site Responsibility

UAP Document 301 gives the Contractor responsibility for precautions for safety of workers and the public, compliance with safety laws and building codes, and protection against accident or property damage. It also states that damages caused by the Contractor or employees to Owner or adjoining property are the Contractor's responsibility.

Defective materials and defective work may be ordered removed or replaced. Under CIAP Document 102, work that fails to comply with the Contract is defective and must be condemned upon discovery and immediately removed and replaced. UAP Document 301 likewise requires correction of defective work and preserves the Contractor's responsibility despite inspections or failures to reject bad work before final acceptance.

## Bonds and Guarantee Period

UAP Document 301 states that the Contractor, before signing the Contract, furnishes a Performance Bond equal to fifteen percent of the Contract amount and a Payment Bond equal to fifteen percent covering payment obligations. These bonds remain effective until replaced by the Contractor's Guarantee Bond. The UAP Guarantee Bond is thirty percent of total Contract cost, for one year from acceptance, guaranteeing good quality materials and workmanship.

CIAP Document 102 keeps the same Performance Bond and Payment Bond percentages at fifteen percent each. It states that the Guarantee Bond is equivalent to the retention released to the Contractor and runs for one year from posting. CIAP also requires the Contractor to guarantee materials and workmanship for one year, or longer when the Specifications provide, and to commence corrective work within five days after written notice by the Owner.

## Real World Scenario

A contractor installs ceiling materials before resolving a drawing/specification conflict and later argues that the architect's inspector saw the work, so the contractor is released from correction. The safer exam answer is that the work still has to conform to the Contract Documents. UAP Document 301 and CIAP Document 102 both keep the contractor responsible for reporting discrepancies, following the contract, protecting the site, and correcting defective work. Inspection or delayed rejection does not become a blanket release from the contractor's own obligations.

## Exam Trap

Do not merge the one-year contractual guarantee with every possible statutory liability issue. For this article, the tested source is the contract guarantee or warranty period and bond replacement path, not a separate Civil Code design-liability rule unless the question supplies that source.
