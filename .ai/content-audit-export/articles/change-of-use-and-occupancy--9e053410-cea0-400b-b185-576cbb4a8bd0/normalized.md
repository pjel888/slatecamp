# Change of Use and Occupancy

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to classify occupancy groups under PD 1096 Rule VII, apply the change-of-use rule in Section 702, and connect a lawful occupancy change to the Certificate of Occupancy process under Rule III Section 309.

## Controlling Source

This article is source-audited against the DPWH official PDF of the 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096, especially Rule VII Sections 701 and 702 and Rule III Section 309.

## Occupancy Classification Comes First

Rule VII Section 701 classifies buildings by use or character of occupancy. The groups are:

| Group | Classification |
| :-- | :-- |
| A | Residential Dwellings |
| B | Residentials, Hotels and Apartments |
| C | Education and Recreation |
| D | Institutional |
| E | Business and Mercantile |
| F | Industrial |
| G | Storage and Hazardous |
| H | Assembly Other Than Group I |
| I | Assembly Occupant Load 1000 or More |
| J | Accessory |

For change-of-use questions, first identify the existing occupancy group and the proposed occupancy group. The answer depends on whether the new use places the building in a different group or division and whether the building can comply with the Code requirements for that new use.

## The Core Change-of-Use Rule

Rule VII Section 702 provides that no change may be made in the character of occupancy or use of a building that would place it in a different division or group unless the building complies with the Code requirements for that new division or group.

The rule is compliance-based. A change of business name, tenant, or interior operation is not the legal endpoint. If the character of use changes the occupancy classification, the building must meet the requirements of that new classification.

## Existing Buildings and Less Hazardous Uses

Section 702 also allows existing buildings to have their occupancy changed subject to approval by the Building Official. An existing building may be occupied for other groups if the new or proposed use is less hazardous, based on life and fire risk, than the existing use.

This is not an owner-only judgment. The Building Official's approval is part of the rule, and the less-hazardous comparison must be based on life and fire risk.

## Certificate of Occupancy Link

Rule III Section 309 provides that no building or structure may be used or occupied until the Building Official issues a Certificate of Occupancy.

The Certificate of Occupancy package includes the application, notarized Certificate of Completion, construction logbook, as-built plans and specifications, and the Building Inspection Sheet. The BFP fire-safety inspection and clearance process is part of the occupancy review.

Section 309 also allows additional Certificates of Occupancy to be issued due to changes in use. This connects the occupancy classification rule in Section 701 and the change-of-use rule in Section 702 to the administrative occupancy document under Rule III.

## What This Rule Does Not Say

The source-audited rule does not support a shortcut that every less intense tenant change is automatically approved. It also does not support ignoring structural, fire-safety, egress, sanitation, parking, or other Code requirements that attach to the proposed occupancy.

For LEA purposes, the safer reading is:

- Same occupancy character: confirm whether any other permit or occupancy document is triggered by the facts.
- Different division or group: require compliance with the new division or group.
- Existing building proposed for another group: Building Official approval is required, and the proposed use must be less hazardous based on life and fire risk.
- Actual use or occupancy: Certificate of Occupancy rules still apply.

## LEA Recall Anchors

Use this decision path:

1. Identify the old occupancy group under Section 701.
2. Identify the proposed occupancy group under Section 701.
3. Ask whether the change places the building in a different division or group.
4. If yes, apply Section 702: the building must comply with the Code requirements for the new division or group.
5. If the item involves an existing building and another group, check whether the new use is less hazardous based on life and fire risk and whether the Building Official approves.
6. For actual use or occupancy, connect the result to the Certificate of Occupancy under Section 309.
