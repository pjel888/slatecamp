## Board Exam Quick Check

For RA 9266 scope questions, first ask whether the item is defining **who may practice**, **what architectural services include**, or **which professional assumes responsibility**. Do not import engineering signing authority just because the work touches structure or utilities.

## Scope of Practice: What Architecture Covers and What It Does Not

RA 9266 frames architecture as a professional service around the planning, design, construction, enlargement, conservation, renovation, remodeling, restoration, or alteration of buildings and groups of buildings. The verified source for this article is the RA 9266 IRR, especially Section 3(2) through Section 3(4).

### Core Definitions

The IRR defines an architect as a person registered and licensed under RA 9266 with a Certificate of Registration and Professional Identification Card. It then separates three practice roles that are easy to confuse in exam questions:

- **Architect-of-Record (AoR):** the registered architect directly and professionally responsible for the total design of the project for the client. The AoR assumes civil liability for the plans, specifications, and contract documents the AoR signs and seals.
- **Architect-in-Charge of Construction (AiCC):** the registered architect directly and professionally responsible and liable for construction supervision.
- **Consulting Architect:** a registered, licensed, or permitted architect with recognized expertise or specialization in a branch of architecture.

### General Practice Versus Listed Services

Section 3(3) describes the general practice of architecture as planning, architectural designing, structural conceptualization, specifying, supervising, and giving general administration and responsible direction for buildings, building environments, and architectural design in engineering structures. That definition is broad, but it is not a license to sign another profession's technical documents.

Section 3(4) then lists ten service categories, lettered (a) through (j). These include planning and architectural designing; consultation and pre-design services; schematic design, design development, contract documents, and construction phases; feasibility studies; architectural plans, specifications, bills of materials, cost estimates, general conditions, and bidding documents; construction and project management; space planning and architectural interiors; building administration, construction arbitration, conservation, and restoration; coordination of building systems and processes; and other work requiring architectural competence, including teaching architectural subjects and architectural computer-aided design.

### Structural Conceptualization Boundary

RA 9266 includes **structural conceptualization** in architectural practice. The source defines it as conceiving, choosing, and developing the type, disposition, arrangement, and proportioning of structural elements while considering safety, cost-effectiveness, functionality, and aesthetics. For LEA purposes, this is a conceptual and coordination role. It should not be converted into authority to sign structural engineering calculations or structural design documents.

### Utility Layouting Is Architectural Coordination

The scope includes architectural layouting of mechanical, electrical, electronic, sanitary, plumbing, communications, and other utility systems, equipment, and fixtures. In exam terms, this is about spatial coordination and architectural integration. It does not replace the professional responsibility of engineers or other specialists for their own technical designs.

### Service Agreement Trap

The IRR defines a service agreement as a notarized written contract or equivalent public instrument that states the scope of services and guarantees compensation for services rendered by a registered and licensed architect. The exam trap is to treat the statutory scope as if every architect automatically owes every listed service on every commission. In practice and in exam analysis, the service agreement identifies which services are actually commissioned.

### Exam Takeaway

Use this hierarchy: Section 3 defines the practice and role boundaries; the service agreement defines the commissioned scope; signing and sealing rules determine responsibility for documents actually issued under the architect's seal.
