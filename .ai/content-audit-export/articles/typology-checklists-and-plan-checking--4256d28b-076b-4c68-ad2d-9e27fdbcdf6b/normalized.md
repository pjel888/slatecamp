# Typology Checklists and Plan Checking

## Learning Goal

Use a typology checklist as a source-backed plan-checking tool: start with the program, translate the program into countable spaces and routes, then check the accessible arrival, circulation, door, toilet, vertical-access, service, and signage requirements against the checked local source family.

## Source-Audited Scope

This Batch 053 local-first rewrite uses raw PDFs first. The Design Process source defines architectural programming as ordered problem definition: gathering, organizing, analyzing, interpreting, and presenting project information so a responsible design solution can be made. The same source shows translation guidelines as countable requirements, such as a fixed number of student organization kiosks with minimum floor areas and specified lobby locations.

The BP 344 2024 Revised IRR source is used for accessibility plan-check dimensions. RA 9442 privilege and incentive claims are not used in this article because the exact RA 9442 raw-law PDF was not found in the local raw source layer during selection.

## Plan-Checking Sequence

1. Confirm the program before checking dimensions. A checklist is not a substitute for programming. The source-backed sequence begins with the ordered definition of the architectural problem and the project requirements.
2. Translate the program into measurable requirements. Count rooms, kiosks, laboratories, offices, service counters, toilets, routes, and entries before checking drawings.
3. Check accessible arrival. Where parking is provided, BP 344 requires accessible parking to follow the table of required accessible slots, be near accessible entrances, and provide an accessible slot at least 4000 mm wide by 5000 mm long with a 1200 mm clear walkway between the front ends of parked cars.
4. Check level changes. Changes in level require a ramp unless served by a dropped sidewalk, curb ramp, elevator, or other mechanical device. Accessible ramps have a 1200 mm minimum clear width; the 2024 IRR gives 1:20 as the general maximum gradient and allows special-case steeper gradients by length.
5. Check routes and walks. Sidewalks, walkways, and pathways require 1500 mm minimum width; 1800 mm is required where two wheelchairs must pass. Long or busy paths need 1800 mm clear rest or turning spaces at maximum 12000 mm intervals.
6. Check entrances and doors. Accessible entrances must connect arrival points to the interior lobby without excluding users from the standard path. Vestibules require at least 1800 mm by 1800 mm level area, and doors require 900 mm minimum clear width.
7. Check toilets and vertical access. Accessible toilets require maneuvering/transfer provisions, a 450 mm toilet-seat height, reachable accessories, emergency call buttons at 400 mm to 600 mm, and strobe alarms where required. Accessible elevator cars require 1400 mm depth by 1200 mm width and at least 900 mm clear door opening.
8. Check service elements. Accessible workstations require desk counters at least 900 mm wide, 700 mm vertical legroom, and 480 mm depth. Dining areas with fixed seating require 10 percent accessible dining tables; those tables are 750 mm high with 700 mm knee space and 480 mm depth.
9. Check signage and tactile wayfinding. The ISA and tactile/directional signage must lead users to accessible routes and facilities. Wall and door signs with graphic, text, and Braille content are installed at a maximum 1500 mm center height.
10. Record exceptions as blockers. If a drawing relies on a privilege, incentive, or legal right outside BP 344, do not infer it from accessibility sources. Park it until the exact legal raw PDF is available.

## Exam Traps

- Do not repeat older values when the checked 2024 IRR source gives a different dimension. In this batch, accessible parking is 4000 mm by 5000 mm, not 3700 mm by 5000 mm.
- Do not treat a checklist as design authority by itself. The checklist must trace to program requirements and the raw source family.
- Do not mix privilege-law claims into a BP 344 plan checklist unless the privilege law has its own checked raw PDF.
- Do not allow a route checklist to stop at the entrance. It must continue through doors, toilets, vertical access, service counters, dining, refuge, and signage where those elements exist.
