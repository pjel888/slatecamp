## Learning Goal

Master the key concepts tested in the LEA for Architectural Design, including: space planning, circulation, building typology, design process, site analysis, anthropometrics, accessibility standards (BP 344, RA 7277/9442), fire safety egress (RA 9514), parking design, and NBCP application (PD 1096). By the end of this study map, you should understand the subject's scope, how its 13 subtopics interrelate, and the optimal sequence for tackling them.

## Subject Coverage

Architectural Design and Site Planning spans 13 subtopics that cover the full scope of architectural practice:

- **Design Process and Methodology** — programming, concept generation, schematic design, design development
- **Site Planning and Analysis** — topography, solar orientation, prevailing winds, drainage, views, access
- **Residential Design** — single-family, multi-family, socialized housing (BP 220)
- **Commercial, Business, and Mixed-Use Design** — offices, retail, hotels, mixed-use
- **Institutional and Public Design** — schools (DepEd), hospitals (DOH), government buildings, civic facilities
- **Industrial, Transportation, and Facilities Design** — factories, warehouses, terminals
- **Environmental Design** — passive solar, natural ventilation, daylighting, thermal comfort, biophilic design
- **Fire Safety, Egress, and Life Safety Systems** — means of egress, exit widths, travel distances (RA 9514)
- **Parking Design and Vehicular Circulation** — stall dimensions, drive aisles, ramp geometry, turning radii
- **Graphic Presentation and Architectural Drawings** — plans, sections, elevations, details, drawing conventions
- **Accessibility Standards (BP 344 / RA 7277 as amended by RA 9442)** — ramp slopes, door widths, accessible toilets, parking, tactile guidance
- **Anthropometrics and Human Dimensions** — body dimensions, reach zones, clearances
- **National Building Code Application (PD 1096)** — setbacks, FAR, height limits, occupancy classification

<ExamTip>LEA questions on Architectural Design frequently cite code provisions without naming the code. "The minimum clear width of an accessible ramp is ___" is a BP 344 question. "The minimum number and size of accessible parking slots is ___" links BP 344 with RA 7277/RA 9442. "The maximum travel distance to an exit in a non-sprinklered building is ___" is an RA 9514/NBCP question. Knowing which code governs which design decision is as important as knowing the number itself.</ExamTip>

## Suggested Study Sequence

### Phase 1 — Design Backbone (Weeks 1–2)

Start with **Design Process and Methodology** to establish the conceptual framework. Then cover **Anthropometrics and Human Dimensions** — these numbers underpin every building type.

### Phase 2 — Site and Environment (Weeks 3–4)

Cover **Site Planning and Analysis** and **Environmental Design** together. Solar angles, monsoon winds, and passive cooling strategies are baseline design moves for Philippine climate.

### Phase 3 — Building Types (Weeks 5–7)

Work through **Residential**, **Commercial/Mixed-Use**, **Institutional**, and **Industrial/Transportation** design in sequence. The most tested building types are residential (including BP 220), educational, and healthcare.

<Tip>The most tested building types in the Philippine LEA are residential (including BP 220 Economic and Socialized Housing), educational (schools and universities), and healthcare (hospitals, clinics). Invest proportionally more time in these three before moving to industrial and transportation.</Tip>

### Phase 4 — Codes and Safety (Weeks 8–9)

Study **Fire Safety and Egress**, **Accessibility Standards**, **Parking Design**, and **PD 1096** together. These generate a high volume of specific, testable questions.

<Warn>Do not skip the parking design subtopic because it seems mundane. Philippine LEA questions on parking are highly specific: minimum stall dimensions (2.5 m × 5.0 m standard, 3.7 m × 5.0 m accessible), drive aisle widths for 90-degree parking (6.0 m minimum for two-way), ramp slopes, and the formula for computing required parking slots by building use under PD 1096. These are reliable exam questions that reward preparation.</Warn>

### Phase 5 — Graphic Communication (Week 10)

**Graphic Presentation and Architectural Drawings** — practice reading unfamiliar drawing sets, not just producing plates.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Ignoring code application in favor of design theory.** Precision matters: BP 344 ramp slopes (1:12 max), PD 1096 setbacks, RA 9514 travel distances are specific and not forgiving of approximation.
- **Weak graphic communication.** Practice reading working drawing sets, not just perspectives and renders.
- **Studying building types without governing codes.** Residential = BP 220 + PD 1096; Hospital = DOH guidelines; Schools = DepEd standards.
- **Neglecting site planning.** Setbacks, easements, solar orientation, drainage, and buildable area calculations generate substantial exam content.
- **Confusing codes across systems.** Know which code governs which design decision: BP 344 for accessibility, RA 9514 for fire safety, PD 1096 for building permits and occupancy.
