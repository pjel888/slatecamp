## PRBoA Powers and Functions: Section 7 In Full

---

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Enumerate the powers and functions of the PRBoA under RA 9266 §7.
- Distinguish the PRBoA's regulatory powers from the PRC's administrative supervision.
- Explain the Board's role in licensure, discipline, CPD, and standard-setting.
- Apply §7 powers to exam scenarios involving board jurisdiction.



<LawCallout code="ra9266" section="Section 7 - Specific Powers">
Legal Basis: RA 9266 (Architecture Act of 2004), Section 7 (Powers and Functions of the Professional Regulatory Board of Architecture); RA 9266 IRR, Section 7.
</LawCallout>

Section 7 of RA 9266 enumerates the specific powers, functions, and responsibilities of the Professional Regulatory Board of Architecture. The statute lists fourteen (14) distinct powers, from (a) through (n), creating a comprehensive grant of regulatory authority. These are not permissive ("may") but mandatory ("shall exercise"), imposing upon the Board an affirmative duty to perform these functions.

The Board's authority rests on the understanding that professional regulation is a public function; the PRBoA acts on behalf of the state to protect the public, not on behalf of the profession to advance member interests. This distinction is foundational: regulatory power derives from state sovereignty and must be exercised for public benefit.

### Power (a): Prescribe and Adopt the IRR

Section 7(a) grants the Board authority to "Prescribe and adopt the 'IRR of the Architecture Act of 2004' for carrying out the provisions of R.A. No. 9266." This power gives the Board rulemaking authority subordinate to the statute itself. The Board cannot contradict or exceed the statute; it prescribes rules implementing the statute's intent within statutory boundaries.

This rulemaking power is essential because statutes are general; they require operational rules addressing procedural details, timelines, and technical specifications that the statute does not and cannot address. The Board transforms statutory mandates into executable regulations: examination schedules, application procedures, documentation requirements, disciplinary timelines, and continuing professional development guidelines.

The rulemaking process typically requires Board deliberation, public notice (in official gazette), opportunity for stakeholder comment, and final adoption. Once adopted, the IRR has binding force equal to statute for practical purposes. Subsequent amendments require following the same rulemaking process, ensuring consistency and transparency.

### Power (b): Supervise Registration, Licensure, and Practice

Section 7(b) grants the Board authority to "Supervise the registration, licensure and practice of architects." This is the Board's core power; all other functions support this overarching supervisory mandate. Supervision encompasses:

- Registration: maintaining the registry of qualified architects, issuing certificates of registration, and ensuring that only registered persons hold themselves out as architects
- Licensure: overseeing the examination process, grading, pass-fail decisions, and issuance of professional identification cards
- Practice: monitoring the conduct of practicing architects, receiving complaints, investigating violations, and enforcing professional standards

This supervision is continuous and proactive, not merely reactive. The Board should establish monitoring mechanisms, receive reports from clients and building officials, and investigate patterns of misconduct even without formal complaints.

### Power (c): Administer Oaths

Section 7(c) grants the Board authority to "Administer oaths in connection with the administration of R.A. No. 9266." This seemingly technical power is essential for regulatory proceedings. When investigating alleged violations, the Board must obtain testimony under oath to ensure truthfulness. Witnesses may be sworn to tell the truth, and failure to answer truthfully constitutes perjury.

The Board may administer oaths at disciplinary hearings, during investigations, at examination proceedings, and in any other context where truthfulness must be assured. This oath-taking authority reinforces the Board's status as a public body exercising state power.

### Power (d): Issue, Suspend, Revoke, or Reinstate Credentials

Section 7(d) grants the Board authority to "Issue, suspend, revoke, or reinstate the Certificate of Registration and the Professional Identification Card for the practice of the architecture profession." This is the Board's enforcement lever; it controls the credential that authorizes architectural practice.

Issuance follows successful examination completion. The Board reviews examination results, verifies that the applicant meets all registration requirements, and issues the certificate. Suspension temporarily removes practice rights pending rehabilitation or expiration of the suspension period. Revocation permanently removes the credential unless the decision is overturned on appeal.

Reinstatement occurs when a suspended architect completes the suspension period and meets reinstatement conditions (often including proof of continuing education or successful reexamination). This power ensures that the Board can enforce its disciplinary decisions through credential control.

### Power (e): Adopt an Official Seal

Section 7(e) grants the Board authority to "Adopt an official seal of the Board." The seal is used on official documents, licenses, and regulations to authenticate them as emanating from the Board. The seal becomes the Board's official insignia and carries legal weight; documents bearing the Board's seal and signature are presumed authentic and official.

This power seems administrative but has significance: the seal distinguishes official Board action from private conduct. Forging the Board's seal or using it without authority constitutes a serious offense.

### Power (f): Monitor Conditions and Adopt Maintenance Measures

Section 7(f) grants the Board authority to "Monitor the conditions affecting the practice of architecture and adopt such measures as may be deemed proper for the enhancement and maintenance of high professional, ethical and technical standards of the profession." This is an adaptive power enabling the Board to respond to evolving practice conditions.

As new materials, technologies, building types, or service models emerge, the Board can issue guidance, revised standards, or new regulations without waiting for legislative amendment. For example, as sustainable design becomes standard practice, the Board can incorporate environmental performance standards into the Code of Ethical Conduct. As digital fabrication and parametric design tools proliferate, the Board can address competency expectations around these technologies.

This power prevents regulatory obsolescence; it enables the Board to be proactive in maintaining professional standards as practice evolves.

### Power (g): Prescribe and Adopt Code of Ethical Conduct and Standards of Professional Practice

Section 7(g) grants the Board authority to "Prescribe and/or adopt the Code of Ethical Conduct and Standards of Professional Practice." These documents define behavioral expectations beyond what the statute specifies. The Code addresses:

- Confidentiality: architects must protect client information except as required by law
- Conflicts of interest: architects must disclose conflicts and avoid taking positions that compromise professional judgment
- Truthful representation: advertising, credentials, and experience claims must be accurate
- Fairness: competition must be ethical; undercutting fees to the point of rendering inadequate services violates standards
- Competence maintenance: architects must keep knowledge current through continuing education
- Public service: architects should contribute to public welfare through pro bono work or advocacy

The Code and Standards carry force equal to statute for disciplinary purposes; violations ground suspension or revocation under Section 23.

### Power (h): Hear and Decide Administrative Cases

Section 7(h) grants the Board authority to "Hear and decide administrative cases involving violations of R.A. No. 9266, the 'IRR of the Architecture Act of 2004', the Code of Ethical Conduct and Standards of Professional Practice." This is the Board's judicial power; it acts as a quasi-judicial body in disciplinary proceedings.

The Board receives complaints, conducts investigations, issues subpoena ad testificandum (commanding witness testimony) and subpoena duces tecum (requiring document production), hears evidence from both complainant and respondent, and issues decisions. The Board may impose discipline: censure (written reprimand), suspension (temporary license removal), or revocation (permanent license removal).

The Board's decision becomes final fifteen (15) days after notice unless appelead to the Commission. The Commission may appeal to the Court of Appeals. This provides multiple levels of review and ensures due process.

### Power (i): Prescribe CPD Guidelines

Section 7(i) grants the Board authority to "Prescribe guidelines for the Continuing Professional Development (CPD) program in consultation with the integrated and accredited professional organization of architects." This power recognizes that initial licensure is not sufficient; practitioners must maintain and enhance competency throughout their careers.

The Board establishes CPD expectations: minimum hours of training annually, acceptable types of activities (seminars, workshops, reading, teaching, research), provider accreditation criteria, and documentation requirements. Notably, Section 7(i) states that "the attendance at said CPD shall not be a mandatory requirement for the renewal of a professional Identification Card," making it advisory rather than mandatory. This balances professional development incentive against the burden on practitioners, particularly rural architects or those with heavy project loads.

### Power (j): Prepare and Administer Examinations

Section 7(j) grants the Board authority to "Prepare, adopt, issue or amend the syllabi of the subjects for examinations in consultation with the academe, determine and prepare questions which shall be within the scope of the syllabi of the subject for examination as well as administer, correct and release the results of the licensure examinations."

This comprehensive power covers the entire examination process. The Board determines what knowledge licensure candidates must possess (syllabus), drafts questions testing that knowledge, administers the examination, grades responses, and announces results. The Board may also release results to candidates and public, ensuring transparency.

The Board must consult with academia; universities shape what architects learn and thus should have input into what licensure examinations assess. However, the Board retains final authority; academia provides input, but the Board decides final examination content.

### Power (k): Approve, Issue, Limit, or Cancel Special Permits

Section 7(k) grants the Board authority to "Approve, issue, limit or cancel temporary or special permit to practice architecture." These permits allow foreign architects to practice temporarily in the Philippines, often for special projects or under agreements facilitating reciprocal practice.

Temporary permits are typically issued for fixed periods (e.g., one year) and limited to specific projects. This allows the Board to facilitate international collaboration while maintaining quality control. Limits might include requirement of a Filipino counterpart architect, supervision by a registered local architect, or specific project scope.

The Board can cancel permits if the permit holder violates conditions or engages in misconduct, ensuring that temporary practice rights do not circumvent professional standards.

### Power (l): Ensure Curriculum Compliance with CHED Standards

Section 7(l) grants the Board authority to "In coordination with the CHED, ensure that all higher educational instruction and offerings of architecture comply with the policies, standards and requirements of the course prescribed by the CHED in the areas of curriculum, faculty, library and facilities." This power extends the Board's influence beyond practice into education, ensuring that schools prepare students for professional practice and licensure.

The Board and Commission may enter into a Memorandum of Agreement with CHED to implement this provision. The Board monitors schools' compliance, may visit institutions, review curricula, and recommend improvements. This prevents schools from offering accredited architecture degrees that do not prepare students adequately for practice.

### Power (m): Computerize Licensure Examinations

Section 7(m) grants the Board authority to "Adopt a program for the full computerization of the licensure examination." This reflects recognition that technology improves efficiency, security, and accessibility. Computerized examinations can be delivered at multiple sites simultaneously, can randomize questions to prevent cheating, and can be scored with absolute objectivity.

The Board oversees transition to computerized testing, ensuring that the technology does not compromise examination integrity and that questions remain valid and reliable.

### Power (n): Discharge Additional Duties

Section 7(n) grants the Board authority to "Discharge such other duties and functions as may be deemed necessary for the enhancement of the architecture profession and the upgrading, development and growth of the architecture education." This catch-all provision allows the Board to adapt to unforeseen needs.

As the profession and education evolve, the Board can assume additional responsibilities falling within professional regulation's scope, provided the activities relate to professional enhancement, education development, or growth—not unrelated governmental functions.

### Review and Approval by the Commission

A critical limitation on Board power: The statute provides that "The policies, resolutions, rules and regulations, issued or promulgated by the Board shall be subject to review and approval of the Commission." This means the Board cannot unilaterally implement policy; the Commission must approve Board-issued regulations and policies.

However, "the Board's decisions, resolutions or orders rendered in administrative cases shall be subject to review only if on appeal." This distinction is important: in disciplinary matters, the Board has relative autonomy; appeals go directly to the Commission. In rulemaking matters, the Commission must affirmatively approve before rules take effect.

This dual structure prevents the Board from becoming a runaway agency while preserving Board autonomy in adjudicatory matters where members' expertise and judgment are most valuable.

<Warn>The distinction between Commission review of **rules** (must pre-approve) vs. **disciplinary decisions** (appeal only) is one of the most frequently tested concepts. For rules: Commission pre-approval is required. For disciplinary decisions: Board decides independently; Commission only reviews if appealed. Know this asymmetry cold.</Warn>

### Implied Limitations on Board Power

While Section 7 grants extensive authority, implied limitations exist. The Board cannot violate the statute or Constitution. It cannot exercise powers reserved to other agencies (e.g., the Board cannot issue professional identification cards to non-architects or regulate engineers). It cannot impose rules beyond architecture practice regulation.

Additionally, the Board's authority is ministerial where the statute is specific (e.g., calculating the ten-year practice requirement) but discretionary where the statute uses flexible language (e.g., "as may be deemed proper"). Courts reviewing Board action apply an arbitrary-and-capricious standard: the Board's decisions are upheld if they are rational, supported by evidence, and within the Board's authority.

### The Board's Role as Public Trustee

<Tip>The 14 powers of the PRBoA can be grouped into four categories: (1) **Rulemaking** — IRR, ethical code, CPD guidelines; (2) **Licensing** — registration, examination, special permits; (3) **Adjudicatory** — hearing cases, issuing subpoenas, imposing discipline; and (4) **Supervisory** — monitoring practice conditions, ensuring education quality, computerizing exams. Exam questions often test which power falls into which category.</Tip>

Underlying all these powers is a foundational principle: the Board acts as public trustee, not as a professional guild advancing member interests. If the Board's powers and the profession's interests conflict, public interest prevails. The Board may adopt standards stricter than the statute, more extensive examination requirements, or broader disciplinary grounds—all in service of protecting the public.

This public-trustee orientation distinguishes professional regulation from industry self-governance. The Board is a regulatory agency, not a trade association.
