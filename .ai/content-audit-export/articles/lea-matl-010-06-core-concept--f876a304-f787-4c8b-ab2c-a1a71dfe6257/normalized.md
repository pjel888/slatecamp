## What You Need to Know for the LEA
## Learning Goal

Calculate tile quantities by floor or wall area including trim, thresholds, and a 5-15% waste factor for cutting and breakage, and determine grout and adhesive requirements based on tile size and joint width.
## Core Concept

Tile quantity is determined by dividing the total surface area by the area covered by one tile. The formula is: _Number of tiles = Total surface area (m²) ÷ Area per tile (m²)_. Tile area includes the grout joint thickness (typically 10 mm), so a nominal 300 mm × 300 mm tile with 10 mm joint covers 310 × 310 mm = 0.0961 m² when accounting for spacing.

Alternatively, many estimators use a simpler direct count: for standard 300 × 300 mm tiles with 10 mm joint, approximately 11 tiles are needed per square meter (1 ÷ 0.0961 ≈ 10.4 ≈ 11). For 600 × 600 mm tiles, approximately 2.8 tiles per square meter (1 ÷ 0.36 ≈ 2.8).

Waste factors account for breakage during transport, installation, and trimming. Typical waste is 5–10% for straight runs, and 10–15% for complex layouts with many cuts or patterns.

## Philippine Construction Context

In the Philippines, major tile suppliers include Tiles Philippines (locally manufactured ceramic and vitreous china), Toto (Japanese premium), Porcelanosa (Spanish high-end), and various imported brands. Locally manufactured tiles dominate the market at 300–600 pesos per square meter for standard ceramic. Premium porcelain and imported tiles range from 800–1,500 pesos/m².

Standard tile sizes reflect international standards: 300 × 300 mm is most common for residential bathrooms and kitchens. Large-format 600 × 600 mm tiles are increasingly popular for modern residential and commercial projects, with professional installation requiring specialized techniques.

Climate: Philippines' humidity and salt-spray exposure in coastal areas drive demand for porcelain tiles (lower water absorption) and glazed finishes. Some projects specify anti-slip surfaces for bathrooms and wet areas.

## How It's Tested

On the LEA, tile estimation questions focus on:

1. **Basic tile count**: Given area and tile size, calculate tiles needed using the tiles per m² factor.

2. **Waste adjustment**: Add 5–15% waste based on layout complexity.

3. **Box conversion**: Convert tile count to number of boxes (tiles per box varies by product).

4. **Cost estimation**: Area × price per m² = material cost.

5. **Mixed sizes**: Different areas with different tile sizes, calculated separately and summed.

6. **Grout and adhesive**: Based on tile count and joint dimensions.
