## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Tile quantity is an area problem. Fajardo shows two equivalent approaches: divide the surface area by the area of one tile, or use a source table that already gives the number of pieces per square meter. Use the method that matches the information given in the item.

## Core Concept

For a direct tile count, solve the area of one tile first, then divide the wall or floor area by that tile area. A 10 cm x 20 cm tile covers 0.02 sq m, so a 12 sq m wall needs 12 / 0.02 = 600 pieces before any allowance. A 20 cm x 20 cm tile covers 0.04 sq m, so a 15 sq m floor needs 15 / 0.04 = 375 pieces before any allowance.

Fajardo also gives table factors. The table method includes common commercial sizes, such as 20 cm x 20 cm tile at 25 pieces per sq m and 40 cm x 40 cm ceramic tile at 6.25 pieces per sq m. If the problem asks for adhesive or joint filler, use the source table rate: tile adhesive at 0.11 bag per sq m and white cement joint filler at 0.50 kg per sq m.

## How It Is Tested

Expect items that ask for pieces, boxes, adhesive, or filler. Start with net surface area. Use either area divided by tile area or area multiplied by a pieces-per-square-meter factor. Add breakage allowance only when the problem asks for it; Fajardo notes that 5 to 10 percent is satisfactory for breakage allowance.

## Common Mistakes

Do not confuse tile area with pieces per square meter. Do not apply the breakage allowance twice. Do not use a tile table factor for a different commercial tile size.
