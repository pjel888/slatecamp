## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal

Classify kitchen exhaust hoods (Type I vs Type II) by NFPA 96 requirements based on cooking appliance type (grease-producing vs steam/heat-only), and identify duct construction, fire suppression, and ventilation rate requirements for commercial kitchen exhaust systems.
## Kitchen Exhaust Hood Classification

### Type I Hoods (Grease-Laden Vapor)

Type I hoods capture grease-laden vapors from cooking equipment including griddles, woks, charbroilers, and deep fryers. These hoods require:

- _Grease filters_ to remove particulate matter before exhaust enters ductwork
- _Fire suppression systems_ integrated within the hood and ductwork
- _Duct systems_ constructed from minimum gauge per PD 1096 2004 IRR (NFPA 96 references 18 gauge steel or 20 gauge stainless steel for Type I hoods) with full seam welding

- _Sloped ductwork_ at minimum 1:12 gradient to direct grease back toward the hood
- _Exhaust rate_ calculated at 50–100 cubic feet per minute (CFM) per linear foot of cooking surface length, per PD 1096 2004 IRR Rule VI Section 606 (or equivalent mechanical code section)

A 2-meter linear cooking line requires: 2 m = 6.56 feet × 75 CFM/foot (mid-range) = approximately 492 CFM exhaust capacity.

Type I installations in Philippine commercial kitchens must comply with RA 9514, which mandates automatic wet chemical fire suppression systems in the hood plenum and grease duct (NFPA 17A standard).

### Type II Hoods (Steam, Heat, Odor)

Type II hoods capture non-grease-laden steam, heat, and odors from kettles, tilting skillets, ovens, and dishwashing equipment. These hoods:

- _Do not require_ grease filters or fire suppression
- _Operate at lower exhaust rates_: 40–75 CFM per linear foot per PD 1096 2004 IRR mechanical systems requirements Table 506.2
- _Use standard galvanized steel or aluminum ductwork_ (per PD 1096 2004 IRR specifications (verify minimum gauge requirement))
- _Allow horizontal ductwork_ without sloping requirements

## Kitchen Exhaust Rate Calculation Method

For Type I (grease) hoods, exhaust CFM is calculated as:

**Exhaust CFM = Hood Length (feet) × Design Rate (CFM/foot)**

For a 1.5-meter hood with three burner stations at 100 CFM per linear foot:
- 1.5 m ÷ 0.3048 = 4.92 feet
- 4.92 feet × 100 CFM/foot = 492 CFM

Philippine systems operating at 220V/60Hz three-phase power must use properly sized exhaust fans with ample motor overload protection, per PD 1096 2004 IRR or Philippine Electrical Code (PEC).

## Grease Duct Design and Construction

Grease ducts in Type I systems must comply with both PD 1058 and RA 9514:

### Material Requirements
- _Minimum gauge_: 1.5 mm (16 gauge) mild steel
- _Joints_: Fully welded, not riveted or screwed
- _Internal surface_: Smooth to prevent grease accumulation
- _No horizontal runs_ longer than 75 feet (22.86 meters) without intermediate support
- _Slope_: Minimum 1:12 toward the hood to drain grease

### Fire Rating
- _Type II fire-rated ducts_ (1-hour rating minimum) required in fire-separated compartments per RA 9514
- _Insulation_: When required, use non-combustible materials only; combustible foam insulation is prohibited

### Access Points
- _Cleanout doors_ at minimum every 3 meters (10 feet) of duct length for maintenance
- _Each cleanout door_ must seal tightly with a removable, fire-rated cover

## Make-Up Air Requirements

Kitchen exhaust systems must be balanced with adequate make-up air to prevent negative pressurization. The Philippine design standard requires:

**Make-Up Air = 80–90% of Total Exhaust Volume**

For a 500 CFM exhaust hood:
- Minimum make-up air: 500 CFM × 0.80 = 400 CFM
- Recommended: 500 CFM × 0.90 = 450 CFM

Make-up air may be provided through:
- _Dedicated make-up air units_ with heating/cooling capability
- _Transfer air_ from adjacent dining or prep areas
- _Gravity-driven louvers_ with backdraft dampers (for perimeter locations)

Undersized make-up air causes negative building pressure, drawing in outside air and odors through uncontrolled openings, violating PD 1096 2004 IRR mechanical systems requirements.

## Kitchen Hood Fire Suppression Systems

All Type I kitchen hoods in Philippine establishments must have automatic wet chemical fire suppression per RA 9514 2019 Revised IRR (verify NFPA 17A applicability in Philippine context):

### System Components
- _Wet chemical agent_ (potassium carbonate-based, saponifies cooking oils)
- _Sensor activation_: Fusible link or temperature sensor at 200°F (93.3°C) nominal [minor rounding; verify against RA 9514 and NFPA 17A]
- _Nozzle coverage_: Minimum one nozzle per 6 square feet (0.56 m²) of hood surface
- _Agent concentration_: 5.5–6.0% by volume per NFPA 17A

### Ductwork Protection
- _Hood plenum_: Covered with fire-suppressant nozzles
- _Vertical duct sections_: Protected with internal baffles and nozzles every 1.5 meters (5 feet)
- _Horizontal sections_: Sloped at 1:12 with cleanout access for maintenance

### Inspection and Certification
Wet chemical systems must be inspected annually and certified by a licensed fire protection contractor per RA 9514. Documentation must be retained for per RA 9514 2019 Revised IRR (verify specific retention period).

## Special Mechanical Exhaust Systems

### Laboratory Fume Hoods

Laboratory fume hoods require constant, reliable exhaust to safely remove chemical vapors:

- _Design exhaust rate_: 100–120 CFM per linear foot of hood opening (per PD 1096 2004 IRR mechanical systems requirements)
- _Face velocity_: 80–120 feet per minute (0.41–0.61 m/s) across the sash opening
- _Make-up air_: 100% of exhaust volume, provided by dedicated system
- _Ductwork_: Minimum 1.2 mm steel; epoxy-lined interior for corrosive vapor resistance
- _Discharge location_: Above roof line, minimum 3 meters from any occupied structure

A 0.9-meter wide fume hood requires: 0.9 m ÷ 0.3048 = 2.95 feet × 110 CFM/foot = approximately 325 CFM exhaust.

### Paint Spray Booth Exhaust

Paint spray operations produce solvent-laden air streams that require specialized exhaust design:

- _Exhaust rate_: 100–150 CFM per linear foot of spray area per PD 1058
- _Airflow pattern_: Cross-draft or down-draft, ensuring uni-directional flow across the work surface
- _Make-up air_: 100% of exhaust, filtered through dry-type or electrostatic pre-filters (85% minimum efficiency)
- _Ductwork_: Non-ferrous metal (aluminum) to prevent static buildup; all seams bonded
- _Grounding_: All conductive ductwork bonded to ground to prevent electrostatic discharge hazard
- _Discharge location_: Above roof line, minimum 6 meters from any re-entry opening per NFPA

### Welding Shop Exhaust

Welding shops generate metal fumes and particulates requiring localized capture:

- _Exhaust rate_: 200–400 CFM per welding station per PD 1096 2004 IRR mechanical systems requirements (ANSI Z49.1 as international reference)
- _Capture velocity_: 1.0–1.5 m/s at source (fume hood or arm distance)
- _Ductwork_: Minimum 1.2 mm steel, slope for condensation drainage
- _Dust collection_: High-efficiency particulate air (HEPA) filtration or electrostatic precipitator recommended
- _Make-up air_: 90% of exhaust volume; replacement air filtered and temperature-controlled

## Parking Garage Ventilation

Enclosed parking structures require mechanical ventilation to manage vehicle exhaust and maintain acceptable air quality per PD 1096 2004 IRR and ASHRAE 62.1 (international reference):

**Design Standard: 7.5 Liters per second per square meter (L/s/m²) or minimum 6 Air Changes per Hour (ACH)**

For a 2,000 m² basement parking garage:
- Exhaust volume: 2,000 m² × 7.5 L/s/m² = 15,000 L/s (approximately 530 CFM/1,000 sq ft)
- Alternatively: 2,000 m² ÷ 6 ACH = 333 m³/min exhaust = 11,778 CFM

### System Features
- _Carbon monoxide (CO) monitoring_: Sensors trigger fan speed increase at ≥35 ppm (per ASHRAE 62.1-2019 and RA 9514 Fire Code (if applicable))
- _Intake louvers_: Minimum 4.5 meters above ground; protected from weather and street-level contaminants
- _Exhaust stacks_: Minimum 1.5 meters above roof line, non-re-entry design
- _Fan type_: Centrifugal fans with variable frequency drives (VFD) for energy efficiency and demand-controlled operation

### Smoke Control and Pressurization

High-rise or multi-level parking structures may require smoke control systems:

- _Pressurized stairwells_: 25 Pa (0.1 inch water column) minimum pressure differential per PD 1096 2004 IRR or RA 9514 Fire Code requirements
- _Smoke dampers_: Fire-rated dampers (if required by local authority) in mechanical ductwork crossing fire-rated separations
- _Evacuation signage_: Emergency lighting on all exhaust stacks and louver locations

## Laundry Exhaust Systems

Commercial laundry facilities require exhaust for moisture, lint, and odor control:

- _Exhaust rate_: 100–200 CFM per linear meter of laundry equipment bank per PD 1096 2004 IRR mechanical systems requirements
- _Dryer exhaust_: Captured directly at the dryer discharge; non-flammable ductwork (aluminum or corrosion-resistant) required
- _Humidity control_: Exhaust fans triggered by humidity sensors (>70% relative humidity) for part-load operation
- _Lint removal_: Lint filters in exhaust ductwork; cleanout access every 3 meters

A laundry with eight 45-kilogram capacity dryers operating at 150 CFM each requires: 8 × 150 = 1,200 CFM exhaust minimum, per PD 1058.

## Trash Room and Waste Area Ventilation

Trash rooms, compactor rooms, and waste storage areas require dedicated exhaust to manage odor and bio-aerosol contamination:

- _Exhaust rate_: 10 Air Changes per Hour (ACH) minimum per PD 1096 2004 IRR mechanical systems requirements
- _Make-up air_: 100% of exhaust, directed through the trash room door (creates slight negative pressure)
- _Filtration_: Activated charcoal or HEPA filter for odor and particulate removal
- _Ductwork_: Stainless steel (304 or 316 grade) for corrosion and odor resistance
- _Discharge_: Rooftop location, minimum 3 meters from occupied spaces, with odor-control cartridge in discharge stack

A 20 m² trash room with 10 ACH requires: (20 m² × 10 ACH) / 60 minutes = 3.33 m³/s exhaust = 118 CFM.

## System Maintenance and Compliance Documentation

### Inspection Schedules per RA 9514 and PD 1058
- _Monthly_: Visual inspection of grease filters, fire suppression system integrity
- _Quarterly_: Grease duct cleaning (Type I systems)
- _Annually_: Professional duct cleaning, fire suppression system testing, fan performance verification
- _Every 5 years_: Structural integrity assessment of ductwork and support systems

### Documentation Required
- _As-built drawings_: Complete mechanical schematic with CFM calculations and all equipment nameplate data
- _Commissioning reports_: Test results confirming exhaust rates, make-up air balance, and pressure differentials
- _Maintenance logs_: Signed-off records of all inspections and service work
- _Fire suppression certification_: Annual wet chemical system inspection and certification letters

## Critical Fire Safety Reminders

1. _Type I kitchen hoods without wet chemical suppression are non-compliant_ with RA 9514 and present immediate fire hazard.
2. _Grease duct material and slope_ directly affect fire spread risk; use only 1.5 mm steel with full welds and 1:12 minimum slope.
3. _Make-up air imbalance_ creates negative pressure, compromising smoke control and pressurization in high-rise buildings.
4. _Maintenance neglect_ (clogged grease ducts, inoperative dampers) invalidates both fire suppression and smoke control effectiveness.
5. _Annual professional certification_ of kitchen fire suppression systems is legally required and must be documented for inspection authority review.
