# Participatory Planning in CLUPs and Urban Design

## Board Exam Quick Check

Participatory planning is tested as a workflow and governance issue. Ask who supplies local knowledge, who makes policy decisions, who supplies technical analysis, and how feedback enters the CLUP, urban design, or project program.

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

The HLURB CLUP guide describes a combined bottom-up and top-down approach. It says CLUP work integrates national, regional, local, barangay, and community priorities, and that participatory assessment comes from barangay/community consultations, focus groups, key informants, and multi-sectoral meetings.

The RPS 2008 manual separates the political and technical components of local planning. The Sanggunian and Local Development Council shape policy direction and decisions, while the local planning and development coordinator, sectoral committees, LGU departments, national agencies, civil society, and technical groups supply planning content and process.

Participation is not only a public hearing at the end. The RPS source family points to sectoral committees, civil society/private-sector representation, barangay consultations, household surveys, and seminar-workshops as ways to elicit goals, issues, and technical input.

DHSUD resilient urban design guidance also treats placemaking as collaborative and participatory. It says community expertise, partnerships, short-term tests, and public-space feedback help shape places that are vibrant, inclusive, and functional.

The climate-resilience reference tool adds that climate action planning should involve multiple city government departments, stakeholders, and communities, especially marginalized groups, through all phases of planning.

## Exam Focus

- Bottom-up plus top-down means community and barangay input must be aligned with higher-level plans and policies.
- Technical assessment uses data; participatory assessment uses community consultations and stakeholder feedback.
- Sectoral committees and civil society help make planning continuous, not one-shot.
- Placemaking is a design-side example of participatory public realm work.
- Climate-resilient planning should include stakeholders and communities through the process.

## How It Is Tested

Expect questions comparing technical assessment with participatory assessment, identifying which body supplies policy direction, or choosing a method such as barangay consultation, focus group, sectoral committee work, placemaking, or design workshop. The best answer shows both technical evidence and genuine stakeholder input.
