# Walter Gropius

## LEA Point

Walter Gropius is the high-yield bridge between modern industrial architecture and design education. The exam pattern is not only "Bauhaus style"; it is Gropius organizing architecture, craft, industry, and social reform into a school and built examples.

## Source Anchors

- Bauhaus: founded by Walter Gropius in 1919; active through 1933; moved from Weimar to Dessau in 1925; Gropius resigned as director in 1928.
- Bauhaus Building at Dessau: opened in 1926, designed by Gropius's office, with steel, concrete, glass, functional wings, and a curtain-wall workshop expression.
- Fagus Factory: an earlier Gropius industrial work with vast glass panels and functionalist industrial aesthetics that foreshadowed Bauhaus ideas.
- Gropius House: 1938 Lincoln, Massachusetts, a teaching house combining New England materials with Bauhaus simplicity, functionality, economy, geometry, and modern details.

## Exam Pattern

If the stem says founder of the Bauhaus, Weimar 1919, craft plus industry, complete building, or Dessau school building, answer Walter Gropius. If it asks for the Bauhaus relocation year, use 1925. If it asks for Gropius House, place it after his move to the United States and Harvard context, not in the German Bauhaus campus.

## Guardrails

Do not reduce Bauhaus to a style label only; it was a school, curriculum, workshop system, and reform project. Do not attribute Sullivan's "form follows function" phrase to Gropius. Do not confuse Gropius's Bauhaus founding role with later directorships by Hannes Meyer and Mies van der Rohe.

## Real World Scenario

For an architecture school studio building, a source-safe Gropius precedent study would combine workshop visibility, rational circulation, industrial materials, and integrated craft-production learning rather than treating design as decoration added after planning.
