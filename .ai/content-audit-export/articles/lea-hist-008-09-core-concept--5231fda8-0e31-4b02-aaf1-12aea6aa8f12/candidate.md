# The Pritzker Architecture Prize

## LEA Point
The Pritzker Architecture Prize is a high-frequency contemporary architecture recognition topic. The safest source anchors are the official Pritzker About page and Laureates list: the prize was established in 1979 by the Pritzker family/Hyatt Foundation and honors living architect(s) whose built work demonstrates talent, vision, and commitment with significant contributions to humanity and the built environment.

## Source Anchors
- Philip Johnson was the first laureate in 1979.
- Gordon Bunshaft and Oscar Niemeyer shared the 1988 prize.
- Zaha Hadid became the first woman laureate in 2004.
- Frei Otto was named the 2015 laureate shortly before his death, making the award's posthumous context an exam-safe special case.
- Alejandro Aravena won in 2016 and is source-safe for ELEMENTAL, social housing, and public-interest work.
- Diébédo Francis Kéré won in 2022 and is source-safe as the first African laureate.
- Liu Jiakun is the 2025 laureate; Smiljan Radić Clarke is the 2026 laureate and the second Chilean laureate after Aravena.

## Exam Pattern
Pritzker questions commonly test firsts, years, and architect-work matching. Memorize Johnson 1979, Niemeyer/Bunshaft 1988, Gehry 1989, Ando 1995, Hadid 2004, Wang Shu 2012, Frei Otto 2015, Aravena 2016, Kéré 2022, Liu Jiakun 2025, and Radić Clarke 2026.

## Guardrails
Do not treat the prize as a building award for one project only; it recognizes a body of built work. Do not assume geographic diversity was always the same: recent laureate patterns have more visibly recognized social impact, sustainability, public interest, and non-Western contexts than many early lists.

## Real World Scenario
When an exam item describes an annual architecture honor for living architect(s), a 1979 establishment date, and a body of built work showing talent, vision, and contribution to humanity and the built environment, the source-safe answer is the Pritzker Architecture Prize.
