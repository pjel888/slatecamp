## Board Exam Quick Check

For ceiling-finish questions, identify whether the finish is directly applied, fastened to furring, suspended from a grid, or integrated with lighting and air distribution. Then check whether the item is asking about acoustics, access above the ceiling, humidity resistance, or gypsum board support.

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal

Compare gypsum board ceilings, acoustical ceiling tiles, suspended acoustical ceilings, integrated ceiling systems, linear metal ceilings, and sprayed acoustical treatments.

## Core Concept

BCI describes gypsum board as usable on ceilings either fastened directly to the underside of framing or supported by a grid of furring channels as a suspended ceiling. Gypsum board gives a smooth surface and can be finished by painting or wall covering, but support spacing, joints, and base condition still matter.

Acoustical ceiling tiles are modular units made from soft sound-absorbing materials such as cork, mineral fiber, or glass fiber. Their faces may be perforated, patterned, textured, or fissured so sound can enter fiber voids. BCI lists common modules such as 12 x 12 in., 24 x 24 in., and 24 x 48 in., and instructs the designer to consult the manufacturer for sizes, patterns, finishes, noise-reduction coefficient, fire rating, light reflectance, and suspension-system details.

Direct acoustical ceilings need a solid backing such as concrete, plaster, or gypsum board, or furring when the base is not flat enough. Salvan also describes sprayed mineral-fiber acoustical treatment applied after priming the area with adhesive.

Suspended acoustical ceilings create a concealed space for mechanical ductwork, electrical conduit, and plumbing lines. BCI describes suspension systems made from main runners, cross tees, and splines, with exposed, recessed, or concealed grids. Tiles are often removable for replacement or access. Integrated ceiling systems combine acoustical, lighting, and air-handling components. Linear metal ceilings use narrow metal strips, with open slots able to absorb sound when backed by insulation.

## How It's Tested

Exam items usually ask why a suspended ceiling is selected or why direct-applied tile is not enough. If the item needs access to services above the ceiling, suspended systems are the safe answer. If it asks for acoustic properties, manufacturer data is part of the source-supported coordination.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating every ceiling tile as removable even when it is adhesive-set to a backing.
- Ignoring manufacturer data for acoustic, fire, light-reflectance, and suspension details.
- Choosing gypsum board where the question requires access to services above the ceiling.
- Omitting the grid components in a suspended ceiling answer.
- Treating sprayed mineral-fiber treatment as the same thing as modular suspended tiles.
