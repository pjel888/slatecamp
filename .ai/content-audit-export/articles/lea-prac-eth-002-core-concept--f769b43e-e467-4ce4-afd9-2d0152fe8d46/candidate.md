## What You Need to Know for the LEA
## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to:

- Explain the purpose and aspirational nature of the Architect's Credo as distinct from the enforceable Code of Ethical Conduct.
- Identify the core pledges in the Architect's Credo (duty to self, country, God, profession, and clients).
- Distinguish the Credo's role in character evaluation from the Code's role in disciplinary proceedings.
- Apply the distinction between aspirational ethics and enforceable conduct rules to LEA exam scenarios.

The Architect's Credo (promulgated via PRBoA Resolution No. 02, Series of 2006) condenses ethical principles into core commitments recited at professional oaths. It reinforces but does not replace the Code of Ethical Conduct; the Credo expresses aspirational values while the Code establishes enforceable obligations.

## Core Concept

The Architect's Credo is a compendium of principles and values embodied in professional identity and conduct. The Credo is a multi-statement oath beginning with: "I shall work with this virtuous commitment: to exercise to the utmost my duty to myself, my country, and my God..." (full text as per PRBoA Res. No. 02, Series of 2006).

The Credo is a mandatory recitation at mass oath-takings, IAPOA affairs, and PRBoA events. It establishes *tone* and *character* expectations; the Code establishes *conduct* standards. Architects who violate Code provisions may additionally be found to have abandoned Credo principles (grounds for character evaluation).

<LawCallout code="ra9266" section="PRBoA Res. No. 02, Series of 2006" title="The Architect's Credo — Legal Status">
The Architect's Credo is promulgated under PRBoA authority. It is a mandatory recitation but is aspirational rather than directly enforceable through discipline. Violations of the Credo alone cannot trigger suspension or revocation — only Code violations can. However, the Credo is used as supporting evidence in character evaluations during disciplinary proceedings.
</LawCallout>

## Key Provisions

The Architect's Credo is not structurally divided into named subsections; rather, it is a continuous series of "I shall..." pledges. For LEA review purposes, the core pledges encompass:

| Credo Pledge / Theme | Core Language | LEA Relevance |
|----------------------|---------------|---------------|
| Virtuous Commitment | "duty to myself, my country, and my God" | Tests understanding that architect duty extends beyond the client — encompassing a societal and moral role |
| Norms of Conduct | "uphold the ideals, follow the norms of conduct... abide by the laws... and the Articles of Incorporation and By-Laws of the [IAPOA]" | Tests whether professional standing, reputation, and adherence to legal frameworks are the architect's responsibility |
| Professional Excellence | "seek success not through the measure of solicited personal publicity, but by industrious, meaningful application" | Tests commitment to work quality, honest business practices, and prohibition on unethical marketing |
| Fair Remuneration & Loyalty | "ask for fair remuneration... hold his/her interest over and above my own" | Tests fiduciary duty to the client and proper compensation standards |
| Conflict of Interest | "disclose... any private business investments or ventures that may tend to create a conflict of interest" | Tests the obligation to maintain impartial judgment and transparency |
| Mentorship | "inspire by my behavior the loyalty of my associates... take upon me the mentorship of the aspirants" | Tests willingness to educate and contribute to the profession's advancement |
| Constructive Criticism | "confine my criticisms and praises within constructive and inspirational limits and never resort... to promote any malicious motives" | Tests the prohibition on malicious statements and ensuring competitive fairness |
| Sharing Knowledge | "dedicate myself to the pursuit of creative endeavor... generously sharing the results of my research, experience, and expertise" | Tests the commitment to advancing architectural Science and Art collectively |

## Philippine Practice Context

The Architect's Credo functions as the profession's character covenant. In Philippine licensure examinations and disciplinary proceedings, credo violations are examined alongside Code violations to assess moral fitness.

Recitation of the Credo at public oath-taking ceremonies symbolizes profession entry and commitment renewal. Unlike the Code's strict legal enforceability, the Credo's force derives from professional identity — architects who act against credo principles risk reputational damage, peer censure, and ultimately disciplinary action under the Code.

<Warn>
  **Critical Distinction:** The LEA often tests whether the Credo is "enforceable." The answer is NO — the Credo is aspirational. Only the Code of Ethical Conduct (SPP Doc 200) and RA 9266 are directly enforceable through PRBoA disciplinary action. However, abandoning Credo principles can be used as supporting character evidence in Code violation proceedings.
</Warn>

## How It's Tested

**Question Pattern 1:** Principle-to-conduct alignment — Which Credo principle supports the Code obligation to avoid conflicts of interest?
(Tests the disclosure of private business investments clause)

**Question Pattern 2:** Distinction between Credo and Code — Is the Credo enforceable through PRBoA discipline, or is it aspirational?
(Tests understanding of Credo as a character standard versus Code as enforceable law)

**Question Pattern 3:** Publicity and Marketing — How does the Credo instruct architects to seek success?
(Tests the prohibition on solicited personal publicity in favor of industrious application)

**Question Pattern 4:** Constructive Criticism — Under the Credo, how should an architect handle criticisms?
(Tests the pledge to confine criticisms to constructive limits without malicious motives)

<ExamTip>
  **LEA Watch:** Questions about the Credo often pair it with a Code provision and ask which applies. Remember: If the question involves a "pledge," "commitment," or "character" — it's the Credo. If it involves enforceable obligations, sanctions, or mandatory conduct — it's the Code. The Credo uses "I shall" language; the Code uses numbered sections.
</ExamTip>

<Tip>
  Remember that the Credo is a series of personal pledges ("I shall...")
  covering duty to God/country, ethical norms, fair dealing, impartial judgment,
  mentorship, constructive criticism, and sharing knowledge. The Credo aspires;
  the Code constrains. Both are required for full professional conduct.
</Tip>
