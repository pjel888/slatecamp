<Callout>Smoke control systems are critical life-safety components of any building design. The LEA will test your understanding of smoke zone boundaries, pressurization requirements, egress path protection, and the integration of mechanical smoke management with passive fire-resistive construction. Mastery of these concepts is essential for passing the exam and for competent professional practice.</Callout>

<Tip>**Key distinction**: Smoke zones differ from fire zones. Smoke zones limit smoke migration to maintain tenable egress paths, while fire zones compartmentalize fire spread. Both systems must work together, but they serve different purposes under the building code. The LEA frequently tests whether candidates can distinguish these concepts in scenario problems.</Tip>

<Warn>Common design error: assuming that passive fire-resistive construction alone satisfies smoke control requirements. Many building codes require active smoke management systems (mechanical pressurization, exhaust, or stair pressurization) in addition to passive compartmentation. Overlooking active smoke control can result in non-compliant designs that fail to protect egress paths during a fire event.</Warn>
