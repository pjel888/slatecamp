### Question `ef6f21c7-eed0-4da6-a9e5-c5daafa36b93`

**Current marked-correct answer:** option `c` — "Colossal order (giant order)" — and the rationale asserts that "Palazzo Farnese demonstrates colossal order—giant columns or pilasters spanning two or three stories, creating visual unity and monumentality across the tall facade."

**External finding:** This attribution is incorrect. The Palazzo Farnese facade (Antonio da Sangallo the Younger, completed by Michelangelo with the cornice and upper levels) is the textbook example of **superimposed orders** — each floor carries a distinct order (Tuscan/Doric, Ionic, Corinthian), inspired by the Theatre of Marcellus. It is explicitly contrasted with the **colossal/giant order** approach (a single order spanning multiple stories), which was used elsewhere by Michelangelo (e.g., Laurentian Library vestibule) and Palladio (Palazzo Valmarana), but not on Palazzo Farnese.
