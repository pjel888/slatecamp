# Classification of Buildings by Human Need

## Source-Audited Scope

Use this topic as a theory/design classification topic. The checked local sources support classifying buildings by the human needs and activities they serve. This article does not replace code occupancy classification.

## Board Exam Quick Check

When a question asks for building classification by human need, identify the human activity or need being served. Do not confuse this theory topic with legal occupancy groups or fire-code categories.

## Core Concepts

- Basic human needs include shelter, security, association, recognition, work, recreation, education, and comfort.
- Building types arise because human activities call for accompanying structures.
- Houses, factories, churches, schools, clubs, and other building types can be understood by the needs and activities they support.
- Theory sources also map needs through physiological shelter, security, affiliation, esteem, and self-actualization.
- A source-safe answer classifies by human use and need, not by arbitrary appearance.

## LEA Use

These questions often ask why a type of building exists or what need it primarily serves. The strongest answer starts from the user's activity and need. A distractor usually imports code occupancy or style labels into a theory-of-architecture question.

## Active Recall

1. What is the basis of this classification? Human needs and activities.
2. What should not be confused with it? Legal occupancy or fire-code classification.
3. What is the safe exam move? Match the building type to the need it serves.
