# Spatial Organization Types

## Source-Audited Scope

Use this topic for Ching-style spatial organization questions. The checked local sources support five organization types: centralized, linear, radial, clustered, and grid.

## Board Exam Quick Check

Find the relationship among spaces. A dominant center suggests centralized. A sequence or spine suggests linear. A hub with arms suggests radial. Grouping by proximity or function suggests clustered. A modular field suggests grid.

## Core Concepts

- Centralized organization is a stable, concentrated composition with secondary spaces grouped around a large dominant central space.
- Linear organization is a series of spaces. They may connect directly or through a separate linear space.
- Radial organization combines centralized and linear logic: a dominant central space with linear arms extending outward.
- Clustered organization relates spaces by physical proximity. It may use repetitive cellular spaces that share function, shape, orientation, or another visual trait.
- Grid organization regulates forms and spaces by positions and relationships within a three-dimensional grid pattern or field.
- Complex buildings can combine types, but an exam stem usually asks for the dominant organizing logic.

## Real World Scenario

A clinic has a central waiting hall, diagnostic rooms along one corridor, administrative rooms grouped together, and a structural bay grid. If the question asks about the waiting hall with departments extending from it, radial may be the best answer. If it asks about the grouped offices, clustered may be better. Match the answer to the part of the plan described.

## LEA Use

Do not confuse centralized and radial: both have a center, but radial has arms. Do not confuse clustered and grid: both can have repeated spaces, but grid depends on a regular field while clustered depends on proximity or functional grouping.

## Active Recall

1. What distinguishes radial from centralized? Radial adds linear arms extending from a dominant center.
2. What defines clustered organization? Physical proximity or functional grouping without strict central or grid order.
3. What defines grid organization? A regular three-dimensional grid pattern or field.
4. What is the exam trap? Naming the entire building instead of the specific relationship described in the stem.
