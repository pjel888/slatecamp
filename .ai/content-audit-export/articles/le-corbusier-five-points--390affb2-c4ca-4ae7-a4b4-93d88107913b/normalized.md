# Le Corbusier Five Points

## Board Exam Quick Check

Memorize the five points as one set: pilotis, roof garden, free plan, ribbon windows, and free facade. The checked sources connect the set to Le Corbusier's ideas for modern living and identify Villa Savoye at Poissy as the key example.

## Source-Grounded Core

The local HOA reviewer identifies Le Corbusier as Charles Edouard Jeanneret and gives the board-review quote: the house is a machine for living in. It then lists the Five Points of Architecture as the basis of modern architecture according to Le Corbusier.

The checked 20th Century Architects source gives the same core list: pilotis, roof garden, free plan, ribbon windows, and free facade. It also notes that Villa Savoye became a model for the International Style and that Le Corbusier later moved toward a more brutalist direction after World War II.

TOA 5 reinforces the idea that Le Corbusier's houses gave physical form to the machine-for-living idea and incorporated his trademark five points of architecture.

## The Five Points

- Pilotis: raising the building on supports.
- Roof garden: recovering usable roof plane.
- Free plan: freeing interior planning from load-bearing wall limits.
- Ribbon windows: long horizontal window bands.
- Free facade: separating facade composition from structural wall constraints.

## LEA Answer Pattern

When the exam asks for the five points, choose the complete set rather than a partial list. When the prompt shows Villa Savoye, connect it immediately to Le Corbusier and the five points. When the prompt shifts to Ronchamp, Unite d'Habitation, or Chandigarh, treat it as a later Le Corbusier work rather than the pure five-points diagram.

## Common Exam Traps

- Do not replace free facade with generic curtain wall unless the option clearly preserves the five-point idea.
- Do not confuse roof garden with ordinary roof deck amenity. In this topic, it is one item in Le Corbusier's system.
- Do not assign the five points to Mies, Gropius, or Wright. The checked sources identify them with Le Corbusier.
