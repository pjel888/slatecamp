# SPP Document 204-A: Standards of Professional Practice on Fulltime Supervision Services

## Overview

**SPP Document 204-A** governs **Fulltime Supervision Services** — the engagement of an architect or a Construction Supervision Group (CSG) to provide continuous, on-site, eight-hour-a-day construction supervision for a building project in the Philippines. It is part of the 2010 Standards of Professional Practice (SPP), promulgated as the Supplemental Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of **Republic Act No. 9266** (The Architecture Act of 2004), and replaces the earlier **1979 UAP Doc. 204-A**.

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to:

Identify the scope and compensation for SPP Doc 204-A Full-Time Supervision Services: the architect's duties during construction (daily inspection, quality control verification, interpretation of contract documents, preparation of change orders); distinguish full-time supervision from the periodic observation included in SPP 202 Phase 5; apply the monthly salary basis or percentage-of-salary compensation.
---
This document directly addresses the gap left by SPP 202 Regular Design Services, which only requires the architect to make **periodic site visits** during the construction phase — not full-time 8-hour daily supervision. When a client requires more rigorous, continuous on-site oversight, SPP 204-A provides the separate framework for that engagement.

Fulltime Supervision Services must be rendered by a **Registered and Licensed Architect (RLA)** acting as the **Architect-in-charge of construction (Aicc)**. The Aicc is **directly and professionally responsible and civilly liable** for the construction supervision of the project.

Under **Article 1723 of the New Civil Code of the Philippines**, if an engineer or architect supervises the construction, he shall be **solidarily liable** with the contractor for damages should the structure collapse or fail within 15 years from completion due to defects in the construction or materials. This is the primary legal reason why fulltime supervision carries both greater responsibility and greater professional fee than the periodic inspection role under SPP 202.

## Background: The Evolution of Construction Supervision

For the past 50 years, four principal members have been involved in the design and building construction process:

1. **The Owner** — who orders the implementation of the project
2. **The Architect and Specialist Consultants (SCs)** — who render plan/design services and **limited** inspection work (under SPP 202)
3. **The Contractor** — who performs the actual construction work
4. **Individuals who assist in the supervision and delivery of the work** — traditionally a Clerk-of-Works, and now the CSG (under SPP 204-A)

The evolution from the Clerk-of-Works to the CSG reflects the growing size and complexity of construction projects:

- **Clerk-of-Works era**: When projects were manageable in scale, the architect was assisted by a single construction inspector — the Clerk-of-Works — who monitored day-to-day work on behalf of the architect and owner
- **CSG era**: As projects became larger and more technically complex, a single individual was no longer adequate. The **Construction Supervision Group (CSG)** — comprising several qualified inspectors — became necessary for continuous, multi-discipline on-site inspection

***

## The Construction Supervision Group (CSG)

The **Construction Supervision Group (CSG)** is the operational core of SPP 204-A. Key facts about the CSG:

- It is **normally recommended by the Architect** based on performance track record
- It is **hired directly by the Owner** (not the architect) — the Owner pays the CSG's fees separately
- It is **answerable to both the Owner and the Architect**
- It may be constituted as a qualified architectural firm or as individual licensed professionals

This dual accountability structure — answerable to both Owner and Architect — is a critical legal and operational feature. The CSG must comply with the architect's technical directions while simultaneously reporting the project's progress and status to the owner.

SPP 204-A defines four primary service areas for fulltime supervision. Detailed tasks are specified under the Architect's Guidelines:

### 2.1 Quality Control

Quality control under SPP 204-A covers the systematic verification that all construction work meets the standards established in the Contract Documents. A **Quality Control Plan (QCP)** generally covers:

- **Project personnel** — ensuring qualifications and deployment of key construction staff
- **Quality communication** — information flow between architect, CSG, contractor, and owner
- **Quality assurance surveillance** — regular monitoring of work in progress
- **Subcontractors and suppliers** — verification of credentials, materials, and compliance
- **Project quality specifications** — benchmark standards for materials, finishes, and workmanship
- **Inspections and tests** — schedules and protocols for material testing and site inspections
- **Control of non-conformance** — processes for documenting and rectifying defective work
- **Project implementation schedule** — tracking construction milestones against approved program

### 2.2 Evaluation of Construction Work

The CSG continuously evaluates the executed work against the approved **Contract Documents** (construction drawings and technical specifications) prepared under SPP 202. Evaluation covers:

- **Conformance** of completed works to approved plans, details, and specifications
- **Workmanship quality** — whether methods and finishes meet professional and contractual standards
- **Progress** — whether construction is proceeding in accordance with the approved project implementation schedule
- **Material compliance** — whether materials used match those specified or approved alternatives

### 2.3 Preparation of Daily Inspection Reports

The CSG prepares **Daily Inspection Reports (DIR)** documenting:

- Work accomplished during the day (type of work, location, quantities)
- Labor and equipment deployed by the contractor
- Materials delivered and tested on-site
- Weather conditions
- Observations on quality and conformance
- Instructions issued to the contractor
- Any disputes, incidents, or deviations from the Contract Documents

These reports serve as the primary contemporaneous record of the construction process and are essential for resolving future disputes, preparing progress billings, and establishing the factual basis for change orders.

### 2.4 Filing of Documents

The CSG maintains an organized filing system of all project documentation, including:

- Daily Inspection Reports
- Approved and pending Change Orders
- Shop drawings and material submittals
- Test reports and inspection certificates
- Correspondence between owner, architect, CSG, and contractor
- Progress photographs
- As-built drawing mark-ups

## Manner of Providing Services

There are **two ways** by which the Architect may enter into contract with the Owner for Fulltime Supervision Services:

### 3.1 Dual Capacity: Architect-of-record + Consulting Architect for Fulltime Supervision (or as the CSG)

In this arrangement, the Architect serves simultaneously as the **Architect-of-record (Aor)** for the design and as the **Consulting Architect for fulltime supervision**. This is generally the preferred arrangement because:

- As the Aor, the architect is in the **best position to interpret drawings and documents** and assure conformity by the Contractor — the architect knows the design intent better than anyone
- The architect can **assign staff from his own office** (technical staff, draftsmen, or site inspectors) to undertake the fulltime supervisory work as enumerated in the Architect's Guidelines
- Communication between design intent and on-site execution is seamless, reducing the risk of misinterpretation

### 3.2 Consulting Architect Only (or as the CSG)

In this arrangement, the Architect is engaged **solely to provide fulltime supervision services**, with a different architect serving as the Aor for the project. This may occur when:

- The design architect is based in a different city or region and cannot provide on-site supervision
- The owner prefers to engage a separate specialist firm for construction supervision
- The project involves a design-builder (under SPP 207) who has a separate design team

## Method of Compensation

Compensation for Fulltime Supervision Services is based on the Architect's/architectural firm's **tleants, skill, experience**, and on the **type and level of professional services provided**. Six methods may be applied:

### 4.1 Percentage (%) of Project Construction Cost (PCC)

The most common basis for fulltime supervision fees. The specific percentage applicable to each project type and PCC range is detailed in the **Architect's Guidelines**. Note that the supervision fee is a **separate percentage** on top of the Regular Design Services fee under SPP 202; they are not combined into a single rate.

### 4.2 Multiple of Direct Personnel Expenses (MDPE)

Applicable where the cost of professional time is the most equitable basis. The formula is:

\[
\text\{Direct Cost\} = (A \times AN) + (C \times CN) + (T \times TN)
\]

\[
\text\{Fee\} = \text\{Direct Cost\} \times M
\]

\[
\text\{Total Cost to Client\} = \text\{Fee\} + R
\]

Where:
- \(A\) = Architect's rate per hour
- \(C\) = Consultant's rate per hour
- \(T\) = Rate per hour of Technical Staff, Inspectors, and others involved in the project
- \(AN, CN, TN\) = Number of hours spent by each party
- \(M\) = Multiplier (ranging from **1.5 to 2.5**) to account for overhead and reasonable profit, depending on the office set-up and complexity of the project
- \(R\) = Reimbursable expenses (transportation, out-of-town living allowances for staff assigned more than 100 km from the office, extra printing, overseas communications, lab tests, etc.)

### 4.3 Professional Fee Plus Expenses

Used where there is a **continuing relationship** involving a series of projects. A fixed sum is established above and beyond reimbursement for the architect's technical time and overhead. An agreement on the general scope of work is required to set an equitable fee.

### 4.4 Lump Sum or Fixed Fee

Applied where the **scope of supervision work is well-defined** and predictable — most commonly for government projects, which entail more paperwork and time-consuming administrative processes.

### 4.5 Per Diem, Honorarium Plus Reimbursable Expenses

Applied when the architect's personal time is required for specific site visits, conferences, or project-related meetings — particularly appropriate for limited-duration assignments or when the architect is based far from the project site.

### 4.6 Mixed Methods of Compensation

More than one method may be applied to a single project. For example, the principal architect's oversight role may be compensated on a Professional Fee Plus Expenses basis, while the CSG's daily site work is compensated through MDPE.

## Limitation of Authority

The CSG's authority is deliberately bounded to protect the integrity of the construction process and avoid legal entanglements:

- The CSG shall **not assume the responsibility of the Contractor's project superintendent** — that is, it must not directly manage, direct, or control the contractor's workforce or methods
- The CSG shall **not involve itself directly with the contractor's work** in any way that could be construed as assuming the contractor's liability under Article 1723 of the Civil Code
- The CSG shall **not impose methods, systems, or designs** that will substantially affect the construction schedule or impair the design concept of the Architect

These limitations exist to maintain the clear separation between the **professional role** (verification and reporting) and the **contractual role** (execution and delivery). Blurring this line exposes the CSG — and by extension the Aor — to broader legal liability.

### Cross-Claims and Civil Liability

If the **Aor and Eors are sued by an Owner** for civil liabilities traceable to the CSG's performance or non-performance, the Aor and Eors may **file cross-claims against the CSG**. This is a significant legal protection for the design architect: the law allows the Aor to seek indemnification from the CSG for liabilities that properly belong to the CSG's supervision failures, rather than to design deficiencies.

Conversely, if the architect supervises construction (taking on the Aicc role), Article 1723 of the Civil Code explicitly provides that the architect **shall be solidarily liable with the contractor** for structural defects that manifest within 15 years from project completion. This solidary liability is the most compelling reason why fulltime supervision carries a significantly higher fee and requires a separate, written Owner-Architect Agreement.

## Qualifications of CSG Inspectors

Inspectors of the Construction Supervision Group (CSG) must possess:

1. **A Bachelor of Science in Architecture (B.S. Arch.) degree** — Only licensed architects are qualified to serve as CSG inspectors under SPP 204-A
2. **Extensive experience in design and building construction** — The inspector must have sufficient practical exposure to construction methods, materials, and sequencing to properly evaluate field work
3. **Thorough knowledge of building materials and construction detailing** — The inspector must be able to read and interpret Contract Documents, identify non-conforming work, and advise the contractor on correct execution methods

## Practical Notes for Architects and Clients

**For Architects:**
- The Aicc role under SPP 204-A triggers **solidary liability** under Article 1723 of the Civil Code — do not informally assume fulltime supervision without a proper written agreement and separate fee arrangement.
- When acting in dual capacity (Aor + fulltime supervisor), maintain clear records distinguishing design-phase services from supervision-phase services, as these are separately compensated.
- The CSG works under the architect's technical direction but is hired and paid by the owner — document all instructions to the CSG in writing to establish a clear chain of technical authority.

**For Clients / Project Proponents:**
- Fulltime supervision is **not included** in the regular architect's fee under SPP 202 — budget for it as a separate line item from the outset.
- The CSG is recommended by the architect but **paid by the owner directly** — the CSG fee does not pass through the architect's accounts.
- Engaging a CSG is most advisable for large, complex, or high-value projects where construction quality, material conformance, and progress control are critical — the cost of supervision is typically far lower than the cost of construction defects discovered after turnover.
- Ensure the Owner-Architect Agreement for SPP 204-A clearly states: the scope of the CSG, the number of inspectors, their schedules, the method of compensation, and the reporting structure.
