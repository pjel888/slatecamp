# Megalithic Architecture

## LEA Point
Megalithic architecture is best learned as a family of prehistoric monument types made with large stones, not as one universal building system. For LEA identification, classify the form first: standing stone, alignment, stone circle, trilithon/post-and-lintel setting, passage grave, or Maltese temple complex.

## Source Anchors
- UNESCO Stonehenge and Avebury anchors the late Neolithic/Bronze Age monument landscape and supports cautious claims about standing stones, lintelled arrangements, and ritual/funerary/astronomical interpretation.
- UNESCO Megalithic Temples of Malta anchors Ggantija and related Maltese sites as megalithic temple complexes.
- UNESCO Bru na Boinne anchors Newgrange, Knowth, and Dowth as passage-tomb and chambered mound monuments.
- Historic Environment Scotland anchors Calanais/Callanish as a cruciform standing-stone setting and warns against single-purpose certainty.

## Core Pattern
Megalithic work depends on large stone pieces, but the architectural result changes by region. Stonehenge uses standing stones and horizontal lintels to make trilithon and circle arrangements. Bru na Boinne uses a passage leading to chambered tomb spaces under a mound. Malta uses megalithic stones to form temple complexes rather than stone circles or passage graves.

## Exam Guardrails
- Do not call every megalith a tomb. Some are passage tombs, some are stone settings, and some are temple complexes.
- Do not turn cautious astronomical or ritual interpretations into a single proven purpose for every site.
- A trilithon is two uprights carrying one horizontal stone. It is a post-and-lintel arrangement, not a corbelled chamber.
- Corbelling belongs with chambered/passage-grave construction, where stones project inward course by course.

## Real World Scenario
A review item describes a narrow passage into a covered chamber under a mound. The source-safe answer is passage grave, not stone circle. If the item instead describes two uprights and a lintel, identify the trilithon/post-and-lintel logic before naming a site.
