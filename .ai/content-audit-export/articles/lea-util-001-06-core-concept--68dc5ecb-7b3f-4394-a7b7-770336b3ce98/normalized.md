## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal

Identify waste disposal and treatment systems (septic tanks, grease traps, sewage treatment plants, leach fields) and apply Revised National Plumbing Code requirements for sizing, clearances, and discharge standards in Philippine building design.
## Core Concept

Building waste disposal encompasses both liquid (wastewater/sewage) and solid waste streams. Liquid waste treatment begins at the source through _septic tanks_ for individual buildings, _grease traps_ for food-preparation areas, and _sewage treatment plants (STP)_ for larger developments. The Revised National Plumbing Code mandates primary treatment (settling and sludge removal) before discharge to environment or municipal systems. Secondary treatment removes dissolved and colloidal matter through biological processes. Tertiary treatment polishes effluent to potable or reuse standards, increasingly required in water-stressed regions.

Solid waste management in buildings must segregate waste at source per RA 9003: biodegradable (food, plant matter), residual waste, recyclables (paper, metal, plastic), and hazardous (batteries, chemicals). The Revised National Plumbing Code Section 303 specifies garbage chutes, compactors, and storage rooms. Recycling systems reduce landfill impact; many Philippine projects now integrate waste-to-energy or composting facilities.

For septic tank design, the Revised National Plumbing Code Table 1 requires minimum capacity based on fixture units and groundwater depth. Detention time is typically 24 hours (per Revised National Plumbing Code §305.4). Grease traps must be sized for flow rate and installed upstream of septic systems. Municipal sewage treatment plants (operated by water districts) apply biological and chemical treatment; discharge must meet DENR-EMB Class C standards (BOD ≤50 mg/L per RA 9275 rules).

## Philippine Construction Context

In Metro Manila and major urban centers, most multistory buildings discharge to municipal sewer systems operated by the Metropolitan Waterworks and Sewerage System (MWSS) or local water districts. Building owners must obtain a discharge permit from DENR-EMB or the local Environmental Management Bureau office. Septic tank design remains mandatory for subdivisions, hospitals, and remote facilities outside municipal networks.

Common STP brands installed in Philippine projects include Pureflow (package plants), BioSand (modular), and locally manufactured reinforced concrete tanks. In provinces with limited sewerage, on-site STP + leach field is standard. The Revised National Plumbing Code requires leach field inspection every 3 years and desludging every 3–5 years. Many local government units (LGUs) now enforce mandatory waste segregation (RA 9003) and push for waste-to-energy or community composting initiatives. Private waste collection operators (ABC, Sarangani, Metro Waste) manage solid waste pickup; building waste management plans must specify recyclable material aggregation points and final disposal routes.

## How It's Tested

**Pattern 1: Septic Tank Sizing**
_A 200-unit residential condominium in Cavite with 4 fixture units per unit has groundwater at 2 m depth. Using Revised National Plumbing Code Table 1, calculate minimum total septic tank capacity and estimate leach field area (assuming 1.4 m² per capita per day, 3 persons per unit)._

**Pattern 2: Grease Trap and Treatment Hierarchy**
_A 100-bed hospital has a commercial kitchen (peak flow 15 L/min) and 200 toilets. What size grease trap is required (per Revised National Plumbing Code § 307.4), and what treatment level (primary/secondary) must effluent achieve before discharge to municipal sewer per RA 9275?_

**Pattern 3: Solid Waste Management Plan (RA 9003)**
_An office building in BGC generates 150 kg waste per day. Draft segregation strategy, storage volume (3-day max per RA 9003), and identify approved disposal facilities for biodegradable, recyclable, and non-recyclable streams._

**Pattern 4: Discharge Permit and DENR-EMB Standards**
_A manufacturing facility proposes discharge of treated effluent (BOD = 45 mg/L) to a creek. Is a DENR-EMB Class C water discharge permit required (RA 9275)? What additional treatment is needed if the receiving water is designated Class B?_
