## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Adaptive reuse of heritage properties requires balancing modern building codes with historical preservation. The occupancy classification depends on the _new_ function, not the original use. Heritage buildings must comply with RA 10066 (Cultural Heritage Act) and gain NHCP (National Historical Commission of the Philippines) clearance before modifications affecting character-defining features.

## Learning Goal

Determine occupancy classification based on new programmed function (not original use), apply RA 10066 and NHCP clearance requirements, use reversible interventions and compatible materials, calculate egress and accessibility compliance for heritage constraints, and identify when variances are appropriate.

## Core Concept

Adaptive reuse (or adaptive reuse design) is the practice of converting an existing building to a new use or function while preserving its cultural and historical character. A century-old stone church converted to a library (new function = Group C), a colonial mansion reused as a small hotel (Group B), or an old warehouse adapted as an artist collective (Group C/E) each represents adaptive reuse.

The occupancy classification for an adaptively reused heritage building is determined by its _new_ programmed function, not its original use. A former church (originally assembly, Group C) reused as a museum (new classification = Group C, same) has one occupancy. A colonial warehouse (originally Group F industrial) converted to office suites (new = Group E) changes occupancy and thus fire safety, accessibility, and egress requirements.

Heritage conservation in the Philippines is governed by **RA 10066** (Cultural Heritage Act of 2010) and administered by the **National Historical Commission of the Philippines (NHCP)**. The act protects movable and immovable cultural property, including buildings, archaeological sites, and cultural landscapes. Any alteration, restoration, or adaptive reuse of a registered heritage property requires NHCP review and clearance.

Key design principles for heritage-adaptive reuse:
1. _Character-defining features_ (architectural style, spatial hierarchy, materials, decorative elements) are identified and preserved.
2. _Reversible interventions_ are preferred—temporary alterations that can be undone without damaging the original fabric.
3. _Minimum intervention_ — new systems (electrical, plumbing, HVAC) are routed to minimize visual or structural impact.
4. _Compatible materials_ — replacements use materials matching original color, texture, and performance.
5. _Accessibility integration_ — ramps, lifts, and emergency egress are designed to blend with heritage aesthetics.

Code compliance (NBC, **BP 344**) still applies: the new use must meet all egress, fire-safety, and accessibility standards, even if achieving full compliance requires creative design solutions or seeks variances from the NHCP and local authority.

## Key Provisions

**Heritage Regulatory Framework:**

- **RA 10066** (Cultural Heritage Act): Mandates NHCP review for significant alterations to registered heritage properties
- **NHCP Clearance**: Required before design finalization and construction of any adaptive reuse project
- **Character-Defining Features**: Identified in NHCP documentation; modifications to these require special justification

**Occupancy Classification (Based on New Function):**

| New Function | Occupancy Group | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Office/Administrative | Group E | Business and mercantile |
| Museum/Library | Group C | Education and recreation |
| Hotel/Lodging | Group B | Residential-like (transient) |
| Retail/Commercial | Group E | Business and mercantile |
| Assembly/Event Space | Group C or H | Depends on configuration |
| Residential Conversion | Group A | Permanent occupancy |

**Design Integration Standards:**

- _New system routing_: Electrical, plumbing, HVAC in secondary zones or concealed paths (walls, attics, crawl spaces)
- _Exit/accessibility integration_: Ramps, elevators, stair widening—designed for visual and material compatibility
- _Structural: Minimize alterations to primary structural members; use non-invasive bracing or reinforcement
- _Materials_: Match original in color, texture, composition; use reversible techniques (e.g., lime mortar, wood stain vs. permanent paint)

**Code Compliance:**

New occupancy must meet full NBC requirementExit width: 7.6cm/person (**PD 1096 Rule VIII Section 811**), sized for new occupancy load
- Ramp slope: 1:20 maximum (**BP 344**, 2024 Revised IRR) [Note: supersedes 2013 IRR]
- Accessible door: 900mm clear width (**BP 344**)
- Travel distance: 45m or 60m per occupancy group
- Fire separation: Depends on mixed-use or single-occupancy configuration

If a heritage building cannot physically accommodate a full-width exit (e.g., historic doorway is 800mm), the architect may request a variance from the NHCP and local Building Official, justifying the cultural constraint. Such variances are case-specific and require documented evidence of infeasibility of code compliance.

## Philippine Practice Context

The Philippines is home to centuries of architectural heritage—Spanish-era churches, American-colonial administrative buildings, indigenous bahay na bato structures, and Japanese-period institutional buildings. Many cities face pressure to demolish heritage structures due to age, functional obsolescence, or economic pressure. Adaptive reuse is encouraged as an alternative that preserves cultural identity while enabling economic viability. The National Museum, private heritage hotels, and restored barangay halls demonstrate successful adaptive reuse. The NHCP maintains the National Registry of Cultural Properties; listing provides legal protection and may unlock heritage preservation grants or tax incentives.

The challenge for architects is code compliance without destroying character. A heritage mansion being converted to a restaurant (Group E with commercial kitchen) must achieve modern fire separations and ventilation; integrating ductwork without destroying decorative ceiling plasterwork requires careful design. An old theater adapted as a museum (Group C to Group C) may retain its original exit configuration if the new occupant load permits, but accessibility upgrades (ramp, lift) must be sympathetically designed.

## How It's Tested

**Pattern 1:** _Function-based occupancy determination_ — "A registered heritage building (originally a school, Group C) is being converted to a library and archive (new function = _____ ). The occupancy classification changes based on _____ (original use / new use)."

**Pattern 2:** _Regulatory compliance and variance scenarios_ — "During adaptive reuse design, a historic stone wall prevents a required accessible door width of 900mm. The architect should (a) obtain _____ clearance, (b) prepare a variance request with cultural justification, or (c) use a _____ (reversible/permanent) intervention."

**Pattern 3:** _Character preservation vs. code compliance_ — "A heritage chapel (original 400 sqm, seating 150) is being converted to an office (10 sqm per person, occupant load = 40). The new occupancy load is _____, and exit width requirement is _____ mm/person per NBC."
