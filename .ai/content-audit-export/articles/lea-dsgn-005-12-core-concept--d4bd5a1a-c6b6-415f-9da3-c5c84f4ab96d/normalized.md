# Adaptive Reuse Design

## Board Exam Quick Check

Adaptive reuse questions usually test three decisions: whether the property is protected cultural property, what the new use or occupancy is, and which code upgrades are required for the altered building. Anchor the answer to RA 10066, PD 1096, and accessibility requirements before choosing a design strategy.

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

RA 10066 recognizes conservation as the protection, preservation, restoration, reconstruction, and adaptation of cultural property. The law also identifies heritage zones and states that heritage-zone maintenance may include adaptive reuse of cultural property.

For protected or marked cultural property, intervention works and conservation measures are undertaken through the appropriate cultural agency, which supervises the work and approves methods and materials that adhere to accepted conservation standards. RA 10066 also penalizes modifying, altering, or destroying original features of declared or marked historic edifices and structures without prior written permission from the Commission.

PD 1096 treats conversion as a change in the use or occupancy of a building or part of a building that has different requirements. A building with an existing certificate of occupancy may need another certificate because of changes in use, provided the new use conforms with the Code and IRR. For LEA purposes, classify the reused building by the proposed use, not by nostalgia for the original use.

Examples from the checked PD 1096 occupancy source:

| Proposed use after reuse | Safer code anchor |
| --- | --- |
| Office, retail, restaurant with less than 100 occupants | Group E business and mercantile |
| Hotel, apartment, boarding or lodging use | Group B residential, hotels, and apartments |
| School, instruction, education, or recreation assembly use | Group C education and recreation |
| Nursing home, home for the aged, or similar care use | Group D institutional |

Accessibility still matters in repair, renovation, and change-of-occupancy work when feasible. In the checked local accessibility text, ramps have a maximum gradient of 1:12, doors have a minimum clear width of 0.80 m, accessible parking slots have a minimum width of 3.70 m, and accessible elevators have a minimum dimension of 1.10 m x 1.40 m.

## How It Is Tested

- If the question mentions cultural property or a heritage zone, look for cultural-agency supervision and permission before alteration.
- If the question asks occupancy, classify by the new proposed use.
- If the question gives an accessibility conflict, do not say heritage status automatically waives accessibility. Identify the conflict, preserve the heritage fabric where required, and route the solution through the proper agency and Building Official.
- Avoid memorizing unverified variance shortcuts. Use the local source rule: protected work needs proper permission, code conformance, and documented professional coordination.

## Common Mistakes

- Classifying by original use instead of proposed use.
- Saying heritage designation makes PD 1096 or accessibility irrelevant.
- Naming a specific agency or section without confirming the property category in the stem.
- Treating an undersized historic doorway as compliant without checking the accessible clear-width requirement.
- Cutting, covering, or replacing character-defining fabric before cultural-agency review.
