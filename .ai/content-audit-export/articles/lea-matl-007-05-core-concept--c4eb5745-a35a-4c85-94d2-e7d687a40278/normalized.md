For finish-schedule questions, ask what surface is being coordinated and what field ambiguity the schedule is supposed to remove. A source-safe answer names the room or space, surface, finish material, substrate or backing, color or texture, installation method, and detail or specification reference.

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal

Use a finish schedule to coordinate interior floor, wall, and ceiling finishes with material properties, substrate requirements, installation method, and finish appearance.

## Core Concept

BCI organizes finish work around interior wall, ceiling, and floor surfaces. Walls should resist wear and be cleanable; floors should be durable, comfortable, and safe to walk on; ceilings should be relatively maintenance-free. BCI also says finish selection must consider color, texture, pattern, joints, acoustic qualities, fire resistance, and thermal insulation value.

A finish schedule turns those choices into a room-by-room coordination record. At minimum, it should identify the space, the surface being finished, the finish material, the substrate or backing, the installation method, the color or texture, edge or joint requirements, and the drawing/detail/specification reference that resolves the item.

For floor finishes, the schedule should distinguish ceramic tile thinset from thickset work, terrazzo matrix and divider strip needs, wood flooring acclimation and subfloor needs, resilient flooring substrate condition, and carpet type or pad where relevant. For wall finishes, it should distinguish plaster, gypsum board, tile backing, wood finish, and paint system. For ceilings, it should distinguish direct gypsum board, direct acoustical tile, suspended acoustical tile, integrated ceiling systems, and linear metal ceilings.

Salvan's painting specification reinforces why vague entries are unsafe: paint materials should arrive in original labeled containers; mixing follows manufacturer directions; surfaces are inspected and cleaned; and ordinary painting is three-coat work of primer plus finish coats unless otherwise specified. Entries such as "paint, light color" or "tile, TBD grout" do not give enough information for pricing, installation, review, or punch-list checking.

## How It's Tested

LEA-style items often show an incomplete schedule entry and ask what is missing. The best answer usually fills in substrate, installation method, finish system, color or texture, joint treatment, and reference information rather than merely adding a brand name.

## Common Mistakes

- Listing only a material name without the room, surface, substrate, or installation method.
- Leaving color, texture, grout, or pattern as vague placeholders.
- Scheduling a finish without checking whether it needs a dry, smooth, clean, rigid, or moisture-resistant backing.
- Treating floor, wall, and ceiling schedules as interchangeable.
- Omitting detail references for edges, transitions, joints, and accessories.
