# Indian architecture reflects millennia of religious traditions

## LEA Point
For the LEA, Indian architecture is a comparison problem. Keep three systems separate: Buddhist stupas, Hindu temples, and Mughal garden-tombs. Each has a different spatial logic, ritual path, vertical marker, and material language.

## Source Anchors
UNESCO Sanchi is the Buddhist stupa anchor. Use it for relic-focused monuments, dome/railing/gateway composition, and circumambulation rather than an accessible inner sanctuary.

UNESCO Khajuraho and the local Hindu Architecture source are the northern Hindu temple anchors. Use them for Nagara/shikhara logic: a sanctuary-focused temple with vertical emphasis over the garbhagriha.

UNESCO Great Living Chola Temples is the southern Dravidian anchor. Use it for Chola temple scale, southern temple precinct logic, and the contrast with northern Nagara emphasis.

UNESCO Taj Mahal and HOA Module 3 are the Mughal anchors within the broader India survey. Use them for chahar bagh, marble, pietra dura, Shah Jahan, and garden-tomb composition.

## Exam Pattern
If the item mentions a stupa, relics, gateways, railings, or circumambulation, answer Buddhist and often Sanchi.

If it mentions garbhagriha, shikhara, Nagara, dense sculpture, or Khajuraho, answer northern Hindu temple architecture.

If it mentions gopura, Chola, Tamil Nadu, large temple precincts, or Brihadeshvara, answer Dravidian/South Indian temple architecture.

If it mentions chahar bagh, Taj Mahal, marble inlay, Shah Jahan, or a garden-tomb, answer Mughal.

## Guardrails
Do not treat the stupa like a Hindu temple. A stupa encloses relics and is approached through circumambulation; a Hindu temple organizes worship around a deity in the sanctum.

Do not treat Nagara and Dravidian as interchangeable. Nagara emphasizes the shikhara over the sanctum; Dravidian examples often emphasize precinct scale, gateways, and pyramidal southern forms.

## Real World Scenario
On a board exam image item, a hemispherical relic mound with gateways points to Sanchi and Buddhist architecture. A tower over a sanctum points to Hindu temple architecture. A marble tomb in a four-part garden points to Mughal architecture.
