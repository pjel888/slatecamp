# Sanitary Drainage: System Components and Functions

Sanitary drainage questions are easiest when each component is assigned its job. The Revised National Plumbing Code does not treat every pipe as the same pipe. It separates building drains, building sewers, subdrains, sanitary sewers, vents, traps, fixtures, and private disposal systems.

## Component Map

A building drain is the lowest horizontal piping that receives discharge from soil, waste, and other drainage pipes inside the building and conveys it to the building sewer beginning 0.6 m outside the building wall. A building sewer starts from the end of the building drain and carries that discharge to a public sewer, private sewer, individual sewage disposal system, or another point of disposal.

A building subdrain is the underground portion that cannot drain by gravity into the building sewer. A sanitary sewer is intended to receive sanitary sewage, with or without pre-treated industrial wastes, and without rainwater or groundwater.

## Traps, Cleanouts, and Venting

The basic plumbing principles in RA 1378 and the RNPC require drainage systems to avoid fouling, deposits, clogging, and escaping gases. Cleanouts must be provided so the system can be serviced. Each directly connected fixture needs a water-sealed trap, and drainage systems must be vented to protect trap seals against siphonage and back pressure.

This is why LEA options that omit traps, vents, or cleanouts are usually suspect. Sanitary drainage is not just gravity flow; it is gravity flow plus seal protection, access for maintenance, and gas control.

## Sizing and Special Conditions

The RNPC uses drainage fixture-unit loading and trap size to size piping. Fixtures located below the next upstream manhole cover of the public or private sewer require protective handling such as backwater valves and, where gravity drainage is not practicable, sump or ejector discharge arrangements.

Vent sizing also follows fixture-unit and length controls. Vent terminals must be placed so they do not create a sanitation or air-intake problem, and horizontal vent portions must be graded and connected so condensate or discharge drains back to the drainage pipe.

## Exam Use

First name the component, then name the function. Building drain is inside-to-building-sewer transfer. Building sewer is building-to-disposal transfer. Trap protects the fixture seal. Vent protects the trap seal and air movement. Cleanout protects serviceability. Sump, ejector, and backwater rules handle fixtures that cannot drain safely by ordinary gravity flow.
