### Calculating Total Drainage Load

Drainage Fixture Units (DFUs) quantify the volume and frequency of wastewater from each fixture. For example, a bathroom with one water closet (6 DFU), one lavatory (1 DFU), and one bath (2 DFU) generates a combined load of 9 DFUs. The building drain must be sized to handle the total load according to Revised National Plumbing Code Table 322.1, which cross-references DFU values with required pipe diameters.

### Trap Sealing Best Practices

- P-traps (lavatory sinks, showers): most common; water seal maintained below the outlet
- S-traps (older installations): prone to siphonage; not recommended for new construction
- Each fixture requires its own trap; common traps for multiple fixtures violate code
- Traps must be accessible for cleaning; concealed traps in walls are prohibited

## Building Sewer Connection Systems

The building sewer connects the building drain to either a public sanitary sewer system or a private septic system. Connection type depends on municipal infrastructure availability.

### Public Sewer Connection

Where a public sanitary sewer is available within reasonable distance or the building abuts a street, alley or easement with public sewer, connection is mandatory (RNPC Principle No. 5). The building sewer must:

- Connect at a 45-degree angle (not perpendicular) to reduce turbulence
- Include a grease trap if the building has a kitchen (mandatory per RNPC and local ordinances)
- Maintain minimum 150mm clearance from water supply lines
- Be at least 1.5 meters below finished grade to prevent surface water infiltration
- Include a sewer cleanout accessible at grade level

### Private Septic System (Remote/Rural Areas)

Where no public sewer exists, a septic tank system is required. Design must comply with Department of Health standards:

- Minimum tank capacity: 1,000 liters for residential; larger for commercial
- Septic tank location: minimum 25 meters from water wells or springs per PD 856 Chapter XVII (DOH Sanitation Code)
- Soil absorption field: sized based on soil percolation test per RNPC Section 604
- Maintenance: regular pumping required (3-5 years depending on usage)

## Indirect Waste and Special Waste Streams

Certain fixtures cannot discharge directly into the sanitary sewer due to hazardous or damaging contents. These require indirect waste systems.

### Fixtures Requiring Indirect Waste

[SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK:ui_0013 component="WikiChangedPill" status="preserved"]
Extracted summary:
- Props: (none)
- Text: (none detected)
[/SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK] The following fixtures must discharge through a trapped air break (not directly connected):

- Refrigeration units and water-cooled condensers
- Laboratory sinks and equipment drains (acid/chemical waste)
- Kitchen equipment (dishwashers, pre-rinse spray valves)
- Rooftop HVAC drain lines
- Floor drains in mechanical rooms

### Indirect Waste Connection Method

Wastewater from these fixtures must discharge into a visible trapped fixture or a receiving basin at least 25mm above the receptor's overflow rim. An air break (visible gap) prevents backflow of contaminated water into the fixture. Example: a laboratory sink drain discharges above the rim of a floor sink, allowing visual inspection and preventing siphonage.

### Grease Traps and Interceptors

Commercial kitchens must include a grease trap; residential may be required by local ordinance between the building drain and building sewer per PD 1096. The trap:

- Separates grease and food solids from wastewater
- Slows flow velocity allowing grease to float and settle
- Requires regular pumping (every 1-3 months for commercial kitchens)
- Must be sized for the expected volume (minimum 10 liters for residential)

## Combined Drainage vs. Separate Systems

Municipal storm water infrastructure determines whether sanitary and storm drainage are combined or separate.

### Separate Drainage System (Modern Standard)

- Sanitary wastewater (toilets, sinks, showers) → sanitary sewer
- Rainwater and surface runoff → storm sewer or on-site retention
- Prevents sewer overflow during heavy rain
- Allows storm water treatment before discharge to natural waterways
- Preferred method in developed areas with dual sewer infrastructure

### Combined Drainage System (Older Infrastructure)

- Both sanitary waste and storm water flow through single sewer line
- Common in older Philippine municipalities with single-pipe infrastructure
- Risk: heavy rainfall can exceed pipe capacity, causing raw sewage overflow
- Increasingly being separated into dual systems during municipal upgrades
