# Critical Regionalism

## LEA Point
Critical Regionalism is a modern architectural response to placeless universalism. The safest LEA formulation is Kenneth Frampton's 1983 framework: modern architecture should remain critically modern while being grounded in local climate, light, topography, material, craft, and cultural memory.

## Source Anchors
- CCA anchors Frampton's 1983 Critical Regionalism essay as the theoretical source.
- Glenn Murcutt is source-safe for environmentally responsive modern houses, ordinary materials, and work tuned to Australian land and climate, including Magney House.
- Tadao Ando is source-safe for modern concrete architecture that uses light, wind, geometry, and Japanese spatial sensibility.
- Diébédo Francis Kéré's Gando Primary School is source-safe for community participation, local material logic, thermal mass, ventilation, and a raised roof strategy in Burkina Faso.

## Exam Pattern
Questions often ask for Kenneth Frampton, the year 1983, or examples that use modernist discipline without becoming placeless. The right answer typically emphasizes place, climate, material, and cultural specificity rather than nostalgic revival.

## Guardrails
Do not say Critical Regionalism rejects Modernism completely. It is a critical mediation between universal modern technique and local place. Do not say it is inseparable from sustainability as an absolute; say it often overlaps with sustainable design when local climate, material, and passive strategies reduce environmental load.

## Real World Scenario
A school that uses local earth blocks, community labor, shade, and natural ventilation while keeping a clear modern plan is a source-safe Critical Regionalist reading because it joins contemporary design discipline with place-specific material and climate response.

## Related Articles
Critical Regionalism is best contrasted with Postmodernism: both challenge universal Modernism, but Critical Regionalism works through place while Postmodernism often works through signs and quotation.
