## What You Need to Know for the LEA
## Learning Goal
By the end of this article, you will be able to:

- Identify deformed reinforcing bars by grade (Gr 275, Gr 415) and diameter
- State the standard yield strengths: Fy = 275 MPa and Fy = 415 MPa
- Explain the purpose of deformation patterns on rebar
- Distinguish between standard bar sizes and their metric equivalents

Reinforcing steel is the tensile component of reinforced concrete. Deformed bars are the primary longitudinal reinforcement; stirrups are transverse elements in beams to resist shear; ties are transverse elements in columns to provide lateral confinement; and spirals are continuous helical reinforcement in circular columns. Prestressing tendons—either pre-tensioned or post-tensioned—provide compression and reduce cracking. Each type has a distinct structural role and spacing requirements.

**Deformed Reinforcing Bars** are the primary tensile reinforcement in beams, slabs, and columns. Modern deformed bars have a pattern of ribs rolled onto their surface to improve bond with concrete—the ribs prevent slip and transfer stress through mechanical interlocking. Bars are specified by diameter (10, 12, 16, 20, 25, 32 mm are common in the Philippines) and by grade: Grade 40 (_F_y = 280 MPa) and Grade 60 (_F_y = 414 MPa) are standard. Deformed bars are designated by nominal diameter; the actual cross-sectional area varies slightly with bar type and standard.

**Stirrups** are closed loops of reinforcement placed perpendicular to the longitudinal axis of a beam. They extend into the compression zone (top) and anchor into the tension zone (bottom) to confine the concrete and carry shear forces. Stirrups are bent into a "U" shape with 90-degree bends at the ends. The two vertical legs carry shear; the top horizontal portion (hook) anchors the stirrup to the main longitudinal bars. A stirrup is used in _beams_ and one-way slabs where shear is significant.

**Ties** are closed loops placed perpendicular to the longitudinal axis of a column to confine the core concrete and prevent lateral buckling of the longitudinal bars. Ties are typically bent into a closed rectangular or square shape with 135-degree corner bends. The term "tie" specifically refers to the confinement element in a _column_ (tied column). Ties are spaced vertically along the column height; maximum spacing is 16 times the longitudinal bar diameter or 48 times the tie diameter, whichever is smaller (details depend on PNSC Chapter 7).

**Spirals** are continuous helical reinforcement wound around the concrete core of a circular column. Each loop of spiral steel completely encircles the core, providing uniform confinement in all directions. A spiral column is stronger in compression and more ductile (can deform more before failure) than a tied column of the same size and longitudinal reinforcement, because the continuous spiral provides constant confinement pressure. Spiral spacing (pitch) is typically 25–75 mm (center-to-center spacing between consecutive loops), with smaller pitch providing greater confinement. The minimum spiral diameter is defined by PNSC Chapter 7.

**Prestressing Tendons** are high-strength steel elements (typically _F_y = 1,770–1,960 MPa, much higher than ordinary reinforcement) that apply permanent compression to concrete before or after hardening. Prestressed concrete is covered separately in Article LEA-STRC-006-04, but the tendons themselves are seven-wire strands (typical diameter 12.7 or 15.2 mm) or individual wires (typical diameter 7–12.5 mm), bonded to concrete or housed in ducts.

**Common Rebar Areas (Philippine Standards):**
- 10 mm: 78.5 mm²
- 12 mm: 113 mm²
- 16 mm: 201 mm²
- 20 mm: 314 mm²
- 25 mm: 491 mm²
- 32 mm: 804 mm²

**Yield Strength and Strain:**
- Grade 40: _F_y = 280 MPa; yield strain = 280 / 200,000 = 0.0014
- Grade 60: _F_y = 414 MPa; yield strain = 414 / 200,000 = 0.00207 ≈ 0.0021
- Modulus of elasticity: _E_ = 200,000 MPa (all grades)

The distinction between stirrup and tie is critical: stirrups resist shear in _beams_; ties confine the core and prevent bar buckling in _columns_. This distinction is embedded in design codes and must be understood.

**Steel Properties (All Grades):**
- Modulus of elasticity: _E_ = 200,000 MPa
- Poisson's ratio: ν ≈ 0.30
- Coefficient of thermal expansion: α ≈ 12 × 10⁻⁶/°C
- Grade 40: _F_y = 280 MPa, yield strain = 0.0014
- Grade 60: _F_y = 414 MPa, yield strain = 0.00207 ≈ 0.0021

**Hook Geometry (Simplified; Full Details in LEA-STRC-007-02):**
A 90-degree hook (typical for stirrup ends) has a 12_d_b (90°) or 16_d_b (135°) minimum length, followed by a 4_d_b straight extension. These ensure adequate development length and prevent slip under load. The exact geometry and development length are owned by Article LEA-STRC-007-02.

## Philippine Practice Context

Philippine Standard PS 249:2008 (equivalent to ASTM A615) defines deformed reinforcing bars. Grade 60 (414 MPa) is widely available and preferred for most structural applications in the Philippines because of superior seismic performance and lower quantities of steel (cost-effective). Grade 40 (275 MPa) is less common and typically used only in non-seismic regions or when specified by design. Stirrup and tie spacing rules in the PNSC Chapter 7 are based on bar diameter and structural requirements; engineers must verify spacing during design and in shop drawings. Spiral columns are common in high-rise structures and seismic-resistant design because of their superior ductility; they are more cost-effective in large-diameter columns subject to high axial load.

## How It's Tested

1. **Tensile Test (ASTM A370 / PS 249):** A sample rebar is gripped in a universal testing machine and pulled until failure. The yield strength (_F_y), ultimate tensile strength (_F_u), elongation, and modulus of elasticity are recorded. Grade 60 must have _F_y ≥ 414 MPa and _F_u ≥ 620 MPa with minimum elongation of 12%.

2. **Bond Test (ASTM C234 / PS 249):** A deformed bar is embedded in a concrete prism. The bar is pulled axially to measure the bond stress needed to cause slip. High bond stress (good grip) ensures that stress is transferred to the concrete effectively; poor bond indicates inadequate rib geometry or concrete quality.

3. **Visual Inspection and Dimension Verification:** Diameter, rib pattern, and surface condition are visually inspected. Bars must be free of scale, rust, and damage. Deformation pattern must meet standard specifications.
