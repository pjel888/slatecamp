# Gestalt Psychology and Visual Perception

## Source-Audited Scope

Use this topic as a visual-perception theory topic. The checked local sources support perception, Gestalt theory, grouping of nearby shapes, visual illusions, context effects, and design perception. This article avoids unsourced lists of modern psychology claims beyond the checked locators.

## Board Exam Quick Check

When the stem asks about Gestalt, look for pattern organization: the mind groups visual elements into perceived forms or arrangements. If the stem is about interpersonal distance, that is proxemics, not Gestalt.

## Core Concepts

- Perception is the process of organizing and interpreting patterns of stimuli in the environment.
- Gestalt theory is identified as a theory of perception in the checked local sources.
- The checked Salvan source describes a tendency to group together shapes that are close to each other.
- Visual illusions can occur because perception is influenced by parts within a whole pattern.
- Context can affect what a viewer perceives.

## LEA Use

Gestalt questions usually test visual organization. Choose the answer that explains grouping, whole-pattern perception, context, or figure/background logic. Do not choose answers about legal compliance, body dimensions, or service systems unless those are named in the stem.

## Active Recall

1. What does perception do? It organizes and interprets stimulus patterns.
2. What does the Gestalt locator emphasize? Grouping shapes that are close to each other.
3. Why does context matter? The same form can be perceived differently depending on the visual field.
