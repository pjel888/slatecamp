# Residential load computation: PEC method

Residential load computation is a sequence, not a shortcut. The checked PEC Article 2.20 provisions set the calculation scope for branch-circuit, feeder, and service loads. Use the table and rule that match the load type, then size branch circuits, feeders, and service equipment from the calculated result.

## Start with Article 2.20 scope

Article 2.20 covers branch-circuit, feeder, and service calculations. The checked PEC provisions require branch circuits to supply loads calculated under the applicable rules, and to provide enough circuits for the served loads without exceeding maximum circuit loading.

For dwelling units, the checked provisions include required small-appliance branch circuits and a required laundry branch circuit. These are not optional convenience circuits; they are part of the residential load and branch-circuit method.

## General lighting and receptacle logic

Use the applicable unit load from the PEC lighting-load table for the occupancy. For dwelling units, the general-use receptacle outlets and lighting outlets are handled within the general lighting-load method except where the Code provides separate treatment.

For specific outlets and equipment, the checked PEC provisions direct the calculation to the ampere rating of the appliance or load served. For many receptacle-outlet calculations outside the dwelling general lighting method, the checked provision uses 180 volt-amperes per outlet, subject to the more specific occupancy or equipment rule.

## Feeder and service coordination

After branch loads are identified, combine them under the feeder and service rules. The checked PEC feeder rule requires conductor ampacity not less than the noncontinuous load plus 125 percent of the continuous load. The checked informational notes discuss limiting voltage drop on branch circuits and feeders so the total installation performs acceptably.

## Exam-safe sequence

1. Classify the occupancy and load type.
2. Apply the lighting-load table or the specific load rule.
3. Add required small-appliance and laundry circuits for dwelling units.
4. Treat specific appliances by their rating where required.
5. Convert the calculated load into branch-circuit, feeder, service, overcurrent, and conductor decisions.
6. Check voltage drop after the circuit and feeder path is known.

Avoid memorized demand shortcuts that are not in the checked source. In this batch, the source-safe method is the Article 2.20 sequence plus the checked branch-circuit, outlet, feeder, and voltage-drop provisions.
