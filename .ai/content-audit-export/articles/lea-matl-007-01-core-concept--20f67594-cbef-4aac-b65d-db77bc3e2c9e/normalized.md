## What You Need to Know for the LEA
## Learning Goal

Classify floor finishes (ceramic tile, porcelain, hardwood, engineered wood, vinyl, linoleum, carpet, terrazzo, polished concrete, rubber) by durability, maintenance requirements, moisture resistance, and appropriate application for different room types.
## Core Concept

Floor finishes serve dual functions as protective layers and performance indicators. Ceramic and porcelain tiles, the dominant market choice in the Philippines, are classified by three independent systems:

(1) Water Absorption (ASTM C373): non-vitreous (>7% absorption, wall-only), semi-vitreous (3-7% absorption, light traffic), and vitreous (&lt;0.5% absorption, interior floors). Porcelain tiles, defined by absorption &lt;0.5%, dominate commercial and residential flooring.

(2) PEI Abrasion Rating (0-5): measures glaze durability under foot traffic; Grade III+ is standard for residential kitchens; Grade IV-V for commercial.

(3) COF Slip Resistance (DCOF per ANSI A137.1): minimum ≥0.42 per ANSI standard; No COF requirement in BP 344; ANSI A137.1 recommends ≥0.42 DCOF.

The Porcelain Enamel Institute (PEI) rating system (I through V) measures glaze hardness through abrasion resistance. Grade I suits walls only. Grade II accommodates light residential traffic (bathrooms, bedrooms). Grade III handles standard residential use (kitchens, living areas) without worry. Grades IV and V withstand heavy commercial traffic. Selecting the wrong grade leads to premature wear patterns visible within 2-3 years in high-traffic zones.

Slip resistance, measured by coefficient of friction (COF), protects against falls. The Philippine Accessibility Law (BP 344) mandates minimum COF of 0.60 for level surfaces and 0.80 for sloped surfaces. ASTM C1028 withdrawn 2014; use ANSI A137.1 DCOF; modern practice uses ANSI A137.1 Dynamic Coefficient of Friction (DCOF) testing, which better predicts actual pedestrian slip risk in wet conditions.

Beyond tiles, vinyl, hardwood, marble, terrazzo, and epoxy finishes each carry specific performance envelopes. Vinyl offers moisture resistance and cost efficiency for kitchens and bathrooms. Hardwood demands humidity control and regular refinishing. Marble and granite provide aesthetic appeal but require sealing and careful maintenance. Terrazzo, a composite of stone chips and binder, offers monolithic appearance and durability. Epoxy systems bond to concrete substrates for industrial and high-moisture spaces.

Standard tile sizes in the Philippine market reflect import availability and installation efficiency: 20×20cm (approximately 8×8 in or 7.87×7.87 in), 30×30cm, 40×40cm, 60×60cm (standard 2×2 ft equivalent), and large-format 80×80cm or 60×120cm with rectified edges. Mariwasa and Niro Granite dominate local production, offering both traditional sizes and premium large-format options.

## Philippine Construction Context

The Philippines' tropical climate—high humidity, seasonal flooding, and salt air near coasts—drives finish selection. Mariwasa and Niro Granite, both producing porcelain tiles locally, supply 60×60cm and 40×40cm formats most economically. These brands meet informal quality standards aligned with ASTM but undergo limited third-party certification in-country.

Water absorption becomes critical in low-lying and flood-prone areas; semi-vitreous tiles absorb capillary moisture and are unsuitable for ground-level installations. Vinyl and epoxy finishes gain favor in kitchens and wet rooms due to moisture resistance. Marble, imported or locally quarried, is prized for high-end residential spaces but demands regular sealing in humid conditions.

Slip resistance matters in common areas and ramps. Glazed polished tiles (common luxury finishes) often fall below 0.60 COF when wet and violate BP 344 unless textured or treated. Construction site specifications should call out slip-resistance test results, not just visual appearance.

## How It's Tested

**Question Type 1 (Specification Matching):** A bathroom requires durable, slip-resistant flooring in a Metro Manila high-rise. Which finish combines adequate wear rating and slip resistance? Students must match PEI III with COF ≥0.60 (textured or matte porcelain), not polished marble or glazed ceramic.

**Question Type 2 (Material Property Application):** A ground-floor retail space suffers water seepage during monsoon season. Should you specify semi-vitreous tile or vitreous porcelain? The correct answer recognizes porcelain's &lt;0.5% absorption prevents capillary rise and spalling.

**Question Type 3 (Code Compliance):** An accessible pathway slopes at 8 degrees. What minimum coefficient of friction applies? No BP 344 COF requirement; ANSI A137.1 applies, not the 0.60 for level surfaces.

**Question Type 4 (Troubleshooting):** Newly installed 80×80cm large-format tiles crack within 6 months. What is the most likely cause? Improper subfloor prep (>1/8" in 10 ft variation) combined with thin-set thickness &lt;5mm creates voids and stress concentration.
