# Circulation: Source-Safe Code Framing

## Source Scope

This local-first revision limits circulation claims to checked PD 1096, RA 9514, BP 344, and Fire Code source-family evidence. Generic planning ratios and unsupported circulation-area percentages are not carried forward.

## LEA Anchor

Circulation code questions usually ask one of three things: egress, accessibility, or plan documentation. Identify which source family controls before choosing a dimension or design strategy.

## Egress Circulation

PD 1096 requires exits to be arranged and sized from occupant load rules, limits unsprinklered/sprinklered travel distance in its checked exit section, and gives corridor rules: required corridor width, unobstructed width, access to exits in either direction when more than one exit is required, dead-end length, and one-hour corridor construction in the stated condition.

RA 9514 also controls means of egress and educational circulation, including corridor width, room doorway thresholds, travel distance, exit arrangement, and dead-end limits.

## Accessible Circulation

BP 344 2024 defines accessible route as a continuous unobstructed path connecting accessible elements and spaces, and gives passenger-path corridor width requirements for accessible transport routes. It also directs accessible signage and tactile paths toward elevators, stairs, ramps, entrances, toilets, parking, and similar facilities.

## Exam Use

Do not answer circulation from a generic area allocation rule. Ask whether the item is about exit access, corridor width, dead ends, accessible routes, tactile guidance, or plan documentation, then use the checked source for that branch.
