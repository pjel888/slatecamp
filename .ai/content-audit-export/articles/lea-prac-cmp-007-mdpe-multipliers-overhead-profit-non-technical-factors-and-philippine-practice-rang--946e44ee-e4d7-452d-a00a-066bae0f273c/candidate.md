## Board Exam Quick Check

For MDPE multiplier questions, the source-backed range is 1.5 to 2.5. Treat higher “practice ranges” as unsupported unless a verified source is supplied.

## MDPE Multipliers: Overhead, Profit, and Source-Backed Range

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to state the SPP 202 multiplier range, identify the factors that affect the multiplier, and reject unsupported multiplier ranges or invented sub-components.

---

### The Source-Backed Range

SPP Document 202, Section 5.2 states that the MDPE multiplier ranges from 1.5 to 2.5. It says the range depends on office setup, overhead, experience of the Architect, and complexity of the project. The same section defines M as the multiplier used to account for overhead and reasonable profit.

<Warn>
Do not treat 2.8, 3.0, or 3.5 as source-backed MDPE multipliers for this article. The checked SPP 202 source supports 1.5 to 2.5.
</Warn>

### What the Multiplier Represents

The multiplier is not an arbitrary bonus. It is the factor applied to direct personnel cost to cover the Architect's overhead and reasonable profit. The source gives qualitative factors, not a rigid table of sub-ranges. That means an exam answer should stay close to the source wording: office setup, overhead, architect's experience, and project complexity.

### Avoid Invented Component Math

Some explanations decompose a multiplier into fixed overhead, profit, and non-technical factors. That can be useful for internal firm accounting, but it is not the source text for this batch. If a question asks for the SPP-supported multiplier range, the answer is 1.5 to 2.5. If a question asks why a multiplier might be higher within that range, the source-supported answer is higher overhead, more complex project demands, office setup, or the Architect's experience.

### Real-World Scenario

Suppose a Manila firm is pricing a technical research assignment using MDPE. The direct personnel cost is PHP 1,200,000. If the agreed multiplier is 2.5, the fee before reimbursables is PHP 3,000,000. If the client asks why the firm cannot use 3.2, the architect should not cite SPP 202 for that number because the checked source gives 1.5 to 2.5. The architect may negotiate contract terms, but for LEA review the candidate must answer from the source-supported range. This is a common board trap: a higher number may look commercially plausible, but the exam is asking for the professional-practice standard.

If the problem gives a multiplier inside 1.5 to 2.5, use it. If it asks for the SPP-supported range, answer the range. If it gives a multiplier above 2.5 and asks whether SPP 202 supports it, reject that citation unless another verified source is supplied. The evidence for this batch supports the range and qualitative factors, not a table of premium add-ons.

### Exam Traps

- “Philippine practice range of 2.0 to 3.5” is not supported by the checked SPP 202 source.
- “Straightforward projects use 1.0 to 2.0” conflicts with the source-backed 1.5 to 2.5 range.
- “Add 0.1 to 0.3 for complexity” is an invented adjustment unless separately sourced.
- A multiplier above 2.5 should not be selected as the SPP 202 answer.
