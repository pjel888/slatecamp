# Planning, Housing & Urban Design — Mastery & Connections

## Learning Goal

Achieve synthesis-level understanding of Planning as a scale-based system from individual site to regional governance. Connect site planning to Architectural Design (FAR, setbacks, parking), housing standards to Laws (BP 220, PD 957, RA 7279), governance to Professional Practice (CLUP process, LGU authority), and landscape ecology to Environmental Design. Move fluently between scales and trace the legislative chain governing Philippine planning.

## The Scale-Based Mental Model

**Site Planning and Environmental Analysis** — lot/block scale: solar analysis, drainage, topographic constraints, setbacks, lot coverage.

**Housing and Human Settlements** — subdivision/community scale: lot arrangements, roads, open space, community facilities under BP 220 and PD 957.

**Landscape Architecture and Ecology** — site-to-neighborhood scale: green infrastructure, drainage swales, ecological corridors.

**Urban and Regional Planning** — city/region scale: transportation networks, land use patterns, density gradients.

**Planning Agencies, Governance & Philippine Planning Law** — regulatory framework above all scales: who has authority, what rules apply.

**General Principles and History of Planning** — intellectual genealogy explaining current system structure.

**Innovation Layer** — forward-looking frameworks reshaping practice at every scale.

<Note>When you encounter a planning question on the LEA, your first move should be to identify the sclea it is operating at. A question about lot coverage is a site-planning question. A question about CLUP approval is a governance question. A question about TOD is an urban-planning question. Identifying the sclea focuses your recall.</Note>

## Site Analysis → Urban Planning: The Upward Chain

Same analytical process applied at different scales:

**Site scale:** slope, aspect, drainage, vegetation, winds, solar access, views, adjacent uses, access, hazards.

**Urban scale:** topographic patterns across watersheds, green corridors, transportation routes, zoning, infrastructure capacity, hazard maps.

## Connections to Architectural Design

**FAR and Lot Coverage Ratio (LCR):** Planning controls that directly shape building form. FAR 3.0 + LCR 0.6 allows building up to 60% of lot per floor and 3× lot area total.

**Setbacks:** PD 1096 sets minimums; zoning ordinances may add more.

**Parking requirements:** Differ by land use, set by NBCP and local zoning.

<Tip>The connection between planning and design is most visible in the site plan — the drawing that shows a building in its site context, with setbacks, landscaping, parking, access drives, and open space all shown. If you are comfortable reading and drawing site plans, you are already thinking across the planning-design boundary.</Tip>

## Connections to Laws & Codes

Same law = different angles:

- **PD 957** — Planning: minimum subdivision standards. Laws: buyer protections, developer obligations.
- **BP 220** — Planning: reduced standards for affordable housing. Laws: legal housing category definitions.
- **RA 7279 (UDHA)** — Planning: socialized housing quotas, resettlement. Laws: informal settler rights, eviction procedures.
- **RA 7160 (LGC)** — Planning: CLUP mandate, LGU planning authority. Laws: LGU powers, accountability.
- **PD 1096** — Planning: setbacks, height limits. Laws: permits, penalties, liability.

## Connections to Environmental Design

Bioclimatic design at building scale (Environmental Design) and urban scale (Planning): street orientation, tree canopy, urban heat island mitigation. Stormwater management as site drainage challenge and green infrastructure strategy.

## The CLUP Process

1. Ecological Profile / Situational Analysis
2. Land Use Analysis
3. Vision-Mission-Goals-Objectives
4. Land Use Planning
5. CLUP Document
6. Zoning Ordinance (enacted by Sanggunian)
7. Implementation, Monitoring, Evaluation

## Integration Patterns

**Planning + Design + Laws:** Development project scenario — applicable standards (planning), design implications (arch design), legal requirements (laws).

**Planning + History:** American colonial planning influence (Burnham Plan for Manila and Baguio) visible in contemporary Philippine urbanism.

**Planning + 21st Century:** TOD, smart cities, resilience planning — anchor to Philippine examples (BGC transit spine, Metro Manila Greenway Project, Pasig River corridor).

## Common Exam Traps

- **Confusing HLURB with DHSUD.** HLURB reorganized into DHSUD in 2019.
- **Mixing up BP 220 categories.** Each housing category has different minimum standards.
- **Neglecting governance questions.** Agency jurisdiction is reliably correct-or-wrong.
- **Confusing CLUP with Zoning Ordinance.** CLUP = policy; Zoning Ordinance = legal instrument.
- **Treating site planning and urban planning as unrelated.** Same analytical discipline at different resolutions.
