# Ecological Considerations in Site Planning

## Board Exam Quick Check

For ecological site-planning questions, identify the physical or environmental system first: waterway, drainage, solid waste, biodiversity, slope, open space, or climate hazard. Then anchor the answer to the source family that controls the claim.

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

The HLURB CLUP guide requires natural, physical, biodiversity, and environmental studies as part of the situation analysis. Data requirements include topography, slope, drainage, bodies of water, climate, and other natural characteristics. This makes ecology a planning input, not an aesthetic afterthought.

The DHSUD resilient urban design guidelines connect ecological planning to nature-based solutions, ecosystem-based approaches, water-sensitive urban design, open spaces, green infrastructure, and climate/disaster resilience. Nature-based solutions use natural processes to address climate, water, disaster, and biodiversity challenges.

Water-sensitive urban design manages and reuses stormwater while reducing negative impacts on rivers and creeks. In LEA terms, this supports drainage, flood mitigation, water-quality protection, and open-space planning.

PD 1067 supplies source-safe waterway easement rules: banks of rivers and streams and shores of seas and lakes must keep easement zones free for public use, with widths depending on urban, agricultural, or forest context. Site planning near water must respect these easements.

RA 9275 covers water quality and water bodies, including water-quality management areas and sewerage/septage responsibilities. RA 9003 requires local solid-waste planning, segregation, diversion, and safe facility planning. These laws make drainage, wastewater, and solid waste part of ecological site analysis.

## Exam Focus

- Ecological planning starts with mapped natural and physical data.
- Drainage, slope, water bodies, vegetation, biodiversity, and hazards are planning constraints and opportunities.
- Water-sensitive urban design manages stormwater and supports resilient open-space systems.
- PD 1067, RA 9275, and RA 9003 control waterway, water-quality, wastewater, and solid-waste claims.
- Avoid adding numeric hazard standards unless the local raw source has been checked for that exact number.

## How It Is Tested

Expect scenario questions about waterway easements, drainage, wetlands, solid waste, sewerage, slopes, or site ecology. The best answer usually protects natural systems, keeps development out of legally controlled waterway zones, manages stormwater and wastewater, and uses ecological data before placing buildings or infrastructure.
