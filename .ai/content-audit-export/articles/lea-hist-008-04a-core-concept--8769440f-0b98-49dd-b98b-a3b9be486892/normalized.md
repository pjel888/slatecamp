# The Bauhaus

## LEA Point

The Bauhaus is tested as a school, pedagogy, and design-reform program, not only as a visual style. Its high-yield cue is the integration of art, craft, industry, and architecture through workshop-based education.

## Source Anchors

- Bauhaus-Archiv anchors the Bauhaus from 1919 to 1933, beginning with Walter Gropius in Weimar.
- The director sequence is Gropius, Hannes Meyer, then Ludwig Mies van der Rohe; Le Corbusier was not a Bauhaus director.
- The school moved to Dessau in 1925, and the Bauhaus Building opened in 1926 with steel, concrete, glass, functional wings, and visible workshop identity.
- The curriculum joined preliminary study, workshops, art theory, material study, architecture, and production for industry.
- The school was forced to close in 1933, but its dispersed faculty and students carried Bauhaus ideas internationally.

## Exam Pattern

If the stem says 1919 Weimar, Gropius, art and technology, workshops, preliminary course, Dessau school building, or closure by Nazi pressure in 1933, answer Bauhaus. If it asks who was not a director, choose Le Corbusier. If it asks why Bauhaus mattered, emphasize transferable design principles and production, not style-copying.

## Guardrails

Do not collapse Gropius, Meyer, and Mies into one leadership philosophy. Do not treat the Dessau building as a generic corporate office. Do not say Bauhaus rejected industry; the exam-safe claim is that it tried to unite artistic training with modern production.

## Real World Scenario

For a design-school renovation, a Bauhaus-based strategy would make studios, workshops, material testing, and shared production visible so students learn through making rather than through historical style imitation.
