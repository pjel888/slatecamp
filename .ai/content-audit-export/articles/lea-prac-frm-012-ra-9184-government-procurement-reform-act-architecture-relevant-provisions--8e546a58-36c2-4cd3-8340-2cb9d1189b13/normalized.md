# RA 9184 Government Procurement Reform Act Architecture Relevant Provisions

## Board Exam Quick Check
For architecture practice questions involving government projects, use RA 9184 to distinguish procurement law from design-service scope. RA 9184 controls how a public entity procures infrastructure projects, goods, and consulting services. It does not replace the architect's professional scope under RA 9266 or the Standards of Professional Practice.

## Learning Goal
Apply the architecture-relevant RA 9184 rules on procurement principles, scope, consulting services, competitive bidding, the Bids and Awards Committee, and exceptional alternative procurement methods.

## Source-Grounded Core Rules

### Governing principles
RA 9184 government procurement is governed by transparency, competitiveness, a streamlined process, accountability, and public monitoring. These principles shape how public architecture-related contracts are planned, bid, evaluated, awarded, and monitored.

### Scope and architecture relevance
RA 9184 applies to procurement of infrastructure projects, goods, and consulting services by covered government entities, regardless of whether the source of funds is local or foreign. For architects, the most relevant categories are infrastructure projects and consulting services.

The audited source defines consulting services to include services for infrastructure projects and other government activities requiring external technical and professional expertise, including advisory and review services, pre-investment or feasibility studies, design, construction supervision, management and related services, other technical services, and special studies.

### Competitive bidding is the default
All procurement is done through competitive bidding except as provided for the alternative methods in the Act or IRR. Competitive bidding is open to any interested party and includes advertisement, pre-bid conference, eligibility screening, receipt and opening of bids, bid evaluation, post-qualification, and award.

### BAC role
The procuring entity establishes a Bids and Awards Committee. The Act states that the BAC has at least five and not more than seven members. The BAC advertises or posts the invitation to bid, conducts pre-procurement and pre-bid conferences, determines eligibility, receives bids, evaluates bids, undertakes post-qualification, recommends award, recommends sanctions when applicable, and may create a Technical Working Group.

In proper cases, the BAC may recommend alternative methods of procurement to the head of the procuring entity. The BAC also helps ensure the procuring entity follows the Act and the IRR.

### Alternative methods are exceptions
The IRR states that public bidding is the general mode of procurement and that alternative methods are used only in highly exceptional cases provided in Rule XVI. Treat alternative methods as exceptions, not as the default way to procure architecture-related services.

## Exam Values To Memorize
- Governing principles: transparency, competitiveness, streamlined process, accountability, and public monitoring.
- Scope: infrastructure projects, goods, and consulting services.
- Consulting services include design, feasibility or pre-investment studies, advisory/review services, construction supervision, management and related services, and technical studies.
- Default mode: competitive bidding.
- BAC size under the Act: at least five and not more than seven members.
- Alternative methods: exceptions under Rule XVI or the corresponding Act provisions.

## Common Trap
Do not treat RA 9184 as a schedule of architectural design deliverables or professional fees. It is a public procurement law. Use it to answer procurement process, BAC, competition, scope, and government-contracting questions.
