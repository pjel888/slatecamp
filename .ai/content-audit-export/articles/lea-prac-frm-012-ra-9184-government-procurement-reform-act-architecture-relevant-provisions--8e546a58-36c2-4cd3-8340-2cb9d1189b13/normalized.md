# RA 9184 Government Procurement Reform Act Architecture Relevant Provisions

## Board Exam Quick Check

For architecture practice questions involving government projects, use RA 9184 to distinguish procurement law from professional-scope law. RA 9184 controls how a public entity procures infrastructure projects, goods, and consulting services. It does not replace the architect's professional responsibility under RA 9266 or the architect-of-record rules in the RA 9266 IRR.

## Learning Goal

Apply the architecture-relevant RA 9184 rules on procurement principles, scope, consulting services, competitive bidding, the Bids and Awards Committee, documentation, and exceptional alternative procurement methods.

## Source-Grounded Core Rules

### Governing Principles

RA 9184 government procurement is governed by transparency, competitiveness, a streamlined procurement process, accountability, and public monitoring. The Updated 2016 IRR also states the policy of competitive and transparent procurement and links the system to transparency, accountability, equity, efficiency, and economy. These principles shape how public architecture-related contracts are planned, bid, evaluated, awarded, implemented, and monitored.

### Scope and Architecture Relevance

RA 9184 applies to procurement of infrastructure projects, goods, and consulting services by covered government entities. For architects, the most relevant categories are infrastructure projects and consulting services.

The audited source defines consulting services to include government activities requiring external technical and professional expertise, including advisory and review services, feasibility or pre-investment studies, design, construction supervision, management and related services, other technical services, and special studies.

### Procurement Planning Comes Before Procurement

RA 9184 requires procurement planning and budget linkage. No government procurement should be undertaken unless it is in accordance with the approved Annual Procurement Plan of the procuring entity. For infrastructure projects, the plan includes engineering design and right-of-way acquisition. In exam questions, this is why an architect should not treat a government project as an informal client engagement even when the technical scope is small.

### Competitive Bidding Is the Default

All procurement is done through competitive bidding except as provided for alternative methods in the Act or IRR. Competitive bidding includes public notice and a structured sequence of eligibility, bidding, evaluation, post-qualification, and award. The Updated 2016 IRR states the same default and treats alternative methods as highly exceptional cases provided in Rule XVI.

### BAC Role

The procuring entity establishes a Bids and Awards Committee. The Act states that the BAC has at least five and not more than seven members. The BAC advertises or posts the invitation to bid, conducts pre-procurement and pre-bid conferences, determines eligibility, receives bids, evaluates bids, undertakes post-qualification, recommends award, recommends sanctions when applicable, and may create a Technical Working Group.

In proper cases, the BAC may recommend alternative methods of procurement to the head of the procuring entity. The BAC also helps ensure the procuring entity follows the Act and the IRR.

### Audit Trail and Observers

RA 9184 contains architecture-relevant record controls. The electronic procurement system includes an audit-trail feature and allows the Commission on Audit to verify security and integrity. The BAC must invite a COA representative and at least two other observers in all stages of the procurement process to enhance transparency. These provisions are why architects working on government projects should keep the procurement and technical record traceable.

## Exam Values To Memorize

- Governing principles: transparency, competitiveness, streamlined process, accountability, and public monitoring.
- Scope: infrastructure projects, goods, and consulting services.
- Consulting services include design, feasibility or pre-investment studies, advisory/review services, construction supervision, management and related services, and technical studies.
- Procurement planning must be tied to the approved budget and Annual Procurement Plan.
- Default mode: competitive bidding.
- BAC size under the Act: at least five and not more than seven members.
- Alternative methods: exceptions under Rule XVI or corresponding Act provisions.

## Real World Scenario

A municipal government wants an architect for a new health-center expansion. The mayor knows a qualified architect and asks whether the office can simply issue a letter to start schematic design. The source-safe answer is no. The professional scope may be architectural, but the public procurement route is governed by RA 9184. The procuring entity needs an approved procurement plan and budget basis, appropriate consulting-service documents, BAC action, and the required bidding or justified alternative-procurement route. The architect should keep the technical proposal, contract scope, change records, and design decision file separate from the RA 9266 signing-and-sealing record.

## Common Traps

- Do not treat RA 9184 as a schedule of architectural design deliverables or professional fees.
- Do not treat alternative procurement methods as the ordinary route.
- Do not answer "RA 9266" when the question asks how a government entity procures the architect or construction work.
- Do not answer "RA 9184" when the question asks who signs, seals, or professionally assumes responsibility for architectural documents.
