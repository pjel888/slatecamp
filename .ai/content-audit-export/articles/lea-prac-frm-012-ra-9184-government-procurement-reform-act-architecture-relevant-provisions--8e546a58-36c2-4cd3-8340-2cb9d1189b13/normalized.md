## RA 9184—Government Procurement Reform Act: Architecture-Relevant Provisions

Republic Act 9184 (Government Procurement Reform Act) establishes the legal framework for all government procurement of goods, services, and infrastructure projects. For architects, understanding RA 9184 is critical because it governs how government clients select architects, procure design services, and manage consulting contracts. The act applies to all branches and instrumentalities of government, including government-owned or controlled corporations (GOCCs) and local government units (LGUs).

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Explain the architecture-relevant provisions of RA 9184 (Government Procurement Reform Act).
- Describe the bidding requirements for government architectural projects.
- Identify the architect's role in government procurement.
- Distinguish RA 9184 from private procurement rules.

### Scope and Coverage of RA 9184

RA 9184 applies to procurement of infrastructure projects, goods, and consulting services regardless of source of funds (local or foreign). All government entities must follow its provisions when engaging architects and engineering consultants. The act was modernized through its Revised Implementing Rules and Regulations (2016), and further enhanced by RA 12009 (signed into law in July 2024), which strengthens transparency and competition in government procurement.

For architects, RA 9184 creates a formal competitive process. Government clients cannot simply hire an architect of choice; they must follow prescribed procurement methods—typically competitive bidding, limited bidding, or single source procurement under specific conditions. Understanding these methods and their compliance requirements protects both the client and the architect.

### Governing Principles: Transparency and Competitiveness

The act mandates two core principles that affect architectural practice. First, _transparency_ requires that procurement processes and contract implementation be open and accountable. Second, _competitiveness_ ensures equal opportunity for eligible and qualified consultants to participate in public bidding.

For architects, transparency means government clients must publicize the project scope, design requirements, and selection criteria. Competitiveness means an architect cannot secure a government contract solely through personal connection or informal arrangement. Selection must be based on published qualifications, experience, and (typically) competitive fee proposals. This levels the playing field but also requires architects to formalize their credentials, prior project experience, and fee structures.

### Consulting Services Procurement

Architectural consulting falls under RA 9184's consulting services framework. Government entities procure design services through a formal competitive selection process overseen by the Bids and Awards Committee (BAC). The BAC evaluates qualifications, shortlists firms, and either selects based on qualifications (for smaller projects) or conducts competitive bidding on fees.

Architects must prepare detailed qualifications documents including firm registration, professional licenses, insurance, prior relevant projects, and team composition. The government may request a "design concept" or technical proposal, which should address the scope of services, methodology, and project approach without revealing proprietary design innovation (which should be protected during the procurement phase).

### Contract Documents and Scope Definition

RA 9184 requires comprehensive contract documents: the contract itself, special provisions or conditions, general conditions, and technical specifications. For architectural consulting, the contract must clearly state the scope of services—preliminary design, schematic design, design development, contract documents, construction administration, or combinations thereof.

Architects must ensure the contract aligns with the Standards of Professional Practice defined in the Architects' National Code. The contract should specify deliverables, submission schedules, revision rounds, and compensation structure. Ambiguity about scope increases disputes; precise scope definition protects the architect from uncompensated additional services.

### Three Procurement Methods

RA 9184 allows three methods: competitive bidding (open to all qualified consultants), limited bidding (restricted to a pre-qualified list, used when only few firms have required expertise), and single source procurement (used in exceptional cases—emergency, sole source availability, or direct contracting with existing framework agreements).

Single source procurement for architecture is rare but may apply when a firm has specialized expertise or when continuing an ongoing design for a complex project.

### Prohibited Activities and Sanctions

RA 9184 strictly prohibits collusion, fraud, bribery, conflict of interest, and bid-rigging. Architects and their firms must not communicate with other bidding consultants to arrange fees, divide work, or coordinate responses. Doing so violates the law and can result in permanent disqualification from government procurement.

Architects must disclose conflicts of interest. If a firm has worked for the client recently on a related project, or if the same personnel would be assigned to conflicting projects, disclosure and recusal may be required. Failure to disclose creates liability.

### Contract Administration and Changes

Once contracted, architects must perform strictly according to the contract. Changes to scope require formal change orders signed by both parties. RA 9184 emphasizes economy and efficiency, so architects must avoid cost overruns and schedule delays. Change orders should document the reason, describe the additional work, and clearly state the additional fee or time impact.

Government contracts are subject to government accounting rules and COA audit. Architects must maintain complete records of work performed, invoices, supporting documents, and correspondence. Prompt payment is important; if government delays payment, architects should follow contractual dispute resolution provisions rather than abandoning the contract.

### Recent Updates and RA 12009

RA 12009 (2024) modernizes RA 9184 further. Key updates include enhanced digital procurement systems (e-Procurement), stricter compliance monitoring, and refinements to procurement thresholds and methods. Architects should stay informed of updated implementing rules and familiarize themselves with digital submission and bidding platforms used by their government clients.
