## Overview

**SPP Document 202** governs the **Regular Design Services** of Registered and Licensed Architects (RLAs) in the Philippines — the core of architectural practice, covering everything from the first consultation with the client all the way through the turnover of the completed project. It is part of the 2010 Standards of Professional Practice (SPP), promulgated as the Supplemental Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of **Republic Act No. 9266** (The Architecture Act of 2004), and replaces the earlier **1979 UAP Doc. 202**.

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to:

Identify the five phases of SPP Doc 202 Regular Design Services (Schematic Design, Design Development, Contract Documents, Bidding Assistance, Construction Administration) and their deliverables, fee percentages, and architect's level of involvement per phase; apply the 20%-30%-50% fee split across SD/DD/CD; and determine when a phase is complete and the next begins.
---
Regular Design Services represent what most people understand as "the architect's job" — translating the client's needs and requirements into physical spaces and forms, preparing all necessary construction documents, managing the bidding or negotiation process, and providing oversight during construction until the project is turned over. SPP 202 is the most frequently referenced document in day-to-day architectural practice because it governs the standard engagement between an architect and a client for any building project.

SPP 202 applies to all **Registered and Licensed Architects (RLAs)** — natural persons with valid PRC Certificates of Registration and 3-year renewable Professional Identification Cards. It equally applies to architectural firms (AFs) organized as DTI-registered sole proprietorships, or SEC-registered professional partnerships or corporations, all subject to Sec. 37 of R.A. **Foreign architects** offering services in the Philippines for projects on Philippine soil must comply with Sec. 38 of R.A. ## The Architect's Role in Regular Practice

In regular practice, the Architect acts as the **Owner's/Client's/Proponent's Adviser and/or Representative**. This dual role means the architect:

- **Translates** the owner's needs and requirements into spaces and forms in the best manner of professional service
- **Starts** work at the inception of the project when the owner outlines requirements
- **Covers** all aspects from analysis and study of needs, to preparation of instruments of service, to supervision during project implementation
- **Ends** work only when the general contractor or builder turns over the completed project to the owner

## Scope of Services: The Six Phases

SPP 202 organizes the architect's services into **six sequential phases**, each with specific deliverables.

### Phase 1: Project Definition Phase

This is the "investigation" stage. The project requirements are defined before any design begins. The architect:

- Consults with the owner to ascertain the **conceptual framework and related requirements** of the project, and confirms these requirements
- Gathers relevant information and data leading to the definition of the project's scope, including the scope of the architect's services
- Reviews and refines the owner's space requirements and **translates them into an architectural program**
- Prepares an **initial Statement of Probable Project Construction Cost (SPPCC)**

The SPPCC is not a fixed guarantee of cost — it is an initial estimate based on available data, subject to refinement in every subsequent phase.

### Phase 2: Schematic Design Phase

Once requirements are defined, the architect moves into conceptual design. The architect:

- **Evaluates** the owner's program, schedule, budget, and project site, and proposes methods of project delivery
- **Prepares the initial line drawings** representing design studies leading to a recommended solution, with a general description of the project for the owner's approval
- **Submits an updated SPPCC** based on current cost parameters

The schematic design output is the first visual representation of the project. Owner approval at this stage is critical before the architect proceeds to the next phase.

### Phase 3: Design Development Phase

Based on approved schematics and conceptual plans, the architect prepares:

- **Design Development documents** — plans, elevations, sections, and other drawings elaborating the approved schematic
- **Outline specifications** — fixing and illustrating the size and character of the entire project as to type of materials, and type of structural, electrical, mechanical, sanitary, electronic, and communications systems
- **Diagrammatic layout** of construction systems
- An **updated SPPCC** for submission to the owner

### Phase 4: Contract Document Phase

Based on approved Design Development documents, the architect:

- Prepares the **complete Contract Documents** — detailed designs and construction drawings setting forth all the work required for architectural, structural, electrical, plumbing/sanitary, mechanical, electronic, and communication works, prepared by the architect and the respective allied professionals
- Prepares **Technical Specifications** describing the type and quality of materials, finish, manner of construction, and general construction conditions
- Submits **seven (7) sets** of all construction drawings and technical specifications to the owner for the purpose of obtaining a **building permit**
- **Updates the SPPCC** based on any changes in scope, requirements, or market conditions
- Assists the owner in filing required documents to secure approval from **government authorities having jurisdiction** over the project design

### Phase 5: Bidding or Negotiation Phase

In this phase, the architect:

- **Prepares the Bid Documents** — forms for contract letting, documents for construction, invitation/instruction to bidders, forms for bidders' proposals, general/specific conditions of contract, etc.
- **Assists the owner** from the early stage of establishing a list of prospective contractors to awarding the construction contract

**For competitive bids/procurements**, the architect:
- Furnishes complete sets of Bid Documents in as many sets as required (loaned to bidders at a cost covering direct and indirect costs of preparation, packaging, reproduction, and delivery)
- Helps organize and conduct **pre-bid conferences**
- Responds to questions from bidders
- Assists the owner in obtaining proposals from contractors, analyzes bid results, and prepares abstract of bids, **Notice of Award**, Notice to Proceed, and other construction contracts

**For negotiated contracts**, the architect performs similar functions but negotiates with **one contractor** instead of many bidders.

### Phase 6: Construction Phase

During construction, the architect:

- **Makes decisions** on all claims of the owner and contractors on all matters relating to the execution and progress of work, or the interpretation of the Contract Documents
- **Prepares change orders**, gathers, and turns over to the owner written guarantees required of the contractor and sub-contractors
- **Makes periodic visits** to the project site to familiarize with general progress and quality of work and to ascertain that work proceeds in accordance with the Contract Documents
- **Determines the amount owing** to the contractor and issues corresponding **Certificates for Payment** based on observations and the Contractor's Applications for Payment
- Conducts the necessary inspection to determine the date of **substantial and final completion** and issues the **final Certificate for Payment**

## Manner of Providing Services

The architect may enter into contract with the owner as **Lead Professional** in two ways:

1. **Single Contract** — One contract between the architect and owner, with separate sub-consultancy contracts between the architect and other allied professionals (engineers, etc.). Professional responsibilities and civil liabilities of each licensed professional remain separate; the architect does not assume the responsibilities of the engineers.

2. **Separate Contracts** — The architect and the engineering/allied professionals each execute separate contracts directly with the owner. Again, professional responsibilities remain separate and distinct.

## What Is Included in the Project Construction Cost (PCC)?

The PCC — the basis of fee computation — means the **cost of the completed building to the owner**, including:

- Structure
- Plumbing/sanitary and electrical fixtures
- Mechanical equipment, elevators, escalators, air-conditioning systems
- Fire protection systems, alarm and clock systems
- Communications and electronic systems
- All elements attached to the building and all items indicated in the plans, designs, drawings, and specifications prepared by the architect and consultants
- Architectural interiors (AI), site development plan elements, and other items additionally planned/designed by the architect

The PCC **does not include** any professional fees (architect, engineers, specialist consultants) or salaries of construction inspectors. If materials are furnished by the owner below market cost, the PCC is nonetheless computed based on **current fair market value costs**.

The broader **Project Development Cost** includes PCC plus all professional fees, permits, clearances, utilities, cost of acquiring the site, and cost of money.

## Owner's Responsibilities

Under SPP 202, the owner's obligations are equally important to the architect's services:

1. **Provide full information** about project requirements
2. **Designate a representative** authorized to act on the owner's behalf
3. **Promptly examine and render decisions** on documents submitted by the architect, to avoid unreasonable delays — all orders to the General Contractor must be issued **through the Architect**
4. **Furnish or direct the architect** to obtain (at the owner's expense) a certified site survey covering topography, utilities, zoning, deed restrictions, soil investigations, borings, and test pits
5. **Promptly pay** for architectural and all engineering/allied services required for the project
6. **Pay separately** for acoustic, communication, electronic, and other specialty system design services
7. **Arrange and pay** for legal, auditing, insurance, and other ancillary services
8. **Pay for all reimbursable expenses** and all applicable taxes (exclusive of income tax) imposed on the architect as a result of services rendered
9. **Notify the architect promptly** in writing if anything is observed that may impair the successful implementation of the project

## Other Conditions on Services

### Fee When Construction Is Not Implemented

The Contract Documents Phase equals **90% of the architect's work**. If the owner fails to implement the plans for construction, the architect is entitled to **90% of the agreed fee**.

### Changes Ordered by the Owner

If the architect renders additional professional services due to **changes ordered by the owner** after approval of outputs, the owner shall pay the architect for extra time, resources, drafting, or other office expenses.

### Suspension or Abandonment of Work

If work is abandoned or suspended in whole or in part, the owner shall pay the architect for **services rendered up to the point of suspension or abandonment**.

### Different Periods of Construction

If portions of the building are erected at different times, construction phase fees shall be **adjusted proportionately**. If the suspension of construction **exceeds six (6) months**, the fee for remaining works shall be **doubled**.

### Per Diem and Travel Expenses

A per diem plus traveling and living expenses are chargeable to the owner whenever the architect or authorized representative must perform services at a locality **beyond 50 km (radial distance)** from the architect's established office.

### Specialist Consultants

If the owner requires specialist consultants (acoustic, illumination, etc.), they are engaged with the architect's consent, paid separately by the owner, and their cost shall **not be deducted from the architect's fee**. A **coordination fee** is payable to the architect for coordinating these specialists.

### scale Models, 3D Models, and Walk-Thru Presentations

Should scale models or 3D/walk-thru presentations be required, costs are paid separately by the owner and are subject to a **coordination fee** payable to the architect.

### Ownership of Documents

All designs, drawings, models, specifications, and contract documents — whether the work was ultimately executed or not — are the **intellectual property and documents of the Architect** under Sec. 33 of R.A. No. 9266. They are not to be reproduced or used on other work without a written agreement with the architect.

### Government Taxes

The architect's fee is a **net amount**. Any tax (exclusive of income tax) that the national or local government imposes on the architect as a result of services rendered shall be paid by the **owner**.

### Signboard Design

All signboards placed at the project site during construction must be **approved by the architect** as to size, design, and contents. After completion, the owner or building lessee shall consult the architect for the design and size of all signboards, directories, and display boards placed on exterior or public areas of the building.

## Practical Notes for Architects and Clients

**For Architects:**
- Always obtain the **owner's written approval** at the end of each phase before proceeding to the next — approvals at the Schematic and Design Development phases are critical to protect the architect if design directions change later.
- The 90% rule is a key protection: ensure the owner-architect agreement explicitly states the fee owing at the Contract Documents Phase to avoid disputes when projects are shelved post-documentation.
- Periodic site visits during the Construction Phase are an obligation, but **full-time 8-hour supervision is a separate service** under SPP 204-A and must be separately contracted and compensated.
- Bid documents are the architect's intellectual property — require a bond or deposit from bidders to ensure their return.

**For Clients / Project Proponents:**
- All orders to the General Contractor **must flow through the architect** — bypassing the architect undermines the construction contract and can expose the owner to legal liability for any resulting defects.
- The architect's fee is a **net amount** — factor in VAT and other applicable taxes (to be shouldered by the owner) when budgeting professional fees.
- Cost estimates (SPPCC) at any stage are professional opinions, not guarantees. Competitive bidding results may deviate from estimates.
- For Group 9 (alterations/renovations), the minimum basic fee is **150% of the applicable Group 1–5 rate** — not a flat rate — reflecting the greater complexity and risk involved in working with existing structures.
