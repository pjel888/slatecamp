# Greek Architecture

## LEA Point
Greek architecture is a post-and-lintel temple and sanctuary tradition organized by orders, proportion, site, and refinement. The LEA high-yield anchors are Doric, Ionic, Corinthian, the Acropolis/Parthenon, Bassae, Delphi/Olympia sanctuary context, caryatids, tholos, and the contrast with Roman arch/vault systems.

## Source Anchors
- UNESCO Acropolis anchors the Parthenon, Erechtheion, Propylaea, Athena Nike, and the Periclean classical program.
- UNESCO Bassae anchors the Temple of Apollo Epicurius as a Greek temple that brings Doric, Ionic, and Corinthian features into one important source-mapped site.
- UNESCO Delphi and Olympia anchor sanctuary settings beyond Athens.
- Vitruvius anchors order distinctions: Doric, Ionic, Corinthian, volutes, acanthus, triglyphs, mutules, dentils, and proportion language.
- Banister Fletcher anchors the Parthenon as peripteral octastyle with seventeen flank columns, Ictinus/Callicrates, Phidias, and treasury/cella distinctions.

## Core Pattern
Greek temples rely on column-and-lintel construction. Doric is visually stockier and uses a triglyph-metope/mutule vocabulary. Ionic is more slender, has a base and volute capital, and is associated with continuous frieze/dentil vocabulary. Corinthian is distinguished by its capital and acanthus-leaf language. Refinements such as entasis and curved lines belong to the Greek pursuit of visual correction and proportion.

## Exam Guardrails
- Do not identify an order from column presence alone; read capital, base, shaft proportion, and entablature.
- Do not confuse Greek post-and-lintel temple space with Roman arch-and-vault interior engineering.
- Spell caryatid correctly and use it for sculpted female supports; atlantes are male supports.
- Do not treat every circular Greek building as a theatre; tholos is the circular temple/building term.

## Real World Scenario
A board item describes an octastyle temple on the Acropolis with 17 columns on each flank and subtle visual correction. The source-safe answer is the Parthenon within Greek Doric temple architecture, with refinements, not a Roman vaulted hall.
