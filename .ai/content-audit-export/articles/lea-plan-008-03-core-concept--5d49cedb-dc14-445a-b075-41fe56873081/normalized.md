## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal
Apply sustainable urban development principles — compact, mixed-use, transit-oriented, walkable neighborhoods — design Complete Streets for Philippine conditions, and align planning with SDG 11 and New Urban Agenda frameworks.

Sustainable urban development integrates environmental, social, and economic goals through compact, mixed-use neighborhoods supported by transit and walkable streets. Complete Streets design moves beyond car-centric infrastructure to create pedestrian, bicycle, and transit-friendly public spaces. Together, they form a planning approach aligned with the UN Sustainable Development Goal 11 (SDG 11: Sustainable Cities and Communities) and the New Urban Agenda from Habitat III (2016).

## Core Concept

Sustainable urban development pursues three objectives simultaneously: (1) minimizing environmental impact through compact, efficient land use; (2) ensuring equitable access to housing, jobs, and services; and (3) supporting long-term economic vitality. Rather than sprawling, car-dependent growth, sustainable development concentrates mixed-use neighborhoods—residential, commercial, office, cultural—within walkable distances, served by efficient public transit.

Key frameworks include:

1. **New Urbanism** (1980s–present) — walkable neighborhoods with mixed uses, human-scale architecture, and transit connectivity. The traditional 5-minute walk radius of 400 meters enables pedestrian-based daily errands.

2. **Transit-Oriented Development (TOD)** — higher-density, mixed-use development within 400–800 meters of transit stations, encouraging mode shift from private vehicles to public transit.

3. **15-Minute City** (Carlos Moreno, Paris 2020) — urban design ensuring that all daily essentials—work, education, healthcare, shopping, culture, leisure—are accessible within 15 minutes of walk or bicycle ride from home.

4. **Smart Growth** — compact development, infill, mixed uses, walkability, transit choice, environmental preservation, and community participation.

5. **Complete Streets** — street design that accommodates pedestrians, cyclists, transit users, and drivers; prioritizes safety and livability over vehicle throughput alone.

These frameworks share a common critique of 20th-century car-dependent suburban sprawl and a commitment to human-scale, livable urban forms. In Philippine context, Complete Streets addresses the reality that most Filipinos walk or use transit; car-centric design ignores the majority.

## Philippine Context

Philippine cities face the tension between rapid growth and livability. Metro Manila's sprawl, traffic congestion, and pollution exemplify car-dependent development that benefits car owners while marginalizing pedestrians and transit users (the majority). Complete Streets design in Philippine cities means prioritizing sidewalk width, shade, accessibility for persons with disabilities, and safe crossing rather than maximizing vehicle lanes. New Urbanism and 15-Minute City concepts address the reality that middle- and low-income Filipinos depend on walking and public transit. Sustainability frameworks in Philippine CLUPs increasingly emphasize mixed-use zoning, TOD around transit stations, and pedestrian infrastructure as alternatives to sprawl-driven congestion.

## How It's Tested

1. **Framework definition and application** — "Which planning approach emphasizes that all daily needs should be accessible within a 15-minute walk from home?" → Answer: 15-Minute City (Carlos Moreno). Tests concept recall and context.

2. **Complete Streets in Philippine context** — "A municipal engineer proposes widening a street by reducing sidewalk width. Does this align with Complete Streets design?" → Answer: No; Complete Streets prioritizes pedestrian safety and comfort, especially in contexts where walking is the primary mode. This tests understanding of Complete Streets principles applied to low-car-dependency contexts.

3. **TOD catchment and zoning** — "A new MRT station is being planned in a residential area. What density and mix of uses should planning allow within the TOD walkable catchment (400–800m)?" → Answer: Higher density, mixed-use development (residential + commercial + office) to support transit ridership and reduce car dependency. Tests application of TOD principles to specific development scenarios.

**Exam Tip:** Complete Streets = streets designed for ALL users (pedestrians, cyclists, transit, cars), not just cars. Key elements: sidewalks, bike lanes, transit lanes/stop areas, pedestrian crossings, street furniture, trees. This is the most tested sustainable urban development concept.
