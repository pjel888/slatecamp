## 1. Big Picture: From Street to Building

Goal: move a wheelchair user from **road → sidewalk → entrance → interior route** using only compliant slopes and widths under the updated BP 344 IRR, coordinated with PD 1096 site controls.[web:2][web:31][web:32]

Elements covered:

- Dropped sidewalks and curb ramps.
- Sidewalks, walkways, and pathways.
- Interface with PD 1096 lot lines, setbacks, PSO, and RROW (Rules VII & VIII).[web:35][web:38][web:40]

## 2. Dropped Sidewalks (BP 344 – Updated IRR)

Key requirements for dropped sidewalks at pedestrian crossings and ends of walkways:[web:2][web:10][web:31][web:33][web:42]

- Provide at **pedestrian crossings** and at ends of walkways of private streets/access roads.[web:2][web:33]
- Width: normally **corresponds to the width of the crossing** (e.g., 4.0 m minimum for national roads or as required by local ordinances for local roads).[web:33]
- For crossings/walkways **less than 1500 mm** wide:
  - Base/level surface at bottom of dropped sidewalk: minimum **1500‑mm depth**, width equal to the crossing/walkway width.[web:2][web:33]
- Maximum cross gradient: **1:100 (1%)**, sloped towards the road to avoid ponding.[web:2][web:33]
- Difference in elevation between base/level area and road/gutter: **≤ 19–25 mm** depending on specific diagram reference; most 2013–2024 materials show a small lip not exceeding around 19–25 mm.[web:2][web:33][web:42]
- Signage (where specified in updated IRR/tabs and local practice):
  - Pole‑mounted ISA sign, typically around **600 × 600 mm**, mounted with at least **2200 mm** headroom and located so it does not obstruct the path of travel.[web:31][web:32]
  - Pavement ISA sign at or near the center of the dropped sidewalk/curb ramp, sized proportionally to ramp width (often 600–800 mm as shown in reviewers).[web:31][web:32]

The **1500‑mm base pad** at the bottom is your primary **maneuvering area** at street level for turning and queueing.[web:2][web:33]

## 3. Curb Ramps at Drop‑Offs

For PWD drop‑off/loading bays (BP 344 IRR Appendix B – “Curb Ramps at Drop‑off Points”):[web:2][web:10][web:34][web:37][web:42]

- **Minimum width:** **900 mm**.[web:2][web:10][web:34][web:37]
- **Maximum gradient for the curb ramp run:** older IRR illustrations commonly show curb ramp runs not steeper than **1:12**, while some newer reviewer decks emphasize **1:15** as a conservative design slope; for exam purposes, keep **1:12** as the strict upper limit and treat 1:15 as a good‑practice gentler slope, especially over short rises.[web:1][web:2][web:34][web:42]
- Must not reduce the required sidewalk/walkway width or required level turn area: the curb ramp should be contained within or adjacent to the **minimum clear sidewalk/walkway width** and must preserve turning pads (see Section 4).[web:2][web:31][web:32]
- Use the same ISA wayfinding logic as dropped sidewalks: pole signage with adequate headroom and pavement markings aligned with the curb ramp.[web:31][web:32]

Think of curb ramps as **short, localized connectors** at the curb line; use full accessible ramps (with the wider minimum width and gentler slopes of the ramp section of BP 344) for larger level changes between street and entrance.[web:31][web:32][web:42]

## 4. Sidewalks, Walkways, and Pathways (Exterior “Corridors”)

Based on updated BP 344 IRR and 2024 reviewer summaries for pedestrian facilities:[web:2][web:31][web:32][web:36][web:41][web:42]

### 4.1 Width and Gradient

- **Minimum clear width:**
  - Older/official IRR: **1.20 m** minimum width for sidewalks/walkways.[web:1][web:2][web:42]
  - 2024 reviewer/amended IRR decks: often teach **1.50 m minimum** for main sidewalks, especially in high‑use public environments.[web:31][web:32][web:36]
  - For this module, treat **1.50 m as the exam‑oriented design minimum**, with the note that **1.20 m is the older absolute minimum** still cited in original IRR.
- Where two wheelchairs must pass: **1800 mm** width is recommended (and frequently cited in 2024 notes).[web:31][web:32]
- Longitudinal gradient: keep as level as practicable; **target not steeper than about 1:20 (5%)**, except where topography or site conditions necessitate ramps.[web:31][web:32]
- Cross gradient: maximum **1:100 (1%)**, to ensure drainage without discomfort.[web:1][web:2][web:42]

### 4.2 Continuity and Surface Quality

- Surfaces must be **slip‑resistant** in wet/dry conditions, continuous, and without abrupt changes in level greater than about **6 mm**; larger changes require bevels or ramps.[web:2][web:31][web:32]
- Gratings are discouraged along accessible paths; if unavoidable:[web:2][web:42]
  - Bars should be **perpendicular** to the direction of travel.
  - Maximum clear spacing around **13 mm** between bars.
  - Grating top surface should not be more than about **6 mm** above or below adjacent pavement.
  - Provide at least **30% luminance contrast** between grating and surrounding surface for visibility (per updated wayfinding/signage notes).[web:31][web:32]

### 4.3 Rest / Turn and Dead‑End Spaces

- In lengthy or busy sidewalks/walkways, provide places where a wheelchair can **turn around or pass another wheelchair**.[web:31][web:32]
- These **turnabout spaces** should be at least **1800 mm** in clear dimension (e.g., 1800 × 1800 mm squares).[web:31][web:32][web:36]
- Maximum spacing: about **12000 mm** between turnabout/rest spaces along long corridors or walkways.[web:31][web:32][web:36]
- Provide a turnabout/turning space at or within approximately **3500 mm** of every dead‑end corridor or exterior walkway dead‑end.[web:31][web:32]

Mnemonic (adjusted): **“1.2 code, 1.5 exam path, 1.8 pass, 12 m between, 3.5 m from dead‑end.”**

## 5. Tactile Paths and Warning Tiles Outside

From tactile floor surface provisions in updated IRR and wayfinding materials:[web:31][web:32][web:37][web:42]

- Tactile paths are required on primary pedestrian routes **within and approaching buildings**, typically linking public sidewalks, transport stops, entrances, counters, lifts, and toilets; recent reviewers commonly describe coverage within **several hundred meters** of major access points (e.g., transport stations to entrances), but check local IRR text for any fixed radius.[web:31][web:32][web:37]
- Use **directional tactile blocks** (parallel bars) along the route to guide visually impaired users to key destinations.[web:37][web:42]
- Use **warning tactile blocks** (domes) at hazards and decision points: stairs, ramps, escalator approaches, platform edges, major crossings, and gates.[web:37][web:42]
- Warning tactile strips typically:[web:31][web:32][web:37][web:42]
  - Have a depth of around **600 mm**.
  - Start approximately **300 mm** back from the actual hazard line (e.g., top/bottom of stairs, platform edge), so the user encounters the tactile cue before the drop.

This ties the **external tactile path** directly into interior tactile cues for stairs, elevators, and washrooms.

## 6. PD 1096 Context: Lot Lines, RROW, and Setbacks

From PD 1096 Rules VII & VIII summaries (classification, light & ventilation, PSO):[web:35][web:38][web:40]

- **Rule VII – Building Classification by Use or Occupancy:** affects occupancy group (residential, commercial, institutional, etc.), which in turn impacts required yards, courts, and allowable projections that your exterior accessible routes must respect.[web:15][web:35]
- **Rule VIII – Light and Ventilation / Percentage of Site Occupancy (PSO):**
  - Dictates **measurement of site occupancy** at ground level, exclusive of required courts, yards, and light wells.[web:35][web:38][web:40]
  - Specifies **maximum PSO** and minimum open space/court dimensions; sidewalks and accessible ramps inside the lot must sit within remaining open spaces without reducing courts and yards below minimum dimensions.[web:35][web:38][web:40]
- You cannot legally “solve accessibility” by over‑occupying open spaces or by permanently encroaching into the **public RROW** contrary to PD 1096, zoning, or local right‑of‑way ordinances; coordination with local road‑right‑of‑way standards is needed when aligning ramps and dropped sidewalks at the property line.[web:38][web:40]

Exam tip: when sketching site sections, show **property line, RROW, required front yard/court, sidewalk, ramp**, and building line all respecting PSO and court rules.
