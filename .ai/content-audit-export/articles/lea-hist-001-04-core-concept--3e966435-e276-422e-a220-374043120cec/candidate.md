# Egyptian Architecture

## LEA Point
Egyptian architecture should be read through funerary architecture, temple architecture, royal ideology, and material control. The exam cues are mastaba, step pyramid, true pyramid, pylon, hypostyle hall, clerestory, sanctuary, Karnak/Luxor, Giza, Saqqara, and Thebes.

## Source Anchors
- UNESCO Memphis and its Necropolis anchors Giza, Saqqara, pyramids, mastabas, temples, and Old Kingdom funerary monument context.
- UNESCO Ancient Thebes anchors Karnak/Luxor, the Valley of the Kings/Queens, and the religious/royal monument landscape of Middle and New Kingdom Egypt.
- The local 30-Second Ancient Egypt conversion anchors mastaba/step/true-pyramid sequence, Djoser/Imhotep, Khufu original height, construction tools/materials, and Karnak as a long-expanded temple complex.

## Core Pattern
Egyptian monuments combine theology and organized labor with material expertise. Most ordinary construction used mud brick and plaster, while monumental pyramids and temples used limestone, sandstone, and granite. Pyramid development runs from mastaba forms to Djoser's step pyramid and then to true pyramids at Giza. Temple development uses processional sequence: pylon, court, hypostyle hall, and sanctuary, with light controlled by column height and high openings.

## Exam Guardrails
- Do not repeat unsupported knife-blade or exact block-count claims unless a local source is added.
- Do not call pyramids temples; pyramids are primarily royal funerary monuments, while temples house and serve divine cults.
- Do not import Jericho or Hittite questions into an Egyptian article unless separately sourced; Batch 118 question edits replace those off-article prompts.
- Use Khufu's original height only because the local conversion anchors it.

## Real World Scenario
A review item describes a processional axis through a pylon into a columned hall with high light. The source-safe answer is Egyptian temple architecture. If the clue shifts to royal burial and a mastaba-to-step-to-true-pyramid sequence, classify it as funerary/pyramid development instead.
