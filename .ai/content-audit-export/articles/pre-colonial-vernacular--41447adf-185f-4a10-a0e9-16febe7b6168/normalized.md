# Pre-Colonial Vernacular

## Earliest Shelter And Regional Forms

HOA Module 4 P1 begins Philippine architecture with early shelters such as the Tabon Cave complex, Tau't Batu cave dwelling, Idjang rock-hewn fortresses in Batanes, Agta lean-to structures, and Kalinga tree houses. It then defines vernacular architecture as common building techniques based on the forms and materials of a historical period, region, or people.

## Tropical Domestic Pattern

The same source lists archetypal Southeast Asian domestic traits: elevated living floor, buoyant rectangular volume, raised pile foundation, and voluminous thatched roof. It then identifies regional house types such as Binuron, Binayon, Fay-u, Inagamang, Bale or Fale, Binangiyan, Rakuh, Bahay Kubo, and Lepa.

## Bahay Kubo Anchor

The Bahay Kubo is identified as a traditional lowland dwelling in northern and central regions and as a passively cooled house. Its noted features include porous surfaces, horizontal windows, roof and window overhangs, and surrounding gardens. Filipino Architecture also identifies the pre-Spanish Bahay Kubo as a dwelling of wood, bamboo, and palm leaves adapted to the tropical climate.

## LEA Takeaway

For exam items, pre-colonial vernacular is not one form. It is a family of climate, material, and regional responses, with the Bahay Kubo as the most repeated lowland anchor.
