# Vitruvius and Classical Architectural Theory

## Source-Audited Scope

Use this topic as a classical theory topic. The checked local sources support the Vitruvian frame that architecture should be built with regard to durability, convenience, and beauty. This is not a substitute for code or legal requirements.

## Board Exam Quick Check

When a question invokes Vitruvius, look for the balanced triad: durability, convenience, and beauty. Do not choose an answer that reduces architecture to appearance alone or to engineering alone.

## Core Concepts

- Vitruvius states that architecture should be built with due reference to durability, convenience, and beauty.
- The triad is a balanced architectural test: the work must stand, serve use, and achieve aesthetic quality.
- Classical theory remains useful because it gives a compact way to evaluate whether a building is more than shelter or engineering.
- Salvan's theory text also frames modern shelter through strength, convenience, and appearance.
- For LEA purposes, the safest answer preserves all three parts of the triad.

## LEA Use

Vitruvian questions usually ask for recognition or application. The correct choice keeps the three requirements together. A distractor often swaps the triad with unrelated project stages, legal classifications, or purely decorative concerns.

## Active Recall

1. What are the three classical requirements? Durability, convenience, and beauty.
2. What does the triad prevent? Reducing architecture to only structure, only function, or only appearance.
3. What is the exam-safe answer pattern? A balanced statement that includes all three requirements.
