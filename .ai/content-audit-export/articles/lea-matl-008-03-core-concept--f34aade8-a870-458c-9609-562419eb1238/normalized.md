## What You Need to Know for the LEA
## Learning Goal

Classify fastener systems (nails, screws, bolts, anchors, rivets, welding, adhesives) by material, load capacity, substrate compatibility, and installation method, and select the appropriate fastener for a given structural or non-structural connection.
## Core Concept

Fasteners are classified by type (nails, screws, bolts, rivets) and material grade. Nails are categorized by length and diameter. Common nails have a larger diameter and are used for general construction and framing. Finishing nails have a smaller diameter and smoother shaft, minimizing surface damage, suitable for trim and cabinetry. Roofing nails include a neoprene washer to prevent leakage. Nail sizes are measured in penny weight, abbreviated 'd' (penny), historically based on the weight of 100 nails. A 16d common nail is 3.5 inches long; a 10d common nail is 3 inches long. Penny weight does not scale linearly; reference tables are essential.

Screws are classified by drive type (Phillips, slotted, square/Robertson, hex head), material (steel, stainless, brass), and application. Wood screws have a coarse thread and sharp point for timber; self-tapping screws (or sheet metal screws) have finer threads and cut their own thread pilot in metal. Lag screws (or lag bolts) are heavy-duty fasteners with a hex head, used for timber-to-timber or timber-to-concrete applications. Machine screws are precision fasteners with consistent thread pitch, used with nuts for metal assembly.

Bolts are classified by head shape and material grade. Hex bolts are the most common. Carriage bolts have a rounded head and a square neck underneath to prevent rotation during installation. Anchor bolts are embedded in concrete and used to secure machinery or structural members. Expansion bolts (toggle bolts, wedge anchors) are used to attach to existing concrete without drilling all the way through. ASTM A307 grade bolts have a minimum tensile strength of 60,000 psi and are used for general-purpose applications. ASTM A325 bolts are high-strength structural bolts with 120,000 psi tensile strength, used in structural steel connections. ASTM A490 bolts provide 150,000 psi tensile strength for ultra-high-stress applications.

Rivets are historical fasteners largely replaced by bolts and welds. They are driven hot, then cooled to create a permanent joint. Riveted connections cannot be disassembled, making them unsuitable for modern construction requiring serviceability.

Pull-out strength (or withdrawal strength) depends on fastener diameter, material, and embedment depth. Wood-to-wood connections: pull-out strength ≈ 25 to 50 lbf per inch of embedment for common nails; lag screws provide 300 to 500 lbf per inch. Concrete anchors vary by type: wedge anchors provide 1,000 to 2,000 lbf per inch of embedment.

## Philippine Construction Context

Philippine construction standards adopt ASTM fastener classifications, though many informal projects use non-standard fasteners. Common wire nails (CWN) are widely available through hardware stores but rarely meet ASTM specifications for uniformity. Filipino contractors often specify "20 inches" for roofing when 1.75-inch roofing nails are required, leading to fastener failure and roof leaks during monsoon rains. Lag screws and expansion anchors are less commonly used in informal residential construction, resulting in inadequate fastening of structural members to concrete foundations. Stainless steel fasteners, essential for typhoon and coastal corrosion resistance, are often substituted with carbon steel due to cost, leading to premature rust. Quality assurance is weak; ASTM A325 bolts may be mislabeled as A490, creating structural reliability issues.

## How It's Tested

**Exam Pattern 1: Nail Sizing.** A framing plan specifies "16d common nails." What is the length and intended application? Answer: 3.5 inches long; general framing and structural connections.

**Exam Pattern 2: Bolt Grade Selection.** A structural steel connection in a commercial building requires a tensile strength of 100,000 psi minimum. Which ASTM bolt grade is appropriate? Answer: ASTM A325 (120,000 psi); A307 (60,000 psi) is insufficient.

**Exam Pattern 3: Pull-Out Calculation.** A wooden roof rafter is fastened to a timber plate with a 10-inch embedment using 0.25-inch diameter lag screws. Assume lag screw pull-out strength is 400 lbf per inch. What is the total pull-out capacity? Answer: 10 inches × 400 lbf/inch = 4,000 lbf.

**Exam Pattern 4: Fastener Material Specification.** A coastal resort in the Philippines specifies carbon steel bolts for exterior connections. What is the risk? Answer: Carbon steel corrodes rapidly in salt spray; stainless steel (duplex or super-duplex) or hot-dip galvanized fasteners are mandatory for coastal applications.
