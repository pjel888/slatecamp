## Board Exam Quick Check

For fastener questions, identify the base material, the joined material, whether removal is expected, and whether the fastener works by shank friction, threads, expansion, toggling, or a mating nut.

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal

Classify nails, screws, bolts, washers, anchors, rivets, and adhesives by connection action and installation condition.

## Core Concept

BCI identifies nails by penny length, shank diameter, shank form, head form, and point type. Nails range from small finish nails to heavy nails for heavy work. A useful source-safe proportion is that nail length should be about three times the thickness of the material being secured. Serrated, barbed, threaded, fluted, twisted, cement-coated, and zinc-coated shanks change grip or corrosion resistance.

Screws are metal fasteners with tapered, helically threaded shanks. Because of their threads, screws have greater holding power than nails and are easier to remove. Screws are classified by use, head type, material, length, and diameter. Wood screws should be slightly shorter than the combined board thickness, with part of their length penetrating the base material; holes for ordinary screws should be predrilled to the base diameter of the threads.

Bolts are threaded metal pins or rods inserted through holes and secured with a mating nut. Carriage bolts are useful where the head may be inaccessible during tightening. Lag bolts or lag screws are used where a nut cannot be placed or where a long fastener is needed to penetrate a joint fully.

Washers distribute pressure, prevent leakage, relieve friction, or insulate incompatible materials. Expansion bolts and shields engage masonry or concrete by expanding inside a drilled hole. Toggle bolts fasten to plaster, gypsum board, and other thin wall materials by opening wings behind the hollow surface. Rivets permanently join steel members, though bolting and welding often replace them for labor reasons.

Adhesives are also fasteners in the broad connection sense. They secure surfaces together and vary by strength, curing time, temperature range, bonding method, and resistance to water, heat, sunlight, and chemicals.

## How It's Tested

LEA-style items often ask for the fastener action. If the item says removable and threaded, think screw or bolt. If it says hollow wall, think toggle bolt. If it says masonry or concrete hole expansion, think expansion bolt or shield. If it says broad sheet bonding, think adhesive.

## Common Mistakes

- Using a finish nail where head area or structural holding is required.
- Choosing a screw without predrilling when the source expects a threaded hole.
- Forgetting washers when pressure distribution, leakage, friction, or material separation matters.
- Confusing an expansion bolt with a toggle bolt.
- Treating adhesive selection as generic when curing, bonding method, and exposure matter.
