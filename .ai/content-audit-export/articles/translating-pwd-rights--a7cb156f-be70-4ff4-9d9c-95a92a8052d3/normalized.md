## REAL-WORLD HOOK

A CHED inspector walks through a newly completed state university campus in Mindanao. She is herself a wheelchair user. She checks the classrooms — the corridors are 1200 mm, just barely enough. The school toilet has no grab bar. The ramp to the library is 1:8 — too steep. The swimming pool has no transfer wall. The university president says proudly: "We complied with all the laws." The inspector opens her laptop and reads back to him: RA 7277 Section 12 (right to education), Section 23 (right to health), Section 30 (right to recreation). Then she asks a single question: *"Where, exactly in this campus, can a student in a wheelchair use any of these rights?"* This module maps every major PWD right in Philippine law to 2–3 concrete architectural details — so that you, the architect, are never the person who cannot answer that question.

## THE MASTER RIGHTS-TO-DETAILS MATRIX

### RIGHT 1: Transport (RA 7277 Sec. 25)

> **Legal Text:** PWDs have the right to accessible public transport — including vehicles, terminals, stations, and pathways leading to and from transport facilities.

| Architectural Feature | Specification | Standard |
|----------------------|---------------|----------|
| **Level boarding platforms** | Floor height matches vehicle floor ±25 mm; platform edge marked with TGSI 300 mm back from edge | BP 344 + DPWH IRR |
| **Tactile paths at stations** | TGSI: directional bars (walking direction) 25 mm raised, 65 mm width; warning domes (hazard/turn) 5 mm raised; minimum strip width 300 mm | BP 344 Appendix A |
| **Ramp at terminal entry** | Slope ≤1:12, width ≥1200 mm, handrails at 700–900 mm both sides, landing 1500 mm at top and bottom | BP 344 |
| **Accessible parking at terminal** | Min stall: 4000 mm wide × 5000 mm long + 1800 mm access aisle; nearest to accessible entrance; ISA signage | BP 344 |
| **Elevator in multi-level transport facility** | Min cab: 1400 mm deep × 1500 mm wide, 900 mm door; Braille panel at 900–1200 mm AFF | BP 344 Sec. 5.6 |
| **Curb cuts / dropped sidewalk at station** | Flush with both sidewalk and road level; slope ≤1:12; detectable warning strip (TGSI domes) at top | BP 344 |
| **Accessible toilets at terminals** | Min 1800 × 2250 mm cubicle; 900 mm door; grab bars; accessible route from terminal concourse | BP 344 Sec. 6 |
| **Queue management / waiting areas** | 5% of waiting seats designated PWD priority; accessible path to ticket counter; two-level counter | BP 344 Sec. 18.2.b.iii |

### Wheelchair Seating in Assembly — 3% Rule Application

| Total Seats | 3% (rounded up) | Old IRR equivalent |
|------------|----------------|-------------------|
| 100 | 3 spaces | 4 (51–300 → 4) |
| 200 | 6 spaces | 4 (51–300 → 4) |
| 300 | 9 spaces | 4 (51–300 → 4) |
| 400 | 12 spaces | 6 (301–500 → 6) |
| 500 | 15 spaces | 6 (301–500 → 6) |
| 1000 | 30 spaces | 11 (6 + 5 extra per 100) |
| 2000 | 60 spaces | 21 (6 + 15 extra per 100) |

> **Observation:** For smaller venues (100–300 seats), the **2024 IRR 3% rule requires fewer spaces** than the old tiered table. For mid-size (500 seats), they are roughly equivalent. For large venues (1000+), the **3% rule requires more spaces**. Know both — the exam tests both.

**Q2.** A deaf PWD checks into a hotel. Under the Right to Information (RA 7277 Sec. 29), what two room features must be present?

> **A:**
> 1. **Visual/strobe alarm** — max 3 flashes/sec, min 2 candelas, white lens, word "FIRE" visible — in all rooms including bathrooms (BP 344 Sec. 17.8)

**Q3.** A 1800 × 2250 mm accessible WC cubicle is designed. The grab bar is on the side wall. What is the correct height of the grab bar above the floor?

> **A:** The grab bar should be **300 mm above the WC seat height**. With a standard accessible WC seat at 450–480 mm AFF, the grab bar is approximately **750–780 mm AFF**. The measurement is from the WC seat, not from the floor independently.

**Q5.** An office renovation for a company with 200 employees. Under the Right to Employment (RA 7277 Sec. 5–8), what are the minimum workstation dimensions for a wheelchair user?

> **A:** **Width: 900 mm; Knee clearance: 700 mm high × 480 mm deep**. A turning space of 1500 × 1500 mm must be available beside the workstation. The workstation must be served by an accessible route of at least 900 mm clear width.

---

**Q6.** A new 2000-seat cinema is being designed. How many wheelchair spaces are required under the 2024 IRR, and what are the dimensions of each space?

> **A:** **3% × 2000 = 60 wheelchair spaces.** Each space must be: **900 mm wide × 1200 mm deep** (front/rear entry), on a clear, firm, level surface. Access to each space must not pass through another wheelchair space.

---

**Q7.** Under RA 9442 (Right Against Discrimination), a bank must provide a priority counter. What are the exact height dimensions of this two-level counter?

> **A:** Lower level (for wheelchair users): **750 mm AFF.** Higher level (for standing users): **950–1100 mm AFF.** Minimum counter width: **900 mm.** Knee clearance at lower level: **700 mm high × 480 mm deep.**

---

**Q8.** A swimming pool complex is being renovated. Under the Right to Recreation (RA 7277 Sec. 30), what physical feature must be added to the pool to make it accessible for wheelchair users who cannot swim independently?

> **A:** A **transfer wall at 450 mm AFF** — a raised ledge at the pool edge that allows a wheelchair user to transfer from their chair to the pool edge and slide in. A **1500 × 1500 mm transfer space** (slope ≤1:12) must adjoin the transfer wall.

---

**Q9.** Under RA 10754 (20% discount + 12% VAT exemption), a PWD goes to a pharmacy. The dispensing counter is 900 mm high with no knee clearance. What architectural deficiency is this, and which specific dimension must be corrected?

> **A:** The counter has no accessible section. The pharmacy must provide a **lower counter level at 750 mm AFF** with **700 mm knee clearance** underneath (and 480 mm depth) so a wheelchair user can approach and claim their discount. The right to the discount is meaningless without accessible physical access to the counter.

---

**Q10.** An emergency call button in an accessible hospital toilet is mounted at 1200 mm AFF. Is this compliant?

> **A:** **No.** The emergency call button must be at **400–600 mm AFF** — low enough to be reached by a person who has fallen to the floor. At 1200 mm, a fallen person cannot reach it. This is a life-safety failure, not just a technical noncompliance.

## TRAP LEARTS (MASTER LIST)

> **🚨 TRAP 1 — Grab Bar Height**
> The grab bar is 300 mm **above the WC seat**, not 300 mm above the floor. With the seat at 450–480 mm, the bar is ~750–780 mm AFF. Distractors often say "300 mm AFF" or "900 mm AFF."

> **🚨 TRAP 2 — Emergency Call Button Height**
> Call button = **400–600 mm AFF** (low, reachable from floor). Do NOT confuse with light switches (1200 mm AFF) or electrical outlets (450 mm min AFF).

> **🚨 TRAP 3 — 3% vs Old Wheelchair Seating Table**
> 2024 IRR = 3% flat rate. Old IRR = tiered (4-50→2, etc.). Both may appear. The question context tells you which to use. If no date context given, use the 2024 IRR (3%).

> **🚨 TRAP 4 — Pool Transfer Wall Height**
> Transfer wall = **450 mm** AFF. This is not a ramp height, not a grab bar height, not a bench height. It is specific to pool transfer walls only.

> **🚨 TRAP 5 — "One Accessible Room per Building" vs 2%**
> The old IRR provided "1 per 50 rooms." The 2024 IRR mandates **2% of total guest rooms** as accessible. Government-owned accommodation: **10%**. Know all three.

> **🚨 TRAP 6 — Two-Level Counter Heights**
> Lower: **750 mm** (always). Higher: **950–1100 mm** (range). The exam may test "what is the higher level height?" — the answer is a range, not a single number. Any value between 950 and 1100 mm is correct.

> **🚨 TRAP 7 — Strobe Flash Rate**
> Maximum **3 flashes per second** (not "at least 3"). More than 3 flashes/sec can cause seizures in photosensitive PWDs. The limit is a MAXIMUM, not a minimum.
