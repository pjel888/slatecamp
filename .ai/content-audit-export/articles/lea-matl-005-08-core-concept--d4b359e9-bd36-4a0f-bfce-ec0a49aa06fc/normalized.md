# Vernacular sustainable materials require suitability, protection, and approval evidence

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

The source-safe rule is simple: local, natural, or rapidly renewable materials can support sustainable design, but they do not become structurally or code suitable by label alone. The material must fit the application, be protected from moisture and weather where needed, and be documented in the approved plans and specifications.

## Core Concept

- Green Building Illustrated identifies rapidly renewable, natural, low-toxicity, and locally procured materials as desirable options after material use has been minimized and reuse or recycling has been maximized.
- Bamboo is cited as an example of a rapidly renewable material, but the same source warns that the right application matters and notes moisture limits for bamboo flooring.
- Wood is described as low in embodied energy, reusable, and durable if protected from weather.
- Rammed earth is described as strong, natural, local, noncombustible, and nontoxic, but it needs moisture protection and may require insulation, suitable soil, labor, and training.
- The Good Building Handbook in the Philippines stresses that correct construction and material quality affect building strength and safety.
- PD 1096 requires the Building Official to check that plans and specifications conform to code and other rules, and approved plans and specifications cannot be changed without approval.

## How It Is Tested

- Match a natural or vernacular material to a suitable use rather than assuming universal structural approval.
- Identify moisture, weather, quality-control, and application limits.
- Recognize when a project-specific approval or documented specification is required.
- Reject unsupported species, service-life, and public-works acceptance claims unless the exact source is audited.

## Common Mistakes

- Do not say all vernacular materials are automatically code-compliant.
- Do not use sustainability as a substitute for structural suitability.
- Do not ignore material cleanliness and quality in concrete or masonry work.
