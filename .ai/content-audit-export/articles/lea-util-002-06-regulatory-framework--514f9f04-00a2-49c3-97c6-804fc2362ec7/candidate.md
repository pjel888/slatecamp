## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Elevator design is both a transportation problem and a regulated building-equipment problem. For LEA answers, do not stop at car speed or finish. Check the hoistway, pit, machine room, safety devices, emergency service, and electrical coordination that make the elevator lawful and maintainable.

## Elevator System Parts

Salvan describes a traction elevator as a coordinated system made up of the car, cables, elevator machine, control equipment, counterweights, shaft or hoistway, rails, penthouse, and pit. The car is the user-facing part, but the exam often tests the hidden parts: buffers in the pit, guide rails in the shaft, traction machinery, controls, and safety switches.

Control equipment includes push buttons, relays, electronic controls, leveling devices, floor indicators, call buttons, and limit switches. Its purpose is safe starting, acceleration, retardation, leveling, stopping, and door operation. A correct answer usually preserves this control chain instead of treating an elevator as only a cabin in a shaft.

## Hoistway and Pit Rules

The checked utilities source states that elevator hoistways must be substantially enclosed through their height, with openings only where needed for doors, windows, or skylights. Ropes, wires, and pipes that are not necessary for elevator operation should not be placed in the hoistway.

The pit must also be coordinated with car travel and buffers. When the car rests on fully compressed buffers, the source requires at least 600 mm clearance between the underside of the car and the bottom of the pit. For exam purposes, this is a life-safety and maintenance clearance, not a finish dimension.

## Multiple Elevators and Machine Rooms

When four or more elevators serve all or the same portion of a building, Salvan's checked regulatory extract requires them to be placed in at least two hoistways, with not more than four elevators in any one hoistway. This limits concentration of service and improves resilience.

Where a machine room or penthouse is provided at the top of a hoistway, it must allow repair and inspection. If the room is more than 600 mm above the adjacent floor or roof surface, access must be by ladder or stair, with the access slope not exceeding 60 degrees from horizontal. The room is not a living area or storage room; it needs adequate ventilation.

## Rope, Relay, and Service Requirements

The checked source gives minimum hoisting-rope rules: three ropes for traction elevators and two for drum-type elevators, with a minimum diameter of 30 mm for hoisting and counterweight ropes. It also requires overload and reverse-polarity relays.

For high-rise apartments or residential condominiums of more than five stories, at least one passenger elevator must remain on 24-hour constant service. In an LEA question, this points to continuity of building service, not convenience alone.

## Electrical and Emergency Coordination

Elevators appear in electrical distribution and emergency planning. Salvan's riser discussion shows elevator feeders as part of service-equipment coordination. The Philippine Electrical Code also treats elevator-related equipment as part of the broader grounding and bonding environment for electrical installations.

For exam answers, choose the option that preserves safe access, adequate working room, clear hoistways, correct pit clearance, required safety relays, and emergency service. Reject answers that use the hoistway as a general chase, convert machine rooms into storage, or ignore maintenance access.

## LEA Focus

Use this checklist when a question involves elevator regulation:

- Hoistway: enclosed and kept free of unrelated services.
- Pit: enough clearance at fully compressed buffers.
- Grouping: four or more elevators require distribution across hoistways.
- Machine room: accessible, ventilated, repairable, and not used for living or storage.
- Safety: required ropes, overload relay, reverse-polarity relay, and limit/control devices.
- Service: required 24-hour passenger service in qualifying high-rise residential buildings.
