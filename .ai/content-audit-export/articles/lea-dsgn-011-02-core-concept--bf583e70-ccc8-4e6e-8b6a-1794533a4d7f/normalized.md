## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal
Read and produce elevations and sections using orthographic projection, label elevations by orientation, indicate finish floor levels and vertical dimensions, show section cuts on plans, and apply standard notation for materials and finishes.

Elevations and sections are orthographic projections that show height, depth, and interior spatial relationships—essential for communicating vertical design intent on drawings. The LEA expects you to read, interpret, and produce these views according to NBC standards.

## Core Concept

An **elevation** is a two-dimensional orthographic view of a building's exterior (or interior wall) projected onto a vertical plane perpendicular to the facade. It shows width and height but not depth. Elevations are labeled by direction: Front Elevation, Rear Elevation, East Elevation, or West Elevation. Each elevation communicates the building's vertical profile, including finish floor levels, roof pitch, fenestration patterns, and material changes.

A **section** is a vertical slice through the building that reveals internal height relationships. Unlike elevations, sections show how spaces stack vertically and how the building relates to grade. Section cuts are indicated on floor plans with a heavy line and labeled with a letter or number pair (e.g., Section A-A). The direction of the arrows indicates the viewing direction.

**Key drawing conventions:**
- **Grade lines** represent finished ground level and are shown as a heavy horizontal line. Reference elevations are noted in meters above sea level (m AMSL) or relative to a site datum.
- **Finish floor levels (FFL)** are the top surface of the finished floor. In Philippine practice, **3.0 m** floor-to-floor height (FFL to FFL) is a common standard per DPW guidelines.
- **Roof pitch notation** uses ratio notation (e.g., 1:4 or 4:12) to indicate slope. This is critical for drainage design and structural spans.
- **Material indication** is shown through conventional hatching or color coding, allowing contractors to distinguish concrete from masonry from timber from steel.
- **Dimension chains** show vertical distances between key levels (floor to floor, floor to soffit, soffit to roof peak).

In exam scenarios, the LEA tests whether candidates can:
1. Extract dimensional data (floor-to-floor heights, story heights, setback compliance)
2. Identify non-compliance errors (e.g., floor-to-floor ≠ 3.0 m, roof heights blocking sight lines)
3. Determine interior headroom requirements (minimum 2.4 m finished ceiling height per NBC for habitable spaces)
4. Relate elevations to floor plans (confirming fenestration alignment, structural grid consistency)

## Key Provisions

| Provision | Standard | Reference |
|-----------|----------|-----------|
| Floor-to-floor height (common practice) | 3.0 m | DPW Guidelines |
| Minimum finished ceiling height (habitable) | 2.4 m | NBC Rule II Sec 204 |
| Minimum ceiling height (toilet/bath) | 2.1 m | NBC Rule II Sec 204(b) |
| Minimum headroom on stairs | 2.0 m | NBC Rule IV Sec 404(e) |
| Ramp grade | 1:20 | BP 344 (Accessibility) |
| Door opening height | 2.0 m | NBC Rule III Sec 303(b) |
| Roof pitch notation | Ratio format (e.g., 1:4, 4:12) | Standard practice |

**Section cut conventions:**
- Cuts are shown as heavy lines (2 mm) on floor plans.
- Arrows at cut line endpoints indicate viewing direction.
- The section drawing is labeled "Section A-A" or similar at the bottom center.
- All visible edges beyond the cut plane are shown in outline; hidden lines are shown dashed if needed for clarity.

## Philippine Practice Context

In Philippine practice, floor-to-floor heights of 3.0 m are standard for multi-story buildings per DPW guidelines. This differs from older practice or other jurisdictions and is a frequent source of exam errors. Sections are particularly important in Philippine practice when communicating stories built on sloping sites (common in Luzon and Visayas), where each floor may have different ground elevations. Elevation drawings must also account for tropical sun angles and prevailing wind directions when showing fixed shading devices (brise-soleil, fins, overhangs).

## How It's Tested

**Pattern 1: Reading and extracting dimensions**
A section drawing shows three stories with FFLs at 0.0 m, 3.2 m, and 6.4 m. The candidate must identify the error (floor-to-floor is 3.2 m, not 3.0 m) and state the corrected FFL for the second floor (should be 3.0 m). This tests comprehension of NBC standards and calculation accuracy.

**Pattern 2: Elevation-to-floor-plan correlation**
An elevation shows five window bays; the corresponding floor plan shows four columns. The candidate must recognize that the fenestration does not align with the structural grid and flag this as a drawing error requiring coordination.

**Pattern 3: Vertical clearance and accessibility**
A section shows a stairwell with headroom of 1.9 m. The candidate must identify this as non-compliant with NBC minimum headroom (2.0 m) and specify the correction.
