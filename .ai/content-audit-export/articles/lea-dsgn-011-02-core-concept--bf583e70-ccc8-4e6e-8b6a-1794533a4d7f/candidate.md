# Elevations

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal
Read and prepare source-safe elevation and section information by applying PD 1096 architectural-document requirements for elevations, sections, reflected ceiling plans, gridlines, grade levels, floor-to-floor heights, openings, materials, and adjoining structures.

An elevation is an orthographic drawing of a building face. It communicates exterior form, openings, levels, materials, grid coordination, and relationship to grade. A section is different: it cuts through the building to show vertical relationships, levels, rooms, structural parts, doors, windows, partitions, and other interior conditions along the cut.

PD 1096 lists elevations as required architectural drawings. It calls for at least four elevations, at the same scale as the floor plans, showing gridlines, natural ground to finish grade elevations, floor-to-floor heights, door and window marks, type of material and exterior finishes, and adjoining existing structures if any. That list is the source-safe content checklist for this article.

PD 1096 also calls for at least two sections. Sections should show gridlines, natural ground and finish levels, the outline of cut and visible structural parts, properly labeled doors and windows reflecting direction of opening, partitions, built-in cabinets, and the rooms or functional spaces cut by the section line.

The reflected ceiling plan is related because it coordinates the view above the finished floor. PD 1096 lists the reflected ceiling plan as showing design, location, finishes and material specifications, lighting fixtures, diffusers, decorations, air-conditioning exhaust and return grilles, and sprinkler nozzles if any, at a scale of at least 1:100.

| Drawing item | Source-safe check |
| --- | --- |
| Elevations | At least four; same scale as floor plans; show gridlines, natural ground to finish grade, floor-to-floor heights, door/window marks, materials, exterior finishes, and adjoining existing structures. |
| Sections | At least two; show gridlines, natural ground and finish levels, cut and visible structural parts, openings, partitions, built-ins, and rooms cut by section lines. |
| RCP | Show ceiling design, finishes, lighting fixtures, diffusers, AC exhaust and return grilles, sprinkler nozzles if any, and related specifications. |
| Ceiling heights | Check PD 1096 Rule VIII minimums by ventilation condition and storey; do not assume a universal 3.0 m floor-to-floor rule from this source packet. |

Floor-to-floor height is information that elevations must show, but the checked PD 1096 text does not create a universal 3.0 meter floor-to-floor standard for every building. The source-safe code check is ceiling height: habitable rooms with artificial ventilation must be at least 2.40 meters, habitable rooms with natural ventilation at least 2.70 meters, with additional storey-specific headroom provisions in Rule VIII.

## What to Watch For

If a floor plan shows four windows and the elevation shows five, the drawing set is not coordinated.

If a section cuts through a stair, shaft, room, or sloping site but omits levels or cut information, it fails its main purpose.

If an item asks whether 3.0 meters is automatically required, answer from the checked source: verify the actual project requirement and code ceiling-height condition rather than applying a universal floor-to-floor shortcut.

## Key Takeaways

Elevations show exterior faces and required level, opening, material, grid, and grade information.

Sections show cut-through vertical relationships and must coordinate with plan section markers.

PD 1096 requires drawing content; it does not justify unsupported drawing rules or uncited floor-to-floor standards.
