# SPP Document 209: Standards of Professional Practice on Professional Architectural Consulting Services (PACS)

## Overview

**SPP Document 209** governs **Professional Architectural Consulting Services (PACS)** — the broadest and most policy-laden document in the entire SPP series, and the **final document in the 2010 SPP framework**. It replaces the **1981 UAP Doc. 210** and is grounded in the Supplemental Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of **Republic Act No. 9266** (The Architecture Act of 2004), as well as in the Joint Resolution No. 2009-01 of the Philippine Technological Council (PTC) and the Council for the Built and Natural Environments (CBNE) dated December 11, 2009.

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to:

Identify the scope of SPP Doc 209 Architectural Consulting Services (PACS): the definition of 'consultant', permitted consulting relationships, fee arrangements (hourly, retainer, fixed fee), and the limitation that a consultant cannot simultaneously serve as the architect of record on the same project; apply the Code-imposed independence requirements to consulting engagements.
---
SPP 209 is structurally distinct from all preceding SPP documents: while SPP 201–208 primarily regulate the relationship between the Architect and a specific project Owner, **SPP 209 operates at the policy level** — defining who may provide consulting services in the Philippines, what those services encompass, how Filipino architects are protected from foreign displacement, and how both government and private sector clients must select and compensate Professional Consulting Architects (PCAs).

***

## 1. Foreword and Historical Context

### 1.1 LoI No. 1000 (1980) — The Starting Point

On **March 20, 1980**, President Ferdinand E. Marcos, recognizing the role of APO members in nation-building and the pursuit of national goals, issued **Letter of Instruction (LoI) No. 1000** directing all government agencies and instrumentalities to give **priority to members of APOs** in the hiring of employees and the engagement of professional services. This demonstrated the government's confidence in the capabilities of Filipino Registered and Licensed Professionals (RLPs).

### 1.2 The PFPA Standard Guidelines on Consulting Services

Consequent to LoI 1000, the **Philippine Federation of Professional Associations (PFPA)** — the umbrella of all APOs under the PRC and PTC — initiated the preparation of the document entitled **"Standard Guidelines on Consultancy/Consulting Services"**. This foundational document was to become the forerunner of SPP 209.

### 1.3 The 2010 Urgency: Foreign Consultant Influx

Three decades after LoI 1000, SPP 209 was revised under pressing circumstances:

- The **influx of foreign consultants (FCs)** had intensified significantly
- The **proliferation of entities projecting themselves as "consultants"** without adequate skills, training, or experience had become a serious problem
- The full utilization of Filipino professional consultants was being **undermined by foreign competition**, despite the fact that Filipino PCAs are well qualified

<Callout variant="note">

*"The problem presented by the dominant presence of foreign architects in the Philippines is precisely defined by the fact that the technology they bring in is already available in such adequate proportion that foreign consultants compete in terms adverse to Filipino registered and licensed professionals (RLPs) and in fact displace Filipino Professional Consultants (FPCs) from participation in significant development projects."*

</Callout>


### 1.4 The Joint Resolution Basis

The 2010 SPP 209 is based on the document jointly drafted by PTC and CBNE in accordance with their **Joint Resolution No. 2009-01 dated December 11, 2009**, incorporating relevant provisions from:

- R.A. No. 9266 (The Architecture Act of 2004) and its IRR
- R.A. No. 8293 (Intellectual Property Code of the Philippines)
- R.A. No. 9184 (Government Procurement Reform Act of 2003) and its latest IRR
- The Code of Ethical Standard (CES)
- Preceding SPP Documents

## 2. rationale

SPP 209 articulates **eight rationale provisions** that establish the policy foundation of the document:

### 2.1 Government and Private Sector Demand for Consultants

The Government, private sector, and civil society organizations regularly engage professional consultants for **efficiency, economy, and expediency** in project conceptualization, feasibility studies, project evaluations, design, and management.

### 2.2 Full Utilization of Local Expertise

One important strategy for strengthening national capability is the **full utilization of local expertise** — generating conditions for increased nationally-evolved scientific and technological knowledge.

### 2.3 Protection of Filipino Professionals

CBNE and PTC spearheaded the formulation of standard guidelines to **enhance participation of Filipino professionals** in national development and **protect their rights** in accordance with existing laws.

### 2.4 Foreign Exchange Conservation

Full utilization of Filipino Professional Consultants (FPCs) **minimizes the importation of foreign expertise**, which entails considerable expenditure of hard-earned foreign exchange. The displacement of FPCs by foreign consultants is described as **"irrational"**.

### 2.5 The Technology Transfer Argument Rejected

The inflow of foreign consultants is often justified on the ground of "technology transfer" — but SPP 209 explicitly challenges this argument:

<Callout variant="note">

*"The problem presented by the dominant presence of foreign architects in the Philippines is precisely defined by the fact that the technology they bring in is already available in such adequate proportion that foreign consultants compete in terms adverse to Filipino RLPs."*

</Callout>


### 2.6 Educational Capital Loss

The disproportionate inflow versus outflow of expertise represents a **tremendous loss to the country in terms of educational costs** — depriving the nation of contributions from returning Filipino professionals.

### 2.7 Competence of Filipino PCAs

<Callout variant="note">

*"The professional competence of Filipino Professional Consultants (FPCs), specifically Professional Consulting Architects (PCAs) is well established and adequate to meet the country's requirements. In fact, in many cases, foreign consultants depend largely on the expertise and information furnished by FPCs, especially PCAs."*

</Callout>


### 2.8 Primary Purpose of SPP 209

The primary purpose is to **complement existing professional regulatory laws** governing the practice of architecture, ensuring nothing in SPP 209 unduly affects those laws, policies, rules, and regulations.

## 3. Objectives

SPP 209 sets out **five specific policy objectives**:

<ComparisonTable>

| No. | Objective |
|---|---|
| **3.1** | Establish standards and parameters for PCAs to develop and nurture competencies, credibility, and integrity in their respective fields/areas of specialization |
| **3.2** | Define the scope, type, and nature of PACS that **only RLA-PCAs** shall be allowed to perform for the Government, private sector, CSOs, and the international community with projects on Philippine soil |
| **3.3** | Ensure full compliance with advisories and guidelines on selecting, commissioning, and engaging **foreign architects/consultants** for projects on Philippine soil — to stimulate the local market for FPCAs and level the field between RLAs and FAs/FCs |
| **3.4** | Apply Filipino Architects' familiarity with **local physical, social, economic, business, institutional, and environmental conditions** towards sustainable and comprehensive development |
| **3.5** | Create a nurturing environment that encourages Filipino PCAs to **practice and develop domestically** — propelling them to the same level as, if not greater heights than, their global counterparts |

</ComparisonTable>

## 4. Declaration of Policy

SPP 209 invokes **Section 14, Article XII of the 1987 Philippine Constitution** as its policy foundation:

<Callout variant="note">

*"The sustained development of a reservoir of national tleants consisting of Filipino scientists, entrepreneurs, professionals, managers, high-level technical manpower and skilled workers and craftsmen in all fields shall be promoted by the State. The State shall encourage appropriate technology and regulate its transfer for the national benefit. The practice of all professions in the Philippines shall be limited to Filipino citizens, save in cases prescribed by law."*

</Callout>


All State-registered and licensed professionals (RLPs) — including RLAs — are **mandatorily required** to strictly adhere to their respective SPPs within the framework of this constitutional provision.

## 5. Key Definitions

SPP 209 introduces a comprehensive set of definitions specific to the consulting context — more extensive than any preceding SPP document:

### 5.1 Professional Consulting Architect (PCA)

<Callout variant="note">

Any person, whether natural or juridical, duly licensed, registered and/or duly accredited by the Commission; must be a Philippine-registered and licensed Architect (RLA), with a valid registration certification and Commission ID card, and a member in good standing of the IAPOA.

</Callout>


### 5.2 Filipino Professional Consulting Architect (FPCA)

A Filipino citizen, a natural person who is an RLA, a member in good standing of the IAPOA, and a specialist in any branch of architecture under R.A. 9266. When an FPCA **signs and seals architectural documents**, he becomes the **Architect-of-Record (Aor)** and assumes the attendant professional responsibilities and civil liabilities.

### 5.3 Foreign Consultant (FC) or Foreign Architect (FA)

An individual, NOT a Philippine citizen, who:

- Satisfies the definition of a PCA as provided in SPP 209
- Has acquired a **Temporary/Special Permit (TSP)** from the PRBoA under Sections 37 and 38 of R.A. 9266
- Is allowed by DOLE, BID, and other agencies to practice architecture in the Philippines
- Is a registered/licensed professional architect in his country of origin

### 5.4 Professional Architectural Consulting Services (PACS)

<Callout variant="note">

The rendering by a PCA or by a Consulting Architectural Firm (CAF/RAF), of **independent advice, extension of technical assistance and services**, as well as undertaking of activities requiring appropriate knowledge, skills, training and experience, recognized competence, integrity, and/or financial and logistical capability.

</Callout>


### 5.5 Consulting Architectural Firm (CAF)

An architectural corporation, association, group, or partnership duly registered with the **Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)** or a sole proprietorship registered with **DTI**, and likewise registered with the Commission/Board as a Registered Architectural Firm (RAF) to perform PACS.

### 5.6 Consortium or Association

A coalition of purely Filipino PCAs/RAFs, or Filipino PCAs/RAFs **in collaboration** with foreign professional consultants authorized to render consulting services. Note: the use of "Consortium" and "Association" may carry different **tax and legal implications** under Philippine law.

### 5.7 Multiplier

A factor that compensates the PCA for:

- Overhead costs of the office
- Fringe benefits and social charges
- Fee for contingencies
- Interest on capital reserves
- Profit

The multiplier **varies** according to the types of architectural consulting work, the organization and experience of the PCA, and the geographic area in which the office is located.

### 5.8 Salary Cost

The cost of salaries (including leave pay and incentive pay) of professional consultants for time directly chargeable to projects, **plus excise, payroll taxes, and contributions** for social security and insurance benefits.

### 5.9 Direct Costs / Reimbursable Expenses

Expenses in connection with the project that may include:

- Living and travelling expenses when away from the home office
- Identifiable communication expenses (telephone, internet, cable, postage)
- Special legal and accounting expenses; computer rental; special consultants; borings; laboratory charges
- Identifiable supplies and materials charged to the project
- Reproduction costs (blueprinting, printing, etc.)

## 6. Scope of PACS

The scope of PACS shall be defined in accordance with the charter, by-laws, policies, rules, and regulations of the Commission and the Board through the IAPOA. It includes — **but is not limited to** — the following 16 service types:

<ComparisonTable>

| No. | PACS Service |
|---|---|
| 6.1 | **Program/project conceptualization and development** |
| 6.2 | **Rendering of technical advice, consultation, and/or counselling** |
| 6.3 | **Preparation of schematic/concept-level through preliminary plans, drawings, designs and specifications** |
| 6.4 | **Teaching, lecturing, coaching, mentoring** |
| 6.5 | **Research and Development (R&D)** |
| 6.6 | **Documentation** |
| 6.7 | **Conduct of pre-investment/pre-feasibility and feasibility studies** |
| 6.8 | **Marketing and promotional studies** |
| 6.9 | **Land use and multi-sectoral development planning, development and management** |
| 6.10 | **Site selection, analyses, evaluation, ranking and development** |
| 6.11 | **Construction** |
| 6.12 | **Project/Construction Management and/or Administration** |
| 6.13 | **Post-construction evaluation** |
| 6.14 | **Monitoring and evaluation** |
| 6.15 | **Training, capability building and Continuing Professional Education (CPE)** |
| 6.16 | **Capital Investment Programming** |

</ComparisonTable>


Note that several of these PACS services overlap with specific SPP documents (e.g., 6.7 overlaps with SPP 201, 6.11–6.12 overlap with SPP 207/204-B, 6.13 overlaps with SPP 205). Under SPP 209, these services are provided in a **consulting capacity** — advisory, technical assistance, or developmental — rather than as a direct Architect-of-Record engagement.

## 7. Qualifications of PCAs

A PCA must satisfy **all five** of the following qualifications:

<ComparisonTable>

| # | Qualification | Natural Person (FPCA) | Juridical Person (CAF/RAF) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **7.1** | Citizenship | Philippine citizen | N/A |
| **7.1** | License | RLA with valid PRC ID card | N/A |
| **7.1** | IAPOA membership | Member in good standing | N/A |
| **7.2** | Corporate registration | N/A | SEC- or DTI-registered; valid RAF Commission certificate |
| **7.3** | Experience | Minimum years of active and relevant professional training in the chosen field/s of specialization (as determined by IAPOA and PRBoA) | Same requirement applies |
| **7.4** | IAPOA certification | Endorsed and certified by IAPOA as member in good standing | Same requirement applies |
| **7.5** | Clean record | Never convicted of any criminal or administrative offense related to deliberate wrongdoing | Same requirement applies |

</ComparisonTable>

## 8. Selection of PCAs

SPP 209 prescribes a **qualifications-based selection (QBS) framework** — emphasizing competence over cost as the primary selection criterion for both Government and private sector clients.

### 8.1 Primary Selection Criteria

Clients shall consider the following criteria in the selection of PCAs:

**a. Professional Competence and Experience**
- Track record of successfully completed projects of similar type and complexity
- Qualifications and competence of assigned professional staff
- Demonstrated experience in the geographic area of the project
- Management capability

**b. Personal and Organizational Integrity**
- Financial capability and stability of the PCA or firm
- Reputation for reliability and adherence to professional ethics
- No conflict of interest with the client's project

**c. Appreciation of Client's Requirements**
- Understanding of the technical scope and objectives of the project
- Ability to work within the client's institutional and administrative framework

**d. Quality of Technical Proposal**
- Responsiveness to Terms of Reference (ToR)
- Innovation and soundness of the proposed methodology
- Clarity and logic of the work plan

### 8.2 Government Projects: GPRA Framework

For **Government projects**, the selection of PCAs shall be **in accordance with R.A. No. 9184** (Government Procurement Reform Act) and its IRR. The key principle is **quality-based selection (QBS)** — quality and technical competence must take precedence over price in the selection of consultants.

<Callout variant="note">

*"It shall be mandatory on the part of the Client, whether in Government, private sector or CSO or the international community, to give priority to Filipino Professional Consultants (FPCs) over foreign consultants in the selection, commissioning and engagement of PCAs for projects on Philippine soil, in accordance with applicable existing laws."*

</Callout>


### 8.3 Priority Rule: Filipino PCAs First

The mandatory priority rule for clients (both government and private) is:

1. **First priority**: Filipino PCAs (FPCAs) or Filipino consulting firms (RAFs)
2. **Second priority**: Filipino-foreign consortium/association with Filipino majority
3. **Third priority**: Foreign consultants (FAs/FCs) with TSP — only when qualified Filipino PCAs are not available or when the project specifically requires foreign expertise not available locally

## 9. Method of Compensation

SPP 209 prescribes **five primary compensation methods** for PACS, with reimbursable expenses always paid by the Client in addition to the professional fee:

### 9.1 Per Diem or Hourly Basis

**Best for**: Projects requiring intermittent personal service or occurring in irregular periods of time; includes travel time.

**Formula**:

\[
\text\{Fee\} = (A \times H_A) + (C \times H_C) + (T \times H_T)
\]

Where:
- \(A\) = Architect's rate per hour
- \(H_A\) = Number of hours spent by the Architect
- \(C\) = Consultant's rate per hour
- \(H_C\) = Number of hours spent by Consultants
- \(T\) = Rate per hour of Technical Staff, Researchers and others
- \(H_T\) = Number of hours spent by Technical Staff

The multiplier ranges from **1 to 2** depending on the office set-up, overhead, experience of the architect, and the complexity of the services. The total fee is then added with the cost of all reimbursables (transportation, living allowance, housing, etc.) required for the professionals assigned to the project.

### 9.2 Retainer

**Best for**: Ongoing consulting relationships where the client requires regular availability of the PCA's expertise — not tied to a single project but to an ongoing advisory relationship.

The retainer fee covers the PCA's **availability and response commitment** — not a fixed volume of work. Additional compensation may be negotiated for work beyond the retainer scope.

### 9.3 Salary Cost × Multiplier + Direct Cost / Reimbursable Expenses

**Best for**: Projects with costs and time constraints that are difficult to determine at the onset.

**Formula**:

\[
\text\{Fee\} = (\text\{Salary Cost\} \times \text\{Multiplier\} \geq 3.0) + \text\{Reimbursables\}
\]

The method is based on the **total basic salaries (Direct Salary Expenses)** of all PCAs and their staff, multiplied by **a factor from 3.0**, plus cost of reimbursable expenses. The multiplier of minimum 3.0 covers:

- Overhead (office expenses, depreciation, vehicles, taxes, insurance, etc.)
- Fringe benefits and social charges
- Contingency fee
- Interest on capital reserves
- Profit

### 9.4 Lump Sum / Fixed Fee

**Best for**: Projects where the scope of services can be clearly and fully defined from the beginning.

Critical requirements for this method:

- The client and the PCA must agree on: Scope of project; Design and approval schedules; Construction schedules; Other variables crucial to the project
- A **time limit for the performance of services** must be included in the service agreement to prevent scope creep
- **Additional compensation is required** for changes required by the client beyond the agreed scope
- The fee is an **appropriate percentage of the total project/construction cost**, with an estimation of the direct cost plus a reasonable margin of profit

### 9.5 Percentage of Project Construction Cost (PCC)

**Best for**: PACS engagements that are directly tied to the scale and complexity of a specific construction project, such as Project Management or Construction Management services provided in a consulting capacity.

The percentage rate varies depending on the type of PACS, the project group classification (per SPP 202), and the agreed scope of services.

### Compensation Methods Summary

<ComparisonTable>

| Method | Best Application | Key Formula/Rate |
|---|---|---|
| **Per Diem/Hourly** | Intermittent, irregular services | (Rates × Hours) × Multiplier (1–2) + Reimbursables |
| **Retainer** | Ongoing advisory relationships | Fixed monthly/quarterly retainer sum |
| **Salary Cost × Multiplier** | Variable-scope, time-sensitive projects | Salary Cost × ≥3.0 + Reimbursables |
| **Lump Sum** | Well-defined, fixed-scope projects | % of PCC + Direct Cost + Profit Margin |
| **% of PCC** | Project-tied consulting services | Variable % per project type and group |

</ComparisonTable>

## 10. Owner's/Client's Responsibilities

SPP 209 places **specific duties** on clients engaging PCAs:

**a. Qualifications-Based Selection Priority**
The Client shall give **priority to FPCAs** over foreign consultants and shall not make decisions solely on the basis of the lowest fee — competence, integrity, and experience must be the primary criteria.

**b. Provide Complete and Accurate Project Information**
The Client shall provide all information necessary for the PCA to render his services effectively — including program of requirements, budget constraints, site information, and regulatory requirements.

**c. Timely Decision-Making**
The Client shall respond promptly to the PCA's requests for approvals, data, decisions, and instructions to avoid delays in the consulting engagement.

**d. Pay All Reimbursable Expenses**
All direct costs and reimbursable expenses — including living and travel allowances for foreign consultants, out-of-town allowances for local consultants, identifiable communication expenses, and reproduction costs — are to be **charged to and paid by the Client**, in addition to the professional fee.

**e. Register and Commission Filipino PCAs First**
When qualified Filipino PCAs are available and capable, the Client **must not engage foreign consultants** — doing so without proper justification violates both SPP 209 and the policy provisions of R.A. 9266.

## 11. Special Conditions and Other Provisions

### 11.1 Foreign Consultants: Three-Condition Framework

Foreign Architects (FAs) or Foreign Consultants (FCs) may provide PACS on Philippine soil only if they satisfy all three conditions:

1. **TSP (Temporary/Special Permit)**: Must have a valid TSP from the PRBoA under Sections 37 and 38 of R.A. 9266 and pertinent Board Resolutions
2. **DOLE/BID clearance**: Must be allowed by DOLE, BID, and other concerned regulatory agencies to practice architecture in the Philippines
3. **Local counterpart collaboration**: Must work **in collaboration with a local/Filipino counterpart RLA** who assumes the requisite professional responsibilities and civil liabilities — in the case of a design or design-build competition

### 11.2 The Non-Qualified Entity (NQE) Clause

A critical protection clause in SPP 209:

<Callout variant="note">

Any individual, partner, firm/corporation/consortium who engages in PACS but is **not qualified** in accordance with Section 7 (Qualifications) shall be considered a **Non-Qualified Entity (NQE)** and is **prohibited** from providing PACS.

</Callout>


The NQE clause directly addresses the "proliferation of entities projecting themselves as consultants" without proper credentials — a key concern that triggered the 2010 revision.

### 11.3 Intellectual Property Rights

All consulting outputs — plans, designs, reports, studies, specifications, and other documents produced by the PCA — are protected by **R.A. No. 8293** (Intellectual Property Code of the Philippines). The PCA retains copyright over all original work unless explicitly transferred by written agreement.

### 11.4 Compliance with R.A. 9184 (GPRA)

For government consulting engagements, all procedures for selection, procurement, and contracting shall be fully compliant with R.A. No. 9184 and its latest IRR. The **Qualifications-Based Selection (QBS)** method under GPRA is the preferred mode for procuring consulting services.

### 11.5 Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR)

All consulting agreements under SPP 209 shall include an ADR clause grounded in **R.A. No. 9285** (Alternative Dispute Resolution Act of 2004). Conciliation, mediation, or arbitration must **precede** any form of litigation.

## 13. Practical Notes for Architects and Clients

**For Architects:**

- **IAPOA membership in good standing** is a non-negotiable qualification for PACS — ensure membership fees, CPE requirements, and professional registration renewals are current before undertaking any consulting engagement.
- When working with **foreign consultants**, ensure that the TSP is valid, the DOLE work permit is in order, and the collaboration agreement clearly defines that the Filipino PCA assumes all professional responsibilities and civil liabilities for any signed and sealed architectural documents.
- The **Salary Cost × Multiplier method** with a minimum multiplier of 3.0 is the most transparent compensation method for complex, variable-scope engagements — it makes overhead, profit, and contingency visible to the client and reduces the risk of fee disputes.
- The **Non-Qualified Entity (NQE) prohibition** is a tool Filipino PCAs should actively invoke when competing against unqualified consultants for government or private sector engagements.

**For Government and Private Sector Clients:**

- The **priority rule (Filipino PCAs first)** is not merely a professional preference — it is grounded in constitutional policy (Art. XII, Sec. 14) and in the objectives of R.A. 9266. Engaging foreign consultants without proper justification exposes the client to legal risk and undermines national policy.
- **Qualifications-Based Selection (QBS)** — not lowest-bid selection — is the legally mandated approach for both government (R.A. 9184) and private sector clients. The investment in a more qualified PCA consistently yields better project outcomes than the savings from choosing the cheapest option.
- The **reimbursable expenses obligation** (Section 10d) means the total cost of a PACS engagement is the professional fee **plus** all documented direct costs. Clients should request a detailed breakdown of anticipated reimbursables at the start of the engagement.
