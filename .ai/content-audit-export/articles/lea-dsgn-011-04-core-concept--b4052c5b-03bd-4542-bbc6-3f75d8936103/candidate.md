## Board Exam Quick Check

For perspective-drawing questions in this batch, anchor the answer in Philippine document requirements before using general drawing theory. The checked local sources support perspective drawings as architectural documents and building-plan components.

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

RA 9266 IRR includes perspective drawings among architectural documents and instruments of service that only an architect can sign and seal when prepared within the architect's scope. The same definition lists vicinity maps, site development plans, architectural programs, floor plans, elevations, sections, ceiling plans, schedules, detailed drawings, technical specifications, and cost estimates.

PD 1096 RIRR requires architectural documents for building plans/construction drawings. It specifically lists a perspective drawn at a convenient scale and taken from a vantage point, either bird's eye view or eye level. The same list also requires floor plans, elevations, sections, and reflected ceiling plans with technical information.

Therefore, a perspective is a presentation and documentation view. It helps communicate the proposed building visually, but it does not replace the scaled plans, elevations, sections, schedules, specifications, and other documents used for code and construction verification.

## Learning Goal

- Identify perspective drawings as architectural documents under RA 9266 IRR.
- Recognize the PD 1096 RIRR requirement for a perspective view in building plans/construction drawings.
- Avoid relying on vanishing-point theory unless the question or source supplies that drawing-theory basis.

## Core Concept

The source-safe LEA answer is documentary: a perspective drawing is part of the architectural-document package. It communicates form and vantage point, while the rest of the drawing set carries dimensions, room identities, material marks, specification callouts, and construction information.

| Drawing/document | Source-safe role |
| --- | --- |
| Perspective | Visual view from bird's-eye or eye-level vantage point. |
| Floor plan | Scaled plan with room or functional-space identification. |
| Elevation | Exterior view with gridlines, heights, marks, and finishes. |
| Section | Cut view showing levels, structural outlines, openings, partitions, and functional spaces. |
| Schedule/specification | Coordinated document data that supports construction and contract documents. |

When an item asks what perspective contributes, answer visual communication and architectural-document completeness. When it asks what proves dimensions or compliance, look to scaled drawings and specifications.

## How It's Tested on the LEA

Expect questions that ask whether a perspective is enough for approval or code verification. The safest answer is no: it is part of the architectural-document set, but it must be coordinated with plans, elevations, sections, schedules, specifications, and required permit documents.

Expect questions about vantage point. PD 1096 RIRR supports bird's-eye and eye-level perspectives; choose the answer that matches the required visual communication rather than unsupported drawing-taxonomy claims.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating a perspective as a substitute for scaled plans, elevations, or sections.
- Assuming perspective drawings are informal presentation art rather than architectural documents.
- Answering with one-point/two-point/three-point theory when the source-backed issue is documentation and permit completeness.
- Forgetting that signed/sealed architectural documents carry architect-of-record responsibility.

## Key Terms

**Perspective Drawing** - A visual architectural document drawn from a selected vantage point to communicate the proposed building.

**Bird's-Eye View** - A perspective vantage point looking from above, named in PD 1096 RIRR.

**Eye-Level View** - A perspective vantage point from typical observer level, named in PD 1096 RIRR.

**Architectural Document** - An architect's output under RA 9266 IRR, including perspective drawings and other instruments of service.
