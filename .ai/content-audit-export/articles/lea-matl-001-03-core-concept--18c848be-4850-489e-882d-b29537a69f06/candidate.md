## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Wood connection questions should be answered from the joint or connector behavior stated in the source, not from unsupported connector capacities. Batch 082 limits the article to source-audited concepts: joint geometry, connector type, seasoning effects, plywood edge-fastening behavior, and moisture-appropriate glue selection.

## Core Concept

Building Construction Illustrated groups joint behavior by how members meet. A butt joint is the simplest contact and usually needs a mediating element such as a connector or fastener. An overlapping joint allows members to bypass each other and transfer force through the overlap. Molded or shaped joints form the connection through the geometry of the pieces.

Connector behavior also follows the source vocabulary. A bolt is a point connector, glue is a surface connector, and a weld is a linear connector in the general connection taxonomy. Pinned joints allow rotation, rigid or fixed joints maintain the angular relationship of connected members, and roller joints allow movement for expansion or contraction.

Wood material condition still matters. Seasoned lumber is more stable than green lumber, with less shrinkage and warping and better nail-holding behavior. Quartersawn lumber has less shrinkage and distortion than plainsawn lumber, which is why it is safer where dimensional stability matters. George Salvan's materials source also supports plywood's resistance to edge splitting under nails or screws and distinguishes glue families by water and humidity resistance.

## How It Is Tested

Expect identification questions: name the joint behavior, choose the connector type, or reject an unsupported capacity claim. If a question asks for a wet or humid exposure adhesive, do not choose a glue identified as poor under high humidity. If a question asks why green lumber is risky in a connection, focus on shrinkage, warping, and reduced nail-holding rather than invented strength numbers.

## Common Mistakes

Do not memorize unverified nail, bolt, split-ring, or hanger capacities for this cluster. Do not assume all glued joints are water-resistant. Do not treat a butt joint as a shaped mechanical joint unless a separate connector or reinforcement is provided.
