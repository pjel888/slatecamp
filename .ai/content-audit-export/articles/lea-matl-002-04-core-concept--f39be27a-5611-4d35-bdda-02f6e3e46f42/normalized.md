## Core Concept

Stone is treated in the checked sources as one of the basic building materials and is now used largely as a surface finishing material for exterior and interior building work. Common architectural stones named in the Masonry Lecture include granite, limestone, sandstone, marble, slate, and travertine.

Building stones may be classified by geological origin. Granite is igneous and is described by Salvan as generally hard, strong, durable, polishable, and suitable for flooring, wall paneling, column and mullion facings, stair treads, and flagstone. Limestone and sandstone are sedimentary stones. Marble and slate are metamorphic stones; marble is recrystallized limestone or dolomite, while slate separates into thin sheets.

The commercial form matters. Rubble is rough or irregular stone. Dimension stone is cut or finished to specified sizes and thicknesses. Flagstone is a flat slab used for walks and floors. Crushed stone is used as aggregate in concrete products.

Stonework vocabulary is also tested. Rubble work uses rough, undressed stones. Random work fits stones without deliberate course lines. Ashlar uses squared stones in regular courses; coursed ashlar keeps uniform courses, while broken-range ashlar varies course height or stone length while keeping horizontal joints.

## How It Is Tested

Expect identification items: stone type by origin, rubble versus dimension stone, rubble work versus ashlar, and basic qualities that make stone suitable for construction. If a question asks for exact ASTM limits, production percentages, or brand-specific quarry data, treat those as outside this Batch 083 source scope unless the exam item supplies its own source.

## Common Mistakes

Do not use hardness, strength, porosity, or local quarry claims unless those values are supplied by the question or source. Do not confuse geological origin with commercial form: granite describes origin and composition, while rubble, dimension stone, and flagstone describe how the stone is supplied or used.
