## What You Need to Know for the LEA
## Learning Goal

Identify natural stone types (granite, marble, limestone, sandstone, slate) by their geological origin, physical properties, durability, and appropriate architectural applications in the Philippine context.
## Core Concept

Natural stone (granite, marble, limestone, sandstone, slate, travertine) offers superior durability, fire resistance, and aesthetic qualities compared to manufactured units. However, each stone type has different strength, hardness, and durability characteristics requiring careful material selection. Stone masonry is typically used as veneer or cladding rather than as primary load-bearing structure in modern construction.

## Stone Types and Properties

### Granite (ASTM C615)

Granite is an igneous rock composed of quartz, feldspar, and mica. It is the hardest natural building stone (Mohs 6—7) and offers excellent durability and weather resistance.

**ASTM C615 Specifications:**
- Compressive strength: Minimum 19,000 psi (131 MPa) typical; many granite specimens exceed 30,000 psi
- Water absorption: Maximum 0.4% by weight
- Modulus of rupture (bending strength): Minimum 1,500 psi (10.3 MPa)
- Abrasion resistance: Low wear on polished surfaces under standard testing

**Applications:** Exterior cladding, monuments, building facings, interior countertops, flooring.

**Advantages:** Superior hardness, weather resistance, low absorption, long service life (100+ years).

**Disadvantages:** High cost, heavy (approximately 170 lb/ft³), requires skilled installation.

### Marble (ASTM C503)

Marble is a metamorphic rock (recrystallized limestone) composed of calcite and dolomite minerals. It is softer than granite (Mohs 3—4) and is prized for interior applications and sculpture.

**ASTM C503 Specifications:**
- Compressive strength: Minimum 2,000 psi (13.8 MPa) for some marbles; premium marbles exceed 10,000 psi
- Water absorption: Maximum 0.75% by weight
- Modulus of rupture: Minimum 1,000 psi (6.9 MPa)
- Acid resistance: Variable; acidic solutions and vinegar etch the surface

**Applications:** Interior flooring, countertops, wall cladding, sculpture, thermal mass in buildings.

**Advantages:** Beautiful aesthetic (white, pink, black, veined varieties), moderate cost compared to granite, workable.

**Disadvantages:** Softer than granite; susceptible to etching by acidic substances; higher water absorption; not recommended for exterior exposure in wet climates.

### Limestone (ASTM C568)

Limestone is a sedimentary rock composed primarily of calcite (CaCO₃). It is softer than granite and marble (Mohs 2—4) but is economical and available in large quantities.

**ASTM C568 Specifications:**
- Compressive strength: Minimum 2,000 psi (13.8 MPa) for selected types; varies widely (2,000—10,000 psi)
- Water absorption: Maximum 3% by weight (significantly higher than granite or marble)
- Modulus of rupture: Minimum 500 psi (3.4 MPa)

**Applications:** Exterior and interior cladding, building facings, acoustic panels (interior use), landscaping.

**Advantages:** Economical, lighter than granite, available in large slabs, warm aesthetic.

**Disadvantages:** Soft (susceptible to scratching and abrasion); high water absorption promotes freeze-thaw damage in cold climates; susceptible to acid etching; weather-resistant only in dry climates.

### Sandstone

Sandstone is a sedimentary rock composed of quartz and feldspar grains cemented by silica or iron oxide. Hardness varies (Mohs 2—3) with grain size and cement strength.

**Properties:**
- Compressive strength: 2,000—4,000 psi (typically lower than limestone)
- Water absorption: 5—15% (higher than limestone, making it unsuitable for wet exposures)
- Availability: Regional; primarily in certain geographic areas (U.S. Midwest, Australia)

**Applications:** Exterior veneer in arid climates, interior features, artistic cladding.

**Limitations:** High water absorption; poor freeze-thaw resistance; not suitable for exposed exterior use in tropical or humid climates like the Philippines.

### Slate

Slate is a metamorphic rock derived from shlea, composed of mica, quartz, and feldspar arranged in parallel sheets. It splits naturally along foliation planes into thin, durable sheets.

**Properties:**
- Hardness: Mohs 2—3 (relatively soft but splits cleanly)
- Water absorption: &lt;0.5% (lower than limestone, higher than granite)
- Compressive strength: 10,000—20,000 psi (higher than limestone despite softer Mohs rating)
- Freeze-thaw resistance: Good; used for roofing in cold climates for centuries

**Applications:** Roofing (traditional and contemporary), flooring, wall veneer, interior and exterior use.

**Advantages:** Excellent freeze-thaw resistance, beautiful cleft finish, long durability (50+ years roofing service life).

### Travertine

Travertine is a sedimentary rock (calcium carbonate) formed from hot springs and mineral-rich waters. It has a porous structure with visible holes and veins.

**Properties:**
- Compressive strength: 2,000—4,000 psi (variable; typically lower than limestone)
- Water absorption: 5—25% (very high; varies significantly by source)
- Hardness: Mohs 2—3

**Applications:** Interior flooring, wall veneer, exterior cladding in arid climates (not tropical).

**Limitations:** High porosity and water absorption make it unsuitable for wet or humid exposures. In the Philippines' tropical climate, travertine requires sealing and maintenance to prevent water damage and biological growth.

## Mohs Hardness Scale

The Mohs hardness scale (1—10) ranks minerals by scratch resistance:

1. Talc (softest)
2. Gypsum
3. Calcite (limestone, marble)
4. Fluorite
5. Apatite
6. Orthoclase feldspar
7. Quartz (granite component)
8. Topaz
9. Corundum
10. Diamond (hardest)

Mohs hardness does not directly correlate to compressive strength (which measures crushing resistance). A material can be hard (high Mohs rating) but weak (low compressive strength), or vice versa. However, higher Mohs hardness generally indicates better resistance to scratching and wear.

## Philippine Stone Resources

### Romblon Marble

Romblon Province (in the Visayas region) is the marble capital of the Philippines, producing approximately 80% of the country's marble. Romblon marble is renowned for its cloudy white appearance and is compared to Italian Carrara marble in quality.

**Varieties and colors:** White, beige, green, pink, black, and variegated patterns.

**Uses:** Both domestic (interior flooring, sculptures) and export (premium finishes, monumental work).

**Quarrying and production:** Mabini and Odiongan towns are the primary production centers, employing sustainable mining practices.

### Cebu Limestone

Cebu is a significant source of limestone for construction aggregates and decorative stone. Cebu limestone is lighter in color and more porous than Romblon marble, making it suitable for exterior cladding, landscaping, and interior panels with proper sealing.

**Uses:** Cladding, flooring (interior with sealing), decorative veneer.

**Characteristics:** Lighter cost than marble, available in large quantities, susceptible to acid etching and water damage if unfinished.

### Other Philippine Stone Sources

Regional sources of limestone, granite, and other stone exist in various Philippine provinces (Mindoro, Davao, Quezon). Availability is regional and not standardized.

## Stone Veneer vs. Solid Stone

### Stone Veneer

Thin-cut stone (typically 1—2 inches thick) applied over a backup wall (brick, concrete, or foam insulation). Veneer reduces weight and cost while maintaining aesthetic appeal.

**Advantages:** Lighter weight, lower material cost, easier installation, faster execution.

**Disadvantages:** Backup wall durability is critical; water management at interfaces is essential; some stone loss risk if anchoring fails.

**Installation:** Adhesive (mastic) or mechanical anchors (dowels, tabs) transfer load to backup wall.

### Solid Stone

Monolithic stone units (4—12 inches thick) used as the primary cladding or structural element.

**Advantages:** Maximizes stone properties; traditional aesthetic; long durability.

**Disadvantages:** Heavy (160—180 lb/ft³), expensive, requires strong backup structure, slow installation.

## ASTM Stone Standards Overview

- **ASTM C615:** Granite dimension stone (compressive strength minimum 19,000 psi)
- **ASTM C503:** Marble dimension stone (compressive strength minimum 2,000 psi)
- **ASTM C568:** Limestone dimension stone (compressive strength minimum 2,000 psi)
- **ASTM C629:** Slate dimension stone (no minimum; slate quality varies by layer)

All standards require water absorption, density, and durability testing. Finish (polished, split-faced, honed) may affect moisture and stain resistance.

## How It's Tested

The LEA expects you to:

1. Identify stone types from descriptions of hardness, density, and appearance
2. Match stones to appropriate applications based on hardness, absorption, and durability
3. Apply ASTM specifications for granite, marble, limestone, and slate
4. Explain why certain stones are unsuitable for tropical or humid exposures
5. Recognize Philippine stone resources (Romblon marble, Cebu limestone) and their characteristics
6. Compare stone veneer and solid stone construction
7. Determine whether stone requires sealing or protective measures based on water absorption and exposure

Typical questions present a building facade or interior finish application and ask which stone type is appropriate, or ask you to identify a stone based on hardness, color, and source.
