# BP 344 vs PD 1096 Conflicts

## Learning Goal

Read PD 1096 and BP 344 together: PD 1096 supplies the building-code and permit framework, while BP 344 supplies specific accessibility minimum requirements for persons with disabilities.

## PD 1096 Framework

The PD 1096 2004 Revised IRR states that the Code provides minimum standards and requirements for buildings and structures, regulating location, site, design, materials, construction, use, occupancy, and maintenance.

The IRR also states that applicable and consistent provisions of allied professional codes and other government agency codes approved by the DPWH Secretary serve as referral codes. The Building Official is responsible for carrying out the Code in the field, processing permits, issuing certificates, and ensuring that construction-phase design changes do not begin until evaluated and an amendatory permit is issued.

## Accessibility in Building Permit Review

The PD 1096 permit application requirements specifically include accessibility facilities such as accessible ramps, stairs, lifts/elevators, entrances, corridors, walkways, functional areas or comfort rooms, switches and controls, drinking fountains, public telephone booths, audio visual and automatic alarm systems, access symbols, directional signs, and reserved parking for disabled persons.

The same source requires plans and specific locations of accessibility facilities and detailed design of those facilities outside and around buildings or structures, including parking areas and safety requirements.

## BP 344 Specific Requirements

BP 344 2024 Revised IRR gives the accessibility details: for example, accessible ramps, tactile surfaces, accessible toilets, elevators, signage, alarms, seating, parking, and other minimum requirements.

When an exam question asks about an accessibility feature, do not use PD 1096 as an excuse to ignore BP 344. Use PD 1096 for the permit and enforcement framework, then use BP 344 for the specific accessibility dimensions or features.

## Practice Cue

If a general building-code provision and an accessibility provision appear to overlap, keep both in view. Confirm the permit framework under PD 1096, then apply the specific BP 344 accessibility rule to the accessible feature being designed or reviewed.
