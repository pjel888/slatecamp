## Foreign Architects in the Philippines

Foreign architects must secure authorization to practice in the Philippines through either temporary permits or special permits issued by the Board of Architecture. RA 9266 Section 38 establishes procedures for foreign nationals who have gained entry to perform professional services as architects or consultants. These permits are project-specific and limited in duration, preventing permanent foreign practice without local registration.

A temporary permit authorizes a foreign architect to work on a specific project for a defined duration connected to that project's completion. The permit application requires submission to the Board with supporting documentation including the architect's home country license, educational credentials, and project details. The permit specifies the project scope, authorized architectural activities, and the required Filipino counterpart or lead firm. Conditions ensure the foreign architect's work complies with Philippine standards and local regulatory requirements.

### Special Permits for Specialized Expertise

Temporary/Special Permits are issued only to individual foreign architects from countries with reciprocity and only for one project. These permits authorize foreign architects to engage in general practice of architecture or consulting roles for a specific project for a defined duration connected to that project's completion. Permit holders must work under a Filipino counterpart who assumes full professional responsibility and liability. The Board evaluates each application based on the foreign architect's credentials, project details, and reciprocity status. Permits are valid only for the permitted period and project and cannot be extended or renewed; a new application is required for subsequent projects. Conditions restrict the specialist to the authorized scope and require compliance with Philippine professional ethics and standards.

### The Endorsement Process and Requirements

Foreign architects seeking temporary permits must submit documentary proof or evidence that their home country allows Filipino architects to practice the profession without limitation or on terms of strict and absolute equality. In the absence of a bilateral agreement, the foreign national must provide this proof for Board evaluation to establish reciprocity. This requirement ensures that Filipino architects receive equivalent treatment in the foreign country.

Documentation requirements include the original or certified copy of official documents from the Bureau of Immigration and Deportation allowing entry and residence in the Philippines. Foreign applicants must present their passport for examination and photocopying of pertinent information. Original or authenticated transcripts of educational records and equivalent documentation of their architectural license from the home country, duly authorized or accredited by the government, are essential. The architect must provide other documents the Board requires for evaluation.

### Reciprocity Principles and Equality

RA 9266 Section 27 establishes reciprocity requirements ensuring foreign architects receive equal treatment when their countries extend equivalent rights to Filipino architects. A foreign citizen seeking licensure examination must prove their country admits citizens of the Philippines to practice the profession without restriction or allows them to take the examination on terms of strict and absolute equality. Unconditional recognition of Philippine prerequisite degrees is required from the home country.

Reciprocity operates on the principle of equal treatment—if the foreign country denies Filipino architects equivalent rights, the Philippines may restrict that country's nationals. This mechanism protects Philippine architects while facilitating legitimate international collaboration. The Board determines reciprocity status for each country and updates classifications as treaties or laws change. Filipino counterparts in joint ventures ensure local accountability even when reciprocity permits foreign participation.

### Limitations and Local Practice Requirements

Foreign architects cannot establish independent practices in the Philippines; they must always work through Filipino counterparts or employ Filipino architects in responsible positions. Marketing and advertising of architectural services by foreign firms must indicate they are foreign entities operating under Board authorization. Permanent licensing of foreign architects is not permitted under RA 9266; foreigners may only practice through temporary/special permits. This restriction ensures architectural practice remains controlled by Filipino professionals. Foreign architects cannot sign and seal architectural documents independently; the Filipino counterpart or responsible Philippine architect must authenticate all work. Project procurement laws often require Filipino participation, reinforcing the requirement that foreign architects work with local partners on Philippine construction projects.
