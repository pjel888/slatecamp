## What You Need to Know for the LEA
## Learning Goal

Classify construction equipment (excavators, cranes, concrete pumps, compactors) by function and application, and explain how equipment selection, site logistics, and material handling affect construction sequencing and project efficiency.
## Core Concept

Construction equipment delivers site materials, shapes terrain, lifts heavy loads, and consolidates materials. Proper equipment selection based on task, site constraints, and production rates optimizes construction schedules and reduces labor costs.

### Earthmoving Equipment

_Backhoe Loader_ — Versatile machine with rear bucket (excavation) and front loader bucket. Typical bucket: 0.5–1.0 m³. Used for foundation excavation, backfilling, light grading, material loading. Power: 50–100 kW. Common in Philippine small-to-medium projects due to cost and flexibility.

_Excavator (Hydraulic)_ — Heavy-duty machine with rotating arm and bucket. Bucket sizes: 0.5–3.0 m³. Used for deep excavation, large material removal, bulk earthwork. Power: 100–300 kW. Faster material movement than backhoe; requires level workspace. Dominates large projects and highway/bridge work.

_Bulldozer_ — Tracked machine with blade. Blade sizes: 3–5 m width. Used for site clearing, grading, pushing material. Power: 150–250 kW. Excellent for rough grading and site preparation. Not ideal for precise excavation.

_Motor Grader_ — Wheeled machine with central blade for fine grading and surface finishing. Blade: 3–4 m width. Used for road/runway construction, site drainage grading, finish work. Power: 150–250 kW. Essential for achieving required slopes and smoothness.

_Wheel Loader_ — Front-bucket loader on wheels. Bucket: 2–5 m³. Used for material loading, stockpile management, horizontal material movement. Power: 100–200 kW. Fast horizontal movement; not ideal for vertical lifting.

### Lifting Equipment

_Tower Crane_ — Vertical mast-mounted crane, stationary during project. Capacity: 10–100 tonnes. Reach: up to 60 m horizontal, 200+ m height. Standard for high-rise buildings. Loading capacity depends on boom length and hook height. Essential for high-rise residential/commercial in Philippine cities. Setup/dismantling: 1–2 weeks.

_Mobile Crane (Truck-Mounted)_ — Self-propelled crane on truck chassis. Capacity: 25–500 tonnes. Reach: 20–60 m. Flexible positioning; can relocate between sites. Used for short-duration work, erection of heavy structural elements. Cost is higher per day than tower cranes for long-duration projects.

_Concrete Pump (Truck-Mounted or Boom Pump)_ — Pumps concrete vertically/horizontally. Output: 30–100 m³/hour. Reach: 20–60+ m vertical, 100+ m horizontal. Essential for high-rise concrete placement. Reduces labor for concrete transport compared to bucket/conveyor methods.

_Conveyor System_ — Belt or inclined chute moves materials horizontally/upward. Output: 50–200 tonnes/hour. Used for aggregate/concrete transport to elevated locations when pumps are unavailable.

### Concrete Production & Placement Equipment

_Transit Mixer (Ready-Mix Truck)_ — Truck-mounted rotating drum carries fresh concrete. Capacity: 4–9 m³. Standard in modern Philippine construction for quality control and rapid delivery from central batching plants.

_Batch Plant (On-Site)_ — Produces concrete on-site from aggregate, cement, water. Output: 30–100 m³/hour. Used when remote sites lack ready-mix availability or volume justifies on-site production. Requires careful quality testing (slump, compressive strength) to maintain standards.

_Vibrator (Internal/External)_ — Consolidates concrete, removes air voids. Internal vibrators (poker vibrators) inserted into fresh concrete; external vibrators mounted on formwork. Essential for quality concrete with minimal defects.

### Compaction Equipment

_Plate Compactor (Vibratory)_ — Vibrating steel plate compacts subgrade, granular materials. Output: ~200–400 m³/day. Used for foundation preparation, embankment compaction. Power: 5–10 kW.

_Sheep's Foot Roller (Vibratory or Static)_ — Drum with protruding feet presses material. Used for cohesive soil (clay) compaction to required density. Removable feet reduce compaction depth for finish passes.

_Smooth Drum Roller (Vibratory)_ — Smooth drum compacts granular materials and asphalt. Used for final finish of embankments and pavement subgrades. Power: 50–150 kW.

## Philippine Construction Context

Philippine equipment rental companies (HERC, Dapco, local operators) supply earthmoving and lifting equipment throughout Metro Manila, provincial cities, and remote sites. Ready-mix concrete is available from suppliers like Golden Concrete, Lafarge, and regional plants in Laguna, Bulacan, and Quezon Province, reducing dependence on on-site batch plants.

Tower cranes are essential for high-rise residential/commercial in Manila, Cebu, and Davao. Rental costs (PHP 30,000–60,000/month) are offset by construction speed and labor savings for multi-story projects. Mobile cranes are used for heavy structural steel erection, bridge work, and temporary lifting where tower crane installation is uneconomical.

Concrete pumps are standard for buildings above 5–10 stories, improving placement speed and worker safety (vs. bucket hoisting). Environmental concerns (wastewater from pump washout) are managed per DENR guidelines and local ordinances.

## How It's Tested

The LEA evaluates equipment knowledge through:

1. _Equipment selection_ — Given a project task (e.g., "excavate 5,000 m³ of foundation for a 25-story building"), recommend appropriate equipment and justify.
2. _Production rates_ — Calculate daily or hourly output to estimate construction schedule impact.
3. _Site logistics_ — Identify space/access constraints and recommend equipment accordingly (tower crane vs. mobile crane, concrete pump reach, equipment staging areas).
4. _Environmental and safety considerations_ — Address noise, dust control, wastewater management, worker safety near equipment.
