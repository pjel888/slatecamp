## Core Concept

Non-ferrous metals are metallic elements or alloys that contain little or no iron. Unlike ferrous metals (iron-based), they do not corrode through rust oxidation in the same manner, making them ideal for exposed architectural elements, coastal environments, and wet areas. Aluminum dominates Philippine construction due to its lightweight, excellent corrosion resistance, ease of extrusion, and aesthetic versatility.

Aluminum alloys are designated by a four-digit system (XXXX) established by the Aluminum Association. The first digit identifies the alloy group, and the three subsequent digits distinguish individual alloys within that group.

### Aluminum Alloy Series (1xxx–7xxx)

_1xxx Series (Commercially Pure)_ — 99.0% purity aluminum. Used for electrical applications and decorative elements. Limited structural use.

_2xxx Series (Al-Cu)_ — Copper-bearing alloys. Higher strength, lower corrosion resistance. Rarely used in exposed outdoor architectural applications.

_3xxx Series (Al-Mn)_ — Manganese-bearing alloys. Good workability, moderate strength. Used in interior components and decorative trim.

_4xxx Series (Al-Si)_ — Silicon-bearing alloys. Excellent for casting and brazing. Limited extrusion use in architectural work.

_5xxx Series (Al-Mg)_ — Magnesium-bearing alloys. Superior corrosion resistance, high strength-to-weight ratio. Marine and coastal applications.

_6xxx Series (Al-Mg-Si)_ — Magnesium-silicon alloys. **The most common for architectural extrusions**. Alloy 6063 dominates window frames, curtain wall systems, door frames, roofing systems, and visible trim throughout the Philippines.

_7xxx Series (Al-Zn)_ — Zinc-bearing alloys. Highest strength but lower corrosion resistance. Aerospace applications, rarely used in building construction.

### Alloy 6063: The Architectural Standard

Alloy 6063 provides exceptional extrudability—the ability to form complex shapes with smooth, highly anodizable surfaces. Ultimate tensile strength differs by temper: 6063-T5 achieves minimum 140 MPa with yield strength of 110 MPa, while 6063-T6 reaches minimum 190 MPa with yield strength of 160 MPa. T6 temper offers superior strength and is specified when higher loads or smaller cross-sections are required. T5 provides cost savings for non-structural or lightly loaded architectural members.

### Surface Finishing Systems

Unprotected aluminum oxidizes spontaneously, forming a thin, naturally adherent aluminum oxide layer that protects against further oxidation. However, this passive film is vulnerable to chloride attack in coastal environments and acidic/alkaline solutions in wet areas.

_Anodizing_ is an electrochemical process that accelerates and thickens the oxide film, creating a durable protective surface available in natural and dyed colors.

- **Type I (Chromic Acid Anodize)** — Produces thin (0.5-7.5 µm) oxide film per MIL-A-8625. Limited corrosion protection. Rarely specified for architectural work.
- **Type II (Sulfuric Acid Anodize)** — Produces medium thickness (5-25 µm) film per MIL-A-8625. Standard choice for architectural extrusions. Provides excellent corrosion resistance and aesthetic appearance in natural and dyed finishes.
- **Type III (Hard Coat Anodize)** — Produces thick (25-75 µm), extremely hard film per MIL-A-8625. Specified when enhanced abrasion resistance or high corrosion protection is critical, such as high-traffic areas or severe coastal/industrial environments.

_Powder Coating_ applies thermosetting polymers electrostatically and cures through heat. Superior adhesion, durability, color retention, and uniform coverage compared to spray painting. Commonly applied to aluminum architectural components in Philippine buildings to achieve custom colors and improved weather resistance.

### Non-Ferrous Metals in Philippine Construction

_Copper_ and its alloys appear as roofing material, gutters, downspouts, and decorative elements. Copper develops a protective patina (green or brown oxide) that provides long-term corrosion protection.

_Brass_ (copper-zinc alloy) and _Bronze_ (copper-tin alloy) are reserved for decorative hardware, door frames, and aesthetic trim.

_Zinc_ galvanizing protects ferrous steel. Pure zinc also serves specialized applications including roofing flashings and cladding.

_Lead_ is obsolete in new construction due to health hazards, though it may be encountered in renovations of older structures.

## Philippine Construction Context

Philippine suppliers including Aluminium Bahay, Hydro Philippines, and regional fabricators stock 6063-T5 and T6 extrusions for curtain wall systems, sliding window frames, and entrance systems. Coastal regions and high-humidity lowland areas demand Type II or Type III anodizing to prevent corrosion failure within the 50-year service life expected by NSCP 2015 durability provisions.

Aluminum alloy curtain wall systems manufactured to ASTM B221 (Standard Specification for Aluminum and Aluminum-Alloy Extruded Bars, Rods, and Profiles) dominate mid-rise residential and commercial construction. Pre-fabricated modular systems from suppliers like Phividec-based manufacturers simplify installation and ensure consistent material certification.

Copper roofing and brass hardware appear in heritage structures and premium projects. Understanding alloy composition prevents galvanic corrosion when dissimilar metals—such as aluminum framing adjoining copper roofing or stainless steel fasteners connecting aluminum members—contact each other in wet environments.

## Galvanic Corrosion

When two dissimilar metals contact in the presence of an electrolyte (water containing dissolved salts or minerals), electron flow between metals creates a galvanic cell. The more active metal (anodic) corrodes preferentially while the less active metal (cathodic) remains protected. The _galvanic series_ ranks metals by activity.

In Philippine coastal projects, aluminum window frames in contact with stainless steel fasteners or copper gutters risk accelerated corrosion at the interface. Prevention requires isolation using EPDM gaskets, nylon washers, or non-conductive spacers. Sealants like polyurethane or silicone prevent water entrapment at joints.

## How It's Tested

The LEA evaluates non-ferrous metals knowledge through:

1. _Alloy identification and temper selection_ — Given a project requirement (e.g., "architectural extrusion for a thermally broken curtain wall system"), select appropriate alloy and temper.
2. _Finishing system specification_ — Match anodizing type to environmental severity and expected service life.
3. _Corrosion prevention_ — Identify galvanic risks when dissimilar metals connect and specify isolation methods.
4. _Philippine code alignment_ — Confirm material selections against NSCP 2015 Sections 1402–1405 (Aluminum Structures) and durability criteria in Section 402.
