## Core Concept

Non-ferrous metals contain no iron. European Building Construction Illustrated lists aluminium, copper, and lead as non-ferrous metals commonly used in building construction. The Metals Lecture also covers aluminum, copper, tin, zinc, brass, bronze, chromium, nickel, and lead.

Aluminum is light, nonmagnetic, thermally and electrically conductive, and naturally forms a transparent aluminum oxide film that gives corrosion resistance. It is subject to galvanic action and should be insulated from direct contact with incompatible metals. It can be rolled, extruded, forged, pressed, drawn, stamped, bent, riveted, bolted, welded, brazed, and soldered. In architecture, the checked source lists rod, bar, extrusion, casting, sheet, strip, doors and windows, panels, sandwich panels, ornamental shapes, mesh, and wire cloth. Anodized finishes are electrolytic finishes that thicken or form a protective oxide coating on aluminum.

Copper is ductile, malleable, highly conductive, and resistant to corrosion by air and salt water. It forms a green patina. The checked sources caution that copper can drive galvanic corrosion of aluminum, steel, stainless steel, and zinc when contact and moisture allow current flow.

Zinc is important as the protective coating in galvanizing steel or iron. If the coating is damaged, galvanic reaction can cause the zinc to corrode sacrificially while continuing to protect the iron or steel as long as zinc remains. Brass is primarily a copper-zinc alloy used for windows, railings, trim, finish hardware, and miscellaneous accessories. True bronze is a copper-tin alloy; the source notes that architectural bronze may be a leaded brass in commercial usage.

## How It Is Tested

Expect classification and caution items: identify non-ferrous metals, select aluminum uses and finishes, explain galvanic separation, identify zinc galvanizing, and distinguish brass from bronze. If an item asks for a specific alloy number or temper, use only source supplied by that item.

## Common Mistakes

Do not call a metal corrosion-proof simply because it is non-ferrous. Do not place aluminum or brass directly against incompatible metals in wet conditions without considering galvanic action. Do not infer specific alloy grades from the general source descriptions.
