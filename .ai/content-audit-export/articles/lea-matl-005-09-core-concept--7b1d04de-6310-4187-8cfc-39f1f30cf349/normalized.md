# Waste reduction starts with source reduction before reuse, recycling, recovery, and disposal

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

RA 9003 anchors waste questions in source reduction, segregation, re-use, recycling, recovery, and environmentally sound disposal. Construction debris is included in the Act's solid-waste definition, so building projects should treat construction waste as a planned material-management issue rather than an afterthought.

## Core Concept

- RA 9003 sets policy for solid waste avoidance and volume reduction through source reduction and waste minimization before collection, treatment, and disposal.
- Re-use means recovering materials for the same or a different purpose without altering their physical and chemical characteristics.
- Recycling means treating used or waste materials so they become suitable for beneficial use or new products.
- Segregation and segregation at source separate materials to promote recycling and re-use and reduce collection and disposal volume.
- Solid waste includes construction debris unless excluded as hazardous or mining waste.
- Green Building Illustrated adds that construction waste management should reduce waste to avoid filling landfills, improve material-use efficiency, protect materials before use, and plan up front for recycling or reuse.

## How It Is Tested

- Apply source reduction before reuse, recycling, recovery, or disposal.
- Distinguish re-use from recycling.
- Identify segregation at source as a requirement for successful diversion.
- Check that a waste plan includes material types, facilities, markets, and tracking instead of assuming all sorted waste will be recycled.

## Common Mistakes

- Do not confuse source reduction with recycling.
- Do not count contaminated or mixed waste as recyclable without a suitable process.
- Do not cite unsupported local recycling rates or facility availability.
