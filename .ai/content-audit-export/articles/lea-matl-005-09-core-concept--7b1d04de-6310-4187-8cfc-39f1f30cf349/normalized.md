## What You Need to Know for the LEA
## Learning Goal

Explain life cycle assessment (LCA) as a methodology for evaluating environmental impacts of building materials from cradle to grave, and identify how LCA results inform green building certifications (BERDE, LEED, EDGE).
## Core Concept

Waste reduction follows a hierarchy: reduce (source elimination), reuse (whole-component reuse), recycle (material reprocessing), recover (energy recovery), dispose (final placement). Construction waste arises from three sources: demolition of existing structures, site clearing, and construction process losses (cutting, breakage, spoilage, errors). Lean construction principles minimize waste through just-in-time material ordering, prefabrication assembly, and accurate quantity takeoffs.

Building Information Modeling (BIM) enables material optimization by calculating exact quantities before fabrication, reducing off-cuts and waste. Prefabrication in controlled factory settings reduces on-site breakage and moisture damage compared to field construction. RA 9003 (Ecological Solid Waste Management Act) and DENR EMB (Environmental Management Bureau) requirements mandate waste management plans on projects exceeding specified scales.

RA 9003 (Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000) establishes waste management responsibilities for local government units (LGUs) and project generators. Covered projects must prepare waste management plans addressing source reduction, segregation, and final disposal. DENR EMB (Environmental Management Bureau) environmental compliance certificate (ECC) approval requires demonstration that construction waste will be managed per RA 9003 protocols.

Lean construction principles reduce waste through: value stream mapping (identify non-value-adding activities), just-in-time ordering (minimize stockpile spoilage and moisture damage), last planner scheduling (ensure material arrival aligns with installation), and defect elimination (reduce rework). International studies show lean construction can reduce material waste by 20-30 percent compared to conventional methods; Philippine-specific data on waste reduction efficacy in local construction contexts remains limited.

Prefabrication of building components (floor systems, wall panels, roof trusses) in factory settings achieves waste reduction through: precision cutting (CNC machinery reduces off-cuts), assembly line processes (minimize handling damage), and quality control inspection before delivery. Prefab components also reduce on-site labor waste and weather-related damage. BIM quantification enables prefab suppliers to calculate exact panel sizes and connector counts, eliminating over-ordering.

Building Information Modeling (BIM) integrates architectural, structural, MEP, and cost dimensions, enabling clash detection (identify conflicts before construction) and material quantification (calculate exact volumes of concrete, steel, drywall by system). BIM-driven quantity takeoff is reported in international studies to reduce error-induced material over-ordering by 5-15 percent; adoption in Philippine construction is increasing but sector-specific waste reduction data is limited.

## Philippine Construction Context

Metro Manila and other urban centers generate approximately 10,000-15,000 tonnes of C&D waste daily, of which less than 5 percent is currently recycled. Recent infrastructure failures (including the February 2026 Rizal Provincial Sanitary Landfill collapse) have exposed critical gaps in waste management capacity and safety protocols, affecting disposal destination reliability. RA 9003 mandates waste management plans for LGU enforcement, though compliance remains inconsistent. DENR EMB requires Environmental Compliance Certificate (ECC) submission including waste management protocols for projects exceeding specific thresholds (typically exceeding 20 hectares or significant environmental risk). However, enforcement capacity is constrained by limited budget allocation for environmental management (Php 349 million for solid waste management in 2026), which may affect ECC review rigor and compliance monitoring.

DPWH (Department of Public Works and Highways) specifications increasingly mandate waste management plans on public works projects; private developers pursuing BERDE certification must document waste reduction and segregation measures. Metro Manila Development Authority (MMDA) regulates C&D waste transport and disposal sites, with restrictions on burning or uncovered landfill placement.

Rice hull ash (byproduct of milling) and coconut husk (coconut processing waste) are being investigated as partial cement replacements and aggregate fillers, transforming agricultural waste into construction material. National government agencies (DOST, DENR) support pilot projects demonstrating waste-to-resource conversion in agricultural regions.

## How It's Tested

LEA candidates must identify C&D waste composition percentages, match reduction strategies to specific waste types (concrete crushing for reuse, metal segregation for recycling, wood chipping for mulch), and calculate waste reduction benefits of lean or prefabrication approaches. Questions present a construction scenario requiring a waste management plan and ask candidates to specify segregation methods, destination facilities, and cost-benefit analysis.

A second test approach asks candidates to evaluate a BIM-based material quantification against traditional method estimates, recognizing that BIM precision reduces over-ordering and material waste. Calculation questions may involve waste reduction percentages: if conventional construction generates 10 kg of waste per m² and lean construction reduces it by 25 percent, the target is 7.5 kg/m². Candidates must apply waste hierarchy logic: reduce first (source design changes), reuse second (component reuse), recycle third (material reprocessing).

## Common Mistakes

[SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK:ui_0005 component="WikiMistakeCard" status="preserved"]
Extracted summary:
- Props: title="Confusing Waste Reduction with Recycling", severity="critical"
- Text: Candidates often use "reduce" and "recycle" interchangeably. Reducing waste (source) is always preferable to recycling (end-of-life). If a prefabrication strategy eliminates 100 kg of concrete waste entirely, that is superior to generating 400 kg of waste and recycling 100 kg. Candidates must prioritize source reduction (design, prefab, lean construction) over end-of-life recycling in waste strategy recommendations.
