## Learning Goal

Explain reinforced concrete design principles — how steel reinforcement and concrete work compositely under tension and compression — and identify rebar grades, sizes, and detailing requirements (cover, spacing, hooks) per NSCP 2015.
## Core Concept

Reinforced concrete combines concrete (compressive strength) with steel reinforcement (tensile strength) to create a composite material resisting bending and tension loads. CIP construction involves building forms on-site, placing rebar, pouring concrete, and curing in place. Precast construction fabricates beams, columns, and wall panels in a plant, then transports and assembles them on-site.

### Cast-In-Place (CIP) Concrete Construction

_Formwork_ temporarily supports concrete until it achieves sufficient strength for self-support. Forms include:

- _Conventional Formwork_ — Timber decking and support beams (most common in Philippine construction, especially with coconut lumber). Labor-intensive but flexible for custom shapes. Form pressure on tall walls requires careful bracing.
- _System Formwork_ — Aluminum or steel panels with integral bracing reduce assembly labor and enable rapid form striking for high-rise projects. Capital cost is higher; economical for repetitive projects (high-rise residential, parking structures).
- _Slip-Form_ — Forms move continuously during concrete placement, suitable for tall structures with repetitive cross-sections (cylindrical cores, towers). Requires specialized equipment and expertise.

_Rebar Placement_ — Steel reinforcement is positioned per design drawings, maintaining concrete cover to protect steel from carbonation and corrosion. Per NSCP 2015 Section 402: cast against ground = 75mm; exposed to weather (20mm+ bars) = 50mm; exposed to weather (16mm and smaller) = 40interior (not exposed) = 40mm for beams/columns, 20-30mm for slabss. Rebar spacing controls crack widths. Improper placement (bunching, misaligned) reduces flexural capacity and increases cracking.

_Concrete Placement_ — Concrete is delivered by transit mixer (transit mixer concrete) or produced on-site (batch plant). Placement is by bucket (tower crane or conveyor pump for small projects) or concrete pump (preferred for large volumes). Vibration (internal vibrators or form-mounted external vibrators) consolidates concrete, removes air voids, and ensures proper rebar embedment. Over-vibration can cause segregation (coarse aggregate settling); under-vibration leaves voids and weak zones.

_Curing_ — Freshly placed concrete requires moisture and temperature control to develop strength. NSCP 2015 and ACI 318 specify minimum curing periods (typically 7 days for standard Portland cement) with maintained moisture (wet burlap, plastic sheeting, or curing compound spray). Strength gain follows a logarithmic curve: 28-day strength is the design reference, though strength continues to increase beyond.

### Precast Concrete Construction

Precast systems fabricate major structural elements (beams, columns, wall panels, hollow-core slabs) in a controlled plant environment, improving quality and enabling parallel production. On-site assembly accelerates construction and reduces weather dependency.

_Hollow-Core Slabs_ — Pretensioned units with internal voids (cores) reducing self-weight while maintaining stiffness. Typical span: 6–12 m. Lighter than CIP, faster to install, and produce cleaner soffits. Shear connections are typically mechanical (bolted) or grouted.

_Double-Tee (DT) Beams_ — Pretensioned units with T-shape cross-section. Typical span: 12–30 m. High strength-to-weight ratio. Installation requires temporary bracing until full connections are made.

_Columns_ — Precast square or rectangular columns with embedded socket connections for beam ends. Faster assembly than CIP; reduced formwork complexity on-site.

_Wall Panels_ — Large precast panels (e.g., 3–4 m wide × 4–6 m tall) form exterior cladding or load-bearing walls. Reduces on-site formwork significantly. Connections are typically bolted or grouted, requiring careful alignment.

### Precast Connections

_Wet Connections_ — Grouted connections between precast units and to supporting structures. Grout (cementitious) fills gaps, ensures bearing, and locks components together. Wet connections provide structural continuity similar to CIP monolithic behavior. Curing time for grout (typically 7–14 days) delays load application.

_Dry Connections_ — Bolted or welded connections without grout. Mechanical fasteners (bolts or welded steel plates) directly transfer loads. Dry connections permit immediate load application but require precise fabrication tolerances and careful alignment during installation. Inspection is more straightforward (no grout testing).

### Reinforced Concrete Specifications

Concrete strength is designated by compressive strength (e.g., f'c = 28 MPa per Philippine standards or 4000 psi per ASTM). Common grades in Philippine construction: 21, 24, 28, 35 MPa. Rebar grade: Grade 40 (fy = 275 MPa) and Grade 60 (fy = 420 MPa) per Philippine standards (equivalent to ASTM A615). Grade 60 is dominant in modern construction due to reduced steel volume and cost per meter.

## Philippine Construction Context

Philippine precast manufacturers (including plants in Bulacan, Cavite, and Laguna) produce hollow-core slabs, DT beams, and wall panels to NSCP 2015 standards. Suppliers like Pn. BetonPro, LRC, and regional fabricators provide catalog designs and custom solutions for residential, commercial, and infrastructure projects.

CIP construction remains prevalent in Manila and provincial cities due to labor cost advantages and site customization flexibility. Coconut (coco) lumber formwork dominates low-rise projects; aluminum/steel system forms appear in high-rise buildings where labor reuse favors standardized systems.

NSCP 2015 Chapter 4 (Reinforced Concrete) specifies minimum concrete strength (f'c), rebar coverage (typically 40 mm for beams/columns, 20-30 mm for slabs), and curing requirements aligned with ACI 318 (ACI 349 for special structures). Precast connections are governed by NSCP 2015 Section 416 and 419 (Connections and Anchors).

## How It's Tested

The LEA evaluates RC construction through:

1. _Formwork selection_ — Match formwork type to project scale, schedule, and reusability constraints.
2. _Rebar placement and cover_ — Specify cover based on exposure class (Section 402) and member type.
3. _Concrete specifications_ — Determine appropriate f'c based on member loads and durability requirements.
4. _Curing protocols_ — Select curing method aligned with climate (tropical rainy season vs. dry season) and schedule.
5. _Precast vs. CIP trade-off_ — Evaluate economy, schedule, transportation, and on-site logistics for feasibility.
6. _Connection design_ — Specify wet or dry precast connections based on structural continuity requirements and schedule.
7. _Philippine standards alignment_ — Apply NSCP 2015 Chapter 4 and precast provisions.
