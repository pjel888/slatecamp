# BP 344 Accessible Ramp Geometry

## Learning objective

Given an accessibility question prompt about a ramp, identify the governing values for width, slope, landing, and handrail height under the 2024 Revised IRR of BP 344. The goal is not to memorize one famous slope and stop. The exam habit is to read the condition, decide whether the general rule or a special table condition applies, then check landings and handrails.

## Governing source

Use BP 344, 2024 Revised IRR, Appendix A, Accessible Ramps, together with the handrail provisions in Appendix A. The ramp values below come from PDF p.2; the handrail height and installation rules come from PDF pp.7-8. When an older note, drawing, or memory aid conflicts with this source, anchor the answer to the 2024 Revised IRR.

## Start with the general ramp rule

The Appendix A ramp excerpt says that changes in level require a ramp, except when served by a dropped sidewalk, curb ramp, elevator, or other mechanical device. For an accessible ramp, the first two numbers are the minimum clear width and the general gradient rule: minimum clear width is 1200 mm, and gradient is not steeper than 1:20.

Source: BP 344, 2024 Revised IRR, Appendix A, Accessible Ramps, Items 1-2, PDF p.2.

This is why an answer choice that gives 900 mm, 1000 mm, or a “comfortable width” without the 1200 mm clear-width rule should be rejected. Clear width is not the same as overall constructed width after handrails, curbs, finishes, or obstructions. In an exam prompt, answer the code value asked for, then separately think about whether the drawing allows that clear width to remain unobstructed.

## Do not misuse the slope table

The same BP 344 excerpt allows steeper gradients only in special cases depending on the length to be covered. Table A.1.1 is therefore not a license to use any slope anywhere. It is a conditional table. The cited table shows the general 1:20 line with a 15 m landing interval, and it shows a 1:15 row with a 10.50 m maximum length, a 700 mm maximum rise, and a 10 m landing interval.

Source: BP 344, 2024 Revised IRR, Appendix A, Accessible Ramps, Table A.1.1, PDF p.2.

A common wrong method is to see “1:15” in the choices and treat it as a better answer because it looks more compact. The better method is to ask: what is the length, what is the rise, and is the question asking for the general rule or the special-case limit? If the prompt asks for the ordinary accessible ramp gradient before any special case is introduced, use 1:20. If the prompt gives a special-condition ramp and asks whether a 1:15 row can fit, check the maximum length and maximum rise together.

## Landing trigger

The excerpt also states that accessible ramps with a total length longer than 10.50 m must be provided with intermediate landings with a minimum length of 1800 mm. It also states that a level area not less than 1800 mm is required at the upper and lower ends of any ramp.

Source: BP 344, 2024 Revised IRR, Appendix A, Accessible Ramps, Items 10-11, PDF p.2.

Worked application: if a question describes one accessible ramp run longer than 10.50 m, do not answer only with the slope. The length has triggered the intermediate-landing rule. The minimum intermediate landing length to remember from the cited excerpt is 1800 mm. If a drawing provides a ramp line but no adequate landing space, the ramp can still fail even if the slope number looks correct.

## Handrails are a second check

Handrails are required for accessible ramps for changes in grade higher than 170 mm. They must be installed on both sides of ramps and stairs, and they are installed at 900 mm and 700 mm above ramps and stairs. The same Appendix A handrail detail also requires a 300 mm horizontal extension before the start and after the end of ramps and stairs, with a closed end that turns downward or fully returns to a wall, post, or floor.

Source: BP 344, 2024 Revised IRR, Appendix A, Handrails and Grab Bars, PDF p.7.

Appendix A further states that handrails attached to walls need at least 50 mm clear distance from the wall, while handrails on ledges need at least 40 mm clear distance. Handrails must remain continuous throughout the ramp length and around landings less than 2100 mm long, except where interrupted by an alternative path of travel or an entry door.

Source: BP 344, 2024 Revised IRR, Appendix A, Handrails and Grab Bars, PDF p.8.

## Fast retrieval checklist

When you see an accessible-ramp question, read it in this order: first, identify whether the question asks for the general rule or a special-case table value; second, check minimum clear width; third, check run length and landing; fourth, check handrail trigger and handrail heights. This order prevents the usual mistake of jumping straight to slope. A ramp can fail because it is too narrow, too long without an intermediate landing, missing the dual handrail heights, or using a special-case slope without satisfying the table condition.

## Real-World Application

Suppose an architect is revising the entrance of a small barangay health station in Iloilo City after a wheelchair user points out that the proposed ramp looks long and tiring. The plan examiner does not ask for a pretty perspective; the examiner asks whether the ramp can actually be used. The architect first checks the clear width, because the 1200 mm requirement can be lost when rails, curbs, or wall finishes are drawn too aggressively. Next, the architect checks the gradient. If the team wants to use a steeper special-case slope, the architect cannot stop at the ratio. The length and rise must still fit the table condition. When the run becomes longer than 10.50 m, the 1800 mm intermediate landing becomes part of the design problem, not an optional comfort feature. Finally, the architect checks the handrails at 900 mm and 700 mm, the 300 mm extensions, and the wall clearance. The practical outcome is a ramp that is wider on paper, shorter in each run, and easier to defend during review.

## Exam traps

- **Trap 1: answering only one handrail height.** The cited accessible-ramp handrail rule uses both 900 mm and 700 mm.
- **Trap 2: treating 1:15 as the general rule.** The general gradient is not steeper than 1:20; steeper values are special cases controlled by length and rise.
- **Trap 3: forgetting the 10.50 m landing trigger.** Once total length exceeds 10.50 m, the intermediate landing must be checked.
- **Trap 4: confusing local memory with the current source.** For this topic, use the 2024 Revised IRR as the governing source.
