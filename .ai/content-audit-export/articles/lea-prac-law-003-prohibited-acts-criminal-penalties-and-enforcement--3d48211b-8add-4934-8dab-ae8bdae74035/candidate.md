## Board Exam Quick Check

For enforcement questions, separate **disciplinary action by the Board** from **criminal prosecution and court-imposed penalties**. RA 9266 uses both, but they operate through different procedures.

## Prohibited Acts, Criminal Penalties, and Enforcement

The RA 9266 IRR protects the public by requiring registration before architectural practice, disciplining architects who misuse their seal or authority, and penalizing unauthorized practice. The verified source for this article is the RA 9266 IRR, especially Sections 22, 23, 25, 29, 30, and 31.

### Registration Is the Entry Gate

Section 25 states the basic rule: a person may not practice architecture in the Philippines, prepare architectural plans or specifications for buildings in the country, use the title Architect, or advertise as an architect unless registered and issued a Professional Identification Card under RA 9266. Foreign architects or unauthorized persons who perform architectural activities in the Philippines are treated as engaging in unauthorized practice unless the law permits them to do so.

### Refusal, Suspension, and Revocation Are Disciplinary Tools

Section 22 allows the Board to refuse registration or a Professional Identification Card for false swearing, misrepresentation, criminal conviction involving moral turpitude, immoral or dishonorable conduct, or unsound mind.

Section 23 allows suspension, revocation, or cancellation of a certificate, PIC, or special/temporary permit after notice and hearing. Grounds include fraud or deceit in obtaining registration, gross negligence or incompetence, unprofessional or dishonorable conduct, signing or sealing work not prepared by the architect or under immediate supervision, aiding unauthorized practice, soliciting projects without a valid service agreement guaranteeing compensation, and violating RA 9266, its IRR, the Code of Ethical Conduct, or Standards of Professional Practice.

### Penal Clause for Unauthorized Practice

Section 29 is the main penal clause. It covers practicing or offering to practice architecture without registration or a temporary/special permit, using another person's certificate, PIC, seal, or permit, giving false or forged evidence to obtain registration, impersonation, using a revoked or suspended credential, using a title or description that creates the impression of being an architect, knowingly allowing unlicensed plans or specifications to be used, and violating RA 9266, the IRR, Board/Commission policies, or professional conduct standards.

The stated penalty is a fine of at least PHP 100,000 and not more than PHP 5,000,000, imprisonment from six months up to six years, or both, at the court's discretion.

### Written Service Agreement Protection

Section 30 makes it a misdemeanor to entice, compel, coerce, require, or force a registered architect to perform architectural services without first executing a written contract or service agreement. The stated penalty is a fine of at least PHP 200,000, imprisonment for a period not exceeding six years, or both, at the court's discretion.

### Representative Liability

Section 31 prevents non-registered persons, firms, or corporations from avoiding RA 9266 by using a representative or employee to seek architectural work on their behalf. Soliciting architectural work is treated as offering to practice architecture; if the represented party is not properly qualified and registered, both the represented party and the representative may be treated as violators.

### Exam Takeaway

Use three buckets: registration required, professional discipline, and penal enforcement. If the fact pattern is about a registered architect's seal misuse or gross negligence, think Section 23. If it is about unauthorized practice or pretending to be an architect, think Section 25 and Section 29. If it is about forcing work without a service agreement, think Section 30.
