## Core Concept

A pile is a slender structural element driven or drilled into soil to transfer loads from a structure to deeper, more competent strata. Piles are classified by two criteria: _load transfer mechanism_ and _installation method_.

**Load Transfer Mechanism:**
- _End-bearing piles_ transfer the majority of load through the pile tip to a firm bearing stratum. The pile shaft typically exhibits minimal friction. This type is optimal when a strong, shallow bearing layer exists (e.g., bedrock, dense sand, stiff clay). Bearing capacity is governed by the bearing capacity of the soil at the tip.
- _Friction piles_ develop load capacity along the entire shaft through skin friction and adhesion with surrounding soil. No reliance on tip bearing. These piles are used when no competent bearing layer exists within economical depth, such as in soft alluvial deposits.

**Installation Method and Geometry:**
- _Driven piles_ are prefabricated (timber, steel, precast concrete) and driven into place using a hammer. They displace soil laterally.
- _Bored (drilled) piles_ are cast in place by excavating a hole and pouring concrete. Bored piles do not densify surrounding soil; they rely on skin friction along the borehole wall.
- _Batter piles_ are inclined (not vertical) to resist lateral or overturning forces. Batter angle per engineering design, commonly 1:6 to 1:12 (H:V). Batter piles appear in bridge abutments, retaining structures, and foundations subject to significant horizontal loads.
- _Sheet piles_ are thin, interlocking structural sections (steel or reinforced concrete) driven in a wall formation to retain soil and resist water pressure. Sheet piles function as a _retaining system_, not a load-bearing foundation. They are common in cofferdams, flood barriers, and excavation support.

**Pile vs. Caisson Distinction:**
The diameter threshold distinguishes piles from caissons:
- **Piles and caissons (drilled shafts):** No strict diameter threshold in NSCP 2015; distinction based on design and installation method per Chapter 1-C.

This distinction reflects a change in behavior: larger diameter shafts exhibit different load transfer, excavation practice, and quality control methodology.

**Related Terms:**
- _Pile cap_: A reinforced concrete beam or slab connecting two or more piles to distribute load from the superstructure.
- _Cofferdam_: A temporary, watertight enclosure constructed to allow construction in or below water; often built using sheet piles.
- _Caisson_: A large-diameter drilled shaft (≥ 600 mm) or a watertight box structure sunk under water for construction. As a foundation element, a caisson behaves like a very large pile.
- _Tremie_: A tube used to place concrete underwater or in boreholes, preventing segregation and contamination from groundwater.

## Key Provisions

| **Pile Type** | **Load Transfer** | **Installation** | **Typical Use** | **Soil Compatibility** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| End-bearing | Tip bearing predominant | Driven or drilled | Shallow firm stratum available | Dense sand, stiff clay, rock |
| Friction | Skin friction primary | Driven or drilled | No competent shallow layer | Soft clay, silt, loose sand |
| Batter | Inclined; resists lateral load | Driven or drilled | Horizontal or overturning force | All; angle 1:6 to 1:12 typical |
| Sheet | Wall retention; no tip bearing | Driven interlocked | Water barrier, excavation support | All soil types |
| Bored (drilled) | Friction or end-bearing | Cast in place | Soft/loose overburden to firm base | All soil types; no lateral densification |

## Philippine Practice Context
In the Philippines, driven piles and bored piles are both prevalent, depending on project scale and soil profile. Metro Manila alluvial deposits favor friction piles to depths of 25–40 m. Mindanao volcanic soils often have competent strata at shallower depths, favoring end-bearing selection. Typhoon-prone regions and bridge design frequently employ batter piles to resist lateral wind and seismic loads. Cofferdam construction using sheet piles is standard for river and coastal projects.

## How It's Tested
1. **Pile Type Identification:** Given a soil profile with a hard stratum at 15 m depth and soft clay above, identify the optimal pile type and justify by load transfer mechanism.
2. **Batter Angle Calculation:** A batter pile at 1:8 slope must resist 200 kN horizontal load; resolve the pile reaction into axial and lateral components.
3. **Caisson vs. Pile Distinction:** A foundation engineer specifies a 700 mm drilled foundation. Name the correct element and explain why diameter threshold matters for construction method.

<Tip>
**Memory Aid:** End-bearing piles say "I stand on a rock"; friction piles say "I hug the soil." Batter piles lean to fight sideways forces. Sheet piles lock arm-in-arm to hold back the earth wall. Diameter 600 mm is the magic threshold: below is a pile, at or above is a caisson.
</Tip>
