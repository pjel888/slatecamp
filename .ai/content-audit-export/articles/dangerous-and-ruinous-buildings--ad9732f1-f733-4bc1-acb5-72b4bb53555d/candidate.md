# Dangerous and Ruinous Buildings

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to identify when a building or structure is dangerous or ruinous under PD 1096 Rule II, what the Building Official may order, what defect categories appear in the 2004 Revised IRR, how the abatement process begins, and why the Code remedies do not eliminate other legal remedies.

## Controlling Source

This article is source-audited against the DPWH official PDF of the 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096. The checked text covers Rule II Sections 214, 215, and 216, plus the Procedure for Abatement/Demolition of Dangerous/Ruinous Buildings/Structures immediately following Section 216.

## Section 214: What Makes a Building Dangerous

Section 214 defines dangerous buildings broadly. A building or structure may be dangerous if it is structurally unsafe, not provided with safe egress, a fire hazard, otherwise dangerous to human life, or a hazard to safety, health, or public welfare because of inadequate maintenance, dilapidation, obsolescence, or abandonment.

The definition also includes buildings or structures that contribute to pollution of the site or community to an intolerable degree.

For LEA questions, do not limit the concept to collapse risk only. Structural danger is one path, but egress, fire hazard, health, public welfare, maintenance, abandonment, and pollution also matter.

## Section 215: The Building Official's Response

When a building or structure is found or declared dangerous or ruinous, Section 215 directs the Building Official to order repair, vacation, or demolition depending on the degree of danger to life, health, or safety.

This is not a one-size-fits-all response. The remedy depends on the degree and type of danger. A repairable defect may lead to repair; an immediate life-safety hazard may require vacation; a condition that cannot safely remain may require demolition.

Section 215 also states that this is without prejudice to further action under Civil Code Articles 482 and 694 to 707. Section 216 reinforces that Code and IRR remedies are in addition to all other available rights, actions, and remedies under existing laws.

## Defect Categories in Section 215

The 2004 Revised IRR lists six major categories of conditions or defects that render a building or structure dangerous or ruinous.

| Category | What to look for |
| :-- | :-- |
| Structural hazards | Disaster damage below NSCP minimums, portions likely to fall, inadequate anchorage or stability, warped or settled structural portions, probable partial or total collapse, leaning walls outside the middle-third test, 33% or more damage to supporting members, 50% damage to nonsupporting members, nuisance conditions, or insufficient remaining strength/fire/weather resistance. |
| Fire hazards | Fire or explosion sources, missing fire-resistive or fire-protective construction, missing fire-extinguishing systems, inadequate exit width or arrangement, or hazardous conditions violating the Fire Code. |
| Unsafe electrical wiring | Systems or installations not conforming to the Philippine Electrical Code, or inadequately maintained or improperly used wiring, outlets, devices, or equipment. |
| Unsafe mechanical installation | Systems not conforming to the Philippine Mechanical Code, improper mechanical devices, lack of required ventilation or air-conditioning, or improper steam, gas, or fuel supply lines. |
| Inadequate sanitation, plumbing, and health facilities | Nonconforming sanitation or plumbing systems, poor maintenance, vermin, lack of garbage handling, or pollution source. |
| Architectural deficiency | Unauthorized use or occupancy, improper location, insufficient natural light and ventilation due to inadequate courts or yards, inadequate room and space dimensions or window openings, and dilapidated or unpresentable buildings. |

## High-Frequency Structural Triggers

Several structural triggers are numeric or highly testable.

- Damage from fire, earthquake, wind, flood, or another cause that leaves structural strength or stability materially less than before and below NSCP minimums for similar new buildings.
- Exterior walls or vertical structural members leaning or buckling so that a plumb line through the center of gravity falls outside the middle one-third of the base.
- 33% or more damage or deterioration of supporting members.
- 50% damage or deterioration of nonsupporting members, enclosing walls, outside walls, or coverings.
- Non-supporting parts with less than 50% of required strength, fire-resisting qualities, or weather-resisting qualities.
- Supporting parts with less than 66% of required strength, fire-resisting qualities, or weather-resisting qualities.
- Abandonment for more than twelve months when the building or portion becomes a nuisance or hazard to the public.

These values should be used only when the facts match the cited category.

## Fire and Egress Hazards

Section 215 treats egress failures as fire hazards when doors, aisles, passageways, stairways, or other means of exit are not sufficient in width or size, or are not arranged to provide safe and adequate exit in case of fire and panic.

That means an egress defect can move from a design noncompliance issue to a dangerous-building issue when it creates a life-safety hazard.

## Abatement and Demolition Procedure

The procedure following Section 216 starts with a finding or declaration by the Building Official that the building or structure is nuisance, ruinous, or dangerous.

The owner and occupants must be served written notice or advice giving at least fifteen days to vacate or cause the building to be vacated, repaired, renovated, demolished, and removed, as the case may be.

Within the fifteen-day period, the owner may appeal to the Secretary and ask for re-inspection or re-investigation. If the owner asks the Building Official to reconsider, the Building Official is given a period to render a final decision appealable to the Office of the Secretary.

If the appeal is meritorious, the Secretary may designate competent representatives other than the Building Official to undertake re-inspection or re-investigation. Their report must be made or completed within thirty days from termination of re-inspection or re-investigation.

## Other Remedies Remain Available

Section 216 states that the rights, actions, and remedies provided in the Code and IRR are in addition to other rights of action and remedies available under existing laws.

For exam purposes, avoid answers that make the Building Official's dangerous-building order the only possible legal consequence. PD 1096 remedies can operate alongside Civil Code and other legal remedies.

## LEA Recall Anchors

- Dangerous building is broader than collapse: unsafe structure, unsafe egress, fire hazard, life danger, health/public welfare hazard, abandonment, or intolerable pollution.
- Building Official response: repair, vacation, or demolition depending on degree of danger.
- Six defect categories: structural, fire, electrical, mechanical, sanitation/plumbing/health, architectural.
- Structural damage triggers include 33% supporting-member damage and 50% nonsupporting-member damage.
- Abandonment trigger in the checked text requires more than twelve months plus nuisance or hazard condition.
- Initial notice gives at least fifteen days.
- Code remedies are additional, not exclusive.
