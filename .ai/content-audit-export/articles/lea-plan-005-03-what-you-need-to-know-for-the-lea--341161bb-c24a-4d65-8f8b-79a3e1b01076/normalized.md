## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Environmental and land-use planning in the Philippines is governed by a framework of Presidential Decrees and Republic Acts that establish easements, require environmental impact assessment, protect natural areas, manage water quality, and regulate solid waste. Landscape architects, planners, and developers must understand these laws to design compliant projects and avoid legal liability. 
## Learning Goal

Identify the key provisions of the five core Philippine environmental laws (PD 1067, PD 1586, RA 7586, RA 9275, RA 9003) and apply easement distances, ECC requirements, and environmental constraints to site planning scenarios.

## Core Concept

### PD 1067 — Water Code of the Philippines (1976)

**PD 1067** (Presidential Decree 1067) is the Water Code of the Philippines, establishing the framework for use and conservation of water and water bodies. A critical provision for site planning is the **easement requirement** — public right-of-way zones along water bodies where private development is prohibited or strictly limited.

**Key planning implications:**

No structures of any kind may be constructed within these easements (PD 1067 Art 51). The easement is public use space. Property owners cannot restrict access within the easement.

**Application in site planning:** Landscape designs must identify all water bodies on and near a site. Where a stream or estero bounds the site, the designer must reserve the minimum easement distance as open space, preventing building placement too close to water. Easement areas may be designed as parks, greenways, or natural areas.

**Relationship to informal settlements:** Informal settlements frequently violate PD 1067 easements, with structures built within 3 meters of esteros (streams) or other water bodies. Such violations contribute to "danger area" classification under **RA 7279 (UDHA)** Sec 28, supporting eviction from waterways.

### PD 1586 — Philippine Environmental Impact Statement System (1978)

**PD 1586** establishes the Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) requirement for certain projects and creates the **ECC (Environmental Compliance Certificate)** as proof of environmental compliance.

**ECC issuing agency:** The **Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR)** issues the ECC. **[ERR-PLAN-021 verification note: ECC is issued by DENR, NOT DHSUD]**

**Environmentally Critical Projects (ECPs)** requiring an EIS and ECC:

- Heavy industrial facilities (power plants, petrochemical plants, cement plants)
- Mining and quarrying operations
- Large-scale infrastructure (airports, seaports, major highways, dams)
- Foreshore and mangrove land reclamation
- Golf courses (due to chemical and water use)
- Large-scale residential subdivisions in environmentally sensitive areas

**Environmentally Critical Areas (ECAs)** — Areas where even non-ECP projects require EIA if located within:

- Primary forests
- Mangrove forests
- Coral reef zones
- Rivers within urban areas
- Flood-prone areas
- Volcanic hazard zones

**Project screening for ECC requirement:**

A project's need for ECC is determined at the start of design through categorization with DENR. Small residential projects, routine building construction, and projects within already-developed urban areas (not in ECAs) typically do not require ECC. Large subdivisions in new areas or near sensitive ecosystems require EIA and ECC.

**Landscape design implications:** If a site is in or near an ECA (mangrove, forest, river), the project may require ECC. The site plan must incorporate environmental mitigation measures identified during EIA—such as riparian buffers, sedimentation controls, habitat restoration, or vegetation preservation.

### RA 7586 — National Integrated Protected Areas System (NIPAS) Act (1992)

**RA 7586** establishes the National Integrated Protected Areas System (NIPAS), designating national parks, protected landscapes, wildlife sanctuaries, and other protected areas managed by the **DENR**. These areas are protected to conserve biodiversity and natural resources.

**Key restriction:** Development within NIPAS areas is highly restricted. Core zones (strictly protected areas) permit no development. Buffer zones and multiple-use areas may permit limited activities compatible with conservation objectives.

**Planning implication:** Projects located within or adjacent to NIPAS areas require special permits from DENR and must demonstrate that development does not harm protected species or ecosystems. Landscape designs in or near NIPAS must prioritize conservation.

### RA 9275 — Philippine Clean Water Act (2004)

**RA 9275** regulates discharge of effluent (wastewater) into water bodies and groundwater. The law requires pollution control and treatment.

**Key provision:** Commercial establishments and high-density residential buildings must install and operate **sewage treatment plants (STPs)** or connect to municipal sewage systems with treatment. Raw sewage discharge is prohibited.

**Landscape design implications:** Large residential projects and commercial developments must include STP in the site plan. The STP requires land allocation, proper design to avoid groundwater contamination, and maintenance access. Landscape designers must account for STP location, screening, and odor mitigation in overall site design. Treated effluent may be recycled for irrigation, reducing freshwater demand.

### RA 9003 — Ecological Solid Waste Management Act (2000)

**RA 9003** establishes the framework for solid waste management requiring source separation, composting, and recycling.

**Key provision:** Local government units (LGUs) must establish **Material Recovery Facilities (MRFs)** where waste is sorted, and open dumping and open burning of waste are prohibited.

**Landscape design implications:** Residential subdivisions and commercial projects must include adequate waste management facilities within the site. Waste segregation areas must be screened and designed to minimize odor and visual impact on adjacent residential areas. Barangay-level waste management plans include provision for composting and on-site organic waste processing.

### Integrated Water Management and Environmental Site Planning

A comprehensive landscape approach integrates these laws:

1. **PD 1067 easements** establish riparian buffers and prevent building encroachment on water bodies
2. **PD 1586 EIS/ECC** process ensures environmental impacts are assessed and mitigated
3. **RA 7586 NIPAS** constraints protect biodiversity in designated areas
4. **RA 9275 water quality** standards require STP design and wastewater management
5. **RA 9003 waste management** integration reduces environmental contamination

A site plan checklist integrating all laws:

- Identify all water bodies and establish PD 1067 easements
- Determine if site is in ECA or requires ECC (PD 1586)
- Assess NIPAS proximity and restrictions (RA 7586)
- Design STP or wastewater management system (RA 9275)
- Allocate space for waste management and MRF (RA 9003)
- Incorporate environmental mitigation measures throughout design

### Summary

Philippine environmental and land-use laws establish essential constraints on landscape and site planning. PD 1067 easement distances protect water bodies and establish public access rights. PD 1586 requires environmental assessment for critical projects. RA 7586 protects biodiversity in designated areas. RA 9275 mandates wastewater treatment, and RA 9003 requires waste management integration. Successful site planning recognizes these legal requirements as design opportunities to create resilient, compliant, and environmentally beneficial landscapes.
