## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal
Conduct site analysis evaluating topography, climate, access, utilities, views, noise, and vegetation, produce site analysis diagrams documenting existing conditions, and apply findings to building orientation and site planning decisions.

Site analysis is the systematic study of existing site conditions—topography, climate, access, utilities, views, noise, and vegetation—that informs design decisions. The LEA expects you to conduct and interpret site analysis and apply its findings to site planning and building orientation decisions.

## Core Concept

A **site analysis** evaluates the built and natural environment of a property to identify opportunities and constraints for development. Unlike site plans, which show proposed building placement, a site analysis communicates the _existing conditions_ that must be understood before design begins.

**Key site analysis elements:**

1. **Topography and slope classification:**
   - Topography is represented on site plans and sections using contour lines (lines of equal elevation). Contour lines that are close together indicate steep slopes; widely spaced lines indicate gentle slopes.
   - **Slope classification:**
     - 0–3%: Flat (minimal drainage challenges)
     - 3–8%: Gentle (suitable for most building placement)
     - 8–15%: Moderate (requires split-level or stepped design; good for passive drainage)
     - 15–25%: Steep (requires significant site work; often unsuitable for building without terracing)
     - >25%: Very steep (natural slopes; building placement difficult without substantial engineering)
   - Slope percentage is calculated as: Slope (%) = (Vertical rise ÷ Horizontal run) × 100

2. **Climate and solar orientation:**
   - In the Philippines (approximately 14.5°N latitude), the sun's path varies seasonally. Equinox sun angles differ from winter and summer extremes.
   - **Building orientation best practice:** Long axis E-W (east-west). This minimizes the building's exposure to the intense morning (east) and afternoon (west) sun while maximizing north and south exposures, which are lower angle sun in tropical latitudes. This is critical for energy efficiency and thermal comfort.
   - **Sun path analysis:** Shows the sun's position at different times and seasons. In the Philippines, the sun is nearly overhead year-round (high altitude angle), making shading critical for reducing heat gain.

3. **Prevailing wind and ventilation:**
   - In the Philippines, prevailing winds are from the northeast (December–February, cooler) and southwest (June–September, monsoon rainfall). Building orientation and window placement should capitalize on northeast breezes for natural ventilation.

4. **Access and circulation:**
   - Identify street frontage, entry points, emergency access, pedestrian movement, and vehicular circulation. Analyze traffic patterns, sight lines, and safety. For buildings >4 stories, multiple stairs and elevators are required (NBC accessibility).

5. **Views and vistas:**
   - Identify desirable views (water, landscape, monuments) and obstructed or undesirable views. Orientation of glazing and balconies can frame views while blocking visual clutter.

6. **Noise and vibration sources:**
   - Identify sources: traffic (roads, highways), industrial facilities, commercial activities (markets, construction). Locate quiet zones and high-noise zones. Design accordingly: place noise-sensitive spaces (bedrooms, offices) away from noise sources.

7. **Utilities and infrastructure:**
   - Mark existing water supply, sewer connections, electrical service, gas, and communications. Identify utility easements and access points. Verify with local authorities before finalizing utility strategy.

8. **Vegetation and ecological features:**
   - Identify existing trees, vegetation, and ecological value. Large trees provide shade and stormwater management. Native vegetation reduces maintenance and irrigation. In the Philippines, banyan, calamansi, and narra trees are common and valuable.

**Building orientation in the Philippines:**
Per ERR-DSGN-035, the correct building orientation for tropical regions is **long axis E-W (east-west)**. This minimizes east-west sun exposure and maximizes north-south facades (which receive lower-angle, less intense sun). Incorrect orientation (long axis N-S) is a common exam error.

## Philippine Practice Context

Site analysis in the Philippines must account for tropical climate factors: intense solar radiation year-round, high humidity, seasonal rainfall (May–November monsoon), and coastal considerations (salt spray, storm surge). Building orientation to capitalize on northeast breezes and minimize heat gain is essential for energy efficiency and comfort without mechanical cooling. Sloping sites are common in upland and hilly regions (Luzon, Visayas); site analysis must evaluate terracing, cut-and-fill, and drainage. Vegetation analysis is important because large trees (mango, calamansi, coconut) provide natural shade and cooling, reducing construction and operating costs. Noise analysis is critical in dense urban areas (Metro Manila, Cebu, Davao) where traffic and commercial activity create significant sound levels.

## How It's Tested

**Pattern 1: Slope classification and interpretation**
A site plan shows contour lines at 2 m intervals. Over a 50 m horizontal distance, the contour lines drop 8 m (elevation change). The candidate must calculate the slope percentage (8 ÷ 50 = 16%) and classify it as "moderate" (8–15% range, actually 16%, so boundary case or slightly steep). This tests quantitative and qualitative interpretation.

**Pattern 2: Building orientation decision**
A candidate is given a site with primary street frontage on the east side and asked to position a building. Correct reasoning: orient the long axis E-W so the street-facing side is north or south (lower sun angle), not east (direct morning sun). Incorrect orientation (long axis N-S) is a common error.

**Pattern 3: Site constraint analysis**
A site analysis shows high noise on the western boundary (major road), view opportunities to the north, and gentle slope (5% gradient). The candidate must infer: place noise-sensitive spaces (bedrooms, offices) on eastern side; orient living areas and views toward north; use the gentle slope for terracing or multi-level design. This integrates multiple site analysis findings.

<Tip>
**Memory aid:** "E-W Orientation Rule" — In tropical Philippines, orient buildings with long axis E-W (east-west). This minimizes harsh E and W sun, maximizes gentle N-S sun. Place primary views and living spaces on N or S facades. This is ERR-DSGN-035 territory—incorrect orientation is a common exam error.
</Tip>

**Exam Tip:** Site analysis must document: N arrow, prevailing wind direction, sun path, adjacent uses, access points, utility locations, easements, flood hazards, noise sources, and significant vegetation. Omissions = design errors.
