# Site analysis

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal
Analyze a site by reading legal boundaries, setbacks, easements, slope, soil, drainage, utilities, sun, wind, views, noise, access, adjacent uses, and cultural or natural resources before fixing the building form.

Site analysis studies the contextual forces that influence how a building is situated, how spaces are oriented, how the enclosure is shaped, and how the project relates to the landscape. The checked construction source begins with physical site data: legal boundaries, setbacks, easements, rights-of-way, site area and shape, building program needs, slopes, subsoil, drainage, water table, runoff, trees, water features, climate, solar access, winds, utilities, access, views, noise, adjacent land uses, and neighborhood character.

Topography is read through contour lines. Contours join points of equal elevation. Widely spaced contours indicate gentler slopes; closely spaced contours indicate steeper slopes; equally spaced contours indicate a constant slope. Slope percentage is elevation gain divided by horizontal distance, multiplied by 100.

Soils and water are part of site analysis, not separate afterthoughts. A subsurface investigation helps reveal shear resistance, compressive strength, water content, permeability, settlement behavior, and groundwater risk. Drainage patterns and water features must be mapped before building placement or grading is finalized.

Climate and energy also affect orientation. The DOE energy guidance flags long east and west facade exposure as a heat-gain issue for air-conditioned buildings when site conditions allow better orientation. The site-analysis source also asks designers to map sun path, prevailing winds, rainfall, glare, views, and noise.

| Site-analysis evidence | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Boundaries, setbacks, easements, rights-of-way | Defines what land can be used and what must remain clear. |
| Contours and slope | Guides building placement, grading, drainage, access, and outdoor usability. |
| Soil and groundwater | Affects foundation suitability, settlement risk, drainage, and basement decisions. |
| Sun, wind, rain, glare | Shapes orientation, openings, shading, ventilation, and energy use. |
| Utilities and access | Confirms whether water, sewer, power, fire protection, and circulation can serve the project. |

## Key Takeaways

Read the site before drawing the building.

Contour spacing, slope formula, drainage, soil, utilities, climate, and access are all site-analysis evidence.

Do not import exact setback, zoning, or orientation rules unless the question gives them or the checked source packet supports them.
