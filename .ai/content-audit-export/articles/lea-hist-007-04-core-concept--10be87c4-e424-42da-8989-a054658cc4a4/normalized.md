# Southeast Asian Architecture

## LEA Point

Southeast Asian architecture is a comparison problem, not a single style. Keep Khmer Angkor, Javanese Borobudur, and Vietnamese urban-house examples separate by site, religion, form, and exam cue.

## Source Anchors

- UNESCO Angkor anchors Angkor Wat, Bayon, Khmer temple/urban remains, and hydraulic landscape scale.
- UNESCO Borobudur anchors the Central Java Buddhist monument, stepped/pyramidal base, circular platforms, stupas, and cosmological pilgrimage reading.
- UNESCO Hoi An anchors the trading-port and timber merchant-house context used cautiously for Vietnamese narrow-house and tube-house exam guardrails.
- HOA Module 3 carries local exam anchors for Angkor Wat as temple mountain and Borobudur as a mandala/stepped Buddhist monument.

## Exam Pattern

If the stem says Khmer, Angkor, Angkor Wat, Bayon faces, concentric galleries, or temple mountain, stay in the Cambodian/Khmer bucket. If it says Central Java, mandala, Buddhist pilgrimage ascent, square terraces, circular terraces, or stupas, answer Borobudur. If it says narrow frontage, deep plan, shop or merchant house, and urban lot pressure, answer Vietnamese tube house or Hoi An style merchant-house logic.

## Guardrails

Do not use unsupported mortar/gravity claims as the main Angkor answer; Batch 122 shifts that linked item to source-safe monument identification. Do not treat Borobudur as Khmer or Angkor as Javanese. Do not use Hoi An/tube-house dimensions as universal Southeast Asian measurements.

## Real World Scenario

When teaching a market-street conservation case, compare a Vietnamese narrow merchant house with Angkor and Borobudur by function first: the house is urban-commercial/residential, Angkor is a temple/urban sacred landscape, and Borobudur is a Buddhist pilgrimage monument.
