# Architectural Design Competitions: Types, Rules, and the Architect's Rights

## Source-Audited Core

SPP 208 treats an Architectural Design Competition as a way to obtain architectural ideas or designs through comparative excellence. The checked source describes project, idea, and design-build competition contexts, especially for civic or monumental projects.

The Owner issues the invitation and finances the ADC. The Jury is appointed by the Owner, approved under the SPP 208 framework, and must include an architect majority. The Professional Advisor is an Architect nominated by the Owner and approved by the IAPOA to organize the competition, prepare the Conditions, safeguard anonymity, and assist the Jury without voting.

SPP 208 classifications include Project ADCs and Ideas Competitions. Competitions may be single-stage or two-stage, open or limited by invitation, and classified by project complexity using SPP 202.

The checked source protects fairness through IAPOA approval, anonymity, identical conditions for competitors, mandatory requirements, and specific information. It also protects the author: competition plans may not be used or altered except by agreement with the author, and the Owner may not compose another plan from portions of different entries.

For a Project ADC, the checked source states that awarding first prize obliges the Owner to entrust the commission to the author. If no contract is signed within twelve months, the first prize winner receives a further sum equal to the first prize, while the Owner still does not acquire rights except through collaboration with the author.

## LEA Practice Guardrails

Separate ADC roles cleanly: Owner finances and issues the invitation, Jury judges, and Professional Advisor organizes and safeguards the procedure without a vote. Also remember the rights issue: a winning scheme is not a free design library for the Owner.
