## Learning Goal

## Core Concept

A board foot (BF) is the standard unit of lumber volume in the Philippines and North America. It is defined as a piece of wood measuring 1 foot in length, 1 foot in width, and 1 inch in thickness.

The board foot formula is the foundation of all lumber quantity and cost calculations in construction. Contractors use it to estimate material costs, designers use it to specify structural capacity, and inspectors use it to verify delivered quantities.

## Board Foot Formula

The exact formula is:

**Board Feet (BF) = (T × W × L) / 12**

Where:
- **T** = thickness in inches
- **W** = width in inches
- **L** = length in feet
- **12** = constant (converts cubic inches to board feet)

Note the critical detail: length must be in feet; thickness and width must be in inches. Failure to convert units correctly is the most common error.

## Philippine Lumber Standards and Grading Groups

Philippine timber is classified by specific gravity into grading groups that determine structural capacity and pricing:

- **Group I** (hardest, heaviest): Specific gravity >0.71. Examples: yakal, molave, kamagong. Used for heavy structural applications and fine cabinetry.
- **Group II** (medium-hard): Specific gravity 0.61—0.71. Examples: narra, tanguile, mayapis. Used for general construction and exterior applications.
- **Group III** (medium): Specific gravity 0.51—0.60. Examples: white lauan, red lauan. Used for interior framing and non-structural applications.
- **Group IV** (lightest): Specific gravity ≤0.50. Examples: pine, spruce. Used for temporary construction and non-structural applications in the Philippines (pine is imported).

The DPWH (Department of Public Works and Highways) specifies minimum grades and species for structural lumber in government projects. NSCP 2015 Section 2401 references Philippine lumber grading through the National Lumber Grading Committee (NLGC).

## Philippine Lumber Grading Classifications

Lumber less than 1 inch in thickness is graded and measured the same as 1 inch lumber. All lumber is graded on the poorer side of the piece.

- **First and Seconds (F&S)**: Highest grade, suitable for visible cabinetry and fine joinery. Few knots or defects allowed.
- **Export Common**: Good structural quality, minor surface defects acceptable. Most export lumber from Philippine mills meets this grade.
- **Merchantable**: Standard construction grade, visible defects acceptable. Most locally sold lumber is Merchantable grade.
- **Culls**: Below Merchantable grade, structural use not recommended. Often rejected at delivery.

## Standard Lumber Lengths in the Philippine Market

Lumber is commonly sold in the following lengths (all in feet):

- 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18, 20 feet

Lumber longer than 20 feet is available but may require special order and premium pricing.

Shorter lengths (4—6 feet) are often called "shorts" or "chairstock" and command a lower price per board foot than longer pieces because they have less structural utility and greater waste potential.

## Philippine DPWH Specifications for Structural Lumber

The DPWH specifies lumber grading, species, and sizing for government projects:

- **Structural grade requirement:** Minimum F&S or Export Common grade for primary members (beams, columns)
- **Species for structural use:** Group I or II only (yakal, molave, narra approved; pine not approved for main structural members)
- **Moisture content:** 15% maximum at time of delivery (kiln-dried specification)
- **Defect limits:** Knots limited to 1/4 of face width; slope of grain not to exceed 1:15

NSCP 2015 Section 2401 references DPWH standards and allows use of visual grading rules from the Philippine National Lumber Grading Committee (NLGC) or equivalent.

## How It's Tested

The LEA expects you to:

1. Apply the board foot formula correctly with proper unit conversions
2. Distinguish nominal from actual dimensions and choose the correct dimensions for calculations
3. Calculate total board feet for projects with multiple pieces or variable lengths
4. Work backward from a desired board foot quantity to determine piece count or dimensions
5. Identify common grading and species errors in specifications
6. Estimate material cost using market prices and board feet
7. Account for waste and determine minimum order quantities
8. Apply DPWH and NSCP 2015 requirements for Philippine structural lumber

Typical questions present a project scope (deck, framing, cabinetry) and ask you to calculate total board feet, specify appropriate species/grade, and determine delivered cost. Some questions test your ability to recognize when a given dimension set produces an incorrect or unattainable board foot total.
