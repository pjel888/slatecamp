## What You Need to Know for the LEA

A board foot is a lumber volume unit, not an area or price unit. The checked wood sources define the board foot as 1 inch thick by 12 inches wide by 1 foot long. The exam-safe formula is:

Board feet = (Thickness in inches x Width in inches x Length in feet x Number of pieces) / 12

## Core Concept

Keep the units in the exact pattern used by the formula: thickness and width in inches, length in feet. If the problem gives length in inches, convert the length to feet before applying the formula. If the problem gives multiple pieces, multiply by the number of pieces after computing the per-piece board feet, or include the quantity in the numerator.

The checked sources also distinguish nominal and dressed lumber. Nominal dimensions are the named sizes, while dressed lumber is smaller after surfacing. For an exam item, use the dimensions explicitly given by the problem. If it gives nominal dimensions for a source-style board-foot table, use those nominal dimensions. If it gives actual dimensions, use the actual dimensions.

## How It Is Tested

The fastest check is dimensional: inches x inches x feet divided by 12. For 10 pieces of 2 in x 6 in x 14 ft lumber, the total is 10 x 2 x 6 x 14 / 12 = 140 board feet. For 18 pieces of 1 in x 12 in x 20 ft lumber, the total is 18 x 1 x 12 x 20 / 12 = 360 board feet.

## Common Mistakes

Do not leave length in inches inside the board-foot formula. Do not divide by 144 when the length has already been converted to feet. Do not answer with square feet; board foot is a volume measure.
