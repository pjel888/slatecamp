# Architect Selection and Compensation Methods

## Board Exam Quick Check

For service-compensation questions, identify the service first. The UAP Standards do not give one universal fee rule for every engagement. They list methods for selecting architects and then list compensation methods by service category.

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to:

- Distinguish architect selection methods from compensation methods.
- Recognize the compensation methods listed for pre-design, regular design, and full-time supervision services.
- Use the MDPE formula without treating it as the only professional-fee method.
- Match short-term consulting or expert engagements to per diem or honorarium-style compensation when the source supports it.

## Selection Comes Before Compensation

The UAP Standards list three common ways to select an architect: direct selection, comparative selection, and architectural design competition. These are selection methods. They answer how the client chooses the architect, not how the architect is paid after the service scope is agreed.

The exam trap is to mix these categories. Direct selection is not a fee formula. A design competition is not automatically a construction-supervision fee method. Start with the service category, then choose the source-backed compensation method.

## Compensation by Service Category

For pre-design services, the UAP Standards list the following compensation methods: multiple of direct personnel expense, professional fee plus expenses, lump sum or fixed fee, per diem plus reimbursable expenses, mixed methods, and other methods.

For regular design services, the Standards list percentage of project construction cost and also list multiple of direct personnel expense, professional fee plus expenses, lump sum or fixed fee, per diem plus reimbursable expenses, mixed methods, and other methods.

For full-time supervision services, the Standards again provide a service-specific compensation list that includes percentage of project construction cost, multiple of direct personnel expense, professional fee plus expenses, lump sum or fixed fee, per diem plus reimbursable expenses, and mixed methods.

## MDPE in Exam Terms

Multiple of Direct Personnel Expense is a formula method. The Standards describe it as direct personnel expense multiplied by a factor, plus reimbursable expenses. The factor shown in the source ranges from 1.5 to 2.5, depending on the complexity of the work.

This makes MDPE useful for work where professional time and staff effort can be measured. It is not the only possible method, and it should not be confused with a percentage-of-construction-cost method.

## Practical Distinctions

A short expert consultation, hearing appearance, or limited advisory task is usually easier to match with per diem or honorarium plus reimbursables when the question frames the service by days or appearances. A full regular design engagement may point to percentage of project construction cost. A preliminary planning or research task may point to pre-design service compensation methods.

## How It Is Tested

Expect questions that ask whether direct selection is a fee method, when MDPE applies, what multiplier range the Standards state, and why the percentage-of-construction-cost method is tied to design or supervision categories rather than every architectural service.

## Common Mistakes

- Counting selection methods as compensation methods.
- Treating percentage of project construction cost as the only fee method.
- Treating MDPE as a fixed fee rather than a direct-personnel-expense multiplier plus reimbursables.
- Applying regular-design compensation to a short expert consultation without checking the service facts.

## Real World Scenario

A client interviews three architects and then asks whether the fee should be MDPE, percentage of project construction cost, or per diem. Do not answer from the selection method alone. Direct selection, comparative selection, and architectural design competition explain how the architect is chosen. Compensation is matched to the service category. A time-measurable research or advisory task can point to MDPE or per diem plus reimbursables, while a regular design engagement can point to percentage of project construction cost or another listed method if the facts support it.
