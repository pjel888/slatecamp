## Core Concept

The Architect's Guidelines (formerly 1979 UAP Doc 208-b, Methods of Compensation) establish **six methods of compensation** for architectural services. These methods are not contained in a single standalone SPP document — they are distributed across individual SPP documents (SPP 201 through SPP 209), with each document specifying the appropriate compensation method for its service category.

Understanding all six methods and knowing which SPP document calls for which method is a core LEA competency. The architect selects the compensation method based on the nature of the service, the project delivery method, and the ability to define scope in advance.

### The Six Methods of Compensation

**Method 1 — Percentage of Project Construction Cost (PCC)** calculates the architect's fee as a percentage of the estimated or actual construction cost. This is the primary method for **Regular Design Services under SPP 202**. The percentage is determined by the project's classification group (Groups 1–10 under the Architect's Guidelines). This method aligns architect compensation with project scope — as PCC increases, the fee increases proportionally (subject to the sliding scale for Groups 1–3).

**Method 2 — Multiple of Direct Personnel Expense (MDPE)** calculates the fee using the formula:

<Callout>Fee = ((manhour × rate) × multiplier) + reimbursable expenses</Callout>

The multiplier ranges from **1.5 to 2.5**, depending on office set-up costs, overhead, the architect's experience, and the complexity of the project. MDPE is the primary method for **Pre-Design Services under SPP 201**, **Group 10 (Technical Expertise)** projects, and services where scope cannot be precisely defined in advance. It is also used for **Comprehensive Design and Planning** under SPP 203.

**Method 3 — Professional Fee + Expenses** establishes a fixed professional fee with actual expenses billed separately. This method is used for **single-architect project series** — engagements where one architect handles a defined set of projects. The professional fee covers the architect's expertise; expenses (travel, reproduction, communications) are reimbursed at cost.

**Method 4 — Per Diem + Reimbursable Expenses** compensates the architect on a daily rate basis plus actual out-of-pocket expenses. This method suits short-duration consulting engagements, site visits, expert testimony, and advisory services where the engagement period is known but the total effort is uncertain.

**Method 5 — Lump Sum or Fixed Fee** establishes a single predetermined amount for a defined scope of services. This method is commonly used for **government projects** and situations requiring price certainty. It demands more extensive paperwork and scope definition upfront. The Architect's Guidelines note this method is "more paperwork and time-consuming" because the scope must be precisely defined to prevent disputes.

**Method 6 — Mixed Methods of Compensation** combines two or more of the above methods within a single engagement. This approach is appropriate when a project involves multiple service types requiring different compensation approaches — for example, percentage-of-PCC for design services combined with MDPE for pre-design consulting within the same commission.

### Methods of Selection

The Architect's Guidelines also establish **three methods of selecting an architect**, which precede the compensation discussion:

| Method | Application |
| ------ | ----------- |
| Direct Selection | Used for residential projects |
| Comparative Selection | Used for institutions, corporations, or public agencies |
| Design Competition | Used for civic or monumental projects |

## Key Provisions

| Method | Formula/Basis | Best Used When |
| ------ | ------------- | -------------- |
| % of PCC | Fee = PCC × group percentage | Scope correlates with construction cost (SPP 202) |
| MDPE | Fee = ((manhour × rate) × multiplier) + expenses | Scope is uncertain or effort-based (SPP 201, Group 10) |
| Professional Fee + Expenses | Fixed fee + actual expenses | Single-architect project series |
| Per Diem + Reimbursable | Daily rate + actual expenses | Short consulting engagements |
| Lump Sum / Fixed Fee | Predetermined total amount | Government projects, defined scope |
| Mixed Methods | Combination of above | Multi-service commissions |

## Philippine Practice Context

In Philippine private practice, the percentage-of-PCC method dominates for building design projects because most commissions fall under SPP 202 Regular Design Services. MDPE is increasingly used for specialized consulting and pre-design work where scope is difficult to define at engagement. For government projects procured under RA 9184 (Government Procurement Reform Act), lump sum or fixed fee is standard because government procurement requires price certainty before contract award. Architects engaged in both design and construction management for the same project typically use mixed methods — percentage-of-PCC for design phases and a separate fee structure for CM services.

## How It's Tested

The LEA tests compensation methods in three patterns. First, the exam presents a service type and asks which compensation method applies — the student must match the method to the correct SPP document. Second, the exam provides manhours, hourly rates, and a multiplier, then asks the student to compute the MDPE fee using the formula. Third, the exam describes a multi-service engagement and asks the student to identify which combination of methods would apply under the mixed methods approach.

<Tip>Remember the MDPE formula as **"MRM + R"**: Manhours × Rate × Multiplier, plus Reimbursables. The multiplier (1.5–2.5) covers overhead and profit — it is NOT a separate line item.</Tip>
