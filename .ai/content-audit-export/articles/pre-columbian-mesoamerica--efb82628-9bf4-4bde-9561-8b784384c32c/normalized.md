# Pre-Columbian Mesoamerica

## Source-Audited Scope

Batch 054 limits this article to the checked local source evidence for Mesoamerican materials, cultures, temple pyramids, construction forms, and named monuments. The checked source covers Yucatan limestone, volcanic rock in Mexico, adobe brick, hardwoods, Olmec, Maya, Teotihuacan, Toltecs, Aztecs, Tenochtitlan, temple pyramids, corbel construction, and selected monuments.

## Board Exam Quick Check

For LEA history recognition, watch for stepped or terraced temple pyramids, flat roofs, no windows, square-headed doorways, Maya corbel or arch construction, and named places such as Pyramid of the Sun, Temple I at Tikal, Temple of the Warriors at Chichen Itza, Palace of the Governors, and Tenochtitlan.

## Core Concepts

- The checked source identifies common materials: limestone in Yucatan, volcanic rock in Mexico, adobe brick, and hardwoods.
- It names the Olmec, Maya, Teotihuacan, Toltecs, and Aztecs as key cultural groups or centers.
- The Aztecs founded the twin capitals of Tenochtitlan and Tlatelolco on the site of present-day Mexico City.
- The temple pyramid is described as the house of the god, raised on a terraced or truncated base.
- The checked source describes clay, rubble, adobe, stone, and plaster coating in temple pyramid construction.
- It notes flat roofs, no windows, square-headed doorways, and Maya corbel or arch construction.
- Named examples include the Pyramid of the Sun, the Citadel at Teotihuacan, Temple I at Tikal, Temple of the Warriors at Chichen Itza, Palace of the Governors, and Tenochtitlan.

## LEA Use

Use the temple pyramid and corbel clues to separate Mesoamerican questions from Inca ashlar questions. Mesoamerican temple-pyramid questions point to terraced sacred platforms and corbel construction, while Inca questions point to highland stone fitting, ashlar, and polygonal masonry.

## Common Traps

- Do not answer Inca ashlar questions with Mesoamerican temple-pyramid details.
- Do not treat Tenochtitlan as an Inca city; the checked source ties it to the Aztecs.
- Do not add detailed claims about chinampas or city planning unless a separate checked source supports them.
