## Overview

**SPP Document 208** governs the **Architectural Design Competition (ADC)** — the formal, regulated process by which an Owner seeks design excellence by inviting Architects to submit design proposals, judged by an independent Jury under the oversight of the IAPOA. It replaces the earlier **1979 UAP Doc. 209** and forms part of the Supplemental Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of **Republic Act No. 9266** (The Architecture Act of 2004).

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to:

Identify the types of architectural design competitions under SPP Doc 208 (open, invited, limited, student), the rules for jury composition and judging criteria, the architect's right to retain authorship, and the compensation requirements for competition winners and participants; distinguish a competition from a design-build procurement under RA 9184.
---
SPP 208 is structurally distinct from all other SPP documents: it does not govern a bilateral relationship between Architect and Owner, but rather a **multi-party competitive process** involving Owners, Competitors, a Professional Advisor, a Jury, Technical Advisors, and the IAPOA ADC Committee. Its provisions are drawn up in the interest of **both Project Proponents/Owners and Competitors** — ensuring fairness, design integrity, anonymity, and intellectual property protection throughout the competition process.

***

## 1. Introduction and Purpose

The purpose of SPP 208 is to state the **principles upon which competitions are based** and by which **Promoters/Owners should be guided** in organizing ADCs. A properly conducted ADC serves two strategic goals:

1. **For Owners**: Obtaining **excellence in design** for a project or development concept by accessing the widest possible range of design tleant and creative ideas
2. **For Competitors**: Providing a transparent, fair, and intellectually protected forum in which registered architects can demonstrate their design capability and compete for commissions and prize money

The ADC is one of three methods of selecting an Architect recognized under the SPP/Annex "A" framework, alongside Direct Selection and Comparative Selection.

## 4. IAPOA Approval

Every ADC must pass through a mandatory IAPOA approval process **before any official announcement is made**:

### 4.1 Pre-Announcement Approval

Before the Owner makes any official announcement, **the IAPOA through the ADC Committee shall send the Owner a written approval** of:

- The draft Conditions
- The timetable
- The ADC registration fee (when required)
- The composition of the Jury

### 4.2 Public Announcement

Notice of a National ADC shall be issued by the Owner and/or the IAPOA ADC Committee Secretariat with a request for publication in **technical journals or through other media**. The announcement must:

- Be simultaneous in all media to enable all interested parties to apply
- State where and how the ADC documents may be obtained
- Confirm that the **ADC conditions have received the requisite IAPOA approval**

## 5. Drawing Up the ADC Conditions

The Conditions are the governing document of each individual ADC — drawn up by the Professional Advisor and approved by the IAPOA. Six key principles govern the preparation of the Conditions:

### 5.1 Anonymity — The Cardinal Rule

[SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK:ui_0007 component="Callout" status="preserved"]
Extracted summary:
- Props: variant="note"
- Text: *"It is essential that the ANONYMITY of competitors should be maintained until the final judgment of the competition. In the interest of the competition system, rigorous measures should be taken to ensure that all parties involved adhere to this principle."*
[/SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK]


Anonymity is the bedrock protection that ensures Jury decisions are based on **design merit alone**, free from favoritism, personal relationships, or institutional bias.

### 5.2 Equality of Conditions

The ADC conditions, including the **program of requirements of a National ADC, shall be identical for all Competitors** — no competitor shall receive information or instructions that others do not receive.

### 5.3 Mandatory Content of Conditions

The Conditions for a National ADC — whether single or two-stage, open or limited — **shall state clearly**:

- The **purpose of the ADC** and the intentions of the Owner
- The **nature of the problem** to be solved
- All the **requirements which Competitors must meet**
- A clear distinction between **mandatory requirements** (non-negotiable) and those which **permit the competitor freedom of interpretation**

### 5.4 Precision of Information

All information supplied to Competitors — social, economic, technical, geographical, and topographical — **must be specific and not open to misinterpretation**. Vague or ambiguous site information can lead to Jury disputes and grounds for disqualification challenges.

### 5.5 Submission Requirements

The Conditions must state the **number, nature, scale, and dimensions** of the documents, plans, or models required, and the terms of acceptance. Where a cost estimate is required, the Conditions must prescribe a **standard form of presentation** to ensure comparability across entries.

### 5.6 Supplementary Instructions

In a two-stage competition, **supplementary information and instructions approved by the Jury** may be issued by the Owner to all Competitors selected to proceed to the Second Stage.

## 6. The Professional Advisor

The Professional Advisor is the **Architect who runs the competition** on behalf of the Owner — the operational manager of the entire ADC process.

### 6.1 Appointment

Appointed and **paid by the Owner**, but must be **approved by the IAPOA National Board of Directors (NBD)** through its ADC Committee.

### 6.2 Functions

The Professional Advisor:

- Supervises the **conduct of the ADC** and prepares the **Conditions**
- Ensures the **ADC timetable** is adhered to
- Supervises the **receipt of Competitors' questions** and the **dispatch of replies to all Competitors** (ensuring all receive the same information)
- Supervises the **receipt of competition entries**
- **Safeguards the anonymity** of Competitors at all times
- **Assists the Jury** and is present during its deliberations
- Has **no vote** in Jury deliberations
- Responsibilities are **limited to the organization and conduct** of the competition only

## 7. The Jury

The Jury is the **decision-making body** of the ADC — its awards are final.

### 7.2 Conduct Rules

- **All Jurors must be present** at all meetings of the Jury — no proxy voting
- Each Juror must **see the Conditions before** they are made available to Competitors
- Decisions are taken by **majority vote**, with a **separate vote on each competition plan/design** submitted
- Any drawings, photographs, models, or documents **not required under the regulations shall be excluded** before the Jury examines a Competitor's entry
- The Jury **shall disqualify** any design that does not conform to the mandatory requirements, instructions, or Conditions

### 7.3 Awards and Reporting

- The Jury **must make awards** — it cannot decline to award prizes
- Awards shall be **final** and made public by a date agreed on with the IAPOA and stated in the Conditions
- The Jury must make **full use of the amount set aside for prizes** in the ADC Conditions
- The **list of ADC awards and the Jury's report** shall be **signed by all Jurors** before the Jury is dissolved; one copy shall be sent to the IAPOA

### 7.4 Two-Stage Competition Rule

In a two-stage competition, **the same Jury** must judge both stages. If the competition converts from single-stage to two-stage, the **same Jury must be reappointed** by the Owner.

### 7.5 Fees

**All Jury fees, travel, and subsistence expenses shall be paid by the Owner**.

## 8. Persons Not Eligible for Entry

SPP 208 maintains strict conflict-of-interest exclusions to protect competition integrity:

- **No Jury member** may take part in the competition — either directly or indirectly — nor be commissioned with work connected with the prize-winning design (directly or indirectly)
- **No member of the promoting body** (Owner's organization), nor any associate or employee, nor any **person concerned with the preparation or organization of the ADC** is eligible to compete or to assist a Competitor

These exclusions apply to the **Professional Advisor** and **Competition Secretariat** members as well — anyone who had access to the Conditions before they were made available to all Competitors is barred from competing.

***

## 9. Prizes, Honoraria, and Mentions

### 9.1 General Prize Principles

- The Conditions must state the **amount and number of prizes**
- Prizes must be **related to the size and complexity of the project**, the amount of work involved, and the **expense incurred by Competitors**
- The Owner must allot **adequate prize money** to compensate all Competitors for their work
- For **Ideas Competitions only**, it may be possible to remunerate only the first-prize winner
- The Owner must pay prizes within **one (1) month** of the official announcement of ADC results

### 9.3 The First Prize–Commission Rule

[SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK:ui_0010 component="Callout" status="preserved"]
Extracted summary:
- Props: variant="note"
- Text: *"In Project ADCs, the award of first prize to a plan/design places the Owner under an obligation to entrust the Author of the plan/design with the commission for the Project."*
[/SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK]


This is the most commercially significant rule in SPP 208: winning a Project ADC **automatically entitles the winner to the full design commission** under SPP 202. The Owner cannot take the winning design and hire a different architect to implement it.

**Exception**: If the winning Competitor is **unable to satisfy the Jury of his ability to carry out the design work**, the Jury may require the winner to **collaborate with another Architect of the winning Competitor's choice**, duly approved by the Jury and Owner.

### 9.4 The 12-Month Commission Rule

[SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK:ui_0011 component="Callout" status="preserved"]
Extracted summary:
- Props: variant="note"
- Text: *"In Project ADCs, provisions shall be made in the ADC Conditions for the first prize winner to receive compensation of a further sum equal to the amount of the first prize if no contract is signed within twelve (12) months of the announcement of the Jury's award."*
[/SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK]


If the Owner fails to commission the winning Architect within 12 months, the winning Architect is entitled to **double the first prize amount**. Importantly: even after this additional compensation, **the Owner still does not acquire the right to carry out the project except with the collaboration of the Author**.

## 10. Copyright and Right of Ownership

SPP 208 establishes a **strong, unwaivable copyright framework** for all competition entries:

### 10.1 Competitor's Copyright Retained

[SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK:ui_0012 component="Callout" status="preserved"]
Extracted summary:
- Props: variant="note"
- Text: *"The Author of any plan/design shall retain the copyright of his work; no alterations may be made without his/her written consent."*
[/SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK]


This applies to **all entries** — winners and non-winners alike. The submission of an entry to an ADC does not transfer copyright to the Owner.

### 10.2 Winner's Design: One Execution Only

The design awarded first prize **can only be used by the Owner** upon his commissioning the Author to carry out the plan/design preparation for the project. The Owner's right to use the ADC-generated plan/design **covers one (1) execution only** — the design cannot be replicated or adapted for another project without the Author's consent.

### 10.3 No Piecemeal Use of Other Entries

[SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK:ui_0013 component="Callout" status="preserved"]
Extracted summary:
- Props: variant="note"
- Text: *"No other plan/design may be used wholly or in part by the Owner except by agreement with the Author concerned."*
[/SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK]


The Owner **cannot pick out portions of non-winning entries** and combine them into another design — this is explicitly prohibited. This provision is reinforced by:

- **Section 20(4) and 33 of R.A. No. 9266** (The Architecture Act of 2004)
- **R.A. No. 8293** (Intellectual Property Code of the Philippines) and their respective IRRs

### 10.4 Ideas Competition Copyright

If the Owner in an Ideas Competition decides to make use of all or part of the winning scheme, **the collaboration of the Author is required**, and the terms must be acceptable to the Author.

***

## 11. Registration of Competitors

### 11.1 Registration Requirement and Acceptance

All Competitors must **register with the Owner** upon receiving ADC details. **Registration implies acceptance of the Conditions** of the ADC — it is a binding agreement to abide by the competition rules.

### 11.2 Documentation

The Owner shall issue to all registered Competitors **all necessary documentation** for preparing their plans/designs. If furnishing documentation is conditional on a deposit, **the deposit shall be returned** to Competitors who submit a bona fide plan/design, unless otherwise stated.

### 11.3 Two-Stage Anonymity

The names of Competitors selected to proceed to the Second Stage **shall be made public only under exceptional conditions** agreed on by the Jury before the launching of the ADC — protecting the anonymity principle even after the First Stage.

## 12. Insurance

[SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK:ui_0014 component="Callout" status="preserved"]
Extracted summary:
- Props: variant="note"
- Text: *"The Owner shall insure the Competitors' plans/designs from the time when he assumes responsibility for them and for the duration of his responsibility."*
[/SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK]


The **insurance obligation rests on the Owner** — not on the Competitor. Once the Owner receives submitted designs, he is responsible for their protection against loss, damage, or theft. The **amount of insurance shall be stated in the Conditions**.

## 13. Exhibitions and Entries

After judgment, the Owner has mandatory obligations regarding public exhibition:

- **All designs — including disqualified entries — must be exhibited** for at least **two (2) weeks**, together with a copy of the signed Jury report
- The exhibition **shall be open to the public free of charge**
- The Owner shall notify **all registered Competitors** in a timely manner of the date, place, and results of the public exhibition, and send them a copy of the Jury's report
- The Owner shall similarly inform the **IAPOA**; photographs of the prize-winning designs shall be sent to the IAPOA with an option for publication

The mandatory exhibition of all entries — including disqualified ones — serves the profession's institutional interest in transparency and public architectural discourse.

## 15. Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) or Litigation

SPP 208 mandates a structured ADR-first dispute resolution framework grounded in **Republic Act No. 9285** (the Alternative Dispute Resolution Act of 2004):

### 15.1 ADR Mandatory in Conditions

[SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK:ui_0017 component="Callout" status="preserved"]
Extracted summary:
- Props: variant="note"
- Text: *"Since no regulations, however well drawn up, can preclude the possibility of dispute, provisions for conciliation, mediation and arbitration — i.e. ADR modes under R.A. No. 9285 — must be included in the ADC Conditions and must precede any form of litigation."*
[/SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK]


ADR is not optional — it is a mandatory clause that must be included in every ADC's Conditions.

### 15.2 Jury as Sole Arbiters During the Competition

[SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK:ui_0018 component="Callout" status="preserved"]
Extracted summary:
- Props: variant="note"
- Text: *"The Jury members are the sole arbiters at all stages, up to the final prize-giving."*
[/SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK]


During the adjudication process itself, the Jury's authority is absolute — no external party (not even the IAPOA) can override the Jury's decisions during the competition.

### 15.3 Post-Competition Disputes

For disputes **not related to the adjudication process or awarding of prizes**, the matter shall be settled by **an arbitration process approved by the IAPOA**, without initial recourse to any form of litigation.

### 15.4 Costs

The expenses resulting from any conciliation, mediation, or arbitration procedure shall be **shared equally by the two interested parties** to the ADR proceeding.

## 18. Practical Notes for Owners and Competitors

**For Owners:**

- The **IAPOA ADC Committee** is not merely an approving body — it is an active partner in ensuring the competition's fairness and credibility. Engage the IAPOA early in the planning stage, not after the Conditions have been drafted.
- The **first-prize–commission obligation** (Section 8.7) is non-negotiable. An Owner who organizes a Project ADC without intending to build or commission the winner is abusing the competition system — SPP 208 and R.A. 9266 both protect the winner's right to the commission.
- The **12-month rule** (Section 8.8) means that project delays or budget uncertainties do not relieve the Owner of financial obligations to the winning Architect — either commission the project or pay the additional prize sum.

**For Competitors:**

- **Registration = contractual acceptance**. Before registering, review the Conditions carefully — registration is a binding commitment to abide by all ADC rules, including the anonymity requirement.
- **Copyright is retained unconditionally** — submitting an entry does not transfer any ownership rights to the Owner. If the Owner requests to use a non-winning entry, the Competitor may negotiate terms independently.
- In **Limited ADCs by Invitation**, participants are entitled to an honorarium regardless of whether they win a prize — this is separate from prize money and must be claimed.
- In **two-stage competitions**, if selected to proceed to the Second Stage, a separate honorarium is owed for the additional work — ensure this amount is clearly stated in the Conditions before accepting the invitation to the Second Stage.
