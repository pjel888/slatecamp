# SPP Document 208: Architectural Design Competition (ADC)

## Learning Goal

Use SPP Document 208 to answer LEA questions on Architectural Design Competitions: approval before announcement, anonymity, the Professional Advisor, Jury composition, prizes, copyright, exhibition, and dispute resolution.

## Source-Audited Scope

SPP Document 208 governs Architectural Design Competitions. It replaces the former UAP competition document and sets rules for fair competition, professional control, and protection of the Architect's authorship.

## Parties and Competition Types

SPP 208 defines the Owner, Jury, Professional Advisor, Technical Advisors, Secretariat, Conditions, and IAPOA ADC Committee. It recognizes Project ADCs and Ideas Competitions, and allows classifications such as single-stage or two-stage, and open or limited competitions.

## IAPOA Approval Comes First

An ADC must be approved by the IAPOA before it is announced. This is a common LEA trap: the announcement is not the first professional-control step; IAPOA approval must precede it.

## Conditions and Anonymity

The Competition Conditions govern the requirements, submission rules, awards, and obligations. SPP 208 protects anonymity so that entries can be judged fairly. The Professional Advisor helps formulate and administer the Conditions but does not vote as a Jury member.

## Jury Composition

The Jury must have an odd number of members, with a maximum of seven. Architects must form the majority. In a seven-member Jury, four must be Architects. There must be an IAPOA representative, and not more than one representative of the Owner. In a two-stage competition, the same Jury evaluates both stages.

## Awards, Honoraria, and Commission

SPP 208 governs prizes and honoraria. When a competition leads to a commission, the first-prize winner normally receives the commission. If the project is not implemented within the SPP period stated for compensation, the winner is compensated under the competition rules.

## Copyright and Execution

Copyright remains a central professional issue in an ADC. An awarded design may be used only under the conditions of the competition, and SPP 208 restricts repeated execution of the same design without proper authority.

## Public Exhibition and Disputes

SPP 208 requires the designs, including disqualified entries, to be exhibited as a general rule for at least two weeks with the signed Jury report. The exhibition is open to the public free of charge. Disputes must first be resolved through ADR modes under RA 9285 before court action.

## LEA Traps

- IAPOA approval is required before announcement.
- The Professional Advisor has an administrative and advisory role, not a Jury vote.
- The Jury must be odd-numbered, with architects in the majority and a maximum of seven members.
- Exhibition is public and generally lasts at least two weeks.
- ADR under RA 9285 comes before litigation.
