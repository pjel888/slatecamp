## Grounds for Suspension and Revocation of the Certificate of Registration
## Learning Goal
After reading this article, you should understand the key concepts tested in the LEA for this topic, including the four statutory grounds under RA 9266 Section 23, the distinction between suspension and revocation, and the procedural framework for disciplinary action.

The Certificate of Registration grants architects the legal authority to practice, sign and seal documents, and represent themselves as licensed professionals. The Board of Architecture may suspend or revoke registration upon discovery of grounds establishing violation of law, ethics violations, or professional incompetence.

### RA 9266 Section 23 Statutory Grounds

Section 23 of RA 9266 establishes four distinct grounds (a through d) for suspension or revocation. Each ground independently supports Board action.

[SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK:ui_0001 component="LawCallout" status="preserved"]

**Unauthorized Signing and Sealing (UAP Code of Ethical Conduct, Canon II Sec 2)**
An architect shall not sign and seal plans or documents that the architect did not prepare or that were not prepared under direct supervision. This prohibition protects public safety by ensuring accountability for design content. "Direct supervision" requires the architect's personal involvement in design decisions, not merely administrative review of completed work.

**Aiding Unauthorized Practice (UAP Code of Ethical Conduct, Canon II Sec 3)**
An architect shall not aid or abet unauthorized practice of architecture. Examples include allowing an unlicensed employee to negotiate with clients for architecture services or permitting a non-architect to seal documents under the architect's name.

**Fraud or Deceit in Obtaining Registration (RA 9266 Sec 23(b))**
This ground addresses the integrity of the licensing system itself. Examples include submitting a diploma from an unaccredited institution falsely represented as accredited, falsifying apprenticeship records, cheating on the licensure examination, or making false statements in the registration application.

[SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK:ui_0002 component="Warn" status="preserved"]

**Gross Negligence, Incompetence, or Recklessness (RA 9266 Sec 23(c))**
This ground does not require intentional misconduct — it addresses the quality and diligence of professional work itself. Simple negligence (a mistake or error in judgment) does NOT constitute grounds. The standard requires gross negligence — a clear, substantial, and unjustified departure from professional standards.

[SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK:ui_0003 component="LawCallout" status="preserved"]

### Suspension vs. Revocation Distinction

**Suspension** temporarily removes the architect's legal authority to practice. After suspension expires or the architect demonstrates compliance restoration, practice authority may resume.

**Revocation** permanently withdraws the Certificate. The architect must retake the licensure examination to reregister.

### The Two-Year Reinstatement Opportunity

RA 9266 Section 24 permits reinstatement after two years from the revocation date upon application and Board finding that reasons for revocation have been remedied. However, the Board retains discretion to deny reinstatement if the architect has not genuinely remedied the conduct justifying revocation.

### LEA Test Points

1. **Direct supervision scope** — Reviewing final documents prepared by junior architects without participating in design phases is not supervision
2. **Aiding unauthorized practice** — Permitting unlicensed staff to perform licensed work
3. **Service agreement necessity** — Handshake agreements create vulnerability to exploitation allegations
4. **Motu proprio investigation** — Board may investigate without awaiting formal written complaint
5. **Sworn complaint requirement** — Unsworn allegations alone cannot initiate investigation
6. **Recklessness standard** — Sealing plans with obvious design flaws without review constitutes recklessness
