# Signing and Sealing: Immediate Supervision, Delegation, and Discipline

## LEA Point

RA 9266 treats the architect's signature and seal as a professional accountability act, not a clerical stamp. An architect may not sign or seal architectural plans, specifications, or other documents prepared under another architect's supervision unless the documents clearly identify the part actually performed by the signing architect. The Board may suspend or revoke a certificate or PIC when an architect signs or seals documents not personally prepared or not prepared under immediate supervision.

## Source Anchors

- RA 9266 IRR Section 20 prohibits signing or sealing architectural plans, specifications, or other documents made under another architect's supervision unless the part actually performed is clearly indicated.
- RA 9266 IRR Section 20 states that the architect-of-record is fully responsible for architectural plans, specifications, and other documents issued under the architect's seal or authorized signature.
- RA 9266 IRR Section 23 gives the Board power, after notice and hearing, to suspend or revoke a Certificate of Registration or Professional Identification Card, including where an architect signs and seals documents not prepared personally or not prepared under immediate supervision.
- RA 9266 IRR Section 32 links the architect-of-record signature, official seal, professional numbers, and membership number to architectural plans, specifications, permit documents, and architectural contract documents.

## Delegation Is Not the Problem

A registered architect may use staff, junior designers, or drafting support. The problem begins when the architect signs and seals work the architect did not personally prepare and did not supervise as it developed. Delegation must remain traceable to the architect's professional control.

## Real World Scenario: Borrowed Drawings Before Permit Filing

A client brings a nearly complete architectural set prepared by another office and asks a new architect to "just sign it" so the permit can be filed. The source-safe answer is no unless the architect clearly limits the signature to parts actually performed or brings the work under real professional control before signing. If the architect signs and seals the whole set as architect-of-record, Section 20 and Section 32 attach responsibility to documents issued under that seal or authorized signature. If the set was not personally prepared or executed under immediate supervision, Section 23 creates suspension or revocation exposure after notice and hearing.

## Exam Guardrails

Do not treat the seal as a rubber stamp. Ask whether the signing architect personally prepared the documents or immediately supervised their preparation. Keep the consequence tied to Section 23: suspension or revocation after notice and hearing.
