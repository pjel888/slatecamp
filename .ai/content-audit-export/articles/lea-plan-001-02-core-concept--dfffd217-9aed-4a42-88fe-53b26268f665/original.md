## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal
Distinguish planning by scale (national to site), time horizon (strategic 10+ yr, tactical 1-5 yr, operational 0-1 yr), and approach (rational comprehensive, incremental, advocacy, communicative, radical), and classify given planning scenarios by each dimension.

Planning classifies into distinct types based on scale (how large an area), time horizon (how far ahead), and approach (how decisions are made). 

## Core Concept

Planning operates across a spectrum of scales, time horizons, and methodologies. By **scale**, planning ranges from the macro (national, regional) down to the micro (neighborhood, site). National or regional planning frames broad policy — infrastructure networks, resource protection, settlement hierarchies. City-level planning (municipal comprehensive plans) coordinates development, utilities, and services. District-level planning addresses neighborhood character and district-specific policies. Site-level planning organizes a single development project's internal layout.

By **time horizon**, planning is categorized as strategic (long-term vision, typically 10+ years), tactical (mid-term initiatives, 1–5 years), or operational (day-to-day implementation, 0–1 year). Strategic planning asks "where do we want to be in 20 years?" Tactical planning answers "what projects will move us in that direction?" Operational planning handles permits, budgets, and daily management.

By **approach**, planning frameworks vary. **Rational comprehensive planning** assumes extensive data collection and analysis lead to optimal plans — the postwar dominant model. **Incremental planning** (Lindblom, 1959) recognizes that comprehensive rationality is impossible; planners make small successive decisions. **Advocacy planning** (Davidoff, 1965) asserts planners represent communities, not abstract ideals. **Communicative planning** (Heleay, 1990s) treats planning as dialogue and consensus-building, not technical expertise. **Radical planning** (Clavel, Forester, 1970s–80s) explicitly challenges power structures in service of social justice.

In the Philippines, planning is mandatory at all administrative levels under **RA 7160** (Local Government Code, 1991). The **CLUP (Comprehensive Land Use Plan)** is the required strategic planning instrument at provincial, city, and municipal levels, covering a 10-year horizon. The CLUP is distinctly _strategic_ (not tactical or operational) and is implemented through the zoning ordinance and sectoral plans (transportation, infrastructure, housing). This hierarchy — national framework → regional plans → CLUPs → zoning ordinances → site-level designs — organizes planning authority and aligns development across scales.

## Philippine Context

The **Local Government Code (RA 7160, 1991)** devolved planning authority to all local government units, mandating **Comprehensive Land Use Plans (CLUPs)** at provincial, city, and municipal levels. The CLUP is a strategic 10-year plan; municipalities also prepare **barangay development plans** (tactical, sector-specific initiatives). Implementation occurs through the **zoning ordinance** — adopted by the Sanggunian (city/municipal council) and specifying permitted uses, densities, and setbacks. The zoning ordinance is the legal tool translating the CLUP's vision into regulatory requirements for individual projects.

**DHSUD** (formerly HLURB) reviews municipal and city CLUPs for alignment with national policy. **Environmental Planners** (licensed profession under RA 10587, 2013) hold statutory mandate for CLUP preparation and environmental integration. This framework ensures planning scales nest — national guidelines → provincial plans → municipal CLUPs → zoning ordinances → site-level development — with clear authority and tools at each level.

## How It's Tested

The LEA tests planning classifications through scenario questions: "A city council is reviewing a 10-year land use vision with zoning recommendations — what type of planning is this?" (Answer: Strategic CLUP implementation). "A barangay identifies specific streets for pedestrian improvement over the next 2 years — what planning type?" (Answer: Tactical). "A developer designs a 5-hectare mixed-use site with streets, buildings, and utilities — what planning scale?" (Answer: Site-level). Questions rarely ask for pure definitions; instead, they provide contexts and expect classification. The key distinction tested: strategic (long-term, citywide vision) vs. tactical (medium-term specific projects) vs. operational (day-to-day management).

<Tip>
**Time horizon memory:** STRATEGIC = 10–20 years (think 2045 vision). TACTICAL = 1–5 years (specific projects starting soon). OPERATIONAL = 0–1 year (permits, budgets, now). Always match the time horizon to the classification.
</Tip>


**Exam Tip:** The LEA tests classification through scenarios, not definitions. Always ask: what time horizon (10+ yr = strategic, 1-5 yr = tactical, 0-1 yr = operational)? What approach (rational, incremental, advocacy, communicative)?
