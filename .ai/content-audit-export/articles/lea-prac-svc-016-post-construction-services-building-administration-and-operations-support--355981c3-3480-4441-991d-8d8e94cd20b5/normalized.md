# Post-Construction Services, Building Administration, and Operations Support

SPP Document 205 is the source anchor for Post-construction Services. In the compiled UAP Standards, post-construction work keeps communication open among building owners, operators, architects, engineers, builders, manufacturers, building research groups, utility providers, vendors, and building administrators or property managers so information about performance and upkeep remains available.

The post-construction service scope has two main source categories: Building and Facilities Administration and Post-Construction Evaluation.

Building and Facilities Administration includes building maintenance, grounds and landscaping supervision, building equipment maintenance, and business development and management. The architect may see that the building structure, plumbing, electrical, partitions, finishes, and other parts are in good condition; formulate and enforce rules for proper use of the building and facility; monitor security, cleanliness, exits, parking, and garbage collection; supervise landscape maintenance; maintain orderly entrances, exits, parking areas, streets, walkways, and ramps; assist proper third parties with equipment maintenance; and support occupancy and tenant billing functions.

Post-Construction Evaluation is different. Upon the owner's request, the architect may evaluate the initial design program against actual facility use, determine the effectiveness of building systems and materials, assist proper third parties in evaluating design and construction effectiveness, and study defects or failures such as shrinkage and water seepage. The source refers to this defect-focused work as forensic investigation of building systems, with reference to SPP 203.

For LEA review, do not collapse post-construction services into construction-phase site visits. Construction-phase services test conformity while the work is being executed; post-construction services support the occupied building's performance, upkeep, administration, and evaluation after construction.

## Real World Scenario

After turnover, a mixed-use building owner asks an architect to help the property team handle recurring complaints about corridor cleanliness, parking circulation, landscape upkeep, equipment maintenance, and tenant billing coordination. Under the checked SPP 205 sources, the safer classification is post-construction building and facilities administration, not construction-phase inspection. The architect can help keep communication channels open and coordinate building maintenance, grounds and landscaping, equipment maintenance through proper third parties, and business or occupancy support, while defect-focused review stays in post-construction evaluation when the owner requests it.
