# Alvar Aalto

## LEA Point

Alvar Aalto is the exam bridge between Functionalism and a warmer, human-centered modernism. He remains a modern architect, but the testable difference is that his buildings and furniture soften modern planning through patient comfort, tactile materials, organic form, and site response.

## Source Anchors

- Aalto Foundation anchors Aalto as a Finnish architect and designer whose work moved from Functionalism toward user-friendly design shaped by human needs.
- Paimio Sanatorium is anchored to 1929-1933, tuberculosis care, patient-oriented details, sanatorium furniture, and the Aaltos' international breakthrough.
- Artek anchors Armchair 42, or Small Paimio, to Aalto in 1932, with form-bent birch and form-pressed plywood.
- Villa Mairea is anchored to 1938-1939, Aino and Alvar Aalto, wood and stone materials, free-form entry, flowing domestic spaces, and garden relationship.
- Finlandia Hall is anchored as Aalto-designed and completed in 1971, with white Carrara marble, black granite contrast, civic symbolism, and Helsinki landscape setting.

## Exam Pattern

If the stem emphasizes humane modernism, tuberculosis care, bent plywood furniture, warm natural materials, or a forest/garden-responsive house, choose Aalto. If the stem says Paimio, use 1929-1933. If it says Villa Mairea, use 1938-1939. If it says Finlandia Hall, avoid reducing the building to marble alone; the safer cue is civic-cultural setting plus Aalto's complete work of architecture and interior design.

## Guardrails

Do not call Aalto anti-modern. Do not treat organic curves as Art Nouveau ornament when the stem is about comfort, structure, furniture, or health. Do not make Finlandia Hall a structural-marble question; the marble is an exterior and interior finish within a civic-cultural composition.

## Real World Scenario

For a clinic wing, a source-safe Aalto precedent would ask how daylight, patient-room color, quiet fittings, furniture, and natural materials reduce institutional coldness while preserving efficient modern planning.
