# The Contract Document Package: Who Draws and Who Signs Each Component

## LEA Point

A construction package is not one generic stack of papers. RA 9266 identifies architectural documents that only an architect may sign and seal. UAP Document 301 identifies contract documents as coordinated instruments for bidding and construction, with drawings, specifications, general conditions, special provisions, and related bid documents working together.

## Source Anchors

- RA 9266 IRR Section 3(18): architectural documents are architectural drawings, specifications, and other outputs of an architect that only an architect can sign and seal.
- RA 9266 IRR Section 3(26): contract documents generally include special provisions or conditions, general conditions, drawings, specifications, and other bid documents.
- RA 9266 IRR Sections 20 and 32: architectural plans, specifications, permit documents, and related architectural contract documents must bear the architect's signature and seal.
- UAP Document 301: drawings, specifications, general conditions, special provisions, supplementary specifications, and related bid documents are part of the contract-document system.
- UAP Document 301: contract documents are complementary, and drawings and specifications should be read together to prescribe the complete work.

## Coordination Rule

When drawings and specifications conflict, the professional answer is coordination and clarification before final issuance or reliance. A conflict is not solved by ignoring another discipline, signing assumptions as final, or pushing the issue to the contractor after bidding.

## Shop Drawing Limit

UAP Document 301 also keeps shop-drawing approval distinct from contractor responsibility. Approval does not relieve the contractor of responsibility for accuracy, proper fitting and construction, materials, deviations, or errors unless deviations were called to attention and approved in writing.

## Exam Guardrails

Identify whether the document is architectural, another discipline's technical drawing, or a general contract document. Architectural documents require the architect's signature and seal. Contract documents are complementary and must be coordinated.

### Real World Scenario: Signing a coordinated but borrowed sheet

A project team sends an architect a nearly complete architectural sheet set prepared under another architect's supervision and asks for a signature and seal after minor local edits. The RA 9266 IRR makes that a signing-and-sealing problem: architectural documents must be prepared by or under the architect's direct supervision, and signing documents made under another architect's supervision is unlawful unless the actual part performed is clearly indicated. UAP Document 301 also treats drawings and specifications as coordinated contract documents, but that contract-document workflow does not override the statutory signing/sealing rule.
