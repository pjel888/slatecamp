## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal
Classify resorts and eco-tourism facilities as Group B, apply DOT hospitality standards alongside NBC fire safety provisions, calculate parking ratios (1 slot/room for de luxe), and verify BP 344 accessible features.

Resorts and eco-tourism facilities operate under **Group B** (hotel/dormitory/pension) occupancy classification. This requires careful application of Department of Tourism (DOT) standards alongside National Building Code fire safety provisions. Parking, wayfinding, and landscape integration are critical differentiators from standard hospitality projects.

Resorts and eco-tourism facilities fall under **Group B** occupancy per **PD 1096**. They must comply with both DOT hotel classification standards and specific fire safety egress requirements. Unlike standard commercial hotels, these facilities emphasize landscape integration and environmental stewardship. A resort typically combines overnight accommodation with dining, recreation, and nature-based activities within a single site.

Key occupancy determinants include guest room count, assembly areas like dining halls or function spaces, and outdoor recreation facilities. Per **PD 1096 Rule XVIII**, the parking requirement for hotels de luxe is **1 slot per room**, while first-class hotels require **1 slot per 2 rooms**.

Fire escape routes must accommodate a **45 m** travel distance in unsprinklered areas or **60 m** where sprinkler systems are installed per the **RA 9514 2019 Revised IRR**. Exit width is calculated at **7.6 mm per occupant**, and double egress is mandated once occupancy exceeds 50 persons. The standard floor-to-floor height for resort corridors is typically **3.0 m**.

Accessible features under **BP 344** include ramps at a **1:12 maximum slope** per DPWH MC No. 03 s. 2025. Accessible door widths must be a minimum of **900 mm**. Per **BP 344 2024 Revised IRR §1004/§1005**, accessible parking stalls must be **3.7–4.0 m width × 5.0 m length**, compared to standard stalls at **2.5 m × 5.0 m**.

**Landscape Integration Formula:**

- Outdoor recreation area should equal or exceed 30% of the total site area.
- Pathways connecting lodging to facilities must have a minimum width of 1.5 m.
- Naturalized stormwater management is required in eco-tourism zones, avoiding concrete drainage channels.

## Philippine Practice Context

DOT-classified eco-tourism facilities prioritize environmental certification alongside fire code compliance. A common challenge is reconciling high-density developer interests with strict fire exit capacity limits.

- **Beach Resorts:** Must account for marine access routes that remain separate from building egress.
- **Mountain Resorts:** Face terrain-driven variations in floor-to-floor heights and ramp placement.
- **Heritage Properties:** Repurposed colonial structures often require NHCP review to ensure cultural preservation.

1. **Occupancy Trap:** Do not confuse Group B (resort) with Group E (restaurant) even if the resort has prominent dining areas. Always classify based on the primary use—overnight accommodation is Group B.
2. **Parking Calculation:** A de luxe resort with 150 rooms requires 150 spaces. A first-class resort with 100 rooms requires 50 spaces. Avoid applying restaurant ratios (1/30 sqm) to overall resort parking.
3. **Egress Integration:** Outdoor deck areas used as assembly spaces must be counted in the occupancy load. Egress paths from these areas must still meet the 45/60 m travel distance limits.

> **Resort Occupancy Anchor:** Group B = overnight stays. Parking (Rule XVIII): de luxe = 1/room; first class = 1/2 rooms. Egress: 7.6 mm/person, 45 m unsprinklered, 60 m sprinklered. Double exit required at 50+ persons.

## Common Mistakes

- **ERR-DSGN-001:** Classifying a hotel/resort as Group E (mercantile) instead of Group B.
- **ERR-DSGN-019:** Using 5 mm/person for exit width instead of the required **7.6 mm/person**.
- **ERR-DSGN-021:** Allowing a single exit for spaces with an occupancy of 50 or more persons.
- **ERR-DSGN-014:** Designing ramps with a 1:10 or 1:20 slope; the slope must be **1:12 maximum** per DPWH MC No. 03 s. 2025.
- **ERR-DSGN-015:** Designing accessible doors at 800 mm instead of the required **900 mm** minimum.
