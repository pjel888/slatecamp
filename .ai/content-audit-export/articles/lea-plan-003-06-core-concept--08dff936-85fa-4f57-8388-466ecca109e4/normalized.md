# Informal Settlements, On-Site Upgrading, and Resettlement

## Board Exam Quick Check

When an LEA item mentions informal settlers, identify the legal action first: on-site development, eviction or demolition, relocation or resettlement, or community mortgage. Then check whether the site is a danger area and whether the required safeguards are present.

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

RA 7279 defines on-site development as upgrading and rehabilitation of blighted and slum urban areas while minimizing displacement and providing basic services. The law directs that on-site development be implemented whenever possible to ensure minimum movement of occupants. Resettlement from the existing place of occupancy is undertaken only when on-site development is not feasible and after compliance with the eviction and demolition procedures.

RA 7279 discourages eviction and demolition, but allows it in listed situations: occupation of danger areas such as esteros, railroad tracks, garbage dumps, riverbanks, shorelines, waterways, and certain public places; government infrastructure projects with available funding; or a court order.

For eviction or demolition involving underprivileged and homeless citizens, RA 7279 requires safeguards including at least 30 days notice, consultation on resettlement, presence of local government officials, proper identification of demolition personnel, regular office hours and good weather unless families consent otherwise, limits on heavy equipment, proper police uniforms, and adequate relocation whether temporary or permanent.

RA 7279 also requires local governments, with the National Housing Authority, to implement relocation and resettlement of persons living in danger areas and to provide relocation or resettlement sites with basic services and facilities plus access to employment and livelihood opportunities.

The Community Mortgage Program assists legally organized associations of underprivileged and homeless citizens to purchase and develop land under community ownership. Its objective is to help residents of blighted or depressed areas own the lots they occupy, or where they choose to relocate, and improve their neighborhood and homes according to affordability.

For waterways, PD 1067 states that banks of rivers and streams and shores of seas and lakes are subject to public-use easements: 3 m in urban areas, 20 m in agricultural areas, and 40 m in forest areas. No person may build structures of any kind in those easement zones.

## How It Is Tested

- On-site development is the first preference when feasible.
- Danger-area occupancy may justify eviction or demolition, but RA 7279 still requires safeguards.
- Section 29 resettlement language matters: danger-area households are not simply left without relocation planning.
- PD 1067 easement distances depend on area classification: urban, agricultural, or forest.
- CMP is community ownership and land purchase/development through an organized association.

## Common Mistakes

- Saying danger-area occupants have no RA 7279 protections.
- Using a 40 m water easement for every urban waterway.
- Treating relocation as the default even when on-site development is feasible.
- Forgetting the 30-day notice and consultation requirements.
- Defining CMP as a private individual mortgage instead of a community ownership mechanism.
