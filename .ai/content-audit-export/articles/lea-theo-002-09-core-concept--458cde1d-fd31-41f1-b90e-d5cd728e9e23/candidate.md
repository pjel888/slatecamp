# Enclosure and Openings by Francis D.K. Ching

## Source-Audited Scope

Batch 055 limits this article to checked theory passages on openings, enclosure, light, view, and perception. The checked source ties openings to rooms, views, visual relationships, ventilation, and degree of enclosure.

## Board Exam Quick Check

For LEA theory questions, treat openings as spatial modifiers. They affect entry, light, view, ventilation, visual relationships, degree of enclosure, orientation, and the perceived form of space.

## Core Concepts

- The checked source names windows and doors as openings within a spatial enclosure.
- Windows allow light to penetrate, offer exterior views, establish visual relationships with adjacent spaces, and provide ventilation.
- The quality of degree of enclosure, light, and view is affected by the size, shape, and location of openings.
- Degree of enclosure is determined by defining elements and the pattern of openings.
- Degree of enclosure affects perception of orientation and the overall form of space.
- The checked TOA source lists surfaces, edges, and openings among qualities of architectural space.
- The checked theory source includes perceptual qualities such as sensory perception, light, color, texture, view, and sound.

## LEA Use

If a question asks why window size or position matters, answer in terms of light, view, ventilation, visual relationship, and degree of enclosure. If it asks how enclosure changes perception, point to defining elements and opening patterns.

## Active Recall

1. What do windows provide in the checked source? Light, view, visual relationship, and ventilation.
2. What affects degree of enclosure, light, and view? Size, shape, and location of openings.
3. What does degree of enclosure affect? Perception of orientation and the overall form of space.
