## Board Exam Quick Check

For Article 1723 questions, identify the failure source first: **plans/specifications or ground defects**, **construction defects or inferior materials**, or **supervised construction**. Then apply the two time rules: collapse within fifteen years from completion, and action within ten years following collapse.

## Civil Code Article 1723: The 15-Year Liability Framework

Civil Code Article 1723 is the central building-collapse liability rule for architects, engineers, and contractors. This article is source-audited against the Civil Code text and RA 9266 IRR provisions that define Architect-of-Record and Architect-in-Charge of Construction.

### The Fifteen-Year Collapse Window

Article 1723 states that the engineer or architect who drew up the plans and specifications for a building is liable for damages if, within fifteen years from completion of the structure, the building collapses because of defects in those plans and specifications or defects in the ground.

For exam use, the first clock is not measured from contract signing or permit issuance. It is measured from completion of the structure. If the collapse happens after the fifteen-year window, Article 1723 does not supply the collapse-liability rule for that fact pattern.

### Plans, Specifications, and Ground Defects

When the collapse is by reason of defective plans and specifications or defects in the ground, the source places liability on the engineer or architect who drew up those plans and specifications. Avoid overstating the rule as liability for every future defect. The article is about collapse within the fifteen-year window and the listed causes.

### Contractor Liability

The contractor is likewise responsible for damages if the edifice falls within the same fifteen-year period because of defects in construction, use of inferior-quality materials furnished by the contractor, or violation of the terms of the contract.

### Solidary Liability When the Architect or Engineer Supervises

Article 1723 adds that if the engineer or architect supervises the construction, that professional is solidarily liable with the contractor. RA 9266 IRR Section 3(2)(b) defines an Architect-in-Charge of Construction as the architect directly and professionally responsible and liable for construction supervision.

### Acceptance Does Not Waive the Cause of Action

The Civil Code states that acceptance of the building after completion does not imply waiver of any cause of action by reason of the defects mentioned in Article 1723. Acceptance is therefore not an automatic defense when the collapse and cause fit the article.

### The Ten-Year Action Period

Article 1723 also states that the action must be brought within ten years following the collapse of the building. The two clocks work together: the collapse must occur within fifteen years from completion, and the lawsuit must be brought within ten years after that collapse.

### RA 9266 Connection

RA 9266 IRR Section 3(2)(a) defines the Architect-of-Record as directly and professionally responsible for the total design and as assuming civil liability for signed and sealed plans, specifications, and contract documents. Section 32 states that the AoR's signature, seal, PTR, PRC registration number, and IAPOA membership information on architectural documents signify assumption of the fifteen-year civil liability under Article 1723.

### Real World Scenario

A completed building collapses twelve years after turnover. If the collapse is traced to defective plans or specifications, start with the architect or engineer who drew them; if it is traced to inferior materials furnished by the contractor, start with the contractor; if the architect or engineer also supervised the construction, test for solidary liability with the contractor. The lawsuit still has its own clock: it must be brought within ten years following the collapse.

### Exam Takeaway

Do not answer Article 1723 questions with a generic negligence rule. Match the defect source, check whether collapse occurred within fifteen years from completion, and then apply the ten-year period following collapse for filing the action.
