# Signs Design and Construction

## Board Exam Quick Check

For PD 1096 sign questions, first identify the sign type and whether the issue is traffic confusion, maintenance, structural design, supports, projection, clearance, obstruction, or lighting. The controlling local source for this article is the 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096, Rule XX Sections 2001 through 2006.

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to apply the Rule XX standards for sign location, structural support, projection, clearance, material restrictions, and illumination.

## Source-Grounded Core Rules

### General requirements

No sign or signboard may be erected in a way that confuses or obstructs the view or interpretation of an official traffic sign, signal, or device. Signs written in a foreign language must have a corresponding translation in English or in the local dialect.

Adjacent signboards must follow a common bottom baseline as determined by the Building Official. Sign installation must present a harmonious and aesthetic relationship among the units.

### Maintenance and structural design

All signs, including supports, braces, guys, and anchors, must be kept in repair and in proper state of preservation. The display must be neatly painted and secured at all times.

Sign structures must be designed and constructed to resist all forces in accordance with the National Structural Code for Buildings. For signs on buildings, dead and lateral loads must be transmitted through the building structural frame to the ground without overstressing building elements.

### Supports and anchorages

Supports and anchorages of signs or sign structures must be placed in or upon private property and must conform to Code requirements.

Sign structures may be constructed only where zoning regulations permit them and in accordance with accepted standards of design, construction, and maintenance.

### Roof signs

Roof sign design and construction must conform to Section 1210. No sign may be erected, attached, installed, or fastened on rooftops of wooden structures or buildings with wooden roof framing. Metallic roof-sign parts exposed to lightning must be grounded, and warning or obstruction lights for air traffic must be installed where applicable.

### Ground signs

Ground signs and advertising ground signs must conform with accepted engineering standards, and height control must conform with the Local Zoning Regulation. Ground sign structures must stay within the property line and must not occupy the road right-of-way, street, sidewalk, arcade, or similar accessway.

Public or government signs in sidewalk areas must not obstruct pedestrian passage or distract motorists.

### Projecting signs

On non-arcaded roads or streets, signs must not extend more than 1.20 m over the sidewalk, measured horizontally from the wall line or building line.

On arcaded roads or streets, signs must not project more than 0.60 m from the outermost portion of the wall line of the allowed structure over the arcade.

For buildings abutting roads, streets, or alleys without sidewalks or provisions for sidewalks, signs must not project more than 0.30 m from the outermost portion of the building or structure.

A height clearance of at least 3.00 m from the finished road surface is required below the lowest part of signs projecting over sidewalks on buildings without arcades. A clearance of at least 5.00 m is required below the lowest part of signs projecting over arcaded roads or streets.

Electric neon signboards or similar advertisements projecting over roadways or public streets must also satisfy the listed conditions: at least 4.00 m clear distance between signboards on one building, same vertical line on multi-storey buildings, no extension above the parapet top or eave bottom line, no obstruction of windows or emergency exits, and at least 1.00 m clearance from electric and telephone posts and wires.

### Wall signs and materials

Outdoor display signs against the front exterior surface of buildings must not extend more than 300 mm from the wall, and the lowest portion must be at least 3.00 m above the sidewalk. Display windows or wall signs within 3.00 m above the sidewalk must be flush or recessed.

Signs or sign structures in highly restrictive Fire Zones must have structural members of incombustible materials. Combination signs, roof signs, wall signs, projecting signs, and signs on marquees must be constructed of incombustible materials. Electric signs may use only approved plastics as combustible material.

### Projections, clearances, and lighting

Sign clearances from high-voltage power lines must follow the Philippine Electrical Code. No sign may interfere with the free use of a fire escape, exit, or standpipe. A sign must not obstruct an opening so much that light or ventilation falls below Code requirements.

Signs must be illuminated only by electrical means in accordance with the Philippine Electrical Code.

## Exam Values To Memorize

| Requirement | Source-grounded value |
|---|---:|
| Projecting sign over non-arcaded sidewalk | Maximum 1.20 m projection |
| Projecting sign over arcade | Maximum 0.60 m from outermost wall line over arcade |
| Sign where there is no sidewalk/provision | Maximum 0.30 m projection |
| Clearance below projecting sign, no arcade | At least 3.00 m |
| Clearance below projecting sign, arcaded street | At least 5.00 m |
| Neon signs on same building | At least 4.00 m clear distance |
| Neon sign distance from adjacent building common boundary | At least 2.00 m |
| Neon sign clearance from electric/telephone posts and wires | At least 1.00 m |
| Wall sign projection | Maximum 300 mm |
| Lowest wall-sign portion above sidewalk | At least 3.00 m |

## Common Exam Traps

- Do not use 1.00 m as the non-arcaded projecting-sign limit for this source. The checked DPWH text says 1.20 m.
- Do not say arcaded signs must be flush. The checked source allows up to 0.60 m from the outermost wall line over the arcade and requires 5.00 m clearance.
- Do not forget the 0.30 m projection limit for buildings abutting roads, streets, or alleys without sidewalks.
- Do not allow signs to interfere with exits, fire escapes, standpipes, required light, or required ventilation.

## Real World Scenario

A retail tenant wants a new illuminated sign on a commercial building that faces both an arcade and a non-arcaded sidewalk. Treat it as a Rule XX classification problem before checking aesthetics. Identify whether each proposed face is a wall, projecting, roof, or ground sign; apply the 1.20 m, 0.60 m, or 0.30 m projection rule based on the street condition; keep the required 3.00 m or 5.00 m clearance; and reject any sign that blocks required light, ventilation, exits, fire escapes, standpipes, or pedestrian passage. Illumination still has to follow the Philippine Electrical Code path required by the source.
