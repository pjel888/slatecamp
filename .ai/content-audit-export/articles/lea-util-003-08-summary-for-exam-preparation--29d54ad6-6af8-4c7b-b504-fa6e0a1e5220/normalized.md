## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Solar photovoltaic systems in the PEC are electrical systems, not only roof-mounted energy products. Article 6.90 covers solar PV energy systems, including array circuits, inverters, controllers, storage interface equipment, and systems that may be interactive with a utility network or stand-alone.

For LEA purposes, start with the circuit vocabulary. A PV source circuit runs from modules to the common connection point. A PV output circuit runs from the source-circuit connection point to the inverter or dc utilization equipment. An inverter changes dc to ac. A charge controller manages battery charging, and a building-integrated PV product is part of the building exterior surface.

Design review should then move to electrical separation and current sizing. PV source and output conductors are not treated as ordinary branch-circuit wiring. PEC guidance separates PV source and output circuits from feeders and branch circuits unless the circuits are separated by a partition or are connected together. PV source-circuit maximum current is based on the sum of parallel module short-circuit currents multiplied by 125 percent; PV output and inverter output currents are then coordinated with the equipment that receives or supplies the output.

Disconnects and markings are core exam issues. PV disconnecting means must be grouped and marked, with the grouped disconnecting means limited to six switches or circuit breakers. Separate equipment disconnecting means are also required for inverters, batteries, charge controllers, and similar equipment so maintenance can be performed without leaving energized conductors unaccounted for.

Roof-mounted dc arrays on dwellings require ground-fault protection that detects, interrupts, and indicates a fault. Grounding and bonding are also part of the PV design answer: metal frames, equipment, and exposed conductive parts need an equipment grounding path, and grounded dc circuits must follow the permitted grounding point. For interactive systems, the inverter must stop energizing the utility connection when utility voltage is lost.

RA 11285 places PV in a broader energy-efficiency context. The law promotes energy efficiency and conservation and includes efficient renewable energy technologies within the national policy framework. Designated establishments may include renewable energy technologies in their energy management plans, but the LEA answer should still keep the electrical safety sequence clear: define the PV system, size circuits, provide disconnects, protect against ground faults, ground equipment, and coordinate interactive operation with the utility.

## LEA Memory Anchors

- PV array, inverter, charge controller, battery, and utility-interactive equipment form one coordinated electrical system.
- Source-circuit maximum current uses the sum of parallel module short-circuit currents times 125 percent.
- PV disconnecting means are grouped, marked, and limited to six switches or breakers in the grouped set.
- Roof-mounted dwelling dc arrays need ground-fault detection, interruption, and indication.
- Interactive inverters must stop energizing the utility connection when utility voltage is lost.
