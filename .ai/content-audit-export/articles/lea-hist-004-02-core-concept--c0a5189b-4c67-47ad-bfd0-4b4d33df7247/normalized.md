# Umayyad and Abbasid Architecture

## LEA Point
Umayyad and Abbasid architecture marks two early Islamic imperial building languages. For the LEA, read the Umayyad phase as Mediterranean, Syrian, Byzantine, and Roman-adaptive; read the Abbasid phase as Mesopotamian, Persian-facing, brick-based, palace-scale, and urban-planning oriented.

## Source Anchors
The Dome of the Rock is the safest Umayyad sacred-monument anchor. UNESCO source-maps it as a 7th-century monument in Jerusalem with geometric and floral decoration. In exam logic, it is a central-plan shrine, not a normal Friday mosque.

The Great Mosque of Damascus is the safest Umayyad congregational mosque anchor. UNESCO source-maps it as an 8th-century Great Mosque of the Umayyads and a masterpiece of Umayyad architecture. Use it for early monumental mosque planning and adaptation of older sacred sites.

Umayyad work often uses existing Roman, Byzantine, or local resources: reused columns, mosaics, hypostyle planning, and courtly desert complexes. The answer is adaptation and transformation, not simple copying.

The Abbasid shift begins politically in 750 and is reinforced by the move toward Baghdad and Mesopotamia. The exam contrast is not just date order; it is a change in cultural center and building technology.

UNESCO source-maps Samarra as the Abbasid second capital after Baghdad, preserving large mosques, unusual minarets, palaces, carved stucco, and city planning. Use Samarra for Abbasid scale, brick and stucco culture, palace complexity, and the spiral Malwiya minaret.

## Exam Pattern
If the item mentions Dome of the Rock, Jerusalem, a central octagon, and a gilded dome, answer Umayyad. If it mentions Damascus, an early monumental mosque, hypostyle planning, and reused classical elements, also think Umayyad.

If the item mentions Baghdad, Samarra, brick, stucco, palaces, bureaucratic scale, or a spiral minaret, answer Abbasid.

## Guardrails
Do not make Umayyad and Abbasid architecture a simple progress ladder. Umayyad work is not primitive; it is a sophisticated imperial adaptation of Mediterranean and Byzantine resources. Abbasid work is not merely bigger; it reflects a different political geography and material culture.

Avoid saying muqarnas is fully formed in the earliest Abbasid examples unless a question source explicitly supports that narrower claim.

## Real World Scenario
On a board exam image item, a hypostyle mosque with reused classical columns and rich mosaics should push you toward Umayyad Syria. A vast brick complex with a spiral minaret and palace ruins should push you toward Abbasid Samarra.
