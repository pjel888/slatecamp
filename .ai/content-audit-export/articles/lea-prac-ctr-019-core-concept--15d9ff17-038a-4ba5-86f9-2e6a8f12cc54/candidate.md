# The Certificate of Occupancy

A Certificate of Occupancy is the Building Official's authorization for a completed building, structure, or independent usable part to be occupied or used. Under Rule III, Section 309 of the 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096, occupancy is not a private certificate from the designer or contractor. It is a government permit issued after completion documents, fire-safety clearance, inspection, and fee payment are cleared through the Office of the Building Official.

## Documents submitted to the OBO

For a Certificate of Occupancy application, the owner or permittee submits the application to the Office of the Building Official with the notarized Certificate of Completion, the construction logbook, as-built plans and specifications, and the Building Inspection Sheet. When construction is performed under contract, the Certificate of Completion is also signed by the contractor. The completion documents are signed and sealed by the architect or civil engineer who undertook the full-time inspection and supervision of construction.

## Inspection and fire-safety clearance

The Building Official performs the final inspection using the submitted completion documents, the construction logbook, inspection sheets, and the approved or as-built plans and specifications. The Office of the Building Official also endorses the application to the fire authority for the Fire Safety Inspection Certificate. The source states that the fire authority issues the FSIC within five working days when fire-safety requirements are complied with, and that no Certificate of Occupancy is issued unless the FSIC has been issued.

## Legal effect before and after issuance

The rule is simple: no building or structure may be used or occupied until the Building Official has issued the Certificate of Occupancy. The same rule allows a partial Certificate of Occupancy for the independent portion or portions of a building or structure that can be used separately, provided that those portions will not endanger life, health, and safety of occupants, the public, or adjacent properties.

## Practice takeaway

For LEA practice questions, keep the sequence source-based: completion and inspection records are prepared, the owner applies to the OBO, fire-safety clearance is obtained, the Building Official conducts or completes the final inspection, required fees are paid, and the Building Official issues the Certificate of Occupancy. Do not treat substantial completion, owner acceptance, or architect certification as a substitute for the Building Official's occupancy authorization.
