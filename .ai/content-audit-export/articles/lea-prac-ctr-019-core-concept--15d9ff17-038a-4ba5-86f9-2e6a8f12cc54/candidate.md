# Certificate of Occupancy

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Identify the Certificate of Occupancy as an OBO-issued authorization under the 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096.
- List the completion documents submitted with the application.
- Separate substantial completion, owner acceptance, and architect certification from government occupancy authorization.
- Apply the partial Certificate of Occupancy boundary for independently usable portions.

## Legal Effect

The 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096 states that no building or structure shall be used until the Building Official has issued a Certificate of Occupancy. A partial Certificate of Occupancy may be issued for the use or occupancy of a portion or portions of a building or structure before completion of the entire building.

This makes the Certificate of Occupancy a government authorization, not a private close-out certificate from the architect, contractor, or owner.

## Application Documents

For a Certificate of Occupancy application, the owner or permittee submits the application to the Office of the Building Official with a duly notarized Certificate of Completion, the construction logbook, as-built plans and specifications, and the Building Inspection Sheet. When the work is done by contract, the contractor signs the required documents. The owner's duly licensed architect or civil engineer who undertook full-time inspection and supervision signs and seals the completion documents.

The supervising architect or civil engineer also keeps the daily construction logbook at the jobsite during construction and submits it to the Building Official upon completion, together with as-built plans and other required documents.

## Inspection and Fire-Safety Step

The OBO undertakes final inspection, verification, or review based on the Certificate of Completion, construction logbook, inspection sheets, and original or as-built plans and specifications.

The OBO also endorses the final inspection notification to the city or municipal fire marshal. The checked source states that the fire authority issues the Fire Safety Inspection Certificate within five working days if fire-safety requirements have been complied with. If no action is taken after the prescribed period, the Building Official may issue the Certificate of Occupancy with the condition that fire-safety requirements be complied with within the Fire Code period.

## Fees and Issuance

Before issuance, the OBO prepares the corresponding fees and order of payment. The Building Official issues the Certificate of Occupancy after all required fees are paid.

## Partial Certificate of Occupancy

A partial Certificate of Occupancy may be issued for a portion or portions of a building before the whole building is complete, but only through proper phasing of major independent portions and without posing hazards to occupants, adjacent building residents, or the general public.

## Real World Scenario

An owner wants to occupy an independently usable tower wing after substantial completion, while another wing remains under construction. The source-safe answer is to use the PD 1096 partial Certificate of Occupancy path only if the major independent portion can be used without hazard to occupants, adjacent building residents, or the public; architect certification or owner acceptance alone is not enough.

## Exam Boundary

Do not substitute substantial completion, final completion, a punch list, owner acceptance, or architect certification for a Certificate of Occupancy. For occupancy, the key decision-maker is the Building Official, using the OBO application, inspection, fire-safety, fee, and issuance sequence in the 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096.
