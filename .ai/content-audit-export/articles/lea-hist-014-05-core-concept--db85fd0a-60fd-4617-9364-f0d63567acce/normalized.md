# International Style Modernism in America

## Board Exam Quick Check

Identify whether the item is asking about the International Style specifically or American modernism more broadly. Miesian glass-and-steel regularity is not the same answer as Saarinen's sculptural expression, Kahn's monumentality, Pei's geometric institutional planning, or postmodern critique.

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

The International Style was canonized in the United States through the 1932 Museum of Modern Art exhibition on modern architecture. Its safe exam vocabulary is volume rather than mass, regularity rather than axial historical composition, industrial materials such as steel and glass, and little applied ornament. In American high-rise questions, Mies van der Rohe is the safest anchor for disciplined steel-and-glass modernism and the phrase "less is more."

American modernism, however, was never only International Style. Eero Saarinen used expressive modern structure and sculptural form, as seen in TWA Terminal and the Gateway Arch. Louis Kahn pursued mass, light, and monumentality. I. M. Pei used disciplined geometry and institutional planning, such as the East Building of the National Gallery. Postmodernism later criticized modernism's abstraction and austerity by reintroducing historical reference, symbolism, color, and communicative facades.

## Core Concept

The International Style is a precise subset of modernism. Its exam cue is the rational glass-and-steel vocabulary: thin frame, planar surfaces, regular grids, open volume, and minimal applied ornament. When the clue emphasizes expressive concrete shells, monumental served-and-servant spaces, or historical quotation, choose a broader modernist or postmodern answer.

This distinction matters because many distractors name famous twentieth-century architects correctly but attach the wrong design logic. Kahn and Saarinen are modernists, but they are not tested the same way as Mies. Postmodernism is not simply "after modernism"; it is a critique of modernism's claim that abstraction and austerity were always sufficient.

## Key Exam Anchors

| Anchor | Source-safe recognition cue | LEA use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| International Style | Volume, regularity, industrial materials, little applied ornament | Use for MoMA/Miesian modernism questions. |
| Mies van der Rohe | "Less is more," disciplined glass-and-steel frame | Use for corporate modernism and Seagram-type cues. |
| Saarinen | Sculptural modern form and expressive structure | Use for TWA Terminal or Gateway Arch questions. |
| Kahn | Monumentality, mass, light, and served/servant thinking | Use when the clue is heavy, ordered, and luminous. |
| Pei | Abstract geometry and institutional planning | Use for East Building triangular-site cues. |
| Postmodernism | Historical reference, symbolism, critique of austerity | Use for Johnson/Venturi-type critique questions. |

## Real World Scenario

A licensing review compares four proposals: a gridded glass office tower, a wing-like air terminal, a triangular museum extension, and a facade with historical quotation. The glass tower may fit the International Style. The terminal, museum, and postmodern facade require different modernist or postmodern labels because their design logic is sculptural, geometric-institutional, or symbolic.

## How It Is Tested

Expect direct identification: "less is more," Seagram-like corporate restraint, and glass-and-steel grids point to Mies and the International Style.

Expect comparison questions: Saarinen, Kahn, and Pei show that American modernism had multiple lanes. Do not answer all twentieth-century modern works with the same label.

Expect critique questions: postmodernism questioned whether modernist austerity had become too anonymous, abstract, or culturally thin.

## Common Mistakes

Do not call the Gateway Arch an International Style box. It is a stainless-steel structural monument using an inverted catenary form.

Do not reduce postmodernism to decoration. It is a deliberate argument about meaning, history, symbolism, and communication.

Do not treat modernism as a single style. The source-safe answer often depends on whether the clue emphasizes frame, shell, mass, geometry, or historical reference.

## Key Terms

**International Style** - Modernist vocabulary associated with volume, regularity, industrial materials, and reduced ornament.

**Miesian** - Design associated with Mies van der Rohe: disciplined grids, glass and steel, and "less is more."

**Expressive Modernism** - Modern design using structure and form in a sculptural or symbolic way.

**Postmodernism** - A critique of modernist abstraction that reintroduced history, symbolism, and communicative form.
