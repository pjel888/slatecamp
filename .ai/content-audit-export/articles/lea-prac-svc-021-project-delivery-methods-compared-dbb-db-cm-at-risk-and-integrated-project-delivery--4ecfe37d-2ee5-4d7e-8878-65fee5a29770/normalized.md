## Project Delivery Methods Compared: DBB, DB, CM-at-Risk, and Integrated Project Delivery

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Compare project delivery methods: DBB, DB, CM at Risk, IPD.
- Explain the architect's role in each delivery method.
- Distinguish methods by risk allocation, owner involvement, and sequencing.
- Apply delivery method selection criteria to project scenarios.

### Design-Bid-Build (Traditional)

|Design-Bid-Build separates design and construction into sequential phases. The architect completes design through contract documents. After design approval, contractors bid for construction work, with the lowest responsive bid typically selected. Construction then proceeds under the selected contractor's management.

DBB maintains clear separation of responsibilities. The architect provides design and administers the contract during construction. The contractor bears construction risk and responsibility for schedule and cost. The client receives a completed design before committing construction funds, providing design clarity and competitive construction pricing.

Disadvantages include extended schedule due to sequential phases, limited construction input during design, and potential conflicts when design decisions create construction challenges. This method works well for projects where design clarity and competitive pricing are priorities.

### Design-Build (Integrated)

Design-Build integrates design and construction under single entity responsibility. The architect and builder collaborate throughout design and construction, allowing concurrent planning and value optimization. One contract governs the entire delivery process, simplifying client relationships.

Design-Build compresses schedule through overlapping phases and enables value engineering during design. Cost and schedule risks concentrate with the design-build team. This method suits projects where schedule is critical or where integrated design-construction thinking benefits the project.

### Construction Manager at Risk (CM-at-Risk)

CM-at-Risk represents a hybrid approach. A construction manager participates in design as a consultant, providing construction expertise and cost estimates. After design completion, the CM guarantees a maximum price and manages construction, transitioning from consultant to contractor.

CM-at-Risk allows construction input during design without fully integrating design and construction. The CM assumes construction risk through a guaranteed maximum price while the architect maintains design authority. This method balances design control with construction expertise and cost certainty.

The CM-at-Risk method requires strong communication between architect and CM. When this collaboration functions effectively, the method leverages construction knowledge during design while maintaining clear design authority. The guaranteed maximum price provides cost protection similar to Design-Build with GMC models.

### Integrated Project Delivery (IPD)

Integrated Project Delivery represents the most collaborative approach. Architect, constructor, and owner establish shared goals, shared risk, and shared reward arrangements. Multi-party agreements replace traditional separate contracts.

IPD emphasizes early involvement of all parties, transparent cost and schedule information, and collaborative problem-solving. Risks and benefits are shared among participants, creating alignment toward project success. Early building information modeling and collaborative design sessions are typical.

IPD suits complex projects where stakeholder alignment and collaborative problem-solving are valuable. The method requires high trust, transparent communication, and sophisticated project management. IPD can deliver superior project outcomes when parties are aligned on objectives.

### Comparative Analysis

Design-Bid-Build offers design clarity and competitive pricing but extends schedule and separates construction expertise from design. Design-Build compresses schedule and integrates expertise but concentrates risk and may limit competitive pricing. CM-at-Risk balances these characteristics, providing construction input during design while maintaining design control and cost certainty.

Integrated Project Delivery offers superior collaboration and innovation potential but requires high trust, transparent sharing of information, and sophisticated risk management. IPD works best with experienced teams committed to collaborative delivery.

### Selection Framework

Project characteristics inform delivery method selection. Design-Bid-Build suits projects with stable scope, clear requirements, and priority on competitive pricing. Design-Build suits projects where schedule compression and cost certainty are critical. CM-at-Risk suits projects benefiting from construction input during design and guaranteed pricing. Integrated Project Delivery suits complex projects where stakeholder collaboration and innovation are priorities.

The architect's role varies by delivery method. In DBB, the architect maintains design authority and contract administration. In Design-Build, the architect shares responsibility with the contractor. In CM-at-Risk, the architect collaborates with the CM while retaining design authority. In IPD, the architect is an equal stakeholder in collaborative decision-making.

Understanding these delivery method differences enables architects and clients to select approaches aligned with project objectives and stakeholder capabilities.
