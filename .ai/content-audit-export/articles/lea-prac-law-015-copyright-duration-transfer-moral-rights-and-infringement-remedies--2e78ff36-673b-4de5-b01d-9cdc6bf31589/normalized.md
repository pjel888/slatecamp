# Copyright Duration, Transfer, Moral Rights, and Infringement Remedies

## LEA Point

RA 8293 separates economic copyright from moral rights. Economic rights can be assigned in writing. Moral rights remain personal to the author, are not assignable or licensable, and last for the author's lifetime plus 50 years after death.

## Source Anchors

- RA 8293 Section 213.1 protects works under Sections 172 and 173 during the life of the author and for 50 years after death.
- RA 8293 Section 213.2 uses the life of the last surviving author plus 50 years for joint authorship.
- RA 8293 Section 213.3 uses 50 years from first lawful publication, or from making if unpublished, for anonymous or pseudonymous works when the author is not identified.
- RA 8293 Section 213.4 uses 25 years from making for works of applied art.
- RA 8293 Section 180 allows copyright assignment in whole or in part and requires written indication for inter vivos assignment.
- RA 8293 Section 181 states that copyright is distinct from property in the material object.
- RA 8293 Section 193 lists moral rights: attribution, alteration or withholding before publication, objection to prejudicial distortion or mutilation, and restraint against misuse of the author's name.
- RA 8293 Section 195 allows written waiver of moral rights, but invalidates waivers that permit reputation-injuring altered versions or use of the author's name on a work the author did not create.
- RA 8293 Section 198 states that moral rights last for the author's lifetime plus 50 years after death and are not assignable or subject to license.
- RA 8293 Sections 216 and 226 provide infringement remedies and the four-year damages limit.

## Duration

For ordinary architectural works protected under Section 172, use Section 213.1: life of the author plus 50 years after death. If the question gives a natural-person architect who died in 2010, the term runs to 2060. Do not measure that term from the date of design, completion, occupancy, or registration.

For joint authorship, Section 213.2 uses the life of the last surviving author plus 50 years. For anonymous or pseudonymous works, Section 213.3 uses 50 years from first lawful publication, or 50 years from making if unpublished, unless the author's identity is revealed. Works of applied art have a separate 25-year rule under Section 213.4, so do not import that shorter period into an ordinary architecture question unless the item specifically classifies the work as applied art.

## Transfer And Assignment

Economic copyright may be assigned in whole or in part. Under Section 180.1, the assignee receives the rights and remedies of the assignor within the assignment scope. Under Section 180.2, copyright is not deemed assigned inter vivos unless there is a written indication of that intention.

Section 181 is the exam trap: copyright is distinct from the material object. A client may own a copy of drawings, own the lot, or own the completed building without automatically owning copyright. A written assignment or a source-backed statutory ownership rule is needed before treating economic copyright as transferred.

## Moral Rights

Section 193 gives the author personal rights of attribution and integrity. The author may require authorship to be attributed, object to prejudicial distortion or mutilation, and restrain use of the author's name on a work not created by the author or on a distorted version.

Moral rights are not the same as economic copyright. Assignment of economic rights does not assign or license moral rights. Section 198 makes moral rights last during the author's lifetime and for 50 years after death and states that they are not assignable or subject to license. Section 195 still matters: an author may waive moral rights by written instrument, but the statute invalidates waivers that allow reputation-injuring altered versions or use of the author's name on a work the author did not create.

## Civil Remedies

Section 216 allows injunction against infringement. It also allows recovery of actual damages, legal costs, other expenses, and infringer profits. In lieu of actual damages and profits, the court may award just damages that are not treated as a penalty. The court may order disposal or destruction of infringing copies or devices and may order seizure or impounding of evidence.

Section 226 limits damages recovery: no damages may be recovered after four years from the time the cause of action arose. In exam practice, that means delayed discovery or long-running use should be analyzed carefully. It does not automatically erase copyright, but it can limit recoverable damages.

## Criminal Penalties

Section 217 provides criminal penalties for infringement of rights secured by Part IV of RA 8293 or aiding and abetting infringement. It also has possession-related offenses for a person who knows, or ought to know, that an article is an infringing copy and possesses it for sale, letting for hire, distribution for trade purposes, or other prejudicial trade purpose. Do not convert every civil infringement scenario into automatic criminal liability without the facts needed by Section 217.

## Exam Guardrails

If the question is about term, cite Section 213. If it is about assignment, cite Sections 180 and 181. If it is about attribution or integrity after assignment, cite Sections 193, 195, and 198. If it is about remedies, cite Sections 216 and 226.
