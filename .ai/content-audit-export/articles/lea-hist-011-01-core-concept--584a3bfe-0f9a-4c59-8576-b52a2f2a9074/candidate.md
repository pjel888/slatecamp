# Pre-Colonial African Architecture

## LEA Point

Pre-colonial African architecture is not one style. For LEA history items, treat Great Zimbabwe, Lalibela, and Djenne as three source-backed building logics shaped by material, climate, religion, and social organization.

## Source Anchors

- Great Zimbabwe: dry-stone granite walling, royal/urban hierarchy, and trade-linked settlement organization. The exam-safe point is stone fitting without mortar, not imported European authorship.
- Lalibela: a group of 11 monolithic rock-hewn churches associated with 12th- to 13th-century Ethiopia. The correct construction idea is subtractive carving from living rock.
- Biete Giyorgis: source-safe wording is cruciform or Greek-cross rock-hewn plan. Do not describe it as a generic Western basilica or a defensive work.
- Djenne: Sahelian earthen construction depends on mass, seasonal maintenance, and community replastering. The projecting toron should be read as facade/maintenance members tied to earthen wall upkeep, not as a roof-support-only claim.

## Exam Pattern

The test usually asks for contrast. Great Zimbabwe = dry-stone and social hierarchy. Lalibela = monolithic rock-hewn church carving. Djenne = earthen Sahelian mosque/urban tradition requiring maintenance after rains.

## Guardrails

- Do not make European influence the source of these pre-colonial traditions.
- Do not turn Great Zimbabwe into a simple fortress question; its enclosure also communicates movement control, status, and authority.
- Do not overstate Djenne toron as permanent roof posts. Use maintenance access, facade rhythm, and replastering support.

## Real World Scenario

If a conservation team compares dry-stone walls at Great Zimbabwe with earthen walls at Djenne, the repair plan changes. Dry-stone work focuses on stone displacement and drainage; earthen work expects cyclical replastering and community maintenance after rainy seasons.
