# Ergonomics in Furniture Design

## Learning Goal

Explain ergonomics in furniture design using checked local sources: human measurements, activity fit, furniture vocabulary, and accessible furniture/service dimensions that can be traced to raw PDF families.

## Source-Audited Scope

The Furniture Reviewer source identifies anthropometrics as the study that deals with human measurements and describes ergonomics as human factor engineering. The Salvan design-theory source states that human-body dimensions affect things people handle, heights and distances people reach, furniture used for sitting, working, eating, and sleeping, and the space needed for movement, activity, and rest.

This rewrite removes external names that were not checked and population tables from the older draft. Claims about Panero, Del Prado-Lu, NHANES, ANSUR, BIFMA, and auditorium C-values are not used here because those primary raw sources were not part of the Batch 053 local raw PDF set.

## Core Principles

1. Furniture begins with the body and the task. The checked sources support anthropometrics, ergonomics/human factors, and the connection between body dimensions, reach, furniture, and movement space.
2. Comfort is not only a catalog dimension. Salvan's furniture section discusses deriving comfortable furniture heights from an individual body measure rather than forcing all users into one fixed standard height.
3. Exam vocabulary matters. The Furniture Reviewer source ties specific furniture terms to meanings: butaca is identified as a Philippine furniture item associated with giving birth; almario stores pillows and mats; escritoire is a writing desk; bergere is an arm chair with closed arms; etagere is hanging or standing shelves; finial is a crowning ornament on furniture.
4. Designer attribution must stay source-bound. In the checked Furniture Reviewer source, Le Corbusier is associated with the adjustable chaise lounge, Alvar Aalto with the cantilevered chair, Marcel Breuer with the Cesca cantilevered chair, Mies van der Rohe with the Barcelona Chair, and the Tulip chair is described as molded fiberglass on a cast aluminum pedestal.
5. Accessible furniture dimensions must be checked against BP 344 when the user is a wheelchair user or person with disability. The checked BP 344 source gives dining tables for persons with disabilities at 750 mm high, minimum knee space of 700 mm high by 480 mm deep, and a 900 mm accessible route to those tables.
6. Accessible workstations require more than a desk height. The checked BP 344 source requires desk counters at least 900 mm wide, vertical legroom of 700 mm, counter depth of 480 mm, and support features such as glare reduction and assistive technologies where applicable.
7. Controls must be reachable. Manual switches are installed 1200 mm above the floor, measured to the center of the switch plate, and located 200 mm from the latch side of the door in the checked BP 344 source.

## Practical Furniture Check

- Identify the task: sitting, writing, eating, waiting, transferring, storing, or reaching.
- Identify the body relationship: reach, knee clearance, seat support, visual access, or movement clearance.
- Use source-backed terms for furniture identification questions.
- Use BP 344 dimensions when the furniture is part of an accessible route, dining area, workstation, or control interface.
- Park any claimed international ergonomic data until its raw source is locally checked.

## Exam Traps

- Do not cite invented authorities from older drafts.
- Do not answer Del Prado-Lu, BIFMA, or C-value questions from this Batch 053 article; those claims are outside the checked source scope.
- Do not confuse anthropometrics with carpentry. The checked source separates human measurement from the manufacture of woodworks.
- Do not treat furniture vocabulary as interchangeable: escritoire, bergere, etagere, finial, butaca, almario, and diban point to different things.
