## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal
Integrate plants, hardscapes, water features, and spatial organization into outdoor environments, select appropriate plant materials for Philippine tropical conditions, apply zoning strategies for landscape areas, and coordinate hardscape with softscape.

Landscape architecture integrates plants, hardscapes, water features, and spatial organization to create outdoor environments that enhance site functionality, aesthetics, and ecological health.
Understanding plant selection, zoning strategies, and the relationship between hardscape and softscape is essential to comprehensive site planning.

## Core Concept

Landscape architecture is the design and planning of outdoor space.
It bridges architecture, ecology, and urban design by creating places where people interact with nature.
Within site planning, landscape architecture serves multiple functions simultaneously: stormwater management, microclimatic control, habitat creation, visual placemaking, and recreational opportunity.
**Plant Selection for Climate and Resilience:**

In Philippine practice, tropical landscape design must account for:

- High humidity and intense monsoon rainfall
- Salt spray exposure in coastal sites
- Volcanic soil conditions in some regions
- Intense solar radiation year-round
- Flood and typhoon resilience requirements

Native species (trees, shrubs, groundcovers) are preferred because they require minimal irrigation, withstand local pests, and support biodiversity.
Common Philippine tropical species include narra (Pterocarpus indicus), calamansi (Citrus microcarpa), sampaguita (Jasminum sambac), and various bamboo species.
Non-native species may require more maintenance and irrigation, increasing site operating costs.
**Hardscape vs. Softscape:**

- **Softscape:** Plants, soil, water features, mulch. Provides biodiversity, stormwater absorption, shade, microclimate control, and visual warmth.
  Requires maintenance (pruning, watering, fertilization).
- **Hardscape:** Paving, walls, structures, furniture.
  Provides durability, low maintenance, clear spatial definition, and water drainage (when properly sloped).
  Can absorb heat and create urban heat island effect if excessive.

Balanced hardscape-softscape integration is critical.
Excessive hardscape creates hot, sterile sites; excessive softscape becomes unkempt and expensive to maintain.
**Outdoor Rooms and Spatial Organization:**

Landscape design organizes outdoor space into functional zones or "rooms"—gathering spaces, activity zones, buffer areas, and transition spaces.
Each room serves distinct purposes: seating areas for social gathering, play areas for recreation, circulation zones for movement, buffer zones for privacy and noise reduction.
This zoning improves usability, social equity, and environmental performance.

**Open Space Programming (PD 1216, PD 957, and PD 1096):**

For residential subdivisions, PD 1216 defines open space as an area reserved for parks, playgrounds, and recreational use.
Under PD 1216 (amending PD 957), subdivisions with an area of one (1) hectare or more must allocate a percentage of gross area for parks and playgrounds based on density:

- 20 lots or below per hectare: 3.5%
- 21 to 25 lots per hectare: 4.0%
- 65 lots and above per hectare: 9.0%
  At the individual lot level, the National Building Code (Rule VIII) governs the Total Open Space within Lot (TOSL). For example, a 15m × 20m (300 sqm) R-1 lot typically requires an Unpaved Surface Area (USA) of 20% to 30% depending on whether it is an interior or corner lot. This space must be:
- Accessible to all residents
- Programmed for actual use (not merely leftover land)
- Connected by walkways and seating
- Protected from future encroachment

**Stormwater Management Through Landscape:**

Landscape features can slow, filter, and infiltrate stormwater:

- **Bioretention basins:** Rain gardens that absorb water and filter pollutants
- **Permeable paving:** Allows water infiltration instead of runoff
- **Swleas and berms:** Graded landforms that channel water and reduce velocity
- **Tree trenches:** Allows tree roots to access and filter stormwater

This reduces
strain on municipal storm sewers and improves water quality.

**Microclimate Control:**

Strategic planting modifies site climate:

- Trees create shade, reducing air temperature and cooling building interiors
- Vegetation increases humidity in arid zones
- Wind-breaks (shelter belts) reduce wind speed for comfort and energy savings
- Water features create cooling through evaporation

**Open Space Allocation Summary:**

- Subdivisions (1 ha+): 3.5% to 9% for Parks/Playgrounds (PD 1216)
- Individual Lots: TOSL/USA varies by NBC Rule VIII (e.g., 20-30% for R-1)
- General Practice: Non-sleaable area often totals ~30% but is density-dependent

**Native Species Examples (Philippine Context):**

- Trees: Narra, Pili, Mango, Yakal, Molave, Malunggay
- Shrubs: Santan, Talinum, Ilang-ilang (Ylang-ylang)
- Groundcovers: Vetiver grass, Philippine sedge, native ferns
- Benefits: adapted to local climate, low irrigation need, wildlife value

## Philippine Practice Context

Philippine landscape architecture must respond to tropical conditions: high rainfall, salt spray near coasts, and typhoon exposure.
The National Greening Program encourages native species and reforestation. Barangay ordinances often mandate open space preservation and tree planting in new developments.
Urban heat island effects in Metro Manila drive demand for shade trees and green walls.
The integration of landscape with stormwater management (bioretention basins, permeable paving) is increasingly required in environmental compliance.
Indigenous plants reduce costs over the project lifecycle by minimizing irrigation and chemical inputs.
Community organizing around open spaces strengthens social cohesion in residential subdivisions, particularly when landscape is accessible and well-maintained.

## How It's Tested

**Pattern 1: Open Space Allocation and Compliance**
A 50,000 m² (5-hectare) subdivision is proposed with a density of 25 lots per hectare.
Using PD 1216 standards, calculate the minimum area required for parks and playgrounds (4.0% of 50,000 m² = 2,000 m²). Determine if the proposed site plan complies with the density-specific mandate.
**Pattern 2: Plant Selection for Site Conditions**
Given a site description (coastal exposure, seasonal flooding, acidic volcanic soil, steep slope), select appropriate native species that thrive under those conditions and explain their benefits for site resilience.
**Pattern 3: Hardscape-Softscape Balance**
A site plan shows 70% paving and 30% planting.
Assess whether this balance is appropriate for a residential neighborhood (where shade and social space are priorities) vs. a logistics facility (where durability and accessibility dominate).
Recommend adjustments.

**Pattern 4: Stormwater Management Integration**
Design a bioretention basin to handle a 50m × 20m catchment area sloping at 5%.
Estimate basin depth, infiltration rate, and planted species to achieve volume absorption and water quality improvement.
**Pattern 5: Outdoor Room Zoning**
A 10,000 m² park is planned.
Identify distinct functional zones (gathering, activity, buffer, circulation) and specify landscape treatments (paving, planting, furnishings) for each zone to support the intended uses.
