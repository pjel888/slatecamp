## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal
Use landscape architecture as a site-planning discipline for open spaces, water edges, vegetation, circulation, and erosion/drainage coordination without importing unsupported legal percentages or plant schedules.

## Core Concept
Landscape architecture organizes outdoor space so site use, open-space value, environmental protection, and constructability work together. In the checked DHSUD Open Space Development Guidelines, open spaces are tied to ecosystem services, flood control, climate mitigation, recreation, social interaction, and cultural expression. The same guidance frames open-space development through preserve, conserve, regenerate, and create approaches.

For LEA purposes, begin by classifying the open-space condition. The DHSUD guidance distinguishes blue open space such as rivers, lakes, wetlands, and coastal zones; green open space such as parks, forests, grasslands, greenways, and vegetated landscapes; and non-blue/non-green open space such as plazas, paved surfaces, roads, and other developed open areas. This classification tells you whether the design problem is mainly about water, vegetation, public use, circulation, or developed outdoor surface.

Landscape decisions also interact with water law and sitework. PD 1067 places easements of public use along river and stream banks and the shores of seas and lakes, measured by context. The Water Code also treats drainage, flood-control areas, protected water-adjacent land, and pollution of water sources as controlled matters. Sitework sources add constructability tools: AASHTO soil classification for drainage and bearing capacity, fill materials, riprap, gabions, geosynthetics, and prepared paving bases.

A source-safe landscape answer therefore does not start with decoration. It asks: What kind of open space is this? Is there a water-margin or drainage rule? Does the site need preservation, conservation, regeneration, or creation of open space? Do erosion, soil, or paving conditions require sitework measures? Answer only from those checked constraints.

## Source-Safe Exam Takeaways

- Classify the outdoor area as blue, green, or non-blue/non-green open space before choosing a design response.
- Use preserve, conserve, regenerate, and create as the checked DHSUD development approach set.
- Treat riparian corridors, wetlands, coastal areas, and other water-related spaces as blue-open-space issues with water-margin and drainage implications.
- Use vegetation, greenways, parks, buffers, and habitat-related measures where the problem is a green-open-space issue.
- Use riprap, gabions, geotextiles, fill, prepared bases, and paving only as sitework responses to specific erosion, drainage, bearing, or surfacing conditions.
