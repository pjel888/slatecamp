## PART 1 — SETBACKS (YARDS)

### Source: Rule VIII, Section 804, 2004 Revised IRR[web:2]

---

### VISUALIZATION: WHY SETBACKS EXIST

> _You are standing at the front door of a newly built house in a subdivision. Look left — the neighbor's wall is barely a meter away. There is no sunlight. There is no airflow. If a fire starts, the flames jump the gap in seconds._

[SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK:ui_0001 component="LawCallout" status="preserved"]
Extracted summary:
- Props: code="nbcp"
- Text: _This is why PD 1096 mandates yards. A "yard" is the required open space between the outermost face of the building and the property line. The width of that yard IS the setback. Setbacks serve three critical purposes:_
[/SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK]

1. **Light & Ventilation** — Natural air and sunlight must reach every habitable room.
2. **Fire Separation** — Distance slows fire spread between adjacent buildings.
3. **Public Safety & Aesthetics** — Uniform setbacks create orderly streetscapes and ensure emergency access.

[SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK:ui_0002 component="Callout" status="preserved"]
Extracted summary:
- Props: variant="authority", title="Reason for the Law"
- Text: The Philippines is a tropical country with dense urban areas; without mandated open space, buildings would consume entire lots, creating dark, unventilated fire traps that PD 1096 seeks to prevent.[web:2][web:15]
[/SLATECAMP_UI_BLOCK]

---

### KEY DEFINITION

> **YARD** = the required open space left between the **outermost face** of the building or structure and the **property lines** (front, rear, right side, left side). **The width of the yard is the setback.**  
> _(Rule VIII, Section 804, Subsection 3, 2004 Revised IRR)[web:2]_

---

### TABLE 1: RESIDENTIAL SETBACKS — Table VIII.2 (2004 Revised IRR)

**Title: Minimum Setbacks for Residential Buildings/Structures**[web:2]

| YARD      | R-1 (m)  | R-2 Basic (m) | R-2 Maximum (m) | R-3 Basic (m) | R-3 Maximum (m) | R-4 individual lot/unit (m) | R-5 (m) |
| :-------- | :------: | :-----------: | :-------------: | :-----------: | :-------------: | :-------------------------: | :-----: |
| **Front** | **4.50** |     3.00      |     8.00 \*     |     3.00      |     8.00 \*     |            4.50             |  6.00   |
| **Side**  | **2.00** |   2.00 \*\*   |    2.00 \*\*    |    \*\*\*     | 2.00 (optional) |       2.00 (optional)       |  3.00   |
| **Rear**  | **2.00** |     2.00      |      2.00       |    \*\*\*     |      2.00       |            2.00             |  3.00   |

**NOTES (from the IRR — exam-critical):**[web:2]

- **(a)** These setback requirements are for **newly-developed subdivisions**.
- **(\*)** For R-2/R-3 _maximum_ configuration, the **front setback at grade** totals 8.00 m, broken down as 3.00 m yard + 5.00 m to accommodate part of the minimum parking requirement outside the front yard; upper floors may align with the 3.00 m basic front setback.[web:2]
- **(\*\*)** For R-2, a side setback is required for only **one (1) side**; the other side may be optional.
- **(\*\*\*)** R-3 Basic: Abutments on two sides and the rear may be allowed under specific conditions in Section 804, Subsection 10 (firewalls).[web:2]
- **R-5:** Mixed-use buildings in R-5 lots are treated as **commercial** where at least 55% of GFA is commercial.[web:2]
- **(f)** In certain mass-housing cases where full setbacks are physically impossible, abutments on sides and rear may be allowed and at least **1.50 m** of front yard is left open as a transition area, subject to detailed conditions in the IRR.[web:2]

### TRAP LEART #1 — The 4.50 m R-1 Front Setback

**WRONG:** "The minimum front setback for R-1 is 3.00 m."

**RIGHT:** The minimum front setback for R-1 is **4.50 meters**.[web:2]

Students frequently confuse this with the 3.00 m basic front setback of R-2 and R-3. Remember: **R-1 is the lowest-density zone (single-detached dwelling) and receives the largest default front yard.**[web:2]

Mnemonic: **"R-1 = 4 and a half, R-2/3 Basic = 3 flat"**

### TABLE 2: NON-RESIDENTIAL SETBACKS — Table VIII.3 (2004 Revised IRR)

**Title: Setbacks for Commercial, Industrial, Institutional, and Recreational Buildings**[web:2]

| RROW Width (m)    | Front (m) | Side (m) | Rear (m) |
| :---------------- | :-------: | :------: | :------: |
| **30.00 & above** | **8.00**  |   5.00   |   5.00   |
| 25.00 to 29.00    |   6.00    |   3.00   |   3.00   |

**Reason for the Law:** Wider roads carry more traffic and serve a broader urban territory, so buildings on major roads must step back further to maintain scale, visibility, and safety relative to the roadway width.[web:2]

**CRITICAL NOTE:** These values apply primarily to **newly-developed thoroughfares**; for highly built-up areas with established street lines and grades related to planned widening, the building may abut the side and/or rear property lines so long as all open-space, window, ventilation, and firewall requirements are satisfied.[web:2]

### TRAP LEART #2 — Residential vs. Non-Residential Setback Tables

**WRONG:** Using residential setback values (Table VIII.2) for a commercial building.

**RIGHT:** The two tables work on different **keys**:

- **Residential setbacks** (Table VIII.2) are based on **residential zoning density (R-1 to R-5)**.[web:2]
- **Non-residential setbacks** (Table VIII.3) are based on **RROW width**, regardless of occupancy group (C, I, Ind, Rec).[web:2]

Example: A commercial lot along a 20 m RROW uses **5.00 m** front setback from Table VIII.3, not any R-2/R-3 value from Table VIII.2.[web:2]

### INCREMENTAL SETBACK RULE (Section 804, Subsection 6)

> For buildings **more than 2 storeys**, the minimum width of the required rear or side court increases by **300 mm per storey** above the 2nd, up to the **14th storey**.[web:2]  
> For buildings taller than 14 storeys, use the width computed for 14 storeys (increment caps at 14).[web:2]

**Reason for the Law:** Taller buildings block more sky and air, so courts must grow proportionally to retain light and ventilation for occupants and neighbors.[web:2]

**Worked Example:**

- 10-storey commercial building, RROW = 25 m.
- Base side setback from Table VIII.3 = 3.00 m.[web:2]
- Additional storeys above 2nd = 10 − 2 = 8 storeys.
- Increment = 8 × 0.30 m = 2.40 m.[web:2]
- **Total side court width = 3.00 + 2.40 = 5.40 m.**

### VARIANCE PROVISION (Section 803, Subsection 4e)

When lots are too narrow or too shallow and open spaces can be supplied from public open areas (streets, alleys), TOSL requirements may be waived under strict conditions.[web:2]

| Condition                                 | Open Space Depth Threshold |
| :---------------------------------------- | :------------------------: |
| Lots abutting **1** public open space     |     ≤ **8.00 m** depth     |
| Lots abutting **2 or more** public spaces |    ≤ **12.00 m** depth     |

Always confirm with the specific IRR text when applying variances in a design problem.[web:2]

### EASEMENTS — Quick Reference (Corrected to PD 1067 Article 51)

**Important correction:** Easement widths along rivers, streams, and shorelines come from **PD 1067 (Water Code of the Philippines) Article 51**, not a fixed "3 m or 1 m" per water width.[web:16][web:12]

> Article 51, PD 1067:  
> “The banks of rivers and streams and the shores of seas and lakes throughout their entire length and within a zone of **three (3) meters in urban areas**, **twenty (20) meters in agricultural areas**, and **forty (40) meters in forest areas**, along their margins, are subject to the easement of public use…”[web:16]

| Easement Type                      | Required Easement Zone (from bank or shore)                                         | Governing Law               |
| :--------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------- |
| **Rivers / streams / creeks**      | **3.00 m** in urban areas, **20.00 m** in agricultural, **40.00 m** in forest areas | PD 1067, Art. 51[web:16]    |
| **Sea / lakes shoreline**          | Same 3–20–40 m rule (urban/agricultural/forest)                                     | PD 1067, Art. 51[web:16]    |
| **Power transmission lines**       | Varies by voltage; refer to NEA/NGCP guidelines                                     | EPIRA + utility standards   |
| **Road Right-of-Way (RROW) width** | Per DPWH classification and LGU zoning ordinance                                    | PD 1096 + LGU zoning[web:4] |

> **Reason for Easement Laws:** These buffer strips ensure public access to waterways and provide safety margins against flooding and shoreline erosion, which is critical in a country with seasonal typhoons and high flood risk.[web:16][web:12]

> **Exam Context:** You must deduct the easement strip from the lot's buildable dimension **based on the site’s land classification (urban/agricultural/forest)**, not just water width.[web:16]

## PART 2 — BUILDING PROJECTIONS OVER PUBLIC STREETS

### Source: PD 1096, Rule X, Sections 1001–1008[web:7][web:11]

---

### VISUALIZATION: WHY PROJECTION RULES EXIST

> _You are walking along a narrow Manila sidewalk. Above you, a balcony juts out. Below, a footing extends into the road right-of-way. A canopy shelters the storefront._

> All these projections — balcony, footing, canopy — are regulated by **Rule X** to balance building use versus the public right to a safe clear street.[web:7]

Extracted summary:
- Props: variant="authority", title="Reason for the Law"
- Text: Narrow urban streets can easily become dark, low-clearance tunnels if projections are not controlled, endangering pedestrians and emergency access.[web:7][web:11]

---

### GENERAL RULE (Section 1001)

- No part of any building or its appendages may project **beyond the building/property line** except as allowed in Rule X.[web:11]
- Projection is measured **horizontally** from the property line to the outermost point.[web:11]

---

### UNDERGROUND PROJECTIONS — Section 1002

**Section 1002(1):** No building or appendage shall project into any alley or street, national road, or public highway except as provided.[web:11]

---

### CRITICAL — TWO DISTINCT Underground Provisions

Many reviewers wrongly merge these as a single “depth-progression” rule; Rule X keeps them separate:[web:7][web:11]

---

#### PROVISION 1 — Section 1002(2): FOOTINGS along National Roads

| Parameter               | Requirement                                                  |
| :---------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **What**                | Footings                                                     |
| **Where**               | Along national roads or public highways                      |
| **Minimum depth**       | At least **2.40 m below grade**                              |
| **Maximum projection**  | ≤ **300 mm** beyond property line                            |
| **Utilities condition** | Must not obstruct utilities unless owner pays rerouting cost |

**Reason:** Heavy traffic and construction on national roads demand deeper, limited encroachment to protect both structure and public works.[web:11][web:7]

---

#### PROVISION 2 — Section 1002(3): FOUNDATIONS into Public Sidewalks

| Parameter                          | Requirement                                                 |
| :--------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **What**                           | Foundations (broader than footings)                         |
| **Where**                          | Into public sidewalk areas                                  |
| **Maximum encroachment width**     | ≤ **500 mm**                                                |
| **Min depth of top of foundation** | ≥ **600 mm** below established grade                        |
| **Utilities condition**            | Must not obstruct utilities unless rerouted at owner’s cost |

**Reason:** Sidewalks often carry utilities, so any foundation under them must be shallow but kept below usual service depth while still allowing limited projection.[web:11]

---

### TRAP LEART #3 — Do NOT Merge 1002(2) and 1002(3)

**WRONG:** Presenting "footing 2.40 m deep = 300 mm; foundation 0.60 m deep = 500 mm" as **one** table or graduated rule.

**RIGHT:** These are **two different** rules for **two locations** (national roads vs. sidewalks) and two structural elements (footings vs. foundations).[web:11]

Mnemonic: **"F-3-2 / F-5-6"**

- **Footing**: **3**00 mm projection, **2**.40 m depth.
- **Foundation**: **5**00 mm encroachment, **6**00 mm depth (top below grade).

| :---------------- | :--------------------------------------------- |

### CANOPIES vs. AWNINGS — Know the Difference

| Feature             | Canopy (Marquee) — Sec. 1005       | Awning — Sec. 1006                  |
| :------------------ | :--------------------------------- | :---------------------------------- |
| **Permanence**      | Permanent, fixed                   | Movable, retractable or collapsible |
| **Support**         | Attached/supported by the building | Attached to exterior wall           |
| **Construction**    | Incombustible, ≥2-hr fire rating   | No specific fire rating in text     |
| **Vert. clearance** | **3.00 m**                         | **2.40 m**                          |
| **From curb**       | ≥300 mm                            | ≥300 mm                             |
| **Drainage**        | Must have drainage facility        | Not specifically required           |

> Exam shortcut: permanent and structural overhead cover = **canopy** (3.00 m); fabric/movable = **awning** (2.40 m).[web:11]

### RROW ANGLES & THE OFB (Outer Face of Building)

Later guidelines and PRBOA interpretations introduce angular planes from the RROW to determine **OFB**; these operate together with setback and height provisions.[web:3][web:14]

Key reminder for LEA: RROW angular planes limit **how high and how close** the building envelope can rise relative to the street width; above mid-rise levels, upper floors often need additional “step-backs.”[web:3]

---

### TABLE 1003-A — Projection of Balconies and Appendages

Section 1003 refers to **Table 1003-A** for allowable balcony and appendage projections; the general rule: **narrower streets = smaller projection length** and projections must be **uniform within a block**.[web:7][web:11]

For the exam, remember that vertical clearance (3.00 m) and uniformity are as important as the horizontal projection distances.

## PART 3 — PSO / TOSL INTEGRATION (Table VIII.1 Reference)

### TRAP LEART #5 — Table VIII.1 Is NOT a Setback Table

**WRONG:** Using Table VIII.1 as if it supplied front/side/rear setback distances.

**RIGHT:** Table VIII.1 provides:

- **PSO (Percentage of Site Occupancy)**
- **ISA, USA**
- **TOSL (Total Open Space within Lot)**

It does **not** specify yard widths; those come from Tables VIII.2 and VIII.3.[web:2]

### Quick PSO Reference (Compressed from Table VIII.1)

| Land Use            | PSO (no firewall) | PSO (with firewall) | TOSL without firewall | TOSL with firewall |
| :------------------ | :---------------: | :-----------------: | :-------------------: | :----------------: |
| **R-2 Basic**       |       55%         |        60%          |         45%           |        40%         |

## PART 4 — SYNTHESIS: FROM LOT TO BUILDING ENVELOPE

### Complete Design Workflow for LEA Area 3

```text
Step 1:  Identify LOT TYPE (7 types — Figs. VIII.2–VIII.8)
         ↓
Step 2:  Check ZONING CLASSIFICATION → Get PSO, FLAR, BHL from Table VIII.1
         ↓
Step 3:  Deduct EASEMENTS (PD 1067 Art. 51: 3/20/40 m; powerline/RROW per regs)
         ↓
Step 4:  Apply SETBACKS from Table VIII.2 (Residential) or Table VIII.3 (Non-Residential)
         ↓
Step 5:  Compute AMBF = TLA × PSO
         ↓
Step 6:  Verify that TOSL = TLA − AMBF meets minimum %
         ↓
Step 7:  Compute Total Allowable Floor Area = TLA × FLAR
         ↓
Step 8:  Determine Max Floors = Total Floor Area ÷ AMBF
         ↓
Step 9:  Apply BHL and INCREMENTAL SETBACKS above 2nd floor (300 mm/storey to 14th)
         ↓
Step 10: Check RROW ANGLES → OFB cannot exceed angular plane
         ↓
Step 11: Apply PROJECTION RULES (Rule X) for balconies, canopies, underground works
```

## Exam Tip

ALE Trap: Minimum setbacks by occupancy and fire zone: 1-2 story = front 3.0 m, sides 2.0 m, rear 2.0 m | 3-4 story = front 4.5 m, sides 3.0 m, rear 3.0 m | 5+ story = front 6.0 m, sides 4.5 m, rear 4.5 m. Corner lots: both front-facing street sides = front yard requirements. The 3.0 m front setback for 1-2 story buildings is the most tested.
