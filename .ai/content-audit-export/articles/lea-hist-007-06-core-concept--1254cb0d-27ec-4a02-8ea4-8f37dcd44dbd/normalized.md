# Pacific Architecture

## LEA Point

Pacific architecture questions split into two buckets: indigenous Pacific meeting-house traditions and the Sydney Opera House as a modern twentieth-century landmark in the Pacific region. Keep them related by geography, not by typology.

## Source Anchors

- UNESCO Sydney Opera House anchors Jorn Utzon, Bennelong Point, shell-form landmark identity, and twentieth-century architectural significance.
- Sydney Opera House official material anchors the site, project history, tile/shell identity, and public cultural function.
- Te Ara anchors wharenui as a Maori meeting-house and cultural/ancestral community focus.
- Samoa official cultural/tourism material is used cautiously for fale/open-sided Pacific vernacular vocabulary.

## Exam Pattern

If the stem says Jorn Utzon, Bennelong Point, shells, tiles, harbor silhouette, 1959 to 1973 construction, or twentieth-century architectural achievement, answer Sydney Opera House. If it says fale, bure, wharenui, open-sided communal space, local materials, ventilation, shade, or social ceremony, stay in the indigenous Pacific meeting-house bucket.

## Guardrails

Do not call indigenous Pacific architecture primitive. Its sophistication is in climate response, material knowledge, community ritual, and spatial adaptability. Do not call the Sydney Opera House indigenous vernacular; it is a modern Australian/Pacific-region monument. Batch 122 corrects the older 'flea' typo to 'fale' in linked question edits.

## Real World Scenario

For a coastal community hall, a source-safe Pacific precedent study would separate lessons: use fale or wharenui sources for communal threshold, openness, and social meaning, and use Sydney Opera House only as a modern landmark precedent for harbor identity and engineered roof/shell expression.
