## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Sound isolation is different from room absorption. Absorptive finishes reduce reverberant noise inside the same room, but Salvan warns that porous materials are usually poor sound insulators. A room can sound less echoic after treatment and still transmit noise to the next space if the wall, door, window, duct, or crack remains weak.

Salvan classifies building sound transmission into two main groups. Airborne sound can pass along continuous air paths such as open windows, doors, transoms, cracks around pipes and conduits, and ventilating ducts. It can also force partitions to vibrate like diaphragms, making the partition a secondary sound source on the other side. Impact and structure-borne sound comes from direct impacts or machinery vibration and can travel through rigid structures with little attenuation.

Openings are usually the first isolation problem. Windows, open doors, and small cracks can limit the total insulation that a separating partition can achieve. Salvan states that high sound isolation requires complete closing of cracks around windows, doors, pipes, and conduits. For LEA choices, a sealed weak path often matters more than simply specifying a thicker wall.

Transmission loss is the reduction in sound energy through a partition, expressed in decibels. Noise reduction between two rooms is related to transmission loss, but they are not the same; the receiving room absorption and barrier area also matter. Rigid partitions work mainly because mass resists vibration. Salvan notes that average transmission loss for rigid mass partitions increases by about 4 to 5 dB each time the wall weight is doubled, but very high isolation is often more practical with discontinuous or compound partitions than with thickness alone.

Windows and doors are paths of least resistance in sound insulation. Tight fitting frames, eliminated threshold cracks, heavier glazing, separated double panes, and edge seals improve performance. Salvan's STC discussion gives the broad exam rule: higher STC means a wall or floor blocks sound better, but the rating is only useful when the assembled construction and its leaks are controlled.

## LEA Memory Anchors

- Absorption controls room reverberation; isolation controls transmission to other spaces.
- Airborne sound travels through openings or vibrates partitions.
- Impact noise travels through rigid structure and needs different control methods.
- Cracks, doors, windows, ducts, and penetrations can dominate the result.
- More mass helps, but discontinuity, sealing, and compound construction are often the stronger design answer.
