## Payment Bond: Protection for Payment Obligations

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Define the payment bond using UAP Document 301 and CIAP Document 102.
- Identify the 15% CIAP Document 102 bond amount for contractor obligations to workers, subcontractors, and suppliers.
- Connect payment protection with liens, sworn statements, deductions, and subcontract approval requirements.

## Definition

UAP Document 301 defines the payment bond as the approved form of security furnished by the contractor and surety as a guarantee of good faith that the contractor will pay all obligations arising from the contract.

CIAP Document 102 states that the contractor furnishes a separate 15% bond covering obligations arising from the contract to workers, subcontractors, and suppliers. This is distinct from the 15% performance bond for faithful performance of the work.

## Payment Controls in CIAP Document 102

CIAP Document 102 allows the owner to deduct substantiated and unpaid claims by subcontractors and suppliers from amounts due to the contractor. It also requires the contractor to pay workmen, materials, equipment, taxes, and legally required withholdings promptly.

As a condition to final payment or release of retention, the contractor must release the work from legal liens arising from unpaid subcontractor or supplier claims. If claims remain unpaid, the contractor must furnish an indemnity bond equal to the unpaid claim amount.

## Real World Scenario

A tile supplier tells the owner that the contractor has not paid for materials already installed in the project. The source-safe response is not to treat the payment bond as a completion guarantee. It protects payment obligations, and CIAP Document 102 also gives the owner payment-control tools: deduct substantiated unpaid claims, require lien releases before final payment or retention release, and require an indemnity bond for unpaid claims.

## Exam Boundary

Use the payment bond for payment obligations to workers, subcontractors, and suppliers. Use the performance bond for faithful performance of the work. Do not treat the payment bond as the same instrument as the guarantee bond for post-completion workmanship.
