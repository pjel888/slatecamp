## Payment Bond — Protection for Subcontractors and Suppliers

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Define the payment bond and its purpose: protect subcontractors and suppliers.
- Distinguish the payment bond from the performance bond.
- Explain who can claim against the payment bond.
- Describe the payment bond amount relative to the contract price.

### Purpose and Function of Payment Bonds

A payment bond is security furnished by the contractor and surety to guarantee that all obligations arising from the contract will be paid. Unlike the performance bond which protects the owner against incomplete work, the payment bond protects subcontractors, suppliers, and laborers against non-payment. When the contractor fails to pay subcontractors or suppliers, these parties can file claims directly against the surety.

<LawCallout code="ra9266" section="UAP Doc 301" title="Payment Bond vs. Performance Bond">
Payment Bond: Guarantees payment of obligations to subcontractors, suppliers, and laborers. Beneficiary: subcontractors/suppliers. Performance Bond: Guarantees completion and quality of work. Beneficiary: Owner. A single contract may have BOTH bonds. The contractor may breach one without breaching the other.
</LawCallout>

### Typical Amount and Standard Percentage

Payment bonds are typically required in amounts equal to the full contract price or a specified percentage thereof. Standard practice requires payment bonds at 50% to 100% of the contract value, with 100% being the most common requirement.

### Protection for Subcontractors and Suppliers

If the contractor fails to pay for work performed or materials delivered, subcontractors and suppliers can file claims directly with the surety without being parties to the original contract. Claimants must provide notice to the surety within a specified timeframe — often 60 to 90 days from last furnishing of labor or materials — and present evidence of the unpaid obligation.

<ExamTip>
  **LEA Priority — Payment Bond Key Points:**
  1. Protects subcontractors, suppliers, and laborers (NOT the owner)
  2. Claim period: typically 60-90 days from last furnishing of labor/materials
  3. Surety's liability is capped at the bond amount
  4. If valid claims exceed the bond, surety distributes proportionally
  5. Performance bond and payment bond serve different purposes — both may be required on the same project
</ExamTip>

### Distinction Between Payment and Performance Bonds

While both bonds may be required in the same contract, they serve distinct purposes:

| Feature | Performance Bond | Payment Bond |
|---------|-----------------|--------------|
| Protects | Owner | Subcontractors/Suppliers |
| Trigger | Failure to complete work | Failure to pay obligations |
| Claimant | Owner | Subcontractors, suppliers, laborers |
| Amount | Typically 100% of contract | Typically 50-100% of contract |
| Surety's liability | Capped at bond amount | Capped at bond amount |

<Warn>
  **Exam Trap:** The LEA often tests whether you know which bond covers which risk. If a question involves a subcontractor who was not paid, the answer is the Payment Bond — NOT the Performance Bond. If a question involves incomplete work, the answer is the Performance Bond.
</Warn>
