# Mechanical room location and siting

Mechanical-room questions should be answered as coordination questions before they are treated as layout preferences. The checked local source family supports access, ventilation, maintenance clearance, equipment safety, fire-protection coordination, and electrical working-space rules.

## Exam Anchor

Ask what kind of room or equipment is involved. An elevator machine room, boiler room, refrigeration machinery area, transformer vault, smoke-control equipment space, and electrical equipment space can each trigger different source rules. A safe answer does not apply one number to every mechanical room.

## What The Checked Sources Support

Salvan states that an elevator machine room or penthouse at the top of the hoistway should allow repair, inspection, and maintenance, should have access and ventilation, and should not be used as a storage area. This is a strong LEA coordination rule: mechanical rooms are working rooms, not leftover storage.

For boiler rooms, Salvan describes concrete or masonry construction and warns against using the room for another purpose. For refrigeration systems, Salvan includes safety provisions such as signage, emergency breathing equipment where larger refrigerant quantities are involved, refrigerant storage limits, and stop valves for systems above a stated refrigerant quantity. The safe concept is that mechanical-room siting must account for equipment hazards and maintenance response.

Salvan's electrical and mechanical space-planning discussion lists practical criteria such as ease of maintenance, ventilation, expandability, centrality, access limitation, noise, and adequate space. These are the source-supported planning lenses for locating major service rooms.

Where the mechanical room also contains electrical equipment, the Philippine Electrical Code source family controls working-space and dedicated-equipment-space issues. The PEC requires sufficient access and working space around electrical equipment, headroom where required, illumination for working spaces, and protection of dedicated electrical space from foreign systems such as ducts or piping unless the rules allow the installation.

The DOE guidelines require mechanical ventilation systems, whether continuous or not, to have accessible manual or automatic controls or other means of volume reduction or shutoff when ventilation is not required.

RA 9514 is relevant where mechanical ventilation and smoke-proof enclosure equipment or ductwork are part of the life-safety system. It gives fire-resistive separation conditions for that equipment and requires activation by smoke detector and, where provided, sprinkler water-flow signal or emergency power system operation.

## Safe Answer Workflow

1. Identify the equipment served by the room.
2. Preserve access, inspection, maintenance, and equipment-removal paths.
3. Provide ventilation where the checked source requires or expects it.
4. Keep electrical working space and dedicated electrical space clear when electrical equipment is present.
5. Treat smoke-control or fire-protection equipment as a life-safety system, not as ordinary service equipment.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating mechanical rooms as storage rooms.
- Blocking maintenance access with cable trays, ducts, pipes, or later architectural partitions.
- Applying a generic percentage-of-floor-area rule when the checked source points to equipment, access, ventilation, and hazard criteria.
- Ignoring PEC working-space rules in rooms that include electrical service, switchgear, or control equipment.
- Applying smoke-control separation requirements to every service room without checking the system condition.

## How It Is Tested

LEA-style items usually ask which design proposal protects access, ventilation, fire safety, electrical working space, or equipment maintenance. The correct answer is the one that keeps the room usable for inspection, operation, and emergency response under the checked local source family.

## Checked Local Sources

- George Salvan, Architectural Utilities 2, elevator machine-room access and ventilation, boiler-room and refrigeration safety provisions, and electrical/mechanical space-planning criteria.
- Philippine Electrical Code, working space, access, headroom, illumination, and dedicated electrical space for electrical equipment.
- DOE Energy Conserving Design Guidelines, mechanical ventilation controls.
- RA 9514 Revised IRR, smoke-proof enclosure mechanical ventilation and life-safety activation provisions.
