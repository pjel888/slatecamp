# Topography and Slope Analysis

## Board Exam Quick Check

Topography and slope analysis is not just reading contour intervals. The checked sources connect landform, excavation and fill, drainage, erosion, soil support, habitat corridors, and natural-hazard risk.

## Source-Grounded Core

The climate-resilience reference tool describes neighborhood design outcomes that avoid or minimize alteration to natural features such as drainage lines and waterways, minimize vegetation clearing, retain ecological corridors, minimize alteration to natural topography and the amount of excavation and filling, and avoid increasing risks associated with natural hazards.

This gives a clear LEA rule: the best site plan usually works with the landform before it reshapes it. If a proposal requires heavy cut-and-fill, blocks natural drainage, or increases exposure to a hazard, it needs stronger justification or a safer alternative.

## Soil And Slope Evidence

The Siteworks sources classify soils for suitability as subgrade materials in terms of drainage and bearing capacity. They identify fill materials, borrow fill, granular fill or filters, riprap, gabions, geotextiles, geocells, and erosion-control materials. Geotextiles may reinforce low-load-bearing soils, separate dissimilar materials, filter seepage, protect cut slopes or drainage features, and provide in-plane drainage.

For erosion and channel protection, riprap resistance depends on stone characteristics, channel or bank geometry, and the filter blanket. Gabion thickness is tied to bank-soil erodibility, maximum water velocity, and bank slope. Geocell systems are used for steep slope surface protection and channel linings, and erosion-control mats can anchor seeds and soil particles under erosive flow.

## Slope Analysis Checklist

- Identify contour direction, high and low points, ridges, swales, and natural drainage lines.
- Check whether proposed grading increases erosion, runoff, or instability.
- Limit excavation and filling where the natural topography can be retained.
- Match stabilization systems to soil, slope, flow, and hazard conditions.
- Coordinate vegetation, erosion control, drainage, access, and building placement.

## LEA Answer Pattern

Choose the answer that protects the landform and reduces hazard exposure: read contours and drainage first, minimize unnecessary cut-and-fill, stabilize slopes according to soil and water conditions, and keep natural systems visible in the site plan.
