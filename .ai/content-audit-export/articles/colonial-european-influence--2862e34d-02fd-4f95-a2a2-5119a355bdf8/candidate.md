# Colonial European Influence

## Urban And Institutional Influence

HOA Module 4 P1 frames Spanish colonial urbanism through reduccion, encomienda, a hierarchy of cities and towns, cuadricula, and colonial infrastructure. It describes reduccion as consolidation of formerly scattered barangays into compact communities, and the system of cities and towns as a hierarchy of cabecera or poblacion with adjacent barrios.

## Walled City And Grid

The checked source describes Intramuros as patterned after European walled fortresses and reserved for nobility and clergy. It defines cuadricula as a grid of streets and blocks. For the Laws of the Indies, it lists elevated location, orderly grid, central plaza, defensive wall, and zones for churches, shops, government buildings, hospitals, and slaughterhouses.

## Domestic And Stylistic Influence

Bahay na Bato is the domestic bridge between indigenous and Hispanic traditions. HOA Module 4 P1 calls it a hybrid housing prototype; Filipino Architecture says it evolved from Bahay Kubo while also carrying Spanish, Neo-Classical, Gothic, and Baroque influence through grandeur and solidity.

## LEA Takeaway

Colonial European influence is both urban and architectural. Link it to settlement control, plaza-grid order, walled-city planning, civic-religious zoning, and hybrid house construction rather than treating it as ornament alone.
