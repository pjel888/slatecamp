# Quasi-Delict and Tort in Architectural Practice

## LEA Point

Quasi-delict is negligence liability outside a pre-existing contract. Civil Code Article 2176 applies when an act or omission causes damage, there is fault or negligence, and there is no pre-existing contractual relation between claimant and defendant. This is why a neighbor or other third party can appear in an architectural negligence question even without a contract with the architect.

## Source Anchors

- Civil Code Article 2176: quasi-delict is damage caused by fault or negligence when there is no pre-existing contractual relation.
- Civil Code Article 2180: responsibility can extend to persons for whom one is responsible, including employers for employees acting within assigned tasks.
- Civil Code Article 1146: actions based on injury to rights and quasi-delict prescribe in four years.
- Civil Code Article 1150: prescription is counted from the day the action may be brought.
- Civil Code Article 1723: collapse within fifteen years from completion because of defects in plans, specifications, ground, construction, materials, or supervision is governed by the special construction-liability rule.
- Civil Code Article 2192: third-person damage resulting from an Article 1723 construction defect proceeds under Article 1723 within its fixed period.

## Article 1723 Boundary

Use quasi-delict for negligence damage that does not fall into the Article 1723 collapse framework. When the facts do fit Article 1723, Article 2192 prevents the claim from being casually relabeled as quasi-delict to bypass the Article 1723 period.

## Exam Guardrails

No contract privity is required for quasi-delict. Quasi-delict requires negligence, not perfection. The four-year period is counted from the day the action may be brought. When the question gives a collapse caused by a defect mentioned in Article 1723, check Article 1723 and Article 2192 before choosing a general negligence answer.
