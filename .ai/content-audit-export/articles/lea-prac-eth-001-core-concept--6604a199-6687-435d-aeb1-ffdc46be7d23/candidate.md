# Code of Ethical Conduct (SPP Document 200)

The UAP Document Code of Ethics presents the Architect's Credo first, then the Code of Ethical Conduct. The Code describes architecture as a profession requiring integrity, judgment, business capacity, and artistic and technical ability. It also frames the architect as a professional adviser whose advice to the client must be unprejudiced.

## What The Code Covers

The Code of Ethical Conduct organizes duties by the relationship affected by the architect's conduct:

- Section 1 covers responsibilities in relation to the people. It includes civic and urban service, public safety, health and well-being, heritage, professional organization work, legal compliance, and limits on misleading publicity.
- Section 2 covers responsibilities in relation to the client. It includes good faith, clear scope and charges at the start of the engagement, advice against questionable projects, conditional cost estimates, professional fees, no outside returns, conflict disclosure, and arbitration.
- Section 3 covers responsibilities in relation to the contractor. It requires clear contract information, fair handling of document errors, rejection of nonconforming work after inspection, no gifts or favors from contractors, and prompt inspection/certification when requested.
- Section 4 covers responsibilities in relation to manufacturers, dealers, and agents. It allows technical exchange but bars services, commissions, discounts, fees, gifts, or favors that can compromise the architect's professional judgment.
- Section 5 covers responsibilities in relation to colleagues and subordinates. It addresses compensation, fee competition, commissions already held by another architect, competition conflicts, successor work, reputation, doubtful enterprises, signing and sealing, employment conditions, mentorship, and professional organization service.

## LEA Study Anchor

For exam questions, identify the affected relationship first. Public safety and misleading publicity usually point to Section 1. Scope, fees, cost estimates, client interests, compensation, and arbitration point to Section 2. Contractor fairness points to Section 3. Supplier benefits point to Section 4. Competition, successor work, signing and sealing, colleague reputation, employees, and mentoring point to Section 5.
