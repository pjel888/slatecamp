## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal
Read contour maps and calculate slopes (%, degrees, ratios), determine cut and fill volumes, design positive drainage grading (2% minimum for paved surfaces), apply drainage to direct water away from buildings, and verify erosion control measures.

Grading and drainage are the physical foundations of site development. Understanding contour reading, slope calculation, and drainage principles ensures that water moves safely away from buildings, preventing flooding, foundation damage, and landscape erosion. This is among the most calculation-heavy topics in LEA-DSGN.

## Core Concept

Site grading is the systematic re-shaping of land elevations to achieve positive drainage, safe slopes, and buildable platforms. Drainage directs water away from structures and toward natural discharge points. The slope gradient—expressed as a percentage—is the primary tool for controlling water movement and determining site safety.

**Slope Gradient Calculation:**

Slope % = (Rise ÷ Run) × 100

Where Rise is the vertical elevation change and Run is the horizontal distance.

**Worked Example:**
A contour map shows elevation lines 5 meters apart (horizontally) with a 2-meter elevation change between them. Calculate the slope gradient:

Slope % = (2m ÷ 5m) × 100 = 40%

This represents a steep slope. Contours that are closely spaced indicate steep terrain; widely spaced contours indicate gentle slopes.

**Slope Gradient Classification:**

| Gradient Range | Classification | Characteristics | Design Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3% | Flat | Poor natural drainage; water ponds | Grading/swleas required |
| 3–8% | Gentle | Ideal for development | Minimal grading; good drainage |
| 8–15% | Moderate | Manageable slopes | Standard grading; retaining walls possible |
| 15–25% | Steep | Significant elevation change | Retaining walls likely; geotechnical review |
| >25% | Very Steep | Unsuitable without major engineering | Slope stabilization; terracing; difficult development |

**Contour Spacing and Slope Relationship:**

On a contour map with a consistent contour interval (e.g., 1m or 5m elevation steps):
- Closely spaced contours = steep terrain (slope % is high)
- Widely spaced contours = gentle terrain (slope % is low)

**Positive Drainage Principle:**

All surface water must drain away from buildings. The recommended The recommended minimum slope for effective surface drainage is **2% (1:50 ratio)** (PD 1096 Rule XIII). (NSCP Section 302 or standard engineering practice). This ensures water flows reliably and does not accumulate near foundations, reducing risk of seepage, hydrostatic pressure, and structural damage.

**Cut-and-Fill Concepts:**

- **Cut:** Removing soil from high areas to lower the elevation or create a building platform
- **Fill:** Placing soil in low areas to raise elevation or create finished grades
- **Cut-and-fill balance:** Ideally, the volume of material cut equals the volume filled on site, minimizing off-site disposal and import costs

A site with 2m average cut and 3m average fill requires importing soil (or exporting excess cut material).

**Ramp and Pedestrian Slope Standards:**

- Accessible pedestrian ramps (BP 344 2024 IRR): 1:20 gradient = 5.0% maximum
- Vehicular ramps to parking: 1:10 gradient = 10% maximum (PD 1096)
- Short vehicular ramps (&lt;1.5m rise): 1:8 gradient = 12.5% (maximum)
- Driveway from street: 1:10 gradient = 10% (comfortable maximum)

## Key Provisions

**Slope Calculation Formula:**
Slope % = (Elevation Change ÷ Horizontal Distance) × 100

**Surface Drainage Minimum:** 2% slope away from building perimeters (1:50 ratio)

**Contour Interval and Area:** On a 1:100 scale plan with 1m contour intervals, each contour line represents a 1m vertical change. The spacing between contours (measured on the plan) indicates slope steepness.

**Grading Accuracy Standards:**
- Finished grades within ±0.1m of design elevation (site engineering standard)
- Storm drainage slopes minimum 0.5% (1:200) if surface drainage infeasible
- Subsurface drainage (French drains, perforated pipes) required in zones where surface slope &lt;2%

**Pedestrian and Vehicular Ramp Standards:**

| Type | Gradient Ratio | Slope % | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accessible Pedestrian | 1:20 | 5.0% | BP 344 2024 IRR |
| Vehicular to Parking | 1:6 | 16.7% | Standard Practice |
| Short Vehicular (&lt;1.5m) | 1:4.5 | 22% | Maximum Allowable |
| Driveway from Street | 1:10 | 10% | Typical Maximum |

## Philippine Practice Context

In the Philippines, where monsoon rains deliver intense precipitation and many sites have challenging topography, grading and drainage design are critical. Tropical storms can produce 100+ mm rainfall in hours, overwhelming poorly designed drainage systems. Low-lying areas near Manila Bay and major rivers face seasonal flooding; thus, finished floor elevations must be set well above projected flood levels. Site grading often requires coordination with DPWH flood control initiatives and local watershed management. Hard-to-develop sites (steep hillsides, swampy areas) demand specialized engineering and sometimes cannot be developed economically. The combination of volcanic and sedimentary geology creates variable soil bearing capacity, making geotechnical investigation essential before grading design.

## How It's Tested

**Pattern 1: Slope Percentage Calculation**
Given contour spacing and elevation change, calculate slope %. LEA presents a contour map segment with numeric elevation labels and asks you to determine if a slope meets a design threshold (e.g., "Is this slope steeper than 15%?").

**Pattern 2: Drainage Direction and Grading Logic**
A site plan shows a building and contours. Identify the direction surface water will flow and whether additional grading (swleas, ramps, terracing) is needed to achieve 2% minimum drainage slope away from the building.

**Pattern 3: Ramp Design and Gradient Compliance**
Calculate the required length of a ramp given rise and maximum allowable gradient (e.g., "A wheelchair ramp must clear a 1.2m height limit; what minimum horizontal length is required at 1:20 slope?"). Answer: 1.2m × 20 = 24.0m.

**Pattern 4: Cut-and-Fill Volume Estimation**
Estimate whether a site grading plan will require soil import/export by comparing average cut depth to average fill depth across the site.

**Pattern 5: Contour Reading Under Pressure**
Multiple contour maps are presented; identify which represents the steepest terrain, or calculate slopes from maps with varying scales and contour intervals.
