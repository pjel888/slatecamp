## UAP Document 301 Overview: Purpose, Structure, and the Three-Party Relationship

## Board Exam Quick Check

UAP Document 301 is a General Conditions document for the construction contract. It defines the Owner, Architect, Engineer, Project Representative, Contractor, Sub-Contractor, Surety, bond types, bid-related documents, Drawings, Specifications, and other contract documents. Treat it as a construction-contract administration source, not as the full Owner-Architect design-services agreement.

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to:

- Identify the parties and documents defined in UAP Document 301.
- Explain the Architect's construction-period status and limits of authority.
- Apply source-backed interpretation rules for Drawings, Specifications, and written dimensions.
- Compare UAP Document 301 with CIAP Document 102 where both address private construction contract administration.

## What UAP Document 301 Covers

UAP Document 301 defines Contract Documents as the Agreement, General Conditions, Special Provisions, Specifications, and Drawings, including additions, deletions, and modifications incorporated before execution. It defines the Owner as the person or entity ordering the project, the Architect as the professional commissioned by the Owner, and the Contractor as the person or firm whose proposal has been accepted and to whom the Contract was awarded.

The document also defines bond and bidding terms: Proposal Bond, Performance Bond, Payment Bond, Guaranteed Bond, Advertisement or Invitation to Bid, Bid Bulletin, Instructions to Bidders, Drawings, Specifications, Supplementary Specifications, and Schedule of Materials and Finishes. For LEA purposes, these definitions are often enough to eliminate distractor answers that confuse design services, bidding, and construction administration.

## Three-Party Relationship

During construction, UAP Document 301 places the Architect as the Owner's representative. The Architect may act for the Owner only to the extent expressly provided in the Contract Documents or otherwise in writing, and the Contractor must be shown that authority. The Architect may stop the work when necessary in the Architect's reasonable opinion to ensure proper execution of the Contract, but the Architect's failure to order stoppage does not relieve the Contractor from complying with the Contract Documents or protecting persons and property.

The Architect decides questions about quality and acceptability of materials and work, performance and progress, interpretation of Drawings and Specifications, and acceptable fulfillment of the Contract. As interpreter and judge of performance, the Architect must not side with either Owner or Contractor; the Architect uses contract powers to enforce faithful performance by both.

## Contract Interpretation

UAP Document 301 states that all work must conform to the Contract Documents. If Drawings and Specifications vary, the Specifications control, and detailed specification requirements control over the General Conditions or modifications. The Contractor must report discrepancies or omissions in Drawings or Specifications to the Architect or Engineer, who corrects them after discovery; work involving unreported discrepancies is at the Contractor's risk.

For drawing coordination, UAP Document 301 states that Drawings and models cooperate with the Specifications. Figures are followed in preference to scaled measurements, and work shown in Drawings but not mentioned in Specifications, or vice versa, is treated as included when reasonably implied.

## CIAP Document 102 Cross-Check

CIAP Document 102 is another private construction contract source. It defines Bid Documents, Bid Bulletin, Instruction to Bidders, Contract Documents, Drawings, Specifications, Performance Bond, Payment Bond, and Guarantee Bond. It also provides interpretation rules: detailed drawings prevail over general drawings; figures written on Drawings prevail over the Drawings themselves; calculated dimensions prevail over scaled dimensions; and if Rule 3 does not resolve the conflict, the document priority includes Agreement, Instruction to Bidders, Addenda, Drawings, Specifications, Special Conditions, General Conditions, and other Contract Documents.

## Source Anchors

- UAP Document 301: definitions, conformity, drawings/specifications, suspension and termination, bonds, Architect status, and time limits.
- CIAP Document 102: private construction contract definitions, interpretation rules, suspension and termination, and contractor bond/warranty provisions.
