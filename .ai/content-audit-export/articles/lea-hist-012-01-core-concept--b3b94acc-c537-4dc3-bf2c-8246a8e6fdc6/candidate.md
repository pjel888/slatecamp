# Maya and Aztec Architecture

## LEA Point

Maya and Aztec architecture are both Mesoamerican, but the LEA exam usually rewards precise separation by structure, chronology, and ritual program.

## Source Anchors

- Maya: Tikal, Chichen Itza, and Palenque provide the source-backed site set for this cluster.
- Maya construction: corbelled vaults are made by projecting stones inward until they meet. They are visually powerful but structurally different from a Roman true arch.
- Maya historical record: stelae, inscriptions, dynastic records, tomb chambers, and astronomical alignments appear in site-specific questions.
- Aztec: Templo Mayor is the source-backed double-temple comparison point, with ritual association to Tlaloc and Huitzilopochtli.
- Chronology: Classic Maya achievement peaks earlier than the Aztec imperial period, so do not reverse the timeline.

## Exam Pattern

If the clue says corbelled vault, stela, Palenque, Tikal, or Kukulcan/Chichen Itza, think Maya. If it says twin temple/pyramid summits tied to Tlaloc and Huitzilopochtli in the Mexica capital, think Aztec Templo Mayor.

## Guardrails

- Do not spell Palenque as Pleanque.
- Do not treat all stepped pyramids as interchangeable. The named deity pair and urban precinct matter.
- Do not call the corbelled vault a true arch; its load path and span logic differ.

## Real World Scenario

In a site-identification question, a carved stela in a plaza with glyphic/date recording points toward Maya practice. A double shrine in a central imperial precinct, by contrast, points toward the Aztec Templo Mayor pattern.
