# Urban Regeneration and Placemaking

## Board Exam Quick Check

For LEA planning questions, separate the scale of intervention before choosing an answer. Urban regeneration and infill development reshape underused districts or parcels; placemaking improves public realm value through community participation; tactical urbanism tests small, temporary, low-cost interventions before larger commitments.

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

The DHSUD Resilient Urban Design and Development Guidelines treat resilient urban design as a CLUP and zoning ordinance support tool for greener, safer, and more livable settlements. In this source family, urban regeneration is best handled through concrete design and implementation strategies such as infill development, urban waterfront development, adaptive reuse, and placemaking.

Placemaking is a collaborative and participatory process that lets communities shape public spaces so those spaces become vibrant, inclusive, and functional. The guidelines emphasize that the community is the expert, partnerships matter, public-space form should support community function, and short-term improvements can be refined over time.

Tactical placemaking and tactical urbanism use small-scale, short-term, low-cost interventions to test ideas and build community engagement. These tools do not replace long-term planning; they help planners learn from how people use a place before committing major capital.

Urban renewal tools in the guidelines include infill development and urban waterfront development. Infill development uses unused or underutilized land within existing built-up areas, while waterfront development connects compatible land uses, public spaces, mobility, and resilient adaptation measures near water bodies.

Adaptive reuse is a building-level regeneration strategy: an existing historic or older building is converted for a new use while retaining essential architectural or cultural significance. It belongs with conservation and urban renewal because it extends the useful life of existing built fabric.

## Exam Focus

- Regeneration is district or parcel renewal, often through infill, waterfront redevelopment, adaptive reuse, or other urban renewal programs.
- Placemaking is community-centered public realm improvement.
- Tactical urbanism is temporary, low-cost, and experimental.
- Good regeneration checks compatibility with surrounding community, streetscape, public realm, and disaster or climate resilience.
- Consultation is part of project programming and appears repeatedly in DHSUD sample project workflows.

## How It Is Tested

Expect scenario questions asking which tool fits a situation: a vacant lot in a built-up area points to infill development; a riverside district points to waterfront development; a temporary street closure or parklet points to tactical urbanism; a historic warehouse converted to a new use points to adaptive reuse.
