## What You Need to Know for the LEA
## Learning Goal

Distinguish specification types — prescriptive, performance, proprietary, reference standard — by their approach to defining requirements, and identify when each type is appropriate based on project goals, procurement rules, and design control needs.
## Core Concept

Quality control operates through a systematic process: (1) specification defines requirements, (2) material arrives at site, (3) testing verifies conformance, (4) documentation records results, (5) architect approves or rejects. Testing occurs at three stages: _incoming inspection_ (material upon delivery), _in-process inspection_ (during mixing, placement, or installation), and _final acceptance inspection_ (completed work).

_Concrete slump testing_ per ASTM C143 measures consistency and workability of fresh concrete. A slump cone (300 mm height) is filled with freshly mixed concrete, lifted vertically, and the vertical distance from the top of the cone to the highest point of the slumped concrete is measured in millimeters. Typical slump ranges: 25-50 mm (stiff concrete for columns and walls), 75-100 mm (medium consistency for general work), 150-175 mm (fluid concrete for pumped applications). The ACI 318 and NSCP reference slump as a quality control indicator. High slump indicates excess water and reduced strength. Low slump indicates inadequate workability and placement difficulty. Slump testing frequency should be specified in the project specifications with reference to DPWH Department Order No. 136, s.2016 and ASTM C143. Typical practice requires testing at each batch or pour, with minimum frequency of once per day or per 100 m³, whichever is more frequent.

_Concrete cylinder compression testing_ per ASTM C39 determines compressive strength. Concrete cylinders (150 mm diameter, 300 mm height) are cast at the construction site, cured per ASTM C31 (field curing conditions), and tested at specified ages (typically 7 days, 14 days, and 28 days). A hydraulic compression testing machine applies axial load until failure. Compressive strength (MPa) equals maximum load divided by cross-sectional area. Results are used to verify that concrete meets design strength (e.g., 28-day design strength of 28 MPa). DPWH requires minimum testing frequency: one set of cylinders (three cylinders per set) per 75 cubic meters of concrete or per day's pouring, whichever is more frequent, per DPWH Standard Specifications for Public Works Structures Volume III. Typical NSCP and ACI 318 acceptance: the 28-day average must meet or exceed design strength; individual tests must not fall below 90 percent of design strength.

_Rebar tensile testing_ per ASTM A615 (for deformed bars) or A706 (for low-alloy bars) verifies yield strength and ultimate tensile strength. A sample rebar is placed in a tensile testing machine and pulled until failure. Yield strength (the stress at which permanent deformation occurs) must match the specified grade (e.g., Grade 40 (280 MPa) or Grade 60 (420 MPa) per ASTM or PCA standards). DPWH requires testing of rebar from each mill or lot prior to fabrication. Per DPWH Standard Specifications, the frequency is one quality test per 10,000 kg (10 metric tons) or fraction thereof for each size and grade.

_Shop drawing review_ occurs before fabrication to verify that fabricator designs and details align with specifications and working drawings. For structural steel connections, shop drawings show bolt layout, weld design, connection plates, and inspection access points. For precast concrete, shop drawings show reinforcement placement, lifting points, and bearing details. The architect and structural engineer review and approve shop drawings, often requiring revisions before fabrication proceeds.

_Submittal review_ includes product certificates of compliance, mill test reports, material safety data sheets (MSDS), and samples. For paint, the fabricator submits paint samples on specified substrates, allowing color and finish verification. For windows, submittals include thermal performance data (U-value, SHGC), installation instructions, and warranty information. Submittal review is documented on a submittal log, and the architect stamps submissions "Approved," "Approved as noted," or "Revise and resubmit."

_Mock-ups_ are small-scale reproductions of completed assemblies (e.g., a 3m x 3m wall assembly with waterproofing, insulation, and finish paint) that are installed at the project site and inspected for conformance before full-scale production. Mock-ups are particularly valuable for novel systems or where quality is difficult to verify after installation.

## Philippine Construction Context

The DPWH Guidebook on Project Quality Assurance (2019) establishes mandatory testing schedules and acceptance criteria for public works. DPWH projects require third-party laboratory testing for concrete and rebar, with laboratories seeking ISO 9001 certification and ISO 17025 accreditation. The Engineer's Representative (ER) must be present at critical testing points and document results on Job Control Forms (JCF). Witness points and hold points are established in the project specification to ensure quality checkpoints.

In private practice, the architect is responsible for defining testing frequency and acceptance criteria in Part 1: General of specifications. Testing may be performed by project laboratories (third-party independent), fabricator laboratories (contractor-controlled, subject to approval), or combination. The architect reviews test reports and either accepts the work or requires correction/retesting.

Philippine concrete quality control is challenging due to tropical humidity, variable aggregate quality (local sources often lack quality certification), and cement sourcing. Slump test variability is common due to temperature and humidity effects. NSCP adoption of ACI 318 standards ensures that testing and acceptance criteria are aligned with international practice. Waterproofing testing and mock-ups are particularly important in tropical construction due to persistent moisture exposure.

## How It's Tested

_Pattern 1 — Slump Test Interpretation:_ The exam presents a slump test result (e.g., "Slump measured at 150 mm, specification requires 75-100 mm") and asks the appropriate action. Answer: Slump is too high (excess water); concrete strength may be reduced. The batch should be rejected or adjusted by reducing water or adding coarse aggregate. The contractor cannot proceed with placement.

_Pattern 2 — Cylinder Compression Results:_ Given test results (e.g., "7-day: 18 MPa, 14-day: 24 MPa, 28-day: 26 MPa; specification requires 28 MPa at 28 days"), determine conformance. Answer: The 28-day result is 26 MPa, which is below the 28 MPa requirement. If the specification allows 90 percent, minimum acceptable is 25.2 MPa. The 26 MPa result is marginally acceptable depending on whether the specification allows 90 percent or requires full strength. Documentation of the marginal acceptance is critical.

_Pattern 3 — Testing Frequency Calculation:_ A concrete placement of 250 cubic meters requires testing. DPWH specifies "1 set per 50 m3." How many sets? Answer: 250 / 50 = 5 sets required. Each set consists of 2 cylinders (one for 7-day, one for 28-day testing, or both broken at 28 days for average). Incorrect calculation is a common error.

_Pattern 4 — Shop Drawing Red Flags:_ The exam presents a shop drawing for structural steel connections showing bolts with 30 mm edge distance (specification requires minimum 32 mm). Identify the issue. Answer: The edge distance violates specification and may create prying tension or local buckling. The drawing must be rejected ("Revise and resubmit"). The architect does not approve until corrected.

_Pattern 5 — Submittal Completeness:_ A paint product submittal includes color samples but omits MSDS (material safety data sheet) and surface preparation instructions. Determine adequacy. Answer: Submittal is incomplete. The architect cannot approve without MSDS (regulatory requirement for workplace safety) and surface preparation (critical for paint adhesion). Submittal should be marked "Revise and resubmit."

_Pattern 6 — Philippine Regulatory Alignment:_ The exam presents a concrete testing frequency from an international standard that differs from DPWH requirements. Determine which applies. Answer: For DPWH-funded projects, DPWH requirements supersede. The more stringent requirement applies (DPWH testing frequency typically exceeds ACI minimums). The specification must state: "DPWH testing frequencies apply; if DPWH and ACI 318 differ, the more stringent requirement governs."
