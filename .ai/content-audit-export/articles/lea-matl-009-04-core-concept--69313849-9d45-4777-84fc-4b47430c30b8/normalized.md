## Board Exam Quick Check

For quality-control questions, do not assume an ASTM or product-standard answer unless the question gives that standard. In this batch, the source-safe frame is documentation, specifications, inspection, testing, and professional responsibility under the checked local sources.

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

PD 1096 RIRR states that the National Building Code provides minimum standards and requirements regulating location, site, design, quality of materials, construction, use, occupancy, and maintenance. It also requires buildings proposed for construction to comply with regulations and specifications, including safety standards, for materials, construction methods, occupancy, and classification.

For public buildings, PD 1096 RIRR directs designers to specify good to high quality materials, labor, technologies, and construction methods within the approved budget to ensure permanence, long continued use, and low maintenance cost. It also recognizes inspection and testing controls: the Building Official may inspect installations against approved plans and specifications, and prefabricated assemblies must pass special tests by accredited material testing laboratories.

RA 9266 IRR places specifying, preparation of specifications, bill of materials, cost estimates, and contract documents within the architect's professional scope. UAP SPP 203 identifies Building Testing and Commissioning, Contract Documentation and Review, specifications writing, estimation, and quantity survey as specialized architectural-service areas.

## Learning Goal

- Anchor quality control in specifications, approved documents, inspection, testing, and professional scope.
- Distinguish local source-audited requirements from external product-test standards not provided in the item.
- Identify when a question is asking for documentation control rather than a material-test formula.

## Core Concept

Quality control is a chain of source-backed controls, not a single form or checklist.

| Control point | Source-safe role |
| --- | --- |
| Code minimum standards | PD 1096 regulates material quality, construction, use, occupancy, and maintenance. |
| Specifications | RA 9266 places specifications and related contract documents within architectural practice. |
| Approved plans/specs | Permit and inspection processes compare work with approved documents. |
| Special tests | PD 1096 requires accredited laboratory testing for prefabricated assemblies when applicable. |
| Building testing/commissioning | UAP SPP 203 identifies this as a specialized architectural service. |

A source-safe LEA answer asks: What document controls the requirement? Who is responsible for the architectural document? What inspection or test verifies compliance? Is the cited standard actually given in the problem?

## Key Facts and Testable Items

| Exam cue | Correct source-safe response |
| --- | --- |
| Quality of materials | PD 1096 regulates it as part of minimum building standards. |
| Approved plans and specifications | Use them as the inspection and compliance baseline. |
| Prefabricated assemblies | Special tests by accredited material testing laboratories may be required. |
| Specifications | Architect-prepared architectural documents under RA 9266 signing/sealing rules. |
| Testing and commissioning | Specialized service area under UAP SPP 203. |

## How It's Tested on the LEA

Expect questions that try to pull you toward a generic laboratory standard. If the standard is not named by the question or checked source, answer at the document-control level: specifications, approved plans, inspection, special tests, and architect-of-record responsibility.

Expect submittal-style questions. The safest answer requires enough documentation to verify compliance before approval, not after installation.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating product samples as enough when specifications or safety data still need review.
- Approving a submittal without tying it back to the approved documents.
- Citing external test procedures when the question only asks for local-code documentation control.
- Separating quality from budget, permanence, and long-term maintenance in public-building specifications.

## Key Terms

**Quality Control** - Verification that materials, work, and documents conform to the controlling specifications and code requirements.

**Approved Plans and Specifications** - The baseline documents used for permit, inspection, and compliance checks.

**Special Test** - A test required for particular assemblies or systems, performed by an accredited material testing laboratory when applicable.

**Building Testing and Commissioning** - A specialized architectural-service area identified in UAP SPP 203.
