## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal

Distinguish the two primary Philippine green building frameworks — BERDE (Philippine national standard) and EDGE (World Bank IFC tool) — by their certification levels, assessment criteria, and applicability to different project types.
## Core Concept

Green building in the Philippines operates at the intersection of voluntary excellence (BERDE) and mandatory compliance (Code/RA 9729). This dual framework reflects global practice but with Philippines-specific climate and resource constraints.

**Regulatory Foundation: RA 9729 and the Philippine Green Building Code**

Republic Act 9729, the Philippine Climate Change Act of 2009, establishes the national climate change policy framework and delegates specific architecture/building authority to the Department of Energy and the Green Building Code. The Code itself was issued in 2015 (June 2015 edition) as a mandatory regulatory standard under RA 9729, establishing minimum standards for building envelope efficiency, ventilation, mechanical systems, electrical efficiency, water conservation, material sustainability, waste management, site sustainability, and indoor environmental quality.

**Key RA 9729 Mandate for Architects:**
- Technical professionals (architects, engineers, builders) have a responsibility to ensure buildings are planned, designed, constructed, and operated to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and adapt to climate impacts.
- The Code applies to all new buildings, major renovations, and buildings above certain size thresholds (varies by building type).
- Compliance is verified during design review and final occupancy certification; non-compliance can result in project delay or occupancy denial.

**Philippine Green Building Code Enforcement (2015):**
The Code specifies minimum requirements in seven domains:

1. **Building Envelope:** Roof insulation (50–75mm minimum, U-value dependent on climate region), window properties (U-value &lt;5.8 W/m²K typical), air tightness (joints, service penetrations sealed to limit infiltration).

2. *Natural Ventilation: Minimum 8–12 air changes per hour for naturally ventilated spaces; operable windows must provide 10% minimum of floor area as opening area.rea.

3. Building Envelope Color: Roof surfaces shall be colored white or have a minimum Solar Reflectance Index (SRI) of 70; light-colored or reflective finishes on wall surfaces to reduce solar heat gain.
4. Roof Insulation: Minimum R-value R-8 (approximately 50–75mm fiberglass or equivalent); higher values in intensive solar-load areas.as.

5. **Mechanical Systems:** COP (Coefficient of Performance) minimum 2.5 for air-conditioning units; duct sealing; regular maintenance protocols.

6. **Electrical Systems:** Lighting power density limits (8–10 W/m² depending on occupancy type); LED fixtures minimum 40 lumens/watt; occupancy-based controls.

7. **Water Efficiency:** Minimum 30% reduction in water use versus baseline (rainwater harvesting, low-flow fixtures, greywater systems).

**Enforcement Mechanism:** The Department of Energy works with local building officials to verify Code compliance. Projects must submit Green Building Compliance Certificate; occupancy permit contingent on passing energy audit.

**Voluntary Excellence: BERDE Rating System**

While the Code sets minimums, BERDE rewards performance above baseline. BERDE was established by the Philippine Green Building Council and recognized by the Department of Energy as the National Voluntary Green Rating System.

**BERDE Rating Levels:**
Projects receive a star rating (1–5 stars) based on points earned across 11 impact categories. Point thresholds typically:
- 1 Star: 25–40 points
- 2 Stars: 40–55 points
- 3 Stars: 55–70 points
- 4 Stars: 70–85 points
- 5 Stars: 85+ points

(Exact thresholds vary by BERDE version; v5.0 is the current version.)

**BERDE 11 Impact Categories (Current Framework):**

1. **Energy Efficiency and Conservation (16% weighting in new construction):** Points for HVAC efficiency, lighting power density, occupancy controls, daylight harvesting, renewable energy generation (solar PV, wind).

2. **Water Efficiency (10% weighting):** Points for below-baseline water consumption, greywater/rainwater systems, water-efficient fixtures, treatment/reuse.

3. **Waste Management (8% weighting):** Waste reduction during construction, sorting at source, recycling targets, composting, hazardous-waste protocols.

4. **Management (12% weighting):** Building maintenance protocols, staff training, monitoring systems, energy/water metering.

5. **Use of Land and Ecology (16% weighting in new construction):** Site selection (brownfield vs. greenfield), habitat preservation, urban density (location near transit), native plantings, tree canopy preservation.

6. **Green Materials (10% weighting):** Local/regional sourcing (reduced embodied carbon), rapidly renewable materials, low-VOC finishes, FSC-certified wood, recycled-content products.

7. **Transportation (5% weighting):** Proximity to public transit, car-sharing, bicycle facilities, EV charging infrastructure, reduced parking.

8. **Health and Wellbeing (10% weighting, replacing "Indoor Environmental Quality" in v5.0):** Indoor air quality (CO₂ monitoring, mechanical ventilation), thermal comfort, daylighting, acoustic quality, biophilia (access to nature views).

9. **Emissions (6% weighting):** Refrigerant management (low-GWP alternatives), ozone-depleting substance phase-out, embodied-carbon targets.

10. **Community Engagement (4% weighting):** Stakeholder consultation, local employment, affordable-housing inclusion, community benefit agreements.

11. **Economic Opportunity (3% weighting):** Green jobs created, local supplier engagement, cost-benefit transparency.

(Weighting percentages vary by rating scheme—new construction, retrofit, operations, existing buildings each have distinct weightings.)

**BERDE Certification Process:**
1. Project registration (early design phase)
2. Evidence submission (post-construction)
3. Third-party verification (BERDE assessor reviews documentation)
4. Certification awarded (star rating issued)

**BERDE Rating Schemes:**
- **New Construction (NC):** Applied to new buildings (emphasis: design optimization, site selection, material selection)
- **Retrofits and Renovations (R&R):** Applied to existing buildings undergoing major renovation (emphasis: operational improvements, material substitution, water/energy system upgrades)
- **Operations (Ops):** Applied to occupied buildings (emphasis: energy/water consumption, waste diversion, maintenance, occupant satisfaction)
- **Existing Buildings (EB):** Applied to buildings 5+ years old without renovation (emphasis: operational optimization without major capital investment)

**BERDE vs. LEED Comparison (Critical for LEA)**

This comparison is frequently tested:

| Criterion | BERDE | LEED (v4.1) |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Origin | Philippine Green Building Council | US Green Building Council |
| Climate Specificity | Philippine tropical maritime zones (Type I–IV) | Global/US-focused; tropical optional |
| Mandatory Baseline | Code compliance required first | Code compliance required |
| Water Category | 30% reduction emphasis; rainwater harvesting encouraged | Water budget based on fixture count; no harvesting required |
| Site Category | Brownfield incentives; density near transit | Brownfield incentives; similar density focus |
| Energy Benchmarking | kWh/m²/year targets by building type | IECC baseline minus % improvement |
| Renewable Energy | Solar PV credit (encouraging given typhoon resilience) | Renewable percentage credit |
| Local Materials | Regional sourcing (reduced embodied carbon) | Regional sourcing plus recycled content |
| Indoor Environment | Health and wellbeing (biophilia, air quality, daylighting) | Health + Safety (chemical emissions, lighting, acoustic) |
| Weighting Distribution | Relatively balanced (no single category > 16%) | Energy-heavy (~30% total); site-light (~7%) |
| Ease in Philippines | High (tailored to climate, Code alignment) | Medium (requires adaptation for tropical context) |

**Key Distinction:** BERDE is Philippines-calibrated; LEED is US-biased. A building that achieves LEED Gold might fail BERDE Platinum because LEED underweights water efficiency and tropical site ecology. For LEA exam, if comparing systems, emphasize that BERDE is the *national* standard and accounts for Philippine climate/resources, whereas LEED is *international* and requires adaptation.

**Energy Compliance Pathways Under RA 9729**

The Philippine Green Building Code offers two compliance paths:

**Path A (Prescriptive):** Meet Code minimum in each category (roof insulation, window U-values, lighting power density, etc.). Straightforward but often leaves efficiency on the table. Typical in small residential/commercial projects.

**Path B (Performance):** Demonstrate via energy modeling (simulation) that total building annual energy consumption is ≤90% of baseline (or a specified target). More flexible; allows trade-offs (high-efficiency HVAC offset by larger windows, for example). Increasingly required for 2,000+ m² buildings in metro areas.

**Energy Modeling Software Accepted by DOE:**
- EnergyPlus (US DOE, freely available; most rigorous)
- TRACE 3D+ (proprietary; widely used by MEP engineers in Philippines)
- IES 
- HAP/CHVAC (design-stage estimates)

Models must account for Philippine tropical conditions: 26–28°C baseline temperatures, 75–85% humidity, monsoon wind patterns, and Type I–IV climate variation by region.

**Net-Zero and Beyond**

The Code does not mandate net-zero (renewable generation = annual consumption); however, BERDE provides points for renewable energy, and forward-looking projects pursue net-zero targets (especially in metro areas where grid reliability is questioned post-typhoon).

**Net-Zero Definition (LEA context):**
- Annual on-site renewable energy generation ≥ annual building consumption
- Typically 40–60% solar PV + 10–20% passive/behavioral efficiency + grid export/import balancing

**Embodied Carbon vs. Operational Carbon**

RA 9729 and the Code focus heavily on operational carbon (annual energy use). However, BERDE and forward-looking designers now consider embodied carbon (CO₂ in materials).

**Embodied Carbon Hierarchy:**
1. **Material substitution:** Replace high-carbon materials (concrete, steel) with lower-carbon alternatives (cross-laminated timber, bamboo, recycled materials, rammed earth).
2. **Local sourcing:** Materials from Philippines-based suppliers reduce transport carbon. Concrete from Metro Manila supplier: 0.2 kg CO₂/km transport. Imported steel: 5,000 km = 1,000 kg CO₂.
3. **Recycled content:** Recycled steel (0.4 kg CO₂/kg) vs. virgin steel (1.8 kg CO₂/kg). Significant offset potential.
4. **Material efficiency:** Optimized structural design reduces material volume; design reduction in steel/concrete by 15–20% is achievable without performance loss.

**LEA Testing Focus:** Embodied carbon trade-offs are increasingly tested. Example: "A developer chooses bamboo column frames (low embodied carbon, ~0.5 kg CO₂/kg) over reinforced concrete (1.2 kg CO₂/kg) despite slightly larger cross-sections. Calculate embodied carbon savings for a 50-column building, 0.3m³ per column (concrete density 2,400 kg/m³, bamboo laminate 650 kg/m³)."

**Philippine Green Building Case Study Application**

The LEA often presents a scenario building and asks students to apply BERDE and Code requirements. Example structure:

**Scenario:** "An 8,000 m² office building in Quezon City (Type IV climate, Metro Manila location, mixed-use: office + 150m² retail, on a 1.2 Ha infill urban site adjacent to LRT station). Design a compliance strategy addressing (1) Code minimum path, (2) BERDE 3-Star target, (3) embodied carbon reduction, (4) typhoon resilience per RA 9729 climate goals."

**Expected Answer Components:**
1. **Code Compliance (Path A Prescriptive or Path B Performance):**
   - Roof: White coating (albedo 0.8) + 60mm mineral wool insulation (R-8), meeting Code minimum
   - Windows: Double-glazed low-E (U-value 4.5 W/m²K), occupying 25% facade area south-facing (shaded by 2m overhang per Climate Context article)
   - HVAC: VRF system (COP 3.2 typical) + dedicated OA with ERV (energy recovery ventilation)
   - Lighting: LED (9W/m² target for office) + daylight-responsive dimming
   - Water: Low-flow fixtures (1.9 L/min toilets, 5.7 L/min showers), 4,000 m³ annual baseline → 30% reduction target (2,800 m³/year allowed)

2. **BERDE 3-Star Targeting (55–70 points):**
   - **Energy (16% weighting):** Score 12–14 points by exceeding Code by 15% (Path B simulation showing 85 kWh/m²/year vs. 100 baseline) + 4 kW solar PV system
   - **Water (10%):** Rainwater tank (50 m³ capacity, roof 8,000m² × average 2,000mm / retention 40% = 640 m³ annual; design 50 m³ tank + 25% demand offset)
   - **Waste (8%):** Construction waste plan targeting 75% recycling/diversion; interior source sorting areas
   - **Land/Ecology (16%):** Brownfield + urban infill site (6 points); 20% tree canopy + native plantings (4 points); LRT adjacency (3 points)
   - **Green Materials (10%):** Bamboo interior partitions (local, rapidly renewable), recycled-content concrete (30% fly ash), FSC plywood; targeting 8 points
   - **Health/Wellbeing (10%):** CO₂ monitoring (400–800 ppm setpoint); thermal comfort per ASHRAE 55; Daylight Factor >2% in 75% of occupied areas; acoustic design (NC 45 open office); 6 points
   - **Other categories (28% combined):** Management, Transportation, Emissions, Community, Economic = remaining 14–18 points

3. **Embodied Carbon (Not formally scored by BERDE but increasingly audited):**
   - **Baseline structural:** 450 m³ concrete + 80 tonnes steel = (450 × 2,400 × 1.2) + (80 × 1.8) = 1,296 + 144 = 1,440 tonnes CO₂e
   - **Optimized structural:** Cross-laminated timber frames (80% steel reduction, concrete 30% reduction) = (315 × 2,400 × 1.2) + (16 × 1.8) = 907 + 29 = 936 tonnes CO₂e
   - **Savings:** 504 tonnes CO₂e (35% reduction); secondary benefit = typhoon-resilient flexible framing

4. **Typhoon Resilience (RA 9729 Climate Adaptation):**
   - Wind load design: 1.9g (higher than standard; accounts for Typhoon Belt)
   - Impact-resistant glazing on facade facing prevailing habagat direction
   - Roof fastening: 300mm spacing (vs. standard 600mm) to prevent blow-off in 180 kph gusts
   - Ground floor flood resilience: mechanical systems (HVAC, electrical) elevated 1.5m; water-resistant finishes 2m above grade
   - Backup power: EV-charging station + battery storage (10 kWh) for critical loads post-typhoon

**BERDE and Code Alignment**

Critical for LEA: The Code is a *floor* (mandatory minimum); BERDE is a *ceiling* (voluntary excellence). A building cannot achieve BERDE certification without first meeting Code. The systems reinforce each other: Code prevents low-performing baseline; BERDE incentivizes market leaders.

However, occasional conflicts arise:
- **Roof insulation:** Code mandates 50mm; BERDE might penalize 100mm as excessive embodied carbon.
- **Operable windows:** Code requires 5% opening area for natural ventilation; BERDE might incentivize full-operable facades (30%+ area) for health/wellbeing. Verify BERDE guidance on operable-fraction targets.

**Future Direction: Enhanced Ambition**

As of 2024, PHILGBC is developing BERDE v6.0 targeting 2030 climate goals:
- Carbon-neutral operational targets (net-zero energy + verified offsets)
- Mandatory embodied-carbon tracking (cradle-to-gate)
- Enhanced water resilience (droughts + flooding)
- Biodiversity net-gain requirements (not just preservation)

LEA students should anticipate questions referencing "climate-ambitious design" beyond current BERDE requirements.

## Key Terms

**RA 9729 (Philippine Climate Change Act, 2009)** — National framework establishing climate change policy, delegating building code authority to DOE; mandates that architects/engineers design buildings to reduce GHG emissions and adapt to climate hazards (typhoons, flooding).

**Philippine Green Building Code (2015)** — Mandatory minimum standards for building envelope, ventilation, mechanical/electrical systems, water efficiency, materials, waste, site, and indoor environmental quality; applies to new buildings and major renovations above thresholds.

**Path A (Prescriptive Compliance)** — Code compliance by meeting specified minimums in each category (roof insulation depth, window U-values, lighting power density); deterministic, no modeling required, suitable for buildings &lt;2,000 m².

**Path B (Performance-Based Compliance)** — Code compliance via energy simulation demonstrating annual consumption ≤90% of baseline; flexible trade-offs allowed; requires EnergyPlus or equivalent; typical for ≥2,000 m² buildings in metro areas.

**BERDE (Building Ecological Responsive Design Excellence)** — Philippine national voluntary green rating system; 1–5 star rating across 11 impact categories; administered by Philippine Green Building Council; recognized by DOE.

**BERDE Rating Schemes** — Four program types: New Construction (NC), Retrofits & Renovations (R&R), Operations (Ops), Existing Buildings (EB); weightings vary by scheme (Energy 16% typical for NC; 12% for Ops, etc.).

**Embodied Carbon** — CO₂ released during material extraction, manufacturing, transport, and construction; measured in kg CO₂e per unit (kg CO₂/kg material typical). Exemplar: concrete ~1.2 kg CO₂/kg; bamboo laminate ~0.08 kg CO₂/kg.

**COP (Coefficient of Performance)** — Ratio of heating/cooling output to electrical input; higher COP indicates greater efficiency. Code minimum 2.5 for standard AC units; VRF systems typically achieve 3.0–3.5 in tropical climates.

**Net-Zero Energy** — Annual on-site renewable generation (solar PV, wind, geothermal) equals or exceeds annual building consumption; not mandated by Code but incentivized by BERDE and pursued by forward-looking developers.

**Energy Modeling Software** — Simulation tools predicting annual energy consumption under tropical climate conditions (26–28°C baseline, humidity 75–85%, monsoon wind). EnergyPlus (free, rigorous), TRACE 3D+, IES 

**ERV (Energy Recovery Ventilation)** — System capturing heat/moisture from exhaust air and transferring to incoming fresh air, reducing HVAC load; typically improves efficiency 20–25% in naturally ventilated spaces.

**Water Baseline Reduction** — Code/BERDE target of 30% below design baseline; achieved via low-flow fixtures (1.9 L/min toilets, 5.7 L/min showerheads) + rainwater/greywater systems offsetting 10–20% of demand.

**Albedo** — Solar reflectance of surface (0–1 scale); required minimum 0.6 on 30%+ of facade + roofs per Code; white/light finishes reduce surface temperature 25–30°C vs. dark materials.
