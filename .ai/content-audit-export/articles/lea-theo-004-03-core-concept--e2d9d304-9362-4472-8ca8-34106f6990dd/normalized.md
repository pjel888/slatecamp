# Tropical Green Building Design

In the checked TOA 4 source, tropical architecture is presented as a form of green building for tropical climates. The main energy problem is the cooling load, so green design begins with climate response rather than decorative environmental language.

## Hot-Humid Objectives

- Move filtered air and speed up useful winds.
- Minimize humidity-related problems and avoid mold growth.
- Provide strong shade, especially from late morning through the afternoon.
- Create a cool, darker microclimate around the building.
- Keep building density low enough to support air movement.
- Use vegetation for shade, evaporative cooling, and radiation absorption without blocking air circulation.

## Exam Use

A source-safe green-building answer in this topic connects energy reduction to form, shade, air movement, and landscape. If an option calls a building green but increases heat gain, blocks wind, or ignores the tropical cooling load, it is not aligned with the checked TOA 4 source.

## Common Trap

Do not treat green building as a label detached from climate. In this source family, the green strategy is measurable through reduced cooling demand, shade, airflow, and cooler microclimate design.
