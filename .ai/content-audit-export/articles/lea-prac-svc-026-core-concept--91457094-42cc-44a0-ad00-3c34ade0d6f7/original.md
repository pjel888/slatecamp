Site Development Planning (SDP) under SPP 203.4 tests your ability to distinguish between the architect's dual or single roles in landscape/hardscape coordination, correctly classify project size to determine Landscape Architect requirements, and calculate fees based on civil and landscaping cost estimates. High-stakes because candidates confuse SDP (SPP 203.4) with standalone Landscape Architecture (SPP 209) and misunderstand when a Registered Landscape Professional is mandatory versus optional.

## Core Concept

Site Development Planning (SDP) is sub-service #4 under SPP 203 (Professional Architectural Services) and involves conceptualizing the entire site development plan—civil works, softscape (planting and turf), and hardscape (paving, walls, water features)—along with specifications and bid assistance.

The architect may provide SDP services in two distinct capacities: as both Architect-of-Record (AoR) and Consulting Architect (CA) in dual capacity, or solely as a Consulting Architect (CA) while another architect serves as AoR. This distinction affects role definition and fee calculation but does not affect service scope.

Project size determines whether landscaping remains under the architect's scope or must be assigned to a Specialist Consultant. On small projects, the architect prepares the landscaping layout directly as part of SDP services. On large projects, the architect _must_ hire a Landscape Architect (Registered Landscape Professional or RLP) as Specialist Consultant—the architect cannot perform landscaping design alone. If the client separately engages a Landscape Architect, that Landscape Architect's fee is charged to the client's account; it is not deducted from the architect's SDP compensation.

Compensation for SDP services depends on the sum of estimated costs: civil works cost plus landscaping works (hardscape and softscape combined). The fee is computed per the Architect's Guidelines, with the overhead factor determined by the project's Group classification per the Architect's Guidelines.

<Warn>Do not confuse SPP 203.4 (SDP—a sub-service under Professional Architectural Services) with SPP 209 (Professional Architectural Consulting Services / PACS). SDP includes landscape/hardscape planning under the architect's scope; PACS does not.</Warn>

<Note>Softscape refers to vegetative elements (planting, turf, ground covers). Hardscape refers to constructed elements (paving, walls, steps, water features, site furniture). Both are included in landscaping works cost for fee calculation.</Note>

<Tip>Memory aid: Small projects = architect does landscaping layout directly. Large projects = architect must hire RLP Landscape Architect as Specialist Consultant. If client hires own Landscape Architect, that fee is the _client's_ account, not deducted from architect's fee.</Tip>

## Key Provisions

| Provision | Content |
|-----------|---------|
| **SDP Service Definition** | Conceptualize entire site development plan: civil works, softscape (planting/turf), hardscape (paving/walls/water features); prepare specifications; bid assistance; coordinate with civil engineer and Landscape Architect (if large project) |
| **Two Manners of Providing SDP** | (1) Dual Capacity: Architect serves as both AoR and CA for SDP; (2) CA Only: Architect serves as CA for SDP only (another architect is AoR) |
| **Small Project SDP** | Architect prepares landscaping layout directly as part of SDP services; no separate Landscape Architect required |
| **Large Project SDP** | Architect must hire Registered Landscape Professional (RLP) as Specialist Consultant (SC) for landscaping design; architect cannot perform landscaping alone |
| **Compensation Base** | Estimated cost of civil works + estimated cost of landscaping works (hardscape + softscape) |
| **Man-Month Calculation** | Per Architect's Guidelines; overhead factor determined by Group classification |
| **Client-Engaged Landscape Architect** | If client separately engages Landscape Architect, that fee is charged to client's account—NOT deducted from architect's SDP compensation |
| **Architect as Licensed Landscape Architect** | If architect also holds Landscape Architect license, SDP fee increases to reflect dual expertise |
| **Coordination Responsibility** | Architect coordinates with civil engineer (grading, drainage, utilities) and with Landscape Architect on large projects |

## Philippine Practice Context

In Philippine development practice, site development planning has become standard for residential and commercial projects, especially large-scale estates and commercial mixed-use developments. Architects frequently work with Landscape Architects to integrate planting and hardscape features into master plans. Many architects mistakenly treat landscaping as a minor component outside SDP scope, leading to scope creep and underpricing. SPP 203.4 clarifies that on large projects, engaging a qualified RLP is not optional but mandatory, ensuring quality and professional liability protection. Local clients increasingly expect coordinated site development packages, making clear fee distinction between architect and Landscape Architect essential for contract clarity.

## How It's Tested

**Pattern 1: Capacity and Role Distinction**
Questions present scenarios with multiple architects and ask which role the architect in SDP assumes (AoR, CA, or CA-only). Distractors confuse these roles or suggest they determine service scope (they do not).

**Pattern 2: Project Size Requirements**
Questions describe a project size (small or large) and ask whether the architect must hire an RLP Landscape Architect. Distractors include "optional for large projects" or "required for small projects" (reversed logic).

**Pattern 3: Compensation and Cost Basis**
Questions present civil works cost and landscaping costs and ask the correct fee calculation base. Distractors omit softscape, include hardscape only, or deduct a separately-engaged Landscape Architect's fee from the architect's compensation (incorrect).

**Pattern 4: SDP vs. Landscape Architecture Confusion**
Questions present SDP scope (planning, specifications, bid assistance, hardscape/softscape layout) and ask whether the service falls under SPP 203.4 (SDP) or SPP 209 (PACS). Distractors suggest SDP falls under SPP 209 or that full Landscape Architecture practice is part of architect's SDP scope.

<Tip>When a question mentions "landscape architecture services" or "plant selection and horticultural design," read carefully. If the context is site development planning and layout integration with civil works, it is SPP 203.4 SDP. If it is standalone landscape design services, it may fall outside standard SDP scope or require an RLP Specialist Consultant on large projects.</Tip>
