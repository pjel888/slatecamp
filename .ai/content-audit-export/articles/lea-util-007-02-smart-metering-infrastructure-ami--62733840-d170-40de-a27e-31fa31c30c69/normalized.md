# Smart metering and energy analytics

Smart metering questions should be answered as energy-audit and electrical-planning questions unless the item gives a separate utility tariff or product standard. The checked local sources do not require a specific AMI vendor stack. They do require usable metering, feeder visibility, tenant energy measurement, and energy-management records for buildings that fall within the stated scope.

## Exam Anchor

The safest LEA approach is to ask: is the problem testing electrical plans, metering for energy auditing, or RA 11285 compliance? Electrical plans identify the service, meter, service equipment, feeders, branch circuits, load schedules, grounding method, disconnecting means, and overcurrent protection. The energy-audit layer then asks whether the facility can measure the energy quantities needed for monitoring and reporting.

## Metering For Energy Auditing

The DOE Guidelines on Energy Conserving Design on Buildings require buildings with designed connected electrical load of 250 kVA and above to have metering facilities capable of measuring voltage, current, power factor, maximum demand, and energy consumption. The same rule requires provision for feeder metering facilities.

For monitoring and control, feeder circuits should, where possible, group loads by function. The checked guideline lists lighting load, chillers, air handling units or unitary air-conditioning systems, and other motor loads such as exhaust fans and pumps. In multiple-tenant buildings, each tenant unit has a provision for measuring the tenant's energy consumption. The guideline also requires adequate working space in front of electrical panels and meters so qualified personnel can meter safely and quickly.

## RA 11285 Compliance Layer

RA 11285 classifies designated establishments by annual energy consumption. Type 1 designated establishments are those with annual energy consumption of 500,000 kWh to 4,000,000 kWh for the previous year. Type 2 designated establishments consume more than 4,000,000 kWh for the previous year. The DOE may periodically review and adjust these thresholds.

Designated establishments must integrate an energy-management-system policy, set annual targets, keep monthly energy consumption records and related energy data, submit annual Energy Consumption and Conservation Reports, conduct an energy audit once every three years, and employ the required energy professional: a CECO for Type 1 and a CEM for Type 2.

## What To Avoid

Do not answer local LEA questions with live tariff rates, brand-specific AMI protocols, or net-metering credit values unless the question itself supplies those facts. The source-safe answer is that metering supports voltage, current, power factor, maximum demand, energy consumption, feeder analysis, tenant accountability, and RA 11285 reporting.

## How It Is Tested

Expect questions that ask which quantities a 250 kVA-and-above building must meter, which loads should be separately visible for energy auditing, what a multi-tenant building must measure, or what RA 11285 requires from Type 1 and Type 2 designated establishments. If the option says "install analytics only" but omits actual metering, records, and reporting, it is incomplete.

## Checked Local Sources

- DOE Guidelines on Energy Conserving Design on Buildings, Section 4.5 Metering for Energy Auditing.
- RA 11285, Energy Efficiency and Conservation Act, provisions on designated establishments, records, ECCR, energy audits, CECO, and CEM.
- Philippine Electrical Code, plan and service-equipment documentation requirements.
