# History of Architecture — Mastery & Connections

## Learning Goal

Achieve synthesis-level understanding of architectural history as an interconnected web of influences across periods and cultures. Trace the river of influence from Ancient through Medieval, Renaissance, Baroque, Neoclassicism, Modernism, and Contemporary. Connect cross-cultural threads (Islamic → Gothic, Japanese → Wright, Spanish Colonial → Philippine earthquake Baroque). Integrate History with Theory (movement-to-manifesto relationships), Philippine Architecture, and Architectural Design.

## How Periods Connect: The River of Influence

**Ancient → Medieval:** Roman concrete, arch, vault, dome → Early Christian basilicas and Byzantine churches. The Pantheon's dome directly informed Hagia Sophia's pendentive-supported dome.

**Medieval → Renaissance:** Conscious *return* to Roman and Greek precedents. Brunelleschi's dome solved a Gothic structural problem using Roman construction logic.

**Renaissance → Baroque:** Same classical vocabulary (columns, pediments, domes) curved, undulated, and theatricalized. Bernini's Piazza San Pietro.

**Baroque → Neoclassicism:** Return to Greek and Roman forms with archaeological rigor, informed by Pompeii excavations.

**Historicism → Modern Movement:** Rejection of 19th-century eclecticism. Ornament is dishonest; form follows function; new materials demand new forms.

## Cross-Cultural Influences

**Islamic → Gothic:** The pointed arch appears in Islamic architecture centuries before Gothic cathedrals. Trade routes through Sicily and Spain transmitted building ideas.

**Japanese → Frank Lloyd Wright:** Wright studied Japanese architecture (1893 Ho-o-den Pavilion). Horizontal emphasis, boundary dissolution between interior/exterior, natural materials → core to Prairie Style.

**Indian → Southeast Asian:** Angkor Wat and Borobudur express Hindu/Buddhist cosmological ideas from India. The shikhara form finds echoes in Angkor's towers.

**Spanish Colonial → Philippine Architecture:** Renaissance and Baroque traditions + Filipino labor + local materials + climate/seismic adaptation = uniquely Philippine synthesis. Earthquake Baroque (massive walls, low towers, thick buttresses) is a direct response to Philippine seismic reality.

## Connections to Theory of Architecture

Every major modern movement has a theoretical underpinning published before the buildings:

- Arts & Crafts → Ruskin/Morris: honest materials, craft over industry
- Art Nouveau → organic form, rejection of historical styles
- Bauhaus → integration of art, craft, industry; form follows function
- International Style → Le Corbusier's Five Points, Mies's "less is more"
- Brutalism → béton brut, truth to materials
- Postmodernism → Venturi's Complexity and Contradiction
- Deconstructivism → Derrida's philosophy applied to form

## Connection to Philippine Architecture

**Pre-colonial → Spanish colonial → American period → Post-independence → Contemporary.** Each transition added a new layer without erasing what came before. Bahay kubo persisted through colonization. Bahay na bato synthesized Spanish masonry with Malay spatial logic.

## Integration Patterns

**History + Theory:** "Which theoretical framework best describes this building?" Knowing the movement gives you the theory.

**History + Philippine Architecture:** "Which colonial period produced this building type?"

**History + Architectural Design:** "Which historical precedent influenced this contemporary design?"

**History + Laws:** Heritage conservation (RA 10066) requires knowing what makes a building historically significant.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Mixing up Romanesque vs. Gothic.** Romanesque: round arches, thick walls, small windows. Gothic: pointed arches, flying buttresses, height and light.
- **Confusing architects with movements.** Know at least three works per major architect.
- **Treating Philippine architecture as isolated.** It's a story of layered influences from multiple cultures.
- **Neglecting non-Western chronology.** Map non-Western timelines onto Western eras.
