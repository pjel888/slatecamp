## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Fajardo's concrete estimating method begins with volume. For slabs and pavements, the controlling relationship is volume equals thickness times width times length, with dimensions converted to compatible units before multiplication. The worked examples use meters and cubic meters, then multiply the resulting volume by the selected mixture table.

## Core Concept

A slab estimate is not controlled by floor area alone unless the area method table is being used. In the volume method, first convert thickness to meters, multiply by the plan area or width and length, and obtain cubic meters of concrete. Fajardo then refers the estimator to the appropriate class of mixture and cement-bag column before multiplying for cement, sand, and gravel.

For example, Fajardo's residential floor example converts 4 inches to about 0.10 m, multiplies 200 sq m by 0.10 m, and obtains 20 cu m. The table values then produce the cement, sand, and gravel quantities for the selected mixture. The lesson is procedural: compute the volume first, then apply the table matching the specified mixture and cement-bag size.

## How It's Tested

LEA-style items often hide the unit conversion. If the slab thickness is in inches, convert it before multiplying. If the prompt gives width, length, and thickness, use volume. If the prompt gives an area-method table, multiply area by the table value for the slab thickness and mixture.

## Common Mistakes

Do not multiply a floor area directly by cement bags unless the table is explicitly per square meter. Do not use a 40 kg cement-bag table when the question asks for 50 kg bags. Do not skip the thickness conversion before computing cubic meters.
