# Certificate of Occupancy

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to explain the PD 1096 Certificate of Occupancy workflow: who supervises construction, what completion documents are required, how as-built plans are treated, how BFP inspection fits into final occupancy review, when the Building Official issues the certificate, when partial occupancy may be allowed, and when another certificate may be needed after a change in use.

## Controlling Source

This article is source-audited against the DPWH official PDF of the 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096. The main controlling text is Rule III Sections 308 and 309. The checked raw conversion also contains Rule II penalty language on use without a Certificate of Occupancy/Use and failure to post or display a certificate, but those penalty lines are separate from the Section 309 application workflow.

## Construction Supervision Comes Before Occupancy

Section 308 requires the owner who has been issued a building permit to engage a duly licensed architect or civil engineer to undertake full-time inspection and supervision of the construction work.

The supervising professional may be the same professional responsible for the building design, or a different duly licensed architect or civil engineer. The designing architect or civil engineer is also not precluded from conducting inspection to check compliance with the approved plans and specifications.

## The Construction Logbook

Section 308 requires a construction logbook to be kept at the jobsite at all times. The logbook records actual construction progress, tests conducted, weather conditions, and other pertinent data.

On completion, the licensed architect or civil engineer submits the signed and sealed logbook to the Building Official. The same professional also prepares and submits the Certificate of Completion stating that construction conforms to the Code and the approved plans and specifications.

## Certificate of Occupancy Application Package

Section 309 requires the owner or permittee to submit the Certificate of Occupancy application to the OBO with the required completion documents.

The core package includes:

- Application for Certificate of Occupancy.
- Duly notarized Certificate of Completion.
- Construction logbook.
- As-built plans and specifications.
- Building Inspection Sheet.

If construction was undertaken by contract, the documents must be signed by the contractor. They must also be signed and sealed by the owner's duly licensed architect or civil engineer who undertook full-time inspection and supervision.

## As-Built Plans and Specifications

Section 309 requires the as-built plans and specifications to faithfully reflect changes, modifications, and alterations made from the originally submitted plans and specifications on file with the OBO.

The as-built package may be either:

- An orderly and comprehensive compilation of the originally submitted plans and specifications plus all amendments as actually built; or
- An entirely new set of plans and specifications accurately describing the building or structure as actually built.

The exam trap is the word "as-built." It is not a design intent package. It must reflect what was actually constructed.

## BFP Final Inspection and FSIC Step

Under Section 309, the OBO endorses a notification to conduct final inspection to the city or municipal fire marshal of the BFP. The BFP issues a Fire Safety Inspection Certificate within five working days if the fire safety requirements have been complied with.

If the BFP takes no action after the prescribed period, the Building Official may issue the Certificate of Occupancy with the condition that fire safety requirements will be complied with within the prescribed period set by the Fire Code.

## Final OBO Review and Issuance

The OBO undertakes final inspection, verification, and review based on the Certificate of Completion, construction logbook, Building Inspection Sheet, and original and as-built plans and specifications.

Before issuance, the OBO prepares the corresponding fees and order of payment. The Building Official issues the Certificate of Occupancy after all fees are paid.

Do not add a general filing deadline unless the problem gives another source for it. The checked Section 309 text controls required documents, inspection/review, fees, and issuance, not a general 30-day application period.

## Partial Certificate of Occupancy

Section 309 allows a partial Certificate of Occupancy for the use or occupancy of a portion or portions of a building or structure before completion of the entire building or structure.

The condition is safety. The major independent portions must be properly phased and must not pose hazards to occupants, adjacent building residents, or the general public.

## Another Certificate After Change in Use

A building that already has a Certificate of Occupancy may be issued other Certificates of Occupancy due to changes in use, whether partly or wholly, if the new use or character of occupancy conforms to the Code and its IRR.

For exam purposes, a Certificate of Occupancy is tied to the approved use or occupancy. A later use change can require another certificate and cannot be justified by the old certificate alone.

## Posting and Enforcement Context

The checked source text outside Section 309 treats failure to post or display the certificate in a conspicuous place as an enforceable violation. The same penalty area also treats use or occupancy without the appropriate certificate as a violation.

Keep the distinction clear: Section 309 tells you how the certificate is obtained; the penalty provisions tell you what happens when the certificate is missing or not properly displayed.

## LEA Recall Anchors

- Section 308: full-time inspection and supervision by a duly licensed architect or civil engineer.
- Jobsite logbook: kept at the jobsite and submitted signed and sealed on completion.
- Certificate of Completion: prepared and submitted by the supervising licensed architect or civil engineer.
- Section 309 package: application, notarized Certificate of Completion, construction logbook, as-built plans/specifications, and Building Inspection Sheet.
- BFP final inspection/FSIC: five working days if fire safety requirements are complied with.
- Building Official issues the Certificate of Occupancy after all fees are paid.
- Partial occupancy is possible only when phased portions can be occupied without hazard.
- Change in use may require another Certificate of Occupancy.
