# Fire Zones and Existing Buildings

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to apply PD 1096 Rule V to buildings located in fire zones, especially buildings that straddle more than one zone, moved or prefabricated buildings, temporary structures, and existing buildings that do not comply with the fire-zone requirements for new construction.

## Controlling Source

This article is source-audited against the DPWH official PDF of the 2004 Revised IRR of PD 1096, Rule V Sections 501 through 507.

## What a Fire Zone Controls

Section 501 defines fire zones as areas where only certain types of buildings or structures may be constructed. The allowed construction depends on use or occupancy, type of construction, and resistance to fire.

For LEA work, the fire-zone question is not just a map question. It is a construction-permission question: the zone determines what type of building is allowed, what fire-resistive construction is expected, and whether an existing nonconforming building may be altered, repaired, moved, or expanded.

## Buildings in More Than One Fire Zone

Section 502 supplies the threshold rule. A building or structure located partly in one fire zone and partly in another is treated as being in the more highly restrictive fire zone when more than one-third of its total floor area is located in that more restrictive zone.

The words "more than one-third" matter. Exactly one-third is not more than one-third. If a 1,200 sq. m building has exactly 400 sq. m in a highly restrictive zone, the threshold has not been exceeded on that fact alone.

## Moved and Prefabricated Buildings

Section 503 requires any building or structure moved within or into any fire zone to comply with all requirements for buildings or structures in that fire zone. The same rule applies to pre-engineered or prefabricated buildings that are dismantled and reassembled.

Do not treat a moved building as grandfathered merely because it complied at its former site. The new or receiving fire zone controls after relocation.

## Temporary Buildings

Section 504 allows temporary buildings such as reviewing stands, miscellaneous structures, sheds, canopies, and fences used for public protection around construction work, but only by special permit from the Building Official and only for the limited period stated in the permit.

Temporary buildings or structures in restrictive or highly restrictive zones that do not conform with the allowed construction type may still be allowed by the Building Official for a given period if adequate fire protective, preventive, and suppression measures are provided.

The exam distinction is important: temporary approval is time-limited and conditional. It is not a permanent exemption from the fire-zone rules.

## Center Lines of Streets

Section 505 states that the center line of an adjoining street or alley may be considered an adjacent property line. Distances must be measured at right angles to the street or alley.

This rule matters when fire-zone or property-line calculations use street or alley adjacency. Use the stated measurement basis instead of assuming the lot edge alone controls.

## Existing Buildings That Do Not Comply

Section 506 is the high-frequency rule for existing buildings in fire zones. Existing buildings or structures in fire zones that do not comply with the requirements for a new building in that zone may not be enlarged, altered, remodeled, repaired, or moved except under the listed conditions.

Allowed paths include:

- Entire demolition.
- Moving the building outside the more highly restrictive fire zone to a zone where it meets minimum standards.
- Changes, alterations, or repairs within any 12-month period where the value of the work does not exceed 20% of the value of the existing building, provided the work does not add combustible material and does not increase fire hazard in the opinion of the Building Official.
- Additions separated from the existing building by firewalls as required by the rule.
- Repair after fire, earthquake, typhoon, or other fortuitous event using the same kind of materials, provided the repair cost does not exceed 20% of replacement cost.

The two 20% limits are similar but not identical. One is tied to value of work within a 12-month period for changes, alterations, and repairs. The other is tied to replacement cost after damage by fire, earthquake, typhoon, or other fortuitous event.

## Designating Fire Zones

Section 507 authorizes the LGU legislative body to enact ordinances designating fire zones based on the parameters and guidelines in the section. The designation is for management, prevention, control, and suppression of conflagration in population centers.

## Zone Categories

| Fire zone category | Source-backed description |
| :-- | :-- |
| Non-Fire Restricted Zone | Areas where buildings or structures may be sited without fire-resistivity measures, often countryside or rural areas with sparse or small grouped construction; indigenous materials and Types I and II construction are referenced. |
| Fire Restrictive Zone | Areas where siting is permitted within prescribed fire-resistivity measures, including exterior walls of at least two-hour fire resistivity; Types II, III, and IV construction are referenced. |
| Highly Fire Restrictive Zone | Areas where highly fire-resistive or non-combustible assemblies of no less than three-to-four-hour fire-resistive construction are used throughout, including exterior walls; only Types IV and V construction are permitted. |

## Real World Scenario

An existing warehouse sits across a boundary between a fire restrictive zone and a highly fire restrictive zone. If more than one-third of its total floor area lies in the more restrictive zone, the whole building is treated under that more restrictive classification. If it is a moved, pre-engineered, or reassembled prefabricated structure, it must comply with the receiving fire zone.

The owner wants a quick renovation and small extension. For an existing nonconforming building, Section 506 blocks ordinary enlargement or repair unless one of the listed paths applies. A repair or alteration within a 12-month period must stay within the 20 percent value limit, must not add combustible material, and must not increase the fire hazard in the Building Official's opinion. An addition needs separation from the existing building by firewalls, while damage repair after a fortuitous event has its own 20 percent replacement-cost limit.

## LEA Recall Anchors

- More than one-third of total floor area in the more restrictive zone triggers the more restrictive classification for the whole building.
- Moved, pre-engineered, and prefabricated buildings must comply with the receiving fire zone.
- Temporary buildings require a special permit and are time-limited.
- Existing nonconforming buildings in fire zones are tightly restricted.
- The 20% limits in Section 506 are common exam traps.
- LGU legislative bodies designate fire zones by ordinance under Section 507.
