## REAL-WORLD HOOK

You are a wheelchair user arriving at a mall's cinema restroom. The door looks wide enough — but you press the lever handle and it swings *toward* you. There is no kick plate, the threshold catches your front caster wheel, and the vision panel is a narrow slit that no one can see through. You cannot tell if someone is on the other side, and you cannot push against it with your chair. Every measurement in this module exists because that experience is a failure of architecture.

## SPATIAL SKETCH — HOW TO MEASURE 900 mm CLEAR WIDTH

```
   JAMB                              JAMB
     |                                |
     |<---------- 900 mm ----------->|
     |   (measured face-to-face       |
     |    with door leaf at 90°)      |
     |                                |
 ====[DOOR LEAF]========================
                    ↑
         Door leaf swings to 90°;
         measure the FREE PASSAGE,
         not the nominal door size.
```

> **Why this matters on exams:** A 900 mm door leaf in a standard 100 mm jamb does NOT give 900 mm clear. The leaf itself takes up ~35–45 mm. Always measure the clear passage, not the door leaf dimension.

---

## > TRAP LEART #1 — The "900 mm Nominal" Trick

**Question stem:** *"A single-leaf door has a 900 mm leaf width. Is this compliant with BP 344?"*

**Wrong answer:** Yes — 900 mm is the minimum.

**Correct answer:** **It depends.** The 900 mm requirement is for **clear width**, measured face-of-door-at-90°-to-jamb. A 900 mm leaf in a standard frame typically yields only ~855–870 mm clear. A **950–965 mm leaf** is usually needed to achieve 900 mm clear passage.

---

## > TRAP LEART #2 — Single vs. Double Leaf

**Question stem:** *"A pair of fire exit doors has two 900 mm leaves. What is the minimum required clear width under BP 344?"*

**Wrong answer:** 900 mm (one leaf is enough).

**Correct answer:** Double-leaf doors require **1,800 mm** combined clear width (App. A). Both leaves are measured together. Each leaf must contribute.

---

## > TRAP LEART #3 — Threshold Height Confusion

**Question stem:** *"A threshold is 8 mm high with a beveled profile. Is this compliant?"*

**Correct answer:** **Yes.** Beveling is required for thresholds **between 6 mm and 12 mm**. At 8 mm, the threshold must be beveled — and it is. However, a flat (unbeveled) 8 mm threshold would be non-compliant.

**The rule:** ≤6 mm → flush acceptable. 6–12 mm → **must bevel**. >12 mm → **not permitted**.

---

## > TRAP LEART #4 — Vision Panel vs. Sidelight

The **100 mm** minimum refers to the **vision panel width** (the glazed window in the door leaf). Do not confuse with a full-height sidelight panel beside the door. They serve different purposes. The vision panel lets approaching persons see each other before the door swings open.

---

## > TRAP LEART #5 — Dressing Room Door Swing

**Question stem:** *"Which direction must accessible dressing room doors swing?"*

**Correct answer:** **Outward.** This is a safety requirement, not just an accessibility one. If a person with a disability collapses inside, the outward swing allows rescuers to open the door without the body blocking it.

## REASON FOR THE LAW

| Rule | Why It Exists |
|------|--------------|
| **900 mm clear width** | Minimum for a manual wheelchair (typical width ~680 mm) plus 110 mm each side for hand clearance during propulsion |
| **Lever handles at 900 mm** | Operable with a closed fist, elbow, or forearm — usable by persons with no hand grip. Height matches seated reach envelope (850–1,000 mm AFF) |
| **Kick plate 300–400 mm** | Protects door from footrests and wheelchair footplates. Without it, doors degrade quickly in high-traffic accessible routes |
| **Threshold max 12 mm / bevel if >6 mm** | Front caster wheels of wheelchairs (typically 75–150 mm diameter) can catch on abrupt transitions >6 mm, causing tipping |
| **Vision panel min 100 mm** | A person in a wheelchair sits lower (~1.1–1.3 m eye level). A narrow panel must still be visible from seated height |
| **Frameless glass markings 900–1,500 mm AFF** | Persons with low vision may not see clear glass. The marking band crosses the seated *and* standing field of view |
| **Dressing rooms open outward** | Emergency egress principle: if user falls against door, door must still be openable. Also prevents door from striking a person transferring to/from a wheelchair |
| **Sensory room: no threshold, easy to open** | Persons with autism, anxiety, or sensory processing differences may panic at barriers. Easy egress = safety + dignity |
| **Automatic doors at transport facilities** | Heavy commuter bags, strollers, crutches, and high passenger volume make manual doors a bottleneck and hazard |

## DOOR HARDWARE DECISION TREE

```
Is the door on a major circulation route?
├── YES → Kick plate required (300–400 mm)
│         Handle: lever or vertical pull at 900 mm
│         Vision panel: min 100 mm wide
│         Threshold: max 12 mm, bevel if 6–12 mm
│         Consider: automatic door (transport fac.)
│
└── NO  → Still needs 900 mm clear, lever handle,
          threshold rules still apply
          Kick plate: recommended but check context

Is it a glass door (frameless, full-height)?
└── YES → High-contrast marking band: 900–1,500 mm AFF

Is it a dressing room door?
└── YES → Must swing OUTWARD

Is it a sensory room door?
└── YES → No threshold; easy-to-open hardware

Is it a lodging/guest room?
└── YES → Lever handle required (Sec. 17.1)
          Secondary peephole at 1,000 mm AFF
```

## LOBBY DOORS vs. FIRE EXIT DOORS — COMPARISON TABLE

| Feature | Lobby / Interior Door | Fire Exit Door |
|--------|----------------------|---------------|
| Min clear width | 900 mm | 900 mm (same) |
| Hardware | Lever handle preferred | Panic bar (push-to-open) preferred |
| Closing speed | Not specified for accessibility alone | Must allow safe egress |
| Automatic option | Preferred at transport facilities | Must also be manual-operable during power failure |
| Kick plate | 300–400 mm on major routes | Strongly recommended |
| Vision panel | 100 mm min width | 100 mm min width (BP 344) |
| Opening force | As low as possible; no specific value in BP 344 for standard doors | Governed by fire code — but accessibility requires low force |
| Threshold | Max 12 mm, bevel if >6 mm | Same; fire-rated thresholds must still comply |

## ACTIVE RECALL DRILL

**Q1.** What is the minimum clear width for a single-leaf accessible door under BP 344 2024 IRR?
> **A1.** 900 mm, measured face of door at 90° to jamb.

**Q2.** At what height is the standard handle/pull/latch required to be centered?
> **A2.** 900 mm above finished floor (AFF).

**Q3.** A threshold is 10 mm high. What must be done to make it compliant?
> **A3.** It must be beveled. (Beveling is required for thresholds between 6 mm and 12 mm.)

**Q4.** What is the minimum width of a vision panel in a door leaf?
> **A4.** 100 mm.

**Q5.** At what heights must markings appear on a frameless full-height glass door?
> **A5.** Between 900 mm and 1,500 mm AFF (a marking band spanning this zone).

**Q6.** Which direction must accessible dressing room doors swing, and why?
> **A6.** Outward — so that if a person collapses inside, the door can still be opened by rescuers.

**Q7.** What is the maximum threshold height allowed under BP 344?
> **A7.** 12 mm (and must be beveled if between 6 mm and 12 mm).

**Q8.** Where are kick plates required, and what is the height range?
> **A8.** On major circulation route doors; kick plate height: 300–400 mm.

**Q9.** What are the dimensions for a double-leaf accessible door?
> **A9.** Minimum 1,800 mm combined clear width.

**Q10.** At what height is the secondary peephole installed for wheelchair users?
> **A10.** 1,000 mm AFF (in addition to the standard-height peephole).
