# Passive Design in Tropical Climates

Passive design works with the environment to reduce unwanted heat and use sun and breezes for comfort. In a Philippine hot-humid setting, the checked TOA 4 source frames the main target as maximum wind exposure, maximum internal airflow, and minimum heat gain.

## Core Moves

- Avoid heat gain before trying to cool the heat away.
- Encourage natural ventilation by orienting the building and windows toward prevailing winds.
- Use operable windows and ceiling vents so air can move through the building.
- Use shaded windows for daylight instead of exposing glass directly to sun.
- Create cooler outdoor areas that can temper incoming air and make adjacent spaces more usable.

## Passive Cooling Versus Active Cooling

Passive cooling depends on building form, envelope, openings, shade, and air paths. Active cooling depends on equipment that consumes energy. The exam distinction matters: passive strategies are design decisions built into the building, while active systems are mechanical interventions.

Passive systems can cost more at the envelope stage because the envelope does more work. The tradeoff is lower operating cost and lower environmental impact when the passive strategy is well matched to the climate.

## Common Trap

Do not answer a hot-humid passive design item with heat retention, maximum heat gain, or wind resistance. Those are not the hot-humid priorities in the checked TOA 4 source.
