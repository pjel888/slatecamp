Passive tropical design is a system for reducing heat gain and moving air. The checked source set supports shading, orientation to prevailing winds, cross ventilation, low and high openings, roof heat control, protected openings, and shaded outdoor air paths, with mechanical cooling reserved for spaces needing tighter control.

## Board Exam Quick Check
For hot-humid passive design, reduce solar heat gain, shade openings, align airflow, provide exhaust paths, and keep rain protection compatible with ventilation.

## Core Concept
Passive design in the tropics works by combining climate moves. Shading without ventilation can still feel hot, ventilation without rain protection can fail in storms, and roof design without heat control can overload occupied rooms.

## Real World Scenario
A Cebu clinic has air-conditioned rooms but loses comfort in corridors and waiting areas. A passive-first revision can shade walls and openings, align windward and leeward openings, add high exhaust paths, protect incoming air with planting and shade, and reserve mechanical cooling for rooms that need tighter control.

## Key Facts
- Hot-humid passive design prioritizes minimum heat gain and maximum useful air movement.
- Natural ventilation depends on wind exposure, aligned openings, and limited internal obstructions.
- Low and high openings can support air entry and warm-air release.
- Openings should be shaded and protected so ventilation does not create rain or glare problems.
- Vernacular references such as bahay kubo and bahay na bato are useful for principles, not literal copying.

## Common Mistakes
- Exact overhang dimensions, unsupported Venturi-device claims, and unsourced numerical comfort bands were removed.
- Passive design is framed as a climate response system rather than a single device or decorative tropical style.
