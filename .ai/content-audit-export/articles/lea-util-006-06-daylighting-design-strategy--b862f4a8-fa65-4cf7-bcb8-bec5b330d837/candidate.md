## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Daylighting design is an integrated lighting and envelope problem. DOE requires the lighting system to coordinate daylight with artificial lighting while considering glare, brightness imbalance, and heat buildup in the building interior. The best LEA answer does not treat daylight as free light with no thermal or visual tradeoff.

The envelope issue is window-to-wall ratio. DOE notes that daylighting applications often use larger glazing areas, and glazing admits more heat than an opaque wall. However, artificial-lighting energy savings can exceed the added cooling penalty when the envelope is carefully designed for daylight. Transparent envelope portions should prevent solar gains beyond what is needed for effective daylighting.

Controls are required for daylighting to count as an energy strategy. DOE states that automatic daylighting controls should turn off artificial lights when sufficient natural light is available. Daylighting credit is taken for areas with automatic lighting controls for all lights within 4 meters of an exterior wall. If that automatic daylighting control credit is used, the fenestration visible transmittance for the daylighted exterior wall must not be less than 0.25.

Salvan reinforces the design balance. Daylight varies by time and season, does not penetrate deeply into many spaces, and often needs artificial light to boost darker inner areas. Rooms near windows receive much more available daylight than areas farther from the window, so deep rooms and central service spaces often still need electric lighting. Good design reduces glare and balances daylight with reflected and artificial light rather than placing bright sources next to difficult visual tasks.

For the Philippines, the practical sequence is: control solar heat gain, preserve useful daylight, add automatic or local controls, coordinate artificial lighting rows with daylighted zones, and confirm that visual comfort is maintained. Daylighting is successful when it reduces electric lighting use without creating glare, excessive contrast, or avoidable cooling load.

## LEA Memory Anchors

- Daylighting must be coordinated with artificial lighting.
- Main risks are glare, brightness imbalance, and heat buildup.
- Larger glazing can raise cooling load, so envelope control matters.
- DOE daylighting credit depends on automatic controls within 4 meters of the exterior wall.
- Visible transmittance must be at least 0.25 when the automatic daylighting control credit is used.
