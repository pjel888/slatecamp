## 2. Escalators (BP 344 IRR – Escalator Section)

Key parameters from BP 344 IRR and recent reviewer notes:[web:51][web:53][web:54][web:56]

- **Minimum tread width:**
  - Escalator treads should be at least **S800 type**, which has a nominal tread width of about **804 mm**.[web:51][web:53]
- **Maximum inclination:**
  - **30°** angle of inclination to the horizontal.[web:51][web:53]
- **Horizontal steps at each end:**
  - Provide at least **3 horizontal steps** at the **top and bottom** to facilitate safe transition onto and off the moving steps.[web:51][web:53]
- **Maximum speed:**
  - **30 m/minute** is cited in BP 344‑based notes as the **maximum escalator speed** compatible with PWD‑friendly use.[web:51][web:53]
- **Handrails and safety features:**
  - Continuous handrails on both sides synchronized with escalator movement, slip‑resistant step nosings, and adequate lighting at entry/exit zones are required.[web:51][web:53][web:56]
- **Voice/announcement systems:**
  - Updated reviewer materials emphasize **audible announcements** or voice guidance (e.g., “escalator going up/down”) to leart visually impaired users, consistent with BP 344’s emphasis on audio cues, although exact wording/technology may be detailed more in transport‑sector standards than in the core IRR text.[web:51][web:53][web:54]

### 2.1 Escalator Tactile Requirements

BP 344 tactile provisions for escalators:[web:49][web:51][web:54][web:56]

- **Warning tactile blocks** shall be installed on the **terminating floor finish preceding the escalator’s metal landing plate**.[web:49][web:54]
- **Depth:** warning tactile blocks with a depth of **600 mm**.[web:49][web:51][web:54]
- **Placement / setback:** placed **300 mm before** the hazard/landing plate so that users encounter the tactile cue before reaching the moving step zone.[web:49][web:51][web:54]
- **Width:** the tactile strip spans the **entire width of the escalator**, so a visually impaired user cannot bypass it.[web:49][web:54]

Mnemonic: **“Escalator tactile – 600 deep, 300 back, full width.”**[web:49][web:51][web:54]

## 3. Passenger Conveyors / Walkalators

From BP 344 IRR provisions on passenger conveyors/moving walks:[web:49][web:51][web:54][web:56]

- Passenger conveyors or **walkalators** are essentially **horizontal or low‑slope moving platforms**, commonly used in airports, large malls, and transfer corridors.[web:51][web:54]
- Key accessibility requirements mirror escalators and ramps:[web:49][web:51][web:54]
  - Provide sufficient **clear width** for a wheelchair user plus attendant or passing pedestrians.
  - Use **slip‑resistant surfaces** on the moving belt and adjacent floor finishes.
  - Install **handrails on both sides** at accessible heights, continuous along the moving section.
  - Ensure **approach areas** are level and free of obstacles so wheelchair users and persons with mobility devices can enter/exit safely.
- **Tactile warning at entry/exit:**
  - Warning tactile blocks with a depth of **600 mm** should be installed **300 mm before** the start and end of passenger conveyors, across their full clear width, following the same 600/300 rule used for escalators and ramps.[web:49][web:51][web:54]

Think of passenger conveyors as **“flat escalators”** for tactile and safety logic: same 600‑mm warning strip, 300‑mm setback, full‑width coverage.[web:49][web:51][web:54]

## 4. Transit Platforms and Platform–Vehicle Interfaces

From BP 344 IRR and the 2013–2024 updates on transport facilities:[web:1][web:2][web:10][web:49][web:51][web:54]

- **Tactile floor surfaces on platforms:**
  - Transit platforms (rail, BRT, LRT/MRT, etc.), including those with platform screen doors, must incorporate **tactile floor surfaces** to guide visually impaired users from entrances and circulation nodes to **designated boarding points**.[web:10][web:49][web:54]
- **Directional tactile guides:**
  - **Directional tactile blocks** (longitudinal bars) form continuous routes linking:[web:49][web:51][web:54]
    - Drop‑offs/entrances and sidewalks
    - Security screening areas
    - Ticketing and information counters
    - Ticket gates/turnstiles
    - Stairs, escalators, and elevators
    - Platform waiting zones and accessible boarding doors
- **Warning tactile at hazard edges:**
  - **Warning tactile blocks** (domes) with a depth of **600 mm** should be installed **parallel to and 300 mm away from**:[web:49][web:51][web:54]
    - Platform edges (with or without platform screen doors)
    - Stair heads and foots
    - Escalator entry points
    - Other hazards/equipment where falls or collisions may occur
- **Platform edge to transit vehicle:**
  - The BP 344 IRR explicitly states that **boarding platforms shall be leveled with the finished floor of the transit vehicles, avoiding noticeable horizontal and vertical gaps**, to the greatest extent practicable.[web:10]
  - Any unavoidable gap should be minimized and, where necessary, mitigated using **ramps, gap fillers, or trained staff assistance**, coordinated with transport operator standards.[web:10][web:49]

Even when exact **platform widths** are governed more by PD 1096 and transport regulations, the **tactile geometry (600 mm depth, 300 mm setback)** and **level boarding goal** are exam‑relevant BP 344 points.[web:10][web:49][web:51][web:54]

## 5. Integrated Tactile Flow at a Station (Mental Sketch)

A typical accessible station flow, combining PD 1096 egress logic with BP 344 tactile and vertical‑transport rules:[web:1][web:2][web:10][web:49][web:51][web:54][web:56]

```text
DROP-OFF → SIDEWALK → SECURITY → TICKETS → GATES →
ESCALATOR / LIFT → PLATFORM EDGE / BOARDING POINT
```

- **Directional tactile bars** run continuously along the **main accessible path**, linking the drop‑off/entrance, key counters, gates, and vertical circulation (escalators, ramps, elevators) to the platform.[web:49][web:51][web:54]
- **Warning domes** (600‑mm deep tactile fields, 300‑mm before hazard):[web:49][web:51][web:54]
  - Before stairs and ramps (top and bottom).
  - Before escalators and passenger conveyors.

## 6. Active Recall – Transport Interfaces

1. Minimum number of horizontal steps at each end of an escalator? → **3 horizontal steps** at both top and bottom.[web:51][web:53]
2. Escalator (and conveyor) warning tactile: depth and setback? → **600‑mm depth**, installed **300 mm before** the landing plate or hazard edge, spanning full width.[web:49][web:51][web:54]
3. Maximum escalator speed cited for PWD‑friendly design? → **30 m/minute**.[web:51][web:53]
4. Where must tactile paths in stations lead? → Entrances/drop‑offs; security; ticketing/ information counters; ticket gates; stairs; escalators; elevators; platforms and boarding points.[web:49][web:51][web:54]
5. What is the BP 344 goal for platform edge to transit vehicle? → Boarding platforms **leveled with the vehicle floor**, with minimal horizontal/vertical gaps.[web:10]

## Common Exam Traps

**TRAP 1 — Confusing escalator tactile with stair tactile:**
Both use **600 mm depth**, **300 mm setback**, **full width** warning tactile. The difference: escalator tactile goes on the **terminating floor finish preceding the metal landing plate** (not on the moving step), while stair tactile goes at the **top, bottom, and intermediate landings**.

**TRAP 2 — Omitting walkalator tactile:**
Passenger conveyors (walkalators) require the **same 600/300/full-width tactile** as escalators. They are not exempt from tactile requirements just because they are low-slope.

**TRAP 3 — Platform edge gaps:**
BP 344 requires boarding platforms to be **leveled with the transit vehicle floor** with minimal gaps. Any unavoidable gap must be mitigated by ramps, gap fillers, or trained staff assistance. Don't accept large gaps as permissible.

**TRAP 4 — Tactile path termination at elevator:**
The tactile path must **prioritize elevator access** and terminate at a **warning tactile strip 300 mm in front of the elevator door** with Braille/tactile controls and jambs. Simply stopping the tactile path at the corridor wall is non-compliant.
