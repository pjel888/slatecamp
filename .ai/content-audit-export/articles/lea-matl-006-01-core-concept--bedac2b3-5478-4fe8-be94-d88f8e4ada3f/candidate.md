## What You Need to Know for the LEA
## Learning Goal

Classify roofing materials (corrugated GI sheets, clay tiles, concrete tiles, asphalt shingles, standing seam metal, green roofs) by slope requirements, weather resistance, fire rating, and service life for Philippine climate conditions.
## Core Concept

Roofing materials serve three critical functions: weather protection, structural support, and thermal regulation. In the Philippines, galvanized corrugated iron (GI) sheets remain the most common residential choice due to cost and availability. These sheets are manufactured per ASTM A653 and come in standard gauges: Gauge 26 = 0.46 mm (0.0181 inches), Gauge 24 = 0.61 mm (0.0239 inches), Gauge 22 = 0.76 mm (0.0299 inches). Gauge 26 (0.46 mm) is the common minimum for residential applications per industry practice, though Gauge 24 (0.61 mm) is increasingly standard. These measurements include the zinc coating per ASTM A653 specification. GI coating weight is specified by G-rating (G90 = 0.90 oz/ft² or Z275 metric equivalent = 275 g/m²), which directly affects corrosion resistance in tropical salt-air environments.

Long-span metal roofing (trapezoidal or corrugated profiles spanning 6-12 meters) reduces support framing costs but requires precise fastening and expansion control in temperature-sensitive applications. Clay and concrete roof tiles provide excellent thermal mass and 50+ year durability but demand stronger roof framing due to weight (approximately 5-6 kN/m²). Asphalt shingles are lightweight and affordable but suffer accelerated degradation in tropical UV and humidity. Built-up roofing (BUR) and single-ply membranes (TPO, PVC, EPDM) serve low-slope or flat roofs and require proper slope (minimum 1/4:12 or 2%) for drainage. Metal standing seam systems offer superior panel-to-panel integration and accommodate building movement through hidden fastening.

Selection criteria include: structural capacity of frame, roof slope, expected service life, maintenance accessibility, thermal properties (k-value for insulation bonded to sheets), and fire rating per NFPA 101.

## Philippine Construction Context

Philippine construction heavily relies on GI corrugated roofing in residential and light commercial sectors due to lower material cost and minimal fabrication complexity. The tropical maritime climate with high salt spray (especially near coastal zones) makes G90 coating essential; G60 (0.60 oz/ft²) results in premature rust. Annual rainfall exceeding 2000 mm in many regions demands fastidious slope design and overlapping sheet length (minimum 150 mm lap for horizontal joints, 200 mm for vertical). Major Philippine brands include DN Steel, SteelAsia (formerly Pag-asa Steel), Union Galvasteel Corporation, and Steelworld Manufacturing, all of which dominate the market with consistent ASTM compliance. Thermal comfort in un-insulated GI roofs drives adoption of reflective coatings (high emissivity finish) to reduce interior temperature rise.

## How It's Tested

**Pattern 1: Gauge & Thickness Conversion** — "A roof specification calls for Gauge 24 GI corrugated sheets per ASTM A653. What is the approximate thickness in millimeters?" (Answer: 0.61 mm; tests conversion familiarity and standard knowledge.)

**Pattern 2: Material Selection for Climate** — "For a coastal residential project in Batangas exposed to salt spray, which GI coating specification is required?" (Answer: G90 or Z275; tests understanding of corrosion factors in tropical maritime zones.)

**Pattern 3: Slope Requirement** — "A flat roof design must accommodate a 1000 m² area with a minimum slope for water drainage. What is the minimum slope per NSCP 2015?" (Answer: 1/4:12 or 2%; tests code knowledge for low-slope roofs.)

**Pattern 4: Material Comparison** — "Compare the structural implications of upgrading from Gauge 26 to Gauge 24 GI roofing on rafter spacing. What is the stress reduction factor?" (Answer: Approximately 33% increase in bending capacity; tests understanding of material strength relationships.)

<Tip>Memorize gauge-to-thickness pairs: Gauge 26 = 0.46 mm, Gauge 24 = 0.61 mm, Gauge 22 = 0.76 mm. Remember ASTM A653 G90 = Z275 metric equivleant. In tropical zones, default to G90 unless project budget forces G60.</Tip>
