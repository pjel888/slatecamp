## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Structural steel estimating is safest when the unit weight is taken from the table or problem statement. Fajardo warns estimators to verify weight per meter or weight per bar length from the applicable table, because nominal sizes and actual weights can differ.

## Core Concept

When a member weight is given in kg per meter, total weight equals length multiplied by unit weight. For several members, multiply each member group separately and sum the results:

Total steel weight = sum of quantity x length x unit weight

Convert kilograms to metric tons by dividing by 1000. Apply a waste or fabrication allowance only if the item tells you to include one. For price questions, cost equals metric tons multiplied by the quoted price per metric ton.

## How It Is Tested

Expect items that give a small takeoff table: number of members, member length, and kg per meter. The correct answer follows from arithmetic and unit control, not from memorizing every steel shape. If the question gives a price per metric ton, convert kilograms to metric tons before multiplying by price.

## Common Mistakes

Do not use the reinforcing-bar shortcut for structural steel shapes unless the item is explicitly about reinforcing bars. Do not confuse kilograms and metric tons. Do not add fabrication waste unless the problem requests it.
