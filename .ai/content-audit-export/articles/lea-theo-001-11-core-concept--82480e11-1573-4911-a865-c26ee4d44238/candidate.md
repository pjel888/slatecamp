# Space and Form in Architecture

## Source-Audited Scope

Use this topic for Ching-style questions about the relationship between architectural form and architectural space. The checked local sources support form properties, articulation, openings, enclosure, and the idea that form and space must be read together.

## Board Exam Quick Check

If the stem describes the visible object, silhouette, material surface, orientation, or articulation, it is asking about form. If it describes the occupied void, enclosure, movement, scale of experience, or room/plaza volume, it is asking about space.

## Core Concepts

- Form is the visible and material side of architecture: shape, size, color, texture, position, orientation, and visual inertia.
- Space is the bounded or organized void that users occupy, move through, and understand.
- Form and space are interdependent. Form creates and bounds space; space gives form its experiential and contextual meaning.
- Articulation is the way parts are joined or made clear in relation to each other. It can use material, color, texture, pattern, corners, openings, and light.
- Openings can maintain a wall surface, mark an entrance, project outward, recess inward, provide shelter, or create transition.
- Visual scale and human scale are different checks. A building can look large in a composition while an actual doorway or room remains human-scaled.

## Real World Scenario

A library lobby has a heavy stone facade, a glazed entry recess, and a double-height reading hall. Read the stone mass and entry articulation as form. Read the double-height hall, waiting area, and visual connection to reading rooms as space. The strongest design answer explains how the form organizes and clarifies the space.

## LEA Use

Questions often use examples such as domes, plazas, facades, entries, or rotated masses. Do not answer by style name alone. Identify whether the question tests form property, spatial experience, opening/articulation, or scale.

## Active Recall

1. What is form? The visible/mass property of a building or element.
2. What is space? The bounded or organized void experienced by users.
3. What is articulation? The manner of joining or expressing parts clearly in relation to each other.
4. What is the exam trap? Treating space as leftover after form, or form as only decoration.
