## What You Need to Know for the LEA
## Learning Goal

Classify paint and coating systems (latex, oil-based, epoxy, polyurethane, stain, varnish, enamel, textured) by binder type, durability, sheen level, cleanability, and appropriate substrate for interior and exterior architectural applications.
## Core Concept

Paint systems consist of binder (resin), solvent, pigment, and additives. The binder determines performance: latex (acrylic or vinyl acrylic) uses water as solvent and offers low odor and easy cleanup; oil-based enamel uses mineral spirits and offers superior durability on metal; epoxy uses two-part curing for maximum adhesion and chemical resistance; polyurethane offers flexibility and UV resistance; lacquer provides rapid drying and high gloss but requires ventilation; stain penetrates wood fibers without forming a film.

Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs) are regulated in many jurisdictions. Interior latex paints typically contain &lt;300 g/L VOC (low-VOC), a regulatory threshold in many regions. Oil-based enamels range from 300-650 g/L VOC and are restricted in some areas. The Philippines does not yet enforce strict VOC limits uniformly; however, architects and builders increasingly specify low-VOC products for health and indoor air quality (IAQ) benefits. Boysen, Davies, and Island Paints market low-VOC latex and acrylic-enamel hybrid products that balance durability and environmental responsibility.

Dry film thickness (DFT) is critical. Spreading rate (coverage rate) is typically 8-12 m²/L for interior latex, allowing calculation of coats needed. A 2000 m² project at 10 m²/L needs 200L per coat; 2 coats = 400L of paint. Specifying DFT (75-150µm for topcoats) ensures adequate protection; underspray creates color variation and reduced durability.

Surface preparation determines success. Wood stains require sanding to open grain; enamel requires primer on metal; epoxy demands concrete scarification (removal of surface laitance) for mechanical bond. Primer types vary: latex primer on drywall, epoxy primer on metal in wet environments, and shellac-based primer to block stains and odors.

Specialty finishes include anti-slip coatings for ramps (adding aggregate to paint), anti-bacterial finishes (copper-based additives), and intumescent paint (expands when heated to provide fire protection). These are specified for functional performance beyond aesthetic requirements.

## Philippine Construction Context

Boysen, as Pacific Paint (Boysen), dominates the Philippine market with brands including Permacoat (acrylic latex), Acrytex (enamel/acrylic hybrid), and KNOxOUT (titanium dioxide NOx-reducing paint). Davies Paints competes in the premium segment with wide color selection. Island Paints offers cost-competitive options. All three brands are now formulating low-VOC products in response to market demand and to align with future regulatory tightening.

Tropical humidity and salty coastal air drive premium product selection. Oil-based enamel offers superior durability on metal railings and exterior trim in high-corrosion environments, but high VOC and longer recoat times challenge project schedules. Latex alternatives with rust-inhibiting primers (e.g., Boysen's metal primers) are increasingly used in residential projects to reduce schedule impact.

Intumescent paint is typically specified for structural steel fire protection in high-rise buildings (>15m height per PD 1096) where required by fire resistance ratings in RA 9514 2019 IRR Table 602. Specification must include DFT requirement (typically 2-3mm per fire-rating hour) and testing per ASTM E119. Many contractors and architects are unaware of the distinction between aesthetic paint fire rating and structural steel protection, leading to under-specification.

Anti-slip coatings gain importance as BP 344 accessibility enforcement increases. Exterior ramps and interior accessible routes benefit from anti-slip paint (150-250µm DFT with silica or polymer aggregate). Cost premium is 20-30% over standard paint; specifying where required is cost-effective risk management.

## How It's Tested

**Question Type 1 (VOC Compliance):** A project in an occupied high-rise specifies interior paint. What VOC limit ensures air-quality compliance and occupant safety? Answer: &lt;300 g/L per EPA/ASTM guidelines. Oil-based enamel (300-650 g/L) would create indoor air quality issues.

**Question Type 2 (Coverage Rate):** A 5000 m² interior wall area requires 2 coats. Specify paint coverage at 10 m²/L. How many liters are needed? Answer: (5000 m² × 2 coats) / 10 m²/L = 1000L. Cost estimate and timeline depend on this accuracy.

**Question Type 3 (Primer Selection):** A steel column in a parking structure near the ocean requires paint. Should you specify latex primer, oil-based primer, or epoxy primer? Answer: Epoxy primer in high-corrosion coastal environment. Oil-based or latex are insufficient for salt-spray resistance.

**Question Type 4 (Fire Protection):** Structural steel beams in a 15-story office building must achieve 2-hour fire rating. Should you specify standard latex paint or intumescent paint? Answer: Intumescent paint with specified DFT (2-3mm, verified per ASTM E119). Standard paint has no structural fire rating.
