Architectural Interior (AI) Services is a frequently tested sub-service under SPP 203 that candidates commonly confuse with SPP 208 (Architectural Design Competition) or misapply to exterior work. The LEA tests your ability to correctly identify the scope of AI work, classify the two service delivery models (Dual Capacity vs. CA Only), and calculate compensation using the cost of AI work as the base—_not_ total project cost.

## Core Concept

**Architectural Interior (AI) Services** under SPP 203, Section 203.1 (RA 9266, Sections 3(4)(g) and 14(3)) is the detailed planning and design of indoor and enclosed areas of proposed buildings, including retrofit, renovation, rehabilitation, and expansion work. This includes the architectural laying out of all building engineering systems within those interior spaces.

AI Services are distinct from overall architectural design (SPP 202 as a whole) because they focus exclusively on the _interior_ environment. They do not extend to site planning, landscape design, or exterior architectural expression. The scope encompasses everything that affects the user experience and functionality of enclosed spaces: spatial organization, material finishes, systems distribution, and the technical specifications that support construction and inspection.

<Warn>
  **Critical distinction:** SPP 208 is Architectural Design Competition (for
  design competitions only). SPP 203 Section 203.1 is the standard service for
  Interior Design planning and documentation. Do not confuse these under any
  circumstances—they are entirely different service categories with different
  legal frameworks, scopes, and fee structures.
</Warn>

An architect providing AI Services operates in one of two legal capacities:

1. **Dual Capacity (Most Common in Practice):** The architect serves as both the Architect-of-Record (AoR) _and_ the Consulting Architect (CA) for the AI component. This occurs when the project has an AoR and that same firm or architect also takes responsibility for detailed interior design and specification.
2. **CA Only:** The architect serves as Consulting Architect for AI exclusively, while a different architect serves as the Architect-of-Record for the overall project. This structure is typical for specialized interior architecture firms contracted after the base design is approved.

The two-model framework exists because interior design complexity varies. Some projects treat AI as an integral part of the main architectural contract; others require specialized expertise and separate engagement.

<Example>
  **Worked Example:** A 5,000 m² office building renovation project has a total
  PCC (Project Construction Cost) of PHP 50 million. The interior finishes,
  furniture, and systems layout (the AI work) are estimated to cost PHP 8
  million. The fee for AI Services at 15% is calculated as: PHP 8 million × 15%
  = **PHP 1.2 million**. Note that the PHP 50 million total PCC is never
  used—only the PHP 8 million cost attributable to AI work.
</Example>

## Philippine Practice Context

Interior architecture in the Philippine market operates across two primary contexts. In large commercial and institutional projects, the main architectural firm typically retains AI Services as part of the master contract (Dual Capacity model), reflecting the unified design vision required for premium office towers, hospitality, and government facilities. In boutique interior design practices and specialized renovation firms, architects engage as Consulting Architects (CA Only) for high-end residential and mixed-use projects, allowing the owner to engage proven interior specialists independently.

The SPP 203.1 framework recognizes that interior design—far from being cosmetic—is a technical and regulatory discipline involving building systems, material standards, accessibility compliance, and fire-safety integration. Fee percentages reflect the depth of this work: 15% is not a premium; it accounts for extensive documentation, coordination with engineering consultants, and detailed shop drawing reviews.

## How It's Tested

The LEA tests AI Services through four primary question patterns:

1. **Scope Identification:** "Which of the following is _within_ AI Services scope?" or "_not_ included?" Tests ability to exclude landscape, exterior design, and engineering work.
2. **Service Delivery Model:** "An architect is engaged to design the interior of a retail mall while another architect serves as Architect-of-Record for the overall structure. What is the interior architect's role?" Tests understanding of Dual Capacity vs. CA Only distinction.
3. **Compensation Calculation:** "A building project costs PHP 40 million total. Interior finishes and furniture are estimated at PHP 6 million. Using the standard 15% fee rate, what is the architect's AI compensation?" Tests ability to isolate the AI cost base and exclude total PCC.
4. **Document and Service Delivery:** "Which of the following is _not_ part of AI Services per SPP 203.1?" Tests knowledge of the six scope items (space plans through final inspection).

<Tip>
  **Memory anchor:** AI = "All Interior, No Exterior." The scope is ruthlessly
  limited to enclosed spaces. Any question mentioning site planning,
  landscaping, exterior façade, or structural design is trying to lure you
  outside AI scope. Additionally, remember the compensation formula: **Cost of
  AI Work (only), not Total PCC × 15%.**
</Tip>
