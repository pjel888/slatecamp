# Latin American Modernism

## Board Exam Quick Check

First ask whether the question is about urban planning, sculptural concrete, or color/light/water. Then choose the architect whose source-backed language matches that clue.

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Latin American modernism did not simply copy the Bauhaus or the International Style. Its exam identity is modernism adapted to place: tropical climate, national modernization, landscape, Catholic and vernacular memory, and new reinforced-concrete possibilities.

The safest three-name frame is Costa, Niemeyer, and Barragan. Lucio Costa is the planner-theorist associated with Brasilia's Pilot Plan. Oscar Niemeyer is the architect of sculptural concrete civic modernism, especially in Brasilia. Luis Barragan is the Mexican modernist whose houses and landscapes use color, light, walls, water, and silence to create emotional modern space.

## Core Concept

UNESCO describes Brasilia as a definitive example of twentieth-century modernist urbanism, with Costa's urban design and Niemeyer's architectural works forming the capital's most recognizable source-backed pairing. The city is organized by monumental and residential scales, axes, superblocks, green spaces, and key national buildings.

UNESCO describes the Luis Barragan House and Studio as a regional adaptation of the International Modern Movement that integrates modern design with traditional Mexican, vernacular, landscape, water, and color sensibilities. Pritzker records support Barragan's use of pure planes, texture, color, water, and nature as compositional elements. Pritzker records also support Niemeyer's free-form sculptural approach and his major Brasilia role.

## Real World Scenario

A government campus inspired by Brasilia and a private retreat inspired by Barragan can both be modernist. The first should be judged by city order, axes, civic monumentality, pilotis, and concrete form. The second should be judged by controlled light, walls, color, water, gardens, and sequence. Treating Barragan as anti-modernist would miss the source-backed point.

## How It Is Tested

Expect architect-identification items: curves and monumental concrete point to Niemeyer; Pilot Plan and city structure point to Costa; color, light, water, walls, and gardens point to Barragan.

Expect Brasilia questions: the safe date is 1960 for inauguration, with Juscelino Kubitschek as the president who advanced the new capital. The city predates the later military dictatorship.

Expect comparison questions: Latin American modernism diverges from cold universal rationalism by asking how modernism can express place, climate, landscape, and national identity.

## Common Mistakes

Do not call Barragan non-modern because he used strong color. The source-safe answer is that he made a regional, emotional modernism.

Do not say the military dictatorship designed Brasilia. It was planned and inaugurated before the dictatorship period.

Do not reduce Niemeyer to decoration. His curves are tied to reinforced concrete, civic monumentality, and a free-form modernist language.

## Key Terms

**Pilot Plan** - Costa's source-backed urban plan for Brasilia, organized by axes and sectors.

**Pilotis** - Slender supports lifting parts of a building and freeing ground or landscape below.

**Sculptural Concrete** - Reinforced-concrete modernism used for expressive curves and civic monumentality.

**Regional Modernism** - Modern design adapted to local climate, landscape, culture, craft, and memory.

**Emotional Modernism** - Barragan's sensory use of color, light, water, walls, and gardens.
