# SPP Document 203: Standards of Professional Practice on Specialized Architectural Services

## Overview

**SPP Document 203** governs **Specialized Architectural Services** — a broad and evolving category of architectural practice covering expertise needed to complete, complement, or supplement the totality of any building or planning project. It is part of the 2010 Standards of Professional Practice (SPP), promulgated as the Supplemental Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of **Republic Act No. 9266** (The Architecture Act of 2004), and replaces the earlier **1979 UAP Doc. 203**.

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to:

Identify the nine categories of specialized architectural services under SPP Doc 203 — interior design, landscape architecture, acoustical design, lighting design, preservation/conservation, space planning, urban design, graphic design, and expert witness services — and explain how each is scoped, compensated (per-diem, fixed fee, or percentage), and documented separately from basic services.
---
SPP 203 recognizes that architecture, as the blending of aesthetics, functions, space, materials and environment, now requires the application of various technologies and specialized skills that go beyond what is covered in SPP 202 Regular Design Services. It contains **23 enumerated sub-specialties** (203.1 through 203.23), each constituting a distinct area of advanced or specialized architectural practice.

***

## Legal and Regulatory Basis

<Callout variant="authority" title="Legal Authority: R.A. No. 9266 — The Architecture Act of 2004">

This SPP document is promulgated as part of the Supplemental Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of **Republic Act No. 9266**, approved 27 July 2010. Compliance is mandatory for all Registered and Licensed Architects (RLAs) and Architectural Firms (AFs) in the Philippines.

</Callout>

All specialized architectural services under SPP 203 must be rendered by a **Registered and Licensed Architect (RLA)** acting as a **Consulting Architect (CA)**. The Consulting Architect may also be a separately Registered and Licensed Professional (RLP) — meaning the CA may hold a valid professional identification card for one or more State-regulated professions other than architecture (e.g., Environmental Planner, Landscape Architect). The Board and the Commission may issue a **Specialist Certificate** to a qualifying RLA, upon accreditation by the IAPOA.

The architect's responsibility under SPP 203 remains consistent: to ensure that both the building and its physical environment enhance the lives of people by strictly adhering to national and international standards with regard to **public health, safety, and welfare**.

***

## General Structure of Each Sub-Service

For each of the 23 specialized services, SPP 203 follows a consistent structure:

1. **Introduction** — Context and rationale for the service
2. **Scope of Services** — Specific tasks the Consulting Architect performs
3. **Manner of Providing Services** — How the Architect may be engaged (dual capacity or consulting only)
4. **Method of Compensation** — How fees are determined

For the first six sub-services (203.1–203.6), which involve extensive work, the SPP provides full introductions and detailed scopes. For sub-services 203.7–203.23, the SPP provides focused descriptions without full four-part breakdowns, deferring to the **Architect's Guidelines** for fee structures and detailed procedures.

### Manner of Providing Services (Common to All Sub-Services)

For all specialized services, the architect may enter into contract with the owner in two ways:

1. **Dual capacity** — acting simultaneously as Architect-of-record (Aor) and as Consulting Architect for the specialized service
2. **Consulting Architect only** — engaged solely to provide the specialized service, with another architect serving as Aor

## The 23 Specialized Architectural Services

### Group A: Design and Aesthetic Services

#### 203.1 Architectural Interior (AI) Services

**Introduction:** Architectural Interiors (AI), specifically mentioned under Secs. 3(4)(g) and 14(3) of R.A. No. 9266, involves the detailed planning and design of the indoor/enclosed areas of any proposed building or structure, including retrofit, renovation, rehabilitation, or expansion work covering all architectural and utility aspects, including the architectural lay-outing of all building engineering systems.

**Scope of Services:** The Consulting Architect for AI:
- Prescribes space plans, stacking diagrams/sections, and computations of areas for the different activities and spaces
- Lays out and prescribes furniture, built-ins, and equipment; prepares specifications of AI components including all floor/wall/ceiling finishes, doors and partition systems, hardware, modular or ready-assembled furniture, built-ins, fixtures, signages, and graphic devices
- Assists the client in conducting bids or negotiations with general contractors, sub-contractors, and suppliers
- Checks and approves samples of materials and shop drawings of AI components
- Reviews and approves billings of AI components
- Conducts final inspection and approves installed AI components

**Method of Compensation:** For projects involving extensive detailing of AI components such as custom floor, wall, ceiling construction and finishes, cabinet design, built-in components, equipment, and special fittings, the fee is a **percentage of the cost of the AI work**. Should the client separately hire Specialist Consultants, their fee is for the account of the client directly.

<Callout variant="note">

**Note:** AI under SPP 203 refers specifically to the architectural aspect of interiors — space planning, utility lay-outing, built-ins, and specifications — as distinct from the separate and separately licensed profession of **Interior Design**.

</Callout>


***

#### 203.2 Acoustic Design Services

**Introduction:** Acoustic Design Services involve the detailed planning and design to control sound transmission for compatibility with the architectural design concept. The evolution of products and techniques in sound management has provided greater flexibility in designing interior environments that respond to acoustical demands of varied activities.

**Scope of Services:** The Consulting Architect for Acoustic Design:
- Prepares drawings and specifications for acoustic design and treatment, sound control and reinforcement, sound absorption, reflectance, and insulation
- Assists the owner/client in bidding out the work or negotiating with a specialty sub-contractor
- Checks and approves samples of materials and equipment
- Conducts final inspection of work and equipment
- Assists the owner/client in evaluating the amount due the sub-contractor

**Method of Compensation:** Fee depends on the **complexity of the works to be undertaken**. Any separately hired Specialist Consultants are paid directly by the client.

***

#### 203.3 Architectural Lighting Layout and Design

**Introduction:** Architectural Lighting Layout and Design involves the detailed planning and design of light transmission, timing, and control for compatibility with the architectural design concept. It addresses the lighting demands of varied activities within and outside a building.

**Scope of Services:** The Consulting Architect for Lighting Design:
- Prepares drawings and specifications for lighting design, illumination, fixture placement, efficiency, and energy considerations
- Assists the owner/client in bidding out the work or negotiating with specialty sub-contractors
- Checks and approves samples of materials and fixtures
- Conducts final inspection of work and fixtures
- Assists owner/client in evaluating the amount due the sub-contractor

**Method of Compensation:** Fee depends on the **complexity of the works to be undertaken**. "Cost of the Work" means the total cost of all fixtures and accessories designed, specified, or procured by the Specialist Architect and/or Specialist Consultants for the owner/client that were used or installed in the project.

***

### Group B: Planning and Urban Design Services

#### 203.4 Site Development Planning (SDP) Services

**Introduction:** The space planning, architectural lay-outing, and utilization of spaces within and surrounding a building must be a well-coordinated effort so that both the building and its host environment act as one. The Architect is not merely concerned with the building itself, but with the grounds and surrounding space as well.

**Scope of Services:** The Consulting Architect for SDP:
- Conceptualizes the entire Site Development Plan (SDP), including the generic scope of civil works and the general scope of softscape (plants, grass, trees) and hardscape (paving, walls, water features) requirements
- Conceptualizes the specifications for the needed civil works and utility lines
- Assists the owner/client in bidding out work or negotiating with landscape, waterscape, and rock formation contractors

**Method of Compensation:** Fee depends on the **estimated cost of civil works and landscaping works** (hardscape and softscape), depending on magnitude and complexity. If the architect is also certified and licensed as a Landscape Architect (RLP), the fee increases correspondingly. If the owner separately engages a Landscape Architect, that fee is for the owner's direct account.

***

#### 203.5 Site and Physical Planning Services (Including Master Development Planning, Subdivision Planning, and Urban Design)

**Introduction:** Physical planning refers to the orderly arrangement within a piece of land or property on which vertical structures and horizontal developments (ROWs, open spaces, activity spaces) are to be proposed. The great cities of the world took shape through the activities of visionary architects who actively engaged in physical planning.

**Scope of Services:** The Consulting Architect for Site and Physical Planning:
- Confers with the owner/client, secures primary and secondary data for reliable projections and analyses
- Examines laws, ordinances, rules, and regulations (code searches) and considers best industry practices
- Prepares Framework and Conceptual Master Development Plans (FRDPs and CMDPs) and reports
- Prepares Preliminary up to Detailed Master Development Plans (PMDPs/DMDPs) showing: physical layout, road networks, vehicular and pedestrian movement, legal easements, basic utility layouts, landscape layouts, lot pad elevations, lot primacy matrices, deed of restrictions (DoR), development standards and guidelines (DSG), lot counts, land use tabulations, building footprints, roof-prints, view corridors, and building cast shadow projections
- Prepares the SPPCC for the total physical development
- Undertakes modifications and revisions as required within the engagement period
- Prepares finalized plans, reports, and specifications for government approval
- Prepares phasing of construction with the concurrence of the owner

**Typical Sites:** Industrial estates, commercial centers, religious/institutional/government/civic centers, sports complexes, tourism estates/resorts, amusement parks, educational facilities, residential/housing subdivisions.

**Method of Compensation:** Fee structure as stated in the **Architect's Guidelines**.

***

#### 203.6 Comprehensive Development Planning (CDP) Services

**Introduction:** CDP is an expanded form of physical planning that includes all activities necessary for formulating, implementing, and realizing a Master Development Plan (MDP), including the preparation of Environmental Impact Assessments/Statements (EIA/S). While the MDP puts premium on physical planning and engineering, CDP emphasizes non-physical components — financial, economic/market, socio-cultural, administrative-political-institutional-legal, and environmental.

**Scope of Services (Five CDP Components)**:
1. **Physical Component** — Land use and changes in the physical environment, represented by the MDP
2. **Economic Component** — The nation's assets and their management
3. **Socio-Cultural Component** — People, their living conditions, and ways to ameliorate them
4. **Transport Component** — Road and transit networks, land-sea-air linkages, movement of people and goods
5. **Legal and Administrative Component** — The relationship of development proposals and policies to existing laws

The Consulting Architect performing CDP:
- Identifies existing land use, resources, social behavior, and interaction
- Undertakes environmental analysis, demographic analysis, and feasibility studies
- Examines existing laws, ordinances, and political/social constraints
- Prepares conceptual development plans, policies, and implementing strategies to arrive at CDP solutions

<Callout variant="note">

**Note:** CDP ideally requires the architect to also be a separately qualified and licensed **Environmental Planner (EnP)**. If not, the Architect must engage duly qualified professionals as Specialist Consultants.

</Callout>


**Method of Compensation:** Through **man-months** (22 man-days × 8 man-hours/day), multiplied by a factor covering overhead and other direct/indirect costs, as prescribed in the Architect's Guidelines.

***

### Group C: Heritage and Safety Services

#### 203.7 Historic and Cultural Heritage Conservation and Planning

The Consulting Architect in this specialized area provides **research, assessment, recording, management, interpretation, and conservation of historical heritage**. This requires deep knowledge of history, materials, and conservation protocols such as the Venice Charter and applicable Philippine heritage laws (e.g., R.A. No. 10066, the National Cultural Heritage Act).

***

#### 203.8 Security Evaluation and Planning

The Consulting Architect in this area **arranges and formulates methods of rating and ascertaining the value of structures or facilities** which must be fully secured, kept safe, protected, and provided with sufficient safeguards for the conduct of any work or activity. This includes security system design, threat/risk assessment, and the preparation of the **Fire Safety and Life Assessment Report (FALAR)** required by R.A. No. 9514, the 2008 Fire Code of the Philippines and its 2009 IRR.

***

### Group D: Technical and Building Systems Services

#### 203.9 Building Systems Design

The Consulting Architect in this area engages in methods of **producing building components in a highly engineered, efficient, and cost-effective manner**, particularly for residential and commercial applications. This covers the integration of structural, mechanical, electrical, plumbing/sanitary, communications, and electronic systems in a manner that supports and does not conflict with the architectural design concept.

***

#### 203.10 Facilities Maintenance Support

The Consulting Architect provides the owner/client with means and measures to **ensure the proper function and maintenance of the building/structure and site after final inspection**. This includes creating maintenance programs, schedules, and guidelines for the long-term care of the building.

***

#### 203.11 Building Testing and Commissioning

The Consulting Architect **recommends the systematic process of ensuring that a building's array of systems is planned, designed, installed, and tested to perform according to design intent and operational needs**. The SPP explicitly states: if building materials, equipment, and systems are not installed properly or are not operating as intended, the effectiveness, efficiency, productivity, and other benefits of high-performance plans/designs will not be achieved.

***

#### 203.12 Building Environmental Certification

This sub-service involves the use of a **building environment rating system** to evaluate the environmental performance of a building and to encourage market migration towards sustainable design. The rating system must be:
- **Credit-based** — projects earn points for environment-friendly actions during planning, design, construction, and occupancy
- **Flexible** — projects need not meet identical requirements to qualify
- **Consensus-based and market-driven** — to accelerate the development of green building practices

The Consulting Architect must have expertise in Green Architecture, Environmental/Sustainable Design, and Philippine environmental laws (Solid Waste, Clean Air, and Clean Water Acts) and DENR administrative issuances.

***

#### 203.13 Forensic Architecture

The Consulting Architect **undertakes a scientific study on the built environment's well-being**, focusing on how a building can best maintain itself and prolong its life in a cost-efficient manner. The forensic study may include:
- Determination of causes of building, component, and/or material deterioration
- Causes of observed building deficiencies (e.g., non-compliance with planning/building laws, deviations from original use)
- Research on possible faulty activities and operations during project implementation
- Determination of faulty plan/design and/or construction methodology

***

#### 203.14 Building Appraisal

Appraisal is defined as an **act or process of estimating value**. The Consulting Architect places value on the building/structure's condition and defects, and on its repair and maintenance, including the required improvements.

***

#### 203.15 Structural Conceptualization

The Consulting Architect **conceives, chooses, and develops the type, disposition, arrangement, and proportioning of the structural elements** of an architectural work, giving due consideration to safety, cost-effectiveness, functionality, and aesthetics. Note that structural conceptualization remains within the architect's domain; detailed structural analysis and computation remain with the licensed structural engineer.

***

### Group E: Documentation and Process Services

#### 203.16 Preliminary Services

The Consulting Architect in this area must have advanced knowledge of **Site Analysis, Space Planning and Management, Architectural Programming**, and the other services under SPP Document 201. This service covers early-stage advisory and site preparation work that precedes formal design.

***

#### 203.17 Contract Documentation and Review Services

The Consulting Architect must have advanced knowledge of **Specification Writing, Estimation and Quantity Survey, Architectural Production, Architectural Software, Architectural Support Services, and Contract Document Review**. This service is particularly valuable for peer review of documents prepared by other architects or firms.

***

#### 203.18 Post-Design Services (Including Construction Services)

The Consulting Architect must have advanced knowledge of **Pre-Construction, Construction, Post-Construction**, and the other services under SPP Documents 204, 206, and 207. Included under this specialized practice is the preparation of the **Fire Safety and Life Assessment Report (FALAR)** required by R.A. No. 9514, the 2008 Fire Code of the Philippines and its 2009 IRR.

***

#### 203.19 Dispute Avoidance and Resolution

The Consulting Architect must have advanced knowledge of the various modes of **Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR)** prescribed under R.A. No. 9285 (the ADR Act of 2004) and its IRR, including:
- Construction Arbitration
- Mediation and Conciliation
- Negotiation
- Contract Administration, Quality Surveys, Appraisals and Adjustments
- Expert Testimony

An architect specializing in ADR must preferably be **State-accredited**.

***

### Group F: Research, Planning, and Management Services

#### 203.20 Architectural Research Methods

The Consulting Architect must have advanced knowledge of **Research Methods, Philippine Architecture and its History, Architectural Materials and Finishes, Building Types and Standards, Architectural Design Trends, Architectural Writing, and Architectural Photography**.

***

#### 203.21 Special Building/Facility Planning and Design

The Consulting Architect must have advanced knowledge of the planning and design processes required for:
- Housing Developments
- Recreational and Tourism Estates
- Health Care and Hospitality Facilities
- Transportation and Telecommunications Facilities
- Production and Extractive Facilities
- Utility-related Developments
- Secure Facilities
- Business and Industrial Parks
- Economic Zones
- Community Architecture

***

#### 203.22 Building Components

The Consulting Architect must have advanced knowledge of **Building Materials and Finishes, Construction Methodologies, Building Envelopes** (including cladding and roofing systems), **Architectural Fenestrations**, and **Architectural Hardware, Fixtures, and Fittings**.

***

#### 203.23 Management of Architectural Practices

The Consulting Architect must have advanced knowledge of:
- Types of Architectural Office Operations and Office Management
- Accounting, Finance, Taxation, and Audit
- Labor Code
- Architectural Marketing and Project Development
- Proposals, Negotiations, and Contracts
- Contract Administration
- File Management
- Limitations of Business Process Outsourcing (BPO) and Knowledge Process Outsourcing (KPO) Operations

***

## General Method of Compensation for All Specialized Services

All 23 specialized services may be classified as **additional or extra services** to the regular design services of SPP 202. The general compensation framework for specialized services is:

\[
\text\{Fee\} = \text\{Man-months\} \times \text\{Factor\}
\]

Where:
- **Man-month** = 22 man-days × 8 man-hours/man-day = 176 man-hours per man-month
- **Factor** = a multiplier to cover other direct and indirect costs (e.g., overhead, profit)

Any other applicable mode of fee determination from **SPP Document 201** (MDPE), **SPP Document 202** (percentage of PCC), or the **Architect's Guidelines** may also be applied, depending on the nature of the specialized service.

## Practical Notes for Architects and Clients

**For Architects:**
- The list of 23 sub-services is explicitly stated as **"include but are not limited to"** — meaning new specialized services may be recognized by the Board and IAPOA as the profession evolves.
- Specialist Certificates may be issued by the PRBoA/PRC upon accreditation by the IAPOA. Pursuing these credentials formalizes competence in specific sub-specialties.
- When acting in a **dual capacity** (Aor + Consulting Architect for the specialized service), the architect must ensure clear delineation of responsibilities and fees in the Owner-Architect Agreement, so there is no confusion between SPP 202 and SPP 203 services and their respective fees.

**For Clients / Project Proponents:**
- SPP 203 services are **always separately contracted and compensated** from SPP 202 Regular Design Services — they are never included in the regular architect's fee unless explicitly agreed upon in writing.
- Clients who wish to engage Specialist Consultants (SCs) directly — bypassing the architect — may do so; in that case, the SC fees are paid directly by the owner and do not pass through the architect. However, a **coordination fee** is still due to the architect for managing the interface between the SC's inputs and the architectural design concept.
- For sub-services 203.5 (Physical Planning) and 203.6 (CDP) involving multi-disciplinary teams, the owner must budget separately for each Specialist Consultant's fee, as these are not covered by the architect's SPP 203 fee.

## Exam Tip

LEA Trap: Nine specialized services: Interior Design, Landscape Architecture, Acoustics, Lighting, Preservation, Space Planning, Urban Design, Graphic Design, Expert Witness. Compensation: per-diem, fixed fee, or lump sum — NOT percentage of construction cost (unless the basic services contract also uses percentage). Expert witness is the only one with a forensic/legal character.
