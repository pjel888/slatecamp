# Multi-Purpose Halls: Assembly Classification and Egress

## Source Scope

This local repair is limited to PD 1096 occupancy classification, the RA 9514 2019 Revised IRR, and the local Fire Code source family. Earlier stage-height, chair-storage, room-program, acoustic, and BP 344 statements are not carried forward because those source families were outside this Batch 064 raw-PDF audit.

## LEA Anchor

A multi-purpose hall is first an occupant-load and use problem. The same room can be a barangay meeting hall, gymnasium, dining hall, conference space, worship space, or theater-type assembly depending on how people gather and whether fixed seats or a stage are present.

## PD 1096 Classification

PD 1096 lists Multi-purpose/Barangay Hall in a residential matrix context and lists civic centers, clubhouses, community centers, school auditoriums, gymnasia, little theaters, concert halls, and similar uses in the Group C matrix. For assembly buildings, it also separates Group H and Group I by stage and occupant load thresholds.

## RA 9514 Assembly Rules

RA 9514 assembly occupancy covers gathering spaces for 50 or more persons. Occupant load is calculated by use type: concentrated assembly without fixed seats, less concentrated spaces such as conference rooms or gymnasiums, standing areas, or the actual number of fixed seats. Assembly exits, main exit capacity, corridor width, travel distance, panic hardware, fire alarm, sprinkler triggers, emergency plans, and overcrowding controls are then checked from that occupancy.

## Exam Use

Do not classify a multi-purpose hall by its name alone. Ask whether the problem gives fixed seats, stage, occupant load, standing area, dining or conference layout, mixed use, or public assembly operations; then apply the exact PD 1096 or RA 9514 rule named by the item.
