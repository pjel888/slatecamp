## Subject Coverage

The LEA treats History of Architecture as a survey across 11 subtopic areas:

- **Pre-Historic & Ancient** — Megaliths, Mesopotamia, Egypt, Greece, Rome
- **Medieval to Baroque** — Early Christian, Byzantine, Romanesque, Gothic, Renaissance, Mannerist, Baroque, Rococo
- **Modern & Contemporary** — Arts & Crafts through Postmodernism, High-Tech, Deconstructivism
- **Asian & Pacific Architecture** — India, China, Japan, Southeast Asia, Oceania
- **Islamic Architecture** — Mosque traditions, Persian, Mughal, Ottoman, Moorish
- **Philippine Architecture** — Pre-colonial, Spanish colonial, American period, contemporary
- **African Architecture** — Sub-Saharan, Great Zimbabwe, North African vernacular
- **Latin American Architecture** — Colonial urbanism, Baroque, 20th-century modernism
- **Pre-Columbian & Indigenous American** — Maya, Aztec, Inca, Pueblo
- **Australian & New Zealand Architecture** — Indigenous, colonial, contemporary
- **United States Architecture** — Colonial through Chicago School, Mid-Century Modern, postwar

## Study Strategy

### Phase 1 — Western Timeline (Weeks 1–2)

Lock in Greek Orders (Doric, Ionic, Corinthian) and Roman engineering (arch, vault, dome). Walk from Early Christian through Baroque.

### Phase 2 — Philippine Architecture (Week 3)

Use local knowledge as anchor: pre-colonial → Spanish colonial (earthquake Baroque) → American period → contemporary.

### Phase 3 — Non-Western Traditions (Weeks 4–5)

Study Islamic, South Asian, and East Asian traditions thematically. Map non-Western timelines onto Western eras.

### Phase 4 — Modern and Contemporary (Week 6)

Movements and manifestos: what each movement was reacting *against* is as important as what it proposed.

### Phase 5 — Fill Remaining Regions (Week 7)

Africa, Americas, Oceania in shorter focused sessions.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Mixing up periods within the Western timeline.** Romanesque (round arches, thick walls) vs. Gothic (pointed arches, flying buttresses) — commit the structural logic, not just the names.
- **Confusing architects with movements.** Le Corbusier (International Style, Brutalism) vs. Mies van der Rohe. Know at least three canonical works and the decade for major figures.
- **Treating Philippine Architecture as a separate silo.** It's a story of layered influences — Austronesian, Hindu-Buddhist, Islamic, Spanish, American.
- **Ignoring non-Western chronology.** Map non-Western timelines onto Western eras you already know.
