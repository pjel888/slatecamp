# The Comprehensive Land Use Plan (CLUP)

## Board Exam Quick Check

Anchor a CLUP question to the source of authority first: RA 7160 creates the LGU duty and Sanggunian authority, EO 72 explains the review and implementation path, RA 11201/its IRR transfer the former HLURB land-use planning and monitoring functions to DHSUD, and the HLURB/RPS guides explain the technical planning process.

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

The Comprehensive Land Use Plan is the LGU's long-term spatial policy for managing land and physical resources. RA 7160 Section 20(c) requires LGUs to continue preparing comprehensive land use plans enacted through zoning ordinances, which become the primary and dominant bases for future use of land resources.

For cities and municipalities, RA 7160 gives the Sanggunian authority to adopt the CLUP and enact integrated zoning ordinances in consonance with the approved CLUP. EO 72 describes the preparation flow: the city or municipal development council initiates formulation or updating, the CPDC or MPDC provides technical support, and the completed plan is submitted to the Sangguniang Panlungsod or Sangguniang Bayan for enactment into a zoning ordinance.

Do not treat the mayor, planning office, or DHSUD as substitutes for the Sanggunian. The planning office supplies technical work; the Sanggunian makes the legislative decision. DHSUD, through the functions transferred by RA 11201 and detailed in its IRR, sets land-use planning and zoning standards, provides technical assistance, participates in review processes, and monitors CLUP and zoning ordinance implementation.

The HLURB CLUP Guidebook frames the CLUP as the plan that determines specific uses of land and physical or natural resources, integrates sectoral plans, and provides policy direction for settlements, production, protection, and infrastructure land-use categories. It also treats situation analysis as both technical and participatory: data, maps, community consultations, sectoral studies, and climate/disaster risk information are part of the planning record.

The common exam trap is the planning horizon. RA 7160 establishes the CLUP and zoning mandate, but the precise long-term period is guided by planning standards. The CLUP Guidebook uses projections of at least 10 years and describes CLUPs as long-term plans that may cover 10 years, 15 years, or more. RPS 2008 also says long-term CLUP feedback data should be collected after about 9 to 10 years, while databases and CDP/ELA-linked work may be updated every 3 years. For source-safe answers, say "long-term CLUP, commonly tested as 10 years under planning guidance," not "RA 7160 itself states a 10-year term."

## Exam Focus

- RA 7160: LGUs prepare CLUPs enacted through zoning ordinances.
- Sanggunian: adopts the CLUP and enacts the zoning ordinance.
- Planning office and technical working groups: prepare data, maps, analysis, and recommendations.
- DHSUD: standard-setting, technical assistance, review participation, monitoring, and transferred land-use planning functions.
- Zoning ordinance: the enforceable implementation tool of the CLUP.
- Review path: EO 72 assigns review roles to the province/PLUC, RLUC, and formerly HLRB; DHSUD now holds the relevant transferred functions under RA 11201.

## Practice Cue

When the item asks "who prepares," look for the planning office or development council. When it asks "who adopts" or "who enacts," answer the Sanggunian. When it asks "what makes land-use rules enforceable," answer the zoning ordinance. When it asks about national conformity, look for the EO 72/DHSUD review and standards role.
