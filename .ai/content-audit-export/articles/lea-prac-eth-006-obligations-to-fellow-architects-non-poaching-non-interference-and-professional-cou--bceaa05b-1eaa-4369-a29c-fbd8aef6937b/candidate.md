# Obligations to Fellow Architects

The UAP Document Code of Ethics treats professional courtesy as a duty to the profession, not as optional etiquette. The source section on colleagues and subordinates covers fair competition, commissions already held by another architect, architectural competitions, successor work, reputation, signing and sealing, employment conditions, mentoring, and professional organization service.

## Source-Safe Rules

- Do not render professional services without compensation, except for small civic or charity projects. Do not offer preliminary services on a conditional basis before a definite agreement with legitimate compensation.
- Do not knowingly compete with other architects on the basis of lower professional charges, and do not use donations as a device for competitive advantage, except for worthy civic or religious projects.
- Do not seek a commission already known to be endowed to another architect, whether the other engagement is already agreed upon or still being negotiated.
- Do not enter an architectural competition when you helped formulate the program or have served as professional adviser or juror for that competition. A retained adviser may accept later employment only as consulting architect.
- Do not undertake a commission where another architect was previously employed until the other architect has been notified in writing, the original employment has been conclusively terminated, and the prior architect has been duly compensated.
- For additions, rehabilitation, or remodeling of a structure previously undertaken by another architect, notify the prior architect even if the owner is no longer the same. If the greater mass, area, or design of the original structure remains substantially maintained, limit public claims to the work actually done.
- Do not knowingly, falsely, or maliciously injure another architect's reputation, prospects, or practice.
- Do not sign or seal work prepared by others unless it was done under the architect's direct personal supervision.

## LEA Study Anchor

When an item mentions a prior architect, ask two separate questions. First, is the commission already known to belong to another architect? If yes, the non-poaching rule controls. Second, is the new architect being asked to take over or alter prior work? If yes, written notice, termination, compensation, and limited credit are the safe sequence.
