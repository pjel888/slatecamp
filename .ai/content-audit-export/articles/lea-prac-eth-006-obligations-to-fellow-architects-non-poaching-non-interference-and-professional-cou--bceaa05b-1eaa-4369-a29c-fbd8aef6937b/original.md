## Obligations to Fellow Architects — Non-Poaching, Non-Interference, and Professional Courtesy

Professional relationships form the foundation of architectural practice. The Code of Ethical Conduct establishes clear obligations that govern how architects interact with colleagues, protecting both individual practitioners and the profession's integrity.

### Non-Poaching of Commissions

Under UAP Code of Ethics (non-poaching provision), an architect shall not under any circumstances seek commissions previously endowed to another architect, whether the original arrangement has been definitively agreed upon or remains in negotiation. This provision prevents unfair competition and protects architects from losing work through improper means.

The prohibition is absolute—it encompasses all circumstances and methods. An architect who learns of a commission assignment cannot attempt to acquire it, even if the original engagement appears uncertain. The competing architect must accept the exclusion from pursuing such work and redirect efforts to available opportunities.

### Non-Interference Obligations

UAP Code of Ethics (non-interference provision) establishes that an architect shall not undertake a commission previously assigned to another architect until proper written notification occurs and the original employment termination is conclusively confirmed with compensation settled. This creates a sequential requirement: notification first, then verification of termination, then proof of compensation completion.

UAP Code of Ethics (renovation credit provision) extends protection to renovation and remodeling work. When another architect previously designed an erected structure, the new architect must notify the original architect even if the property owner has changed. When the original design's greater mass, area, or design remains substantially maintained, the new architect must limit public claims to only the work actually performed. This ensures professional credit assignment accuracy.

### Preservation and Historical Responsibility

The Code emphasizes architectural heritage preservation. Architects undertaking additions, rehabilitation, or remodeling of structures originally designed by colleagues must preserve or restore as much of the original design as possible, particularly for historic architectural examples. This obligation extends professional courtesy beyond contractual relationships into cultural stewardship.

### Advertisement and Credit Boundaries

Architects cannot simultaneously claim credit for entire projects while performing only partial work. Public representation of professional accomplishment must align with actual contribution. This prevents misrepresentation while protecting the original architect's legacy and professional reputation.

### Injurious Conduct Prohibition

UAP Code of Ethics (professional conduct provision) prohibits architects from knowingly injuring, falsely or maliciously, another architect's professional reputation, prospects, or practice. This extends beyond simple non-poaching to active protection of colleagues' standing. An architect cannot engage in character assassination, false statements, or deliberate competitive sabotage.

### Professional Courtesy Framework

These obligations reflect a broader professional courtesy principle. Architects must confine criticisms within constructive and inspirational limits, never resorting to malicious motives (Code introductory principle). This requires architects to:

- Inform colleagues of contemplated work affecting their previous projects
- Verify termination before assuming engagement
- Confirm compensation settlement before proceeding
- Limit public claims to work actually performed
- Preserve historical design elements from predecessor architects
- Refrain from false or injurious statements
- Maintain dignity in professional competition

### LEA Test Points

1. **Non-poaching provision scope** — An architect learns a developer is considering a hotel renovation. A competitor has designed the original structure 15 years ago but has no current engagement. The architect must verify prior design involvement and follow UAP non-interference guidelines regardless of current engagement.

2. **Notification requirement timing** — Attempting to acquire a commission already endowed violates UAP Code of Ethics non-poaching provision immediately; the violation occurs at the attempted acquisition, not at successful diversion.

3. **Substantial design maintenance determination** — When substantial original design elements remain, the new architect must limit claims to work actually performed per UAP ethics, not the entire project.

4. **Original architect identification obligation** — An architect cannot determine a previous designer exists after commencing work and claim ignorance excuses the non-notification failure.

5. **Injurious conduct boundaries** — Truthful statements about a competitor's prior errors represent legitimate professional discussion; false or exaggerated statements constitute prohibited injurious conduct.

6. **Compensation settlement verification** — An original architect claims ongoing dispute with a former client over payment. The competitor architect cannot assume the engagement until written evidence confirms compensation completion.

7. **Heritage preservation duty** — An 1920s building's original architectural character requires preservation during renovation, even if modern updates would be technically superior.

8. **Credit claim proportionality** — An architect performs 40% renovation work on an existing structure. Public marketing can describe the architect's 40% contribution but cannot claim primary or equal credit with the original designer.

### Misconceptions

Many architects misunderstand the scope of non-interference. The obligations apply to any prior design involvement, not only to formal, current engagements. An architect cannot pursue a project simply because the original designer lacks active employment if that designer's prior work substantially determines the structure's character.

Second, some assume notification alone satisfies the requirement. Code Section 5-e demands three sequential confirmations: written notice to the original architect, conclusive determination of employment termination, and verification of compensation completion. Notification is the first step, not the entire obligation.

Third, architects sometimes believe modification work justifies claiming primary credit. The Code requires proportional credit assignment. Substantial renovation work may justify featured marketing; routine maintenance cannot. The percentage of original design maintained determines the credit limitation severity.

### Practical Application

Architects encountering potential non-interference situations should immediately identify the prior designer through existing documents, project records, or client information. Written notification should clearly describe the contemplated work and request confirmation of employment status. Documentation of termination and compensation settlement protects both parties.

For renovations of significant buildings, architects should research original design credit through professional registries and historical records. Incorporating historical preservation principles strengthens professional standing while honoring architectural heritage.

These obligations ultimately protect the profession's reputation and individual practitioners' security. Architects practicing ethically within these boundaries build professional standing on competence and integrity rather than improper competitive tactics.
