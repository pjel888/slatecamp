## Board Exam Quick Check

For sealant and caulking questions, identify the joint movement, joint width, substrate condition, backing material, and whether the joint is expected to resist water or air passage.

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal

Classify sealants and caulking materials by movement capability, adhesion, backing, and correct joint design.

## Core Concept

BCI states that an effective joint sealant must be durable, resilient, and have both cohesive and adhesive strength. Sealants are classified by the extension and compression they can withstand before failure. Low-range sealants are often called caulking and suit small joints where little movement is expected. Medium-range sealants suit nonworking mechanically fastened joints. High-range sealants, including polysulfides, polyurethanes, and silicones, suit working joints with significant movement.

Joint geometry controls performance. BCI gives a depth rule: for joints up to 1/2 inch, sealant depth may equal joint width; for joints 1/2 inch and wider, depth should be half of joint width but not more than 2 inches. The joint should be tooled to ensure contact and adhesion with the substrate. The substrate should be clean, dry, and compatible, and a primer may be needed.

Salvan reinforces the movement requirement: caulking compounds must adhere, remain workable across a temperature range, form a tough elastic skin, stretch with joint-width changes, recover after extension or compression, resist water sensitivity, and provide service performance. Backup materials are important because they control sealant depth and serve as bond breakers so the sealant can elongate.

## How It's Tested

Common items ask for the purpose of backer rod, the correct depth relationship for wider joints, or which sealant family is safer for a working joint. The best answer usually protects two-sided adhesion, movement, and depth control instead of simply filling the joint solid.

## Common Mistakes

- Filling a deep joint without backup material, creating poor movement behavior.
- Treating low-range caulking as suitable for high-movement joints.
- Applying sealant to dirty, wet, or incompatible substrates.
- Memorizing unverified class labels when the checked sources support movement ranges and joint geometry instead.
