# Ventilation standards in the Philippines

Ventilation questions in the LEA should start with the local building-utilities source family. The checked local sources for this article are the DOE Energy Conserving Design Guidelines and George Salvan's Architectural Utilities 2. They support a practical distinction between natural ventilation, mechanical ventilation, exhaust ventilation, and fresh-air supply.

## Exam Anchor

First identify what the item is asking you to size or classify. A natural-ventilation question is about openings, air movement, wind effect, and thermal effect. A mechanical-ventilation question is about supplied or exhausted air by equipment. A fresh-air table question is different from a kitchen hood exhaust question, and a car-park or toilet exhaust question is different from an office outdoor-air question.

## What The Checked Sources Support

The DOE guidelines describe the Philippine context as tropical, warm, and humid. For non-air-conditioned buildings, comfort depends strongly on ventilation, air movement, and heat gain from ceilings and walls. Ventilation also removes indoor heat gain, so it is both a comfort issue and an energy issue.

The DOE guidelines give a general practical ventilation rate of 2.8 to 5.7 m3/min per person where body heat is the main heat source and the average indoor temperature rise is limited. Where power-driven equipment or other heat sources are present, a higher ventilation rate is needed.

For cross ventilation, the DOE guidelines relate ventilation rate to design wind speed, opening effectiveness, and opening area. This is the safe exam concept: cross ventilation is not just "having windows"; it depends on air movement through openings and the effectiveness of those openings.

Where natural lighting and ventilation requirements cannot be met, the DOE guidelines state that building regulations may allow mechanical ventilation as a substitute. The fresh-air supply for mechanical ventilation is then taken from the specified table. Table 6.8 lists minimum fresh-air supply values such as 6 air changes per hour for offices, restaurants, shops, supermarkets, and department stores; 8 air changes per hour for classrooms, theaters, and cinemas; 10 air changes per hour for toilets and bathrooms; 20 air changes per hour for commercial, institutional, and industrial kitchens; and 6 air changes per hour for car parks.

The same DOE section cautions that, unless exceptional circumstances justify it, the ventilation rate should not exceed the listed values by more than 30 percent. The exam trap is to assume that "more air is always better." In a source-safe answer, the table value and the stated limit both matter.

Salvan defines ventilation as supplying or removing air by natural or mechanical means. This supports the broad classification but does not replace the DOE tables for fresh-air and hood-exhaust values.

## Safe Answer Workflow

1. Classify the space: office, restaurant, classroom, toilet, kitchen, car park, or another listed occupancy.
2. Decide whether the question is about natural ventilation, mechanical ventilation, or exhaust ventilation.
3. For mechanical fresh-air supply, use the DOE fresh-air table rather than a generic comfort rule.
4. For cross ventilation, look for wind speed, opening area, and effectiveness of opening.
5. For heat-source spaces, remember that equipment and process heat can require a higher ventilation rate than body heat alone.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating a window-area question as if it were a mechanical fresh-air table question.
- Applying kitchen exhaust rates to offices, classrooms, or toilets.
- Assuming that increasing ventilation beyond the table value is automatically correct.
- Importing derivative ASHRAE or product figures when the local DOE table already controls the exam task.
- Confusing recirculated air with outdoor fresh-air supply.

## How It Is Tested

LEA-style questions usually ask which table applies, whether mechanical ventilation can substitute when natural ventilation is not adequate, what a cross-ventilation equation depends on, or which occupancy has the higher fresh-air or exhaust requirement. The safe answer is the option that follows the checked DOE and Salvan source families, not the option with the most familiar generic HVAC number.
