## Board Exam Quick Check

For schedule questions, identify whether the item asks what a schedule is, who is responsible for it, or how it coordinates with drawings and specifications. The source-safe answer is anchored in architectural documents, contract documents, and signed/sealed responsibility.

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

RA 9266 IRR identifies schedules as part of architectural documents. It lists architectural floor plans, elevations, sections, ceiling plans, schedules, detailed drawings, technical specifications, and cost estimates among architectural outputs that only an architect can sign and seal when prepared within the architect's scope.

RA 9266 IRR also defines contract documents as including drawings, specifications, and other bid documents. It requires architectural plans, drawings, specifications, and contract documents prepared by or under the architect's direct supervision to bear the architect's seal, and it makes the architect-of-record responsible for architectural documents issued under the seal or authorized signature.

PD 1096 RIRR supports the documentation role of schedules and callouts. Architectural plans must show room designations, door and window call-outs, specification callouts, elevation references, drawing references, door and window schedules, and finishing schedules. Permit and completion procedures also refer to plans, specifications, and related contract documents.

## Learning Goal

- Recognize schedules as architectural documents that organize repeated drawing/specification information.
- Connect schedules with RA 9266 signing, sealing, and architect-of-record responsibility.
- Use PD 1096 plan requirements to explain why room, door, window, finish, and specification callouts must be coordinated.

## Core Concept

A schedule is a coordination table inside the architectural-document set. It does not replace drawings or specifications. It connects them.

| Schedule function | Source-safe explanation |
| --- | --- |
| Organize repeated items | Door, window, finish, room, and similar data can be tabulated instead of repeated in crowded drawings. |
| Coordinate drawings and specs | Marks, room designations, callouts, and specification references should point to the same requirement. |
| Support contract documents | Schedules are part of the documentation package that supports bidding, permitting, and construction. |
| Carry professional responsibility | When prepared under the architect's direct supervision, signing and sealing rules apply. |

UAP SPP 203 also identifies Contract Documentation and Review as a specialized architectural service connected with specifications writing, estimation and quantity survey, production, software, support services, and contract-document review.

## Key Facts and Testable Items

| Exam cue | Correct source-safe response |
| --- | --- |
| Door/window/finish schedule | Treat it as an architectural-document coordination tool. |
| Specification callout | Coordinate it with drawings and the schedule. |
| Contract document | Drawings and specifications are part of the contract-document set; schedules support that set. |
| Signing and sealing | Architect-of-record responsibility applies to architectural documents issued under seal. |
| Missing schedule | Expect coordination risk, field ambiguity, or incomplete documentation. |

## Real World Scenario

A project architect reviews a permit set for a small clinic. The floor plan uses door marks, room numbers, finish tags, and specification callouts, but one door mark and one finish tag are missing from the schedules. The source-safe correction is to coordinate the drawings, schedules, and specifications before issuance, then keep the architect-of-record responsibility tied to the corrected architectural documents. Do not add product dimensions, fire ratings, or hardware standards unless a checked source in the item supports them.

## How It's Tested on the LEA

Expect questions asking why schedules are used. Choose the answer that emphasizes coordination, clarity, completeness, and professional documentation. Avoid answers that reduce schedules to cost tracking, installation progress tracking, or purely visual presentation.

Expect responsibility questions. If the schedule is an architectural document prepared under direct supervision, do not shift the signing/sealing responsibility away from the architect-of-record.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating a schedule as optional decoration rather than a coordination document.
- Separating schedule marks from plans, elevations, and specifications.
- Using schedules as a substitute for specifications instead of a bridge to them.
- Forgetting RA 9266 signing, sealing, and ownership provisions for architectural documents.

## Key Terms

**Architectural Schedule** - A tabular architectural document that organizes repeated project information such as doors, windows, rooms, finishes, or similar items.

**Specification Callout** - A note or reference that connects the drawing or schedule to a technical requirement.

**Contract Documents** - Agreement attachments that generally include drawings, specifications, and other bid documents.

**Architect-of-Record Responsibility** - Responsibility for architectural documents issued under the architect's seal or authorized signature.
