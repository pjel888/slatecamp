# Anthropometrics

## Source-Audited Scope

Use this topic as a human-factors design topic. The checked local sources support anthropometrics as body measurement, ergonomics as design for the human body, and proxemics/kinesthetics as related spatial requirements. This article does not create code dimensions.

## Board Exam Quick Check

When a question asks about anthropometrics, choose the answer tied to human body measurement and dimensioning for comfort, reach, movement, and use. Do not confuse anthropometrics with proxemics, which concerns spatial separation and interpersonal distance.

## Core Concepts

- Anthropometrics means body measurement.
- Ergonomics is design of devices, spaces, and details for the human body.
- TOA sources connect anthropometric, proxemic, and kinesthetic requirements to architectural design and human comfort.
- Salvan notes that scale relates architectural motifs to each other and to the human figure.
- Human physical needs are more directly measurable than social and psychological needs, so dimensions, reach, furnishings, and movement clearances require careful measurement.

## LEA Use

Anthropometric questions usually test distinctions. The correct answer names body dimensions, reach, scale, comfort, and measurable human fit. A proxemics answer focuses on personal space and social distance instead.

## Active Recall

1. What is anthropometrics? Body measurement.
2. What is ergonomics? Design for the human body.
3. What is the exam-safe distinction? Anthropometrics measures body fit; proxemics studies interpersonal spacing.
