# Penthouses and Roof Structures

## Hook Scenario

The developer calls you at 7 PM, excited: they want to convert the mechanical penthouse of their newly completed 24-storey BGC tower into a rooftop bar and event venue. The penthouse is 35% of the roof area and stands 5.40 meters above the roof deck. You recognize three immediate problems before the conversation is over.

First, the penthouse area at 35% already exceeds the **1/3 (33.3%) aggregate area limit** of Sec. 1210.b — it should have been caught during design. Second, the 5.40-meter height is above the **3.60-meter limit** for non-elevator/non-tank penthouses under Sec. 1210.a, though 8.40 meters is allowed for elevator and tank enclosures. Third — and most critically — converting the penthouse to a bar and event venue violates Sec. 1210.c: penthouses are prohibited from use for any purpose other than sheltering mechanical equipment or shaft openings. The moment the penthouse is used for habitable purposes, it is **reclassified as an additional storey** — restarting every building height, fire-rating, and egress calculation for the entire building.

This article is the definitive guide to penthouses, skylights, and the architectural elements that straddle the boundary between roof and habitable space — where one wrong design decision cascades through BHL calculations, GFA exclusions, and occupancy classifications.

## Core Concepts

### Concept 1: Penthouse Height Limits — Two Tiers (Sec. 1210.a)

PD 1096 establishes two distinct height limits for penthouses measured **above the roof level**:

**Tier 1 — Elevator Machine Rooms and Water Tanks (Non-Type V Construction):**
- Maximum height: **8.40 m** above the roof.
- Applies to: tank enclosures (water storage, fire reserve tanks) and elevator machine rooms, on buildings that are not Type V construction.
- rationale: elevator overrun clearance and tank capacities require greater headroom that cannot be accommodated within the 3.60 m general limit.

**Tier 2 — All Other Penthouses:**
- Maximum height: **3.60 m** above the roof.
- Applies to: stairway enclosures, mechanical equipment rooms (other than elevator machinery), generator rooms, cooling tower enclosures.
- rationale: standard mechanical headroom is achievable within 3.60 m; exceeding this implies the structure is being used as a habitable space (triggering the "additional storey" conversion).

Board exam trap: when a question asks about penthouse height "in all cases," there is no single answer — always identify whether it is for elevator/tank or general use.

### Concept 2: Aggregate Area Limit — 1/3 of the Roof (Sec. 1210.b)

The total aggregate footprint of **all** penthouses and roof structures combined must not exceed **1/3 of the area of the supporting roof below**. This is a collective limit — not per-structure.

- If a roof is 900 sq.m, the maximum total penthouse footprint is **300 sq.m**.
- Multiple smaller penthouses (elevator room + staircase enclosure + tank room) all count toward the 1/3 aggregate.
- This limit prevents penthouses from collectively becoming an additional floor under a nominally different name.

**BHL Interaction**: Penthouses that comply with Sec. 1210 requirements (correct height, correct area, correct use) are **excluded from Building Height Limit (BHL) calculations** under NBCP Rule VIII. They sit above the regulated building height as permitted roof projections. However, if a penthouse violates the prohibited-use rule (Sec. 1210.c), it is reclassified as an additional storey and **counted toward BHL** — potentially making the building non-compliant with the zoning height restriction.

### Concept 3: Prohibited Uses — The Additional Storey Trigger (Sec. 1210.c)

This is the most legally consequential provision in Sec. 1210. Penthouses may be used **only** for:
1. Shelter of mechanical equipment (HVAC, elevators, generators, water tanks).
2. Enclosure of vertical shaft openings (stairwells, elevator shafts).

Prohibited uses include:
- Residential occupancy (apartments, staff quarters).
- Commercial use (restaurants, offices, event venues).
- Any habitable space.

**Consequence of Prohibited Use**: The penthouse is legally treated as an **additional storey** of the building. This has cascading effects:
- Building height recalculation for BHL compliance.
- Additional storey count for fire-rating purposes (Type of Construction adequacy).
- Additional egress requirements (minimum stairwells, travel distances).
- Re-computation of **Gross Floor Area (GFA)** — the converted penthouse floor area is added to the GFA, affecting Floor Area Ratio (FAR) compliance.

**GFA Interaction for Balconies**: In contrast, **balconies** are excluded from GFA calculations when the balcony depth does not exceed **1.50 m** (per NBCP IRR and most zoning ordinances) or where the total balcony area per floor does not exceed a threshold (typically interpreted as balconies ≤ 10 sq.m aggregate per floor per unit are excluded). Balconies exceeding these thresholds are included in GFA, reducing the building's development capacity.

### Concept 4: Skylight Glass Requirements (Sec. 1208)

Skylights present a serious public safety risk — falling glass from overhead skylights can be lethal. Sec. 1208 establishes a hierarchy of glass types and support requirements:

**General Rule — Wired Glass Required:**
All skylights shall use **wired glass** except in specific exempted conditions (Sec. 1208).

**Flat Wired Glass:**
- Maximum span between supports: **625 mm**.
- Use: standard skylight panels over occupied spaces.

**Corrugated Wired Glass:**
- Maximum span between supports: **1.50 m**.
- The corrugation provides additional structural stiffness, justifying the longer span.

**Exemptions from Wired Glass:**
1. **Skylights over vertical shafts** (2+ storeys): plain (non-wired) glass is permitted if the shaft is ventilated with a net free area of at least **1/8 of the shaft's cross-sectional area** (minimum ventilation opening: **1.20 m**).
2. **Non-wired glass with protective screens above and below**: wire screens with wire diameter ≥ 2.5 mm and mesh openings ≤ 25 mm must be installed on both sides of the glass.
3. **Photographer's skylights**: metal frames with plate glass (no wire netting) are permitted — the controlled studio environment removes the public-safety concern.
4. **Greenhouse skylights**: ordinary glass is permitted when the greenhouse is **≤ 6.00 m above grade** — the low height limits fall-impact risk.
5. **Glass in floors/sidewalks**: must be supported by metal or reinforced concrete frames; glass minimum **12.5 mm thick**.
6. **Groups A and J buildings**: metal frames are not required for skylight framing (these are typically low-rise residential and miscellaneous structures).

### Concept 5: Towers, Spires, and the Frontage/Base Rules (Sec. 1210.e)

Architectural towers and spires — clock towers, bell towers, decorative campaniles, communication masts, and neon sign structures — are permitted on rooftops subject to strict dimensional controls:

1. **Street frontage limit**: The base width of any tower or spire must not exceed **1/4 of the street frontage** of the building.
2. **Base area limit**: The base footprint of the tower must not exceed **150 sq.m** unless the tower itself conforms to the Type of Construction requirements for the entire building.
3. **Cross-section rule**: If the tower's cross-sectional area **exceeds 10.00 sq.m** at any point above the roof, its **supporting structural frame must extend down to the ground** (not just to the roof level).
4. **Skeleton towers** (radio masts, neon sign frames): permitted on rooftops without the base area restriction when the structure is an open skeleton frame.
5. **Balcony railing height**: Exterior balconies, landings, and porches **more than 750 mm above grade** must have railings of **incombustible materials** (Sec. 1209). The railing height itself must comply with Rule VIII setback provisions and the NSCP (typically minimum 1.05 m for balconies in residential occupancies).

## PH vs. USA Comparative Analysis

| Topic | PH — PD 1096 (Rule XII, Sec. 1208–1210) | USA — IBC (Sec. 1511, Sec. 2405) |
|---|---|---|
| **Penthouse height — elevator/tank** | Max 8.40 m above roof (non-Type V) | IBC Sec. 1511.2: Max 18 ft (5.49 m) above roof for elevator enclosures |
| **Penthouse height — general** | Max 3.60 m above roof | IBC Sec. 1511.2: Max 12 ft (3.66 m) above roof (similar, slight variation) |
| **Aggregate penthouse area** | ≤ 1/3 of supporting roof area | IBC Sec. 1511.2: ≤ 1/3 of supporting roof area (identical) |
| **Prohibited uses** | Cannot be used for occupancy; triggers additional storey | IBC Sec. 1511.1: Penthouses are unoccupied — violation reclassifies as additional story |
| **BHL exclusion** | Excluded from BHL if code-compliant | IBC: Not counted in building height if meets 1511 requirements |
| **Skylight glazing standard** | Wired glass required (flat: 625mm, corrugated: 1.50m spans) | IBC Sec. 2405.3: Sloped glazing — laminated glass, tempered glass, or wired glass with thickness and span tables |
| **Skylight over shaft** | Plain glass allowed with 1/8 cross-section ventilation (min 1.20m) | IBC Sec. 2405.4: Skylights over shafts allowed with specific glazing types and area limitations |
| **Glass in floors** | Metal/RC frame; ≥ 12.5 mm thick glass | IBC Sec. 2409: Floor and sidewalk lights — safety glass ≥ 6.4 mm or approved assemblies |
| **Greenhouse skylight** | Ordinary glass if ≤ 6.00 m above grade | IBC Sec. 2405.5: Greenhouses — plastic or glass permitted; height-based risk assessment |
| **Tower frontage limit** | ≤ 1/4 of street frontage | IBC: No universal frontage fraction; local zoning governs |
| **Tower base area** | ≤ 150 sq.m unless building-type compliant | IBC: No specific base area limit; governed by structural adequacy per ASCE 7 |
| **Cross-section structure rule** | If > 10.00 sq.m cross-section → frame to ground | IBC: Structural continuity required per ASCE 7; no specific cross-section threshold |
| **Balcony railing material** | Incombustible if balcony > 750 mm above grade | IBC Sec. 1015: Guards required ≥ 30 in. (762 mm); material not restricted to incombustible |
| **Balcony GFA exclusion** | Balconies ≤ 10 sq.m typically excluded from GFA | IBC: No GFA concept; governed by local zoning ordinances |

**Key Philosophical Difference**: The Philippine code's penthouse provisions are embedded in a regulatory ecosystem that includes BHL, GFA, and FAR — meaning penthouse design decisions directly affect the building's maximum development capacity under zoning law. The IBC's penthouse provisions (Sec. 1511) are standalone construction requirements focused on structural and fire safety, with zoning implications handled separately by local ordinances. Philippine architects must simultaneously check Sec. 1210 against the zoning resolution's BHL and FAR tables, something the IBC framework does not require in a single integrated review.

## Cross-Referencing

### BHL (Building Height Limit) Calculations — Rule VIII and Zoning Ordinances
Penthouse compliance with Sec. 1210 is prerequisite to the standard BHL exclusion recognized in Rule VIII and local zoning ordinances. A code-compliant penthouse sits **above** the BHL without being counted in the regulated height — it is treated as a permitted roof projection, similar to parapets, stair bulkheads, and mechanical equipment screens.

When a penthouse exceeds the height (8.40m or 3.60m), exceeds 1/3 of roof area, or is used for habitable purposes, it **loses the BHL exclusion**. The entire height of that penthouse is then added to the measured building height. In zones with tight height restrictions (e.g., height limit of 15m near airports or in heritage zones), a non-compliant penthouse can push a building above its permitted BHL — triggering building permit revocation proceedings.

### GFA Exclusions — Floor Area Ratio (FAR) Compliance
The GFA of penthouses complying with Sec. 1210 is typically excluded from the development's Total GFA for FAR computation. Once a penthouse is reclassified as an additional storey, its floor area is added to the GFA — which may cause the development to exceed the permissible FAR, requiring either redesign of the penthouse or reduction of another floor's area to compensate.

Similarly, balcony areas receive GFA exclusion treatment under most Philippine zoning ordinances — but this is conditional on balcony depth and aggregate area. Balconies with a projection exceeding the permitted exclusion threshold are included in GFA, directly affecting FAR compliance and potentially the number of sleaable or rentable units the building can contain.

### Rule VI — Type of Construction and Penthouse Wall Requirements
Sec. 1210.d ties penthouse construction requirements to the building's Type of Construction. For **Type III and Type IV** buildings: if the exterior walls or roof of the penthouse are at least 1.50 m from the property line, those penthouse elements need only be **1-hour fire-resistive and incombustible**. Walls ≤ 1.50 m from the exterior wall of a Type IV building must also meet the 1-hour fire-resistive incombustible standard. This is a proximity-based fire protection rule — penthouses close to property lines face higher spread-of-fire risk and therefore require fire-resistive construction.

### NSCP — Structural Requirements for Towers and Spires
The cross-section rule (>10 sq.m cross-section = frame to ground) in Sec. 1210.e is a structural stability requirement. Large towers generate substantial overturning moments from wind and seismic forces — these moments must be transferred through a continuous structural frame to the foundation, not just to the roof level. The NSCP governs the actual structural design of these frames, including seismic base shear distribution and foundation design for the additional lateral loads introduced by the tower.

## Active Learning Techniques

### Memory Aid — "8.4 and 3.6: Tanks Float High, Others Stay Low"
Penthouse height limits:
- **8.40 m** for elevator machinery and water **tanks** (they need more room to float their equipment higher).
- **3.60 m** for all other uses (standard mechanical headroom — "keep it low or it becomes a floor").

Associate 8.40 with the number 84 (8 stories × approx. 10.5 ft = a tower), and 3.60 with a single residential floor height — "if you can fit a bedroom in it, it's a storey."

### Memory Aid — "1/3 Roof, 1/4 Frontage, 1/8 Shaft" (The Fraction Rule)
Three critical fractions in this module:
- **1/3** of roof area → maximum aggregate penthouse footprint.
- **1/4** of street frontage → maximum tower/spire base width.
- **1/8** of shaft cross-section → minimum ventilation area for plain glass over shaft skylights (min 1.20m).

Say: *"One-third of the roof, one-quarter of the street, one-eighth of the shaft."*

### Memory Aid — "625 Flat, 1500 Corrugated" (Skylight Spans)
Flat wired glass: **625 mm** max span (about the width of a standard bathroom tile spacing).
Corrugated wired glass: **1,500 mm** (= **1.50 m**) max span.
The corrugation gives it 2.4× the flat glass span — the ribbing converts a weak flat plate into a structural shell.

### Memory Aid — "10 Square Meters: The Two Triggers"
The number **10.00 sq.m** appears twice in these sections — both in Article 20 and here:
- Article 20 (Sec. 1204): Max **single opening** in a 3-hour separation wall = **10.00 sq.m**.
- Article 21 (Sec. 1210.e): Tower cross-section **> 10.00 sq.m** → structural frame must extend to ground.

Both deal with a threshold of 10 sq.m — but in opposite contexts (opening size limit vs. structural trigger). Know which article each belongs to.

### If/Then Scenarios

**If** an architect places the elevator machine room penthouse at 9.00 m above the roof deck on a Type III building…
**Then** this violates Sec. 1210.a — the maximum for elevator/tank penthouses is **8.40 m**. The excess 0.60 m causes the penthouse to be treated as an additional storey.

**If** a developer wants to use a code-compliant penthouse as a co-working space for building tenants…
**Then** this violates Sec. 1210.c — penthouses may only shelter mechanical equipment or shaft openings. Converting it to co-working space triggers reclassification as an additional storey with cascading BHL, GFA, FAR, fire-rating, and egress consequences.

**If** a skylight uses corrugated wired glass with support mullions spaced 1.80 m apart…
**Then** this violates Sec. 1208.b — corrugated wired glass may span a maximum of **1.50 m** between supports. The 1.80 m span exceeds the maximum by 300 mm and requires either intermediate supports or a different glazing type.

**If** a rooftop tower has a cross-section of 12.00 sq.m measured at mid-height above the roof…
**Then** the supporting structural frame for that tower must extend all the way down to the **ground level** per Sec. 1210.e — the cross-section exceeds the 10.00 sq.m threshold that triggers the ground-frame requirement.

## Exam Traps

### Trap 1: The Two Penthouse Heights Are Not Interchangeable
A board question will describe a penthouse used as a generator room at 5.00 m above the roof and ask if it is code-compliant. Many candidates will recall the 8.40 m figure and answer "yes." The correct analysis: **8.40 m applies only to elevator machine rooms and tank enclosures** in non-Type V construction. A generator room is "other mechanical equipment" — it falls under the **3.60 m** limit. A generator room penthouse at 5.00 m above the roof violates Sec. 1210.a and is reclassified as an additional storey. Always identify the specific use before selecting the applicable height limit.

### Trap 2: The 1/3 Area Limit Is Aggregate, Not Per Structure
The exam may describe a building with three separate penthouse structures — an elevator room (80 sq.m), a water tank enclosure (60 sq.m), and a staircase bulkhead (70 sq.m) — on a roof of 600 sq.m, and ask if the largest single structure exceeds the limit. No individual structure exceeds 100 sq.m (which would be 1/6 of the roof), so each looks fine individually. But the **aggregate** is 210 sq.m, which equals **35% of 600 sq.m** — exceeding the 1/3 (200 sq.m) limit. Always sum all penthouse and roof structures for the aggregate check.

### Trap 3: Balcony Railings Must Be Incombustible Only Above 750 mm Grade — Not Below
Sec. 1209 requires incombustible railing materials for balconies, landings, and porches more than **750 mm above grade**. The exam may present a ground-floor patio with a 500 mm raised deck and ask what railing material is required — the answer is that Sec. 1209's incombustible requirement does not apply (the deck is below the 750 mm threshold), and any material permitted by the Type of Construction may be used. Do not apply the incombustible requirement to all railings universally — check the elevation first.

## Active Recall Questions

**Question 1**

A 20-storey condominium tower in Pasig City has a rooftop penthouse housing the elevator machine room. The supporting roof area measures 800 sq.m. The developer proposes a penthouse with a footprint of 220 sq.m and a height of 7.50 m above the roof deck. Evaluate the compliance of these dimensions under PD 1096, Rule XII, Sec. 1210.

A. Both height and area are compliant — elevator rooms may rise 8.40 m and 220 sq.m is within 1/3 of 800 sq.m  
B. Height is compliant but area is non-compliant — 220 sq.m exceeds 1/3 of 800 sq.m (266.67 sq.m maximum)  
C. Area is compliant but height is non-compliant — 7.50 m exceeds the 3.60 m general limit  
D. Both are non-compliant — elevator rooms are limited to 3.60 m height and 1/4 of roof area  

> **Answer: A — Both dimensions are compliant**
>
> Under Sec. 1210.a, elevator machine room penthouses (in non-Type V construction) may rise up to **8.40 m** above the roof deck — the proposed 7.50 m is within this limit. Under Sec. 1210.b, aggregate penthouse area must not exceed **1/3 of the supporting roof area**: 1/3 × 800 sq.m = 266.67 sq.m. The proposed 220 sq.m is below this ceiling. Both dimensions comply. Option B misapplies the arithmetic — 220 sq.m < 266.67 sq.m, so area is compliant. Option C applies the 3.60 m limit incorrectly to an elevator room — 3.60 m applies only to "all other" penthouses, not elevator or tank enclosures. Option D is entirely incorrect on both limits.

**Question 2**

A heritage conservation architect is designing a skylight over a 3-storey library atrium in Intramuros. The proposed skylight uses flat wired glass panels. A structural engineer asks what the maximum allowable span between support mullions is per PD 1096. Concurrently, a corrugated wired glass alternative is proposed for wider bays. What are the correct maximum spans for both glass types?

A. Flat wired glass: 1.00 m; corrugated wired glass: 2.00 m  
B. Flat wired glass: 625 mm; corrugated wired glass: 1.50 m  
C. Flat wired glass: 500 mm; corrugated wired glass: 1.50 m  
D. Flat wired glass: 625 mm; corrugated wired glass: 1.20 m  

> **Answer: B — Flat: 625 mm; Corrugated: 1.50 m**
>
> Per Sec. 1208.b, flat wired glass skylights may span a maximum of **625 mm** between supports. Corrugated wired glass, benefiting from its structural ribbing, is permitted to span up to **1.50 m** between supports. These are hard maximum spans — exceeding them requires either additional support members or an alternative glazing system. Option A doubles both values incorrectly. Option C uses 500 mm for flat glass — below the actual 625 mm limit (not wrong from a safety standpoint but an incorrect code citation). Option D correctly identifies the flat glass span but reduces the corrugated span to 1.20 m, which is not the code figure.

**Question 3**

A property developer converts the mechanical penthouse of a completed 15-storey office building in Makati CBD into a private dining club. The penthouse occupies 28% of the roof area and rises 3.20 m above the roof deck. The zoning ordinance limits the building to 15 storeys and a Floor Area Ratio of 12.0. Which of the following best describes the primary regulatory consequence of this conversion under PD 1096?

A. The conversion is permitted provided the penthouse has proper fire exits, since 28% is within the 1/3 area limit and 3.20 m is within the 3.60 m height limit  
B. The conversion is non-compliant because 28% exceeds the 25% maximum penthouse coverage allowed under Sec. 1210  
C. The conversion causes the penthouse to be reclassified as an additional storey, potentially violating the 15-storey zoning limit and increasing the building's GFA toward the FAR ceiling  
D. The conversion is permitted as a change of use under Rule VII, Sec. 702, subject only to Building Official approval and issuance of a Certificate of Use  

> **Answer: C — Reclassification as an additional storey, triggering BHL and GFA/FAR violations**
>
> Per Sec. 1210.c, a penthouse may only be used to shelter mechanical equipment or vertical shaft openings. Converting it to a private dining club is a habitable use — expressly prohibited. The consequence is not merely a use violation: the penthouse is legally **treated as an additional storey**. This adds a 16th storey to a building permitted for only 15 storeys (violating the BHL zoning limit) and adds the penthouse floor area to the GFA computation (potentially pushing the building above the FAR = 12.0 ceiling). Option A is incorrect because the metric compliance (28% area, 3.20 m height) does not override the prohibited-use trigger — both conditions must be met. Option B invents a "25% maximum penthouse coverage" that does not exist in the Code (the limit is 1/3 = 33.3%). Option D is wrong because Rule VII governs change of occupancy for existing floors within the building — it cannot authorize the prohibited use of a penthouse, which remains governed by Sec. 1210.c regardless of any Certificate of Use.
