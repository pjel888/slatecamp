## Board Exam Quick Check

For roof-truss questions, identify whether the item asks about truss anatomy, load path, purlin location, truss configuration, connector behavior, or bracing. Do not answer from unchecked span tables or wind-speed rules.

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal

Explain how roof trusses use triangular geometry, chords, web members, panel points, purlins, connectors, and bracing to carry roof loads in a coordinated assembly.

## Core Concept

Building Construction Illustrated defines a truss as a structural frame based on the geometric rigidity of the triangle, with linear members subject primarily to axial tension or compression. The top and bottom chords are the principal members. Web members connect the chords. A panel point is a joint between a principal web member and a chord.

The load-path rule is the central exam rule: a truss should be loaded at panel points. If loads are applied away from panel points, secondary stresses can develop. BCI makes this concrete for roof framing: if a purlin does not bear at a panel point, it subjects the top chord to local bending.

### Steel Trusses

Steel trusses are generally fabricated by welding or bolting structural angles and tees together to form a triangulated framework. Slender truss members usually require gusset plates. Heavier steel trusses may use wide-flange shapes or structural tubing.

### Truss Types

BCI identifies common truss configurations by their web behavior:

| Type | Source-safe cue |
|---|---|
| Warren | Inclined web members forming a series of equilateral triangles; verticals may shorten compression panels. |
| Belgian | Only inclined web members. |
| Fink | Belgian truss with subdiagonals that reduce compression web lengths toward the centerline. |
| Pratt | Vertical web members in compression and diagonal web members in tension. |
| Howe | Vertical web members in tension and diagonal web members in compression. |

### Wood Trusses and Trussed Rafters

Heavier wood trusses may be assembled from layered members joined at panel points with split-ring connectors. Loads should be applied at panel points. Vertical sway bracing, horizontal cross-bracing, and lateral bracing may be required depending on the system.

Wood trussed rafters are pre-engineered and shop-fabricated. BCI directs the reader to consult the truss manufacturer for configurations, allowable spans and loads, and construction details. That is the source-safe answer whenever a question tries to force a universal residential span or connection-capacity value not present in the source.

## Real World Scenario

A contractor proposes shifting purlins to avoid a duct run in a small auditorium roof. The safe review response is to check whether the purlins still bear at panel points. If not, the top chord will receive local bending in addition to truss action, and the detail must go back to the truss designer or manufacturer. The issue is not just the truss name; it is where the load enters the triangular system and how the truss is braced after erection.

## How It's Tested

LEA-style questions usually test truss vocabulary, panel-point loading, purlin placement, Pratt-versus-Howe web behavior, Fink subdiagonals, or the need for bracing and manufacturer/engineer guidance.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating a truss as a simple beam and ignoring panel points.
- Memorizing unchecked span ranges for named trusses.
- Missing local bending when purlins bear between panel points.
- Confusing Pratt and Howe tension/compression behavior.
- Omitting lateral bracing during installation and after erection.
