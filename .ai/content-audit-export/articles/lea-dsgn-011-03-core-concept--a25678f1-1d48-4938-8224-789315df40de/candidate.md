## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal
Read and produce site plans showing property boundaries, setback compliance, building footprint, access routes, utilities, and landscaping at appropriate scales, and verify site plan compliance with zoning regulations and CLUP requirements.

Site plans are scaled drawings that communicate the spatial relationship between a building and its context—showing property boundaries, setback compliance, access, utilities, and landscape. The LEA expects you to read and produce site plans that demonstrate understanding of zoning setback regulations and site organization principles.


## Core Concept

A **site plan** is an orthographic (top-down) view of a property showing the building footprint, adjacent landscape, utilities, and context features at a defined scale. It is the primary communication tool for demonstrating compliance with zoning and development regulations.

**Essential site plan elements:**

1. **Property lines** are shown as a heavy solid perimeter line. The dimensions of the property are noted (length, width, or boundary coordinates). This is the legal boundary within which the building must fit.

2. **Setback lines** are offset lines parallel to property boundaries, indicating the minimum distance a building must be set back from the edge. These ensure light, air, access to adjoining properties, and emergency egress. Setbacks are mandated by zoning code (PD 1096 in the Philippines).

3. **Building footprint** is the outline of the building's base, shown in a bold solid line or filled with a light tint. All stories have the same footprint unless there are stepped or cantilevered sections (indicated separately).

4. **Driveway and parking layout** shows the number of parking stalls, access aisles, accessible parking spaces, and their relationship to building entries. Parking stalls are typically 2.5 m wide × 5.0 m long per standard (may vary by jurisdiction).

5. **Utilities** include water supply connections, sewer/septic lines, electrical services, and gas lines (if applicable). These are shown as dashed or colored lines with labels. Locations of utilities must be verified with local utility authorities.

6. **Landscape features** include trees, shrubs, planters, hardscape, and paving patterns. These communicate spatial character and stormwater management intent.

7. **North arrow** is always included and prominently placed, usually in the upper right corner. This allows readers to understand orientation and interpret facade names (North Elevation, South-facing facade).

8. **Scale** is shown graphically (a drawn scale bar) and numerically (e.g., 1:200, 1:500). The choice of scale depends on the size of the site and level of detail required.

**Scale conventions for site plans:**
- **1:500 or 1:1000**: For large sites (> 1 hectare) or master plans; shows overall layout.
- **1:200 or 1:250**: For typical urban lots (0.5–1 hectare); shows building, parking, and immediate context.
- **1:100**: For small infill sites; shows detailed parking layout and access.

Site plans communicate not only building placement but also the designer's understanding of site constraints (slope, utilities, access), regulatory compliance (setbacks), and design intent (landscape, circulation, parking strategy).

- Show the building and immediate context (adjacent buildings, streets, utilities) clearly.

- For exam drawings, 1:200 or 1:250 is typical for a standard urban lot.

## Philippine Practice Context

Under PD 1096 (National Building Code zoning provisions), setbacks vary by building height—a taller building must be set back farther from property lines to reduce shadow and wind impacts. In the Philippines, site plans must also account for prevailing wind direction (northeast in winter, southwest in summer) when placing buildings and planning ventilation. Utilities in the Philippines are often surface-mounted (electrical poles, water lines) and must be coordinated with setback and parking layouts. Sloping sites are common in upland areas; site plans must show contour lines and indicate how the building accommodates the grade change (through sections referenced from the site plan).

## How It's Tested

**Pattern 1: Setback compliance verification**
A site plan shows a building 4.0 m from the front property line on a 9 m tall building. The candidate must verify whether this complies with PD 1096 (it does not—6.0 m required for 9–12 m height range) and state the required distance.

**Pattern 2: Parking layout and access**
A site plan shows 6 parking stalls with a single-direction aisle of width 3.0 m. The candidate must identify that aisles require minimum 3.5 m for one-way traffic and flag this as non-compliant.

**Pattern 3: Site planning and context coordination**
A site plan shows a building on a corner lot with utilities, adjacent buildings, access points, and landscape features. The candidate must assess whether the layout creates safe pedestrian access, complies with setbacks, and integrates utilities logically.

<Tip>
**Memory aid:** "PD 1096 Setback Rule" — For buildings up to 6–9 m, front=4.5 m, sides=2.0 m, rear=2.0 m. For taller buildings (9–15 m), front and rear increase; sides gradually increase. Always check building height first to determine the correct setback row in the table.
</Tip>


**Exam Tip:** Site plans must show property lines with dimensions, setbacks (front, side, rear), building footprint with dimensions, access driveways, parking layout, and utility connection points. Scale typically 1:200 or 1:500.
