# Site Plans

## Board Exam Quick Check

A site-plan question is usually asking what must be shown, measured, or checked before approval. Read the stem for the lot boundary, building position, access, parking, utilities, accessibility, and setback condition.

## What You Need to Know for the LEA

The checked PD 1096 source requires a site development plan to show the technical description, boundaries, orientation, and position of the proposed building or structure in relation to the lot. It must also show existing or proposed access roads and driveways, existing public utilities or services, existing buildings within and adjoining the lot, and distances between proposed and existing buildings.

For drawing coordination, floor plans are drawn to scale and accessibility plans identify the specific locations and detailed design of accessibility facilities, including parking areas and outside building approaches.

Setbacks are the yard widths between the outermost building face and property lines. The checked PD 1096 table gives these residential minimums for newly developed subdivisions:

| Residential use | Front | Side | Rear |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| R-1 | 4.50 m | 2.00 m | 2.00 m |
| Basic R-2 | 3.00 m | 2.00 m | 2.00 m |
| R-5 | 6.00 m | 3.00 m | 3.00 m |

For commercial, industrial, institutional, and recreational buildings, the checked table ties setbacks to road right-of-way width, with a front setback of 5.00 m to 8.00 m depending on RROW width and side/rear setbacks from 2.00 m to 5.00 m.

Parking must be drawn to scale and counted. The checked PD 1096 source states that an average perpendicular or diagonal automobile parking slot is 2.50 m x 5.00 m, while parallel parking is 2.15 m x 6.00 m. For accessible parking in the checked accessibility appendix, the accessible slot minimum width is 3.70 m and a 1.20 m clear walkway is provided between front ends of parked cars.

A north arrow is required for orientation, but source-safe drafting practice is to make it clear and unambiguous; the law source does not require it to be in a single fixed corner or to point toward the top of the sheet.

## How It Is Tested

- Identify whether the drawing shows boundaries, orientation, building position, access, driveways, utilities, adjoining buildings, and distances.
- Check the correct setback row before judging compliance.
- Use the parking dimensions stated in the code source, not a guessed local convention.
- Treat utility locations as coordination data that must be verified, not decoration.
- Use scale math carefully: at 1:500, 1 mm on paper represents 0.5 m in actual distance.

## Common Mistakes

- Applying a commercial setback table to a residential lot.
- Confusing an accessible parking slot width with a standard parking slot width.
- Assuming a north arrow must always point up the sheet.
- Showing parking, access, or utilities without scale or labels.
- Ignoring existing buildings adjoining the lot when the site development plan requires distances.
