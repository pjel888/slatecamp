## Board Exam Quick Check

For corporate-practice questions, do not jump to ordinary corporation rules first. RA 9266 treats the practice of architecture as a professional service where registration still depends on individual personal qualifications and the 75 percent RLA composition requirement.

## Corporate Practice of Architecture

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Define architecture corporation from the RA 9266 IRR source language.
- Apply the 75 percent RLA composition rule to corporate practice.
- Identify the SEC and Board registration baseline.
- Avoid over-claiming corporate-veil, insurance, or share-transfer rules without separate sources.

### Definition and Registration

RA 9266 IRR defines an architecture corporation as a group of professionals in architecture and allied professions, incorporated with Architects for delivering professional services in architecture and allied professions. Section 37 then states that a firm, company, partnership, corporation, or association may be registered or licensed for architectural practice only under the law's conditions.

The entity must be registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission and the Board. The Board, subject to Commission approval, issues a certificate of registration to the firm, company, partnership, corporation, or association upon grant of registration.

### The 75 Percent RLA Composition Rule

RA 9266 Section 37 requires registered and licensed architects to compose at least 75 percent of the owners, shareholders, members, incorporators, directors, and executive officers, as the case may be. This is a composition rule, not merely a one-time filing detail.

The IRR definition of architecture corporation also warns that an existing architectural corporation that does not comply with the 75 percent composition requirement must comply and register again with the SEC and BOA. If share transfers or governance changes would reduce RLA composition below the threshold, the exam-safe response is to prevent or correct the non-compliance before relying on the corporation as a lawful architectural practice vehicle.

### Professional Responsibility

RA 9266 Section 37 states that individual members of such firm, partnership, association, or corporation are responsible for their individual and collective acts as an entity and as provided by law. That is the source-backed liability language for this article.

Do not overstate this source as a complete corporate-liability treatise. Corporate veil, malpractice insurance placement, tax treatment, and ordinary share-transfer mechanics require separate corporate-law or insurance sources. For LEA items tied to RA 9266, answer from the professional-practice rule: personal qualifications matter, the entity must be registered, and the RLA composition threshold must be maintained.

### Exam Use

Use this sequence for corporate practice:

1. Identify whether the entity is an architecture corporation rather than a regular business corporation.
2. Check SEC and Board registration.
3. Check the 75 percent RLA composition rule across the categories named in Section 37.
4. If the item asks about insurance, corporate veil, or succession, require a separate source before treating the detail as law.

### Real World Scenario

A corporation formed for architectural services proposes to add non-architect investors and place non-RLAs in executive roles. The source-safe analysis starts with RA 9266, not ordinary corporation convenience: the firm must remain an architecture corporation registered with the SEC and Board, and RLAs must compose at least 75 percent of the owners, shareholders, members, incorporators, directors, and executive officers as applicable. If a share transfer or governance change drops the RLA composition below the threshold, the exam-safe answer is to correct the structure before relying on the corporation as a lawful architectural-practice vehicle.
