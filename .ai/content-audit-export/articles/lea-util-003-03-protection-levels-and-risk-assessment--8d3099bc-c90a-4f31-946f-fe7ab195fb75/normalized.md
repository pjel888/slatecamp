## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Lightning-protection questions in this batch are answered from the checked PEC grounding, bonding, communications, and CATV provisions, plus Salvan's lightning-arrester context. The safe exam logic is to limit lightning and surge voltage, bond separate electrodes, protect exposed communication circuits, and keep lightning conductors separated from communications wiring where practicable.

## Grounding and Bonding Purpose

The Philippine Electrical Code states that grounded electrical systems must be connected to earth in a way that limits voltage imposed by lightning, line surges, or accidental contact with higher-voltage lines, and stabilizes voltage to earth during normal operation. Non-current-carrying conductive materials must be connected to earth and bonded to create an effective fault-current path.

For ungrounded systems, the PEC still requires grounding of non-current-carrying conductive materials to limit voltage imposed by lightning or accidental contact. The exam point is that lightning protection is not isolated from grounding and bonding. It depends on the building's electrical reference and on low-impedance bonding paths.

## Surge Arresters and Objectionable Current

The PEC permits surge arresters, including lightning arresters, on ungrounded overhead service conductors when installed under the applicable article. It also requires grounding of surge arresters and conductive non-current-carrying materials to be arranged so objectionable current does not flow over grounding conductors or paths.

Salvan's checked utilities source illustrates lightning arresters or surge protectors as devices intended to help protect electrical equipment from destructive voltage surges. For LEA purposes, this supports the practical role of surge protection but does not replace the code grounding and bonding requirements.

## Communications and Lightning Exposure

Communications circuits have separate lightning concerns. The PEC requires, where practicable, at least 1,800 mm separation between communications wires or cables on buildings and lightning conductors. It also requires listed primary protectors for circuits exposed to accidental contact with higher-voltage power conductors or lightning exposure.

Interbuilding communications circuits are treated as having lightning exposure unless specific lower-risk conditions apply, such as short bonded underground or buried runs, dense metropolitan interception conditions, or areas with limited thunderstorm days and low earth resistivity. The design response is protection at exposed circuit entrances, not guessing that communication wiring is low risk.

## Protector Grounding and Electrode Bonding

The PEC requires the primary protector grounding conductor to run to the grounding electrode as straight as practicable and to be guarded from physical damage where needed. If separate communications electrodes are used, they must be bonded to the power grounding electrode system with a bonding jumper not smaller than 14 mm2 copper or equivalent.

The same logic appears for community antenna television systems. Coaxial cable shields are grounded at the building as close to the point of entrance or attachment as practicable. Where separate CATV and power grounding electrodes are used, they must be bonded together to limit potential differences between systems.

## Radio, TV, and Coaxial Cable Rules

For radio and television equipment, the PEC requires separation from lightning conductors where practicable. For coaxial cable, the checked CATV provisions require at least 1,800 mm separation from lightning conductors where practicable and grounding of the outer conductive shield close to the building entrance.

These rules are important for LEA questions because communication and antenna systems can import lightning and surge energy into a building. The correct answer usually keeps entrance protectors, short grounding paths, electrode bonding, and conductor separation together.

## LEA Focus

Use this sequence for lightning-protection and risk questions:

- Limit voltage from lightning and line surges through grounding.
- Bond metal parts and separate electrodes to reduce potential differences.
- Keep communications wiring and lightning conductors separated where practicable.
- Provide primary protectors on exposed communications circuits.
- Ground protectors and coax shields close to the point of entrance.
- Avoid relying on earth alone as the fault-current path.
