The Standards of Professional Practice (SPP) documents are the official regulatory framework governing the architectural profession in the Philippines. Promulgated as part of the Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of **Republic Act No. 9266**, known as "The Architecture Act of 2004," the SPP became effective on March 9, 2011, and replaced the older 1979 UAP Documents (Docs. 201 through 210). The documents are administered jointly by the **United Architects of the Philippines (UAP)** — the Integrated and Accredited Professional Organization of Architects (IAPOA) — and the **Professional Regulatory Board of Architecture (PRBoA)** under the Professional Regulation Commission (PRC).

The SPP establishes the scope, modes, responsibilities, and conditions of architectural practice in the Philippines. It defines who may legally practice architecture, how services are delivered, and how architects are to be compensated. These documents are mandatory compliance requirements for all Registered and Licensed Architects (RLAs) practicing in the country.

***

## Legal Basis and Key Definitions (Annex "A")

The SPP begins with **Annex "A"**, which provides essential definitions and acronyms applicable to all succeeding SPP documents.

### Key Definitions

- **Architect**: Refers exclusively to a **Registered and Licensed Architect (RLA)** — a natural person under Philippine law with a valid Certificate of Registration and a renewable 3-year Professional Identification Card. The term may also refer to the Architect-of-record (Aor), Architect-in-charge of construction (Aicc), or Consulting Architect (CA), depending on the SPP document.
- **Architectural Firm (AF)**: A juridical person registered with the DTI (as a sole proprietorship) or the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) as a partnership or corporation of RLAs, in compliance with Sec. 37 of R.A. No. 9266.
- **Client / Owner / Project Proponent**: These terms may be used interchangeably throughout the SPP.
- **Commission**: The Professional Regulation Commission (PRC), created under R.A. No. 8981.
- **Board**: The Professional Regulatory Board of Architecture (PRBoA).

### Selection of the Architect (Annex "A", General Notes)

Annex "A" also covers general notes on how a client may engage an architect through three methods:

1. **Direct Selection** — For smaller projects; based on reputation, referral, or personal acquaintance
2. **Comparative Selection** — Through invitation, pre-qualification, interview, and ranking; typically used by corporations or government agencies
3. **Architectural Design Competition (ADC)** — Used for civic or monumental projects; subject to IAPOA oversight and the Architect's Guidelines

Foreign architects wishing to practice in the Philippines must secure a Temporary/Special Permit (TSP) from the PRC and a work permit from the Department of Labor and Employment (DoLE), and must work in collaboration with a locally licensed RLA.

## SPP Document 201 – Pre-Design Services

**Pre-Design Services** cover the broadest, earliest phase of architectural engagement, ranging from initial problem identification to conceptualization of architectural solutions before formal design begins.

### Scope of Services

Pre-Design Services nominally include:

- **Consultation** — Oral or written advice, conference attendance, evaluations and appraisals for a contemplated project
- **Pre-Feasibility Studies** — Preliminary assessments using secondary data to aid early client decision-making
- **Feasibility Studies** — Detailed viability analysis based on primary data gathering
- **Site Selection and Analysis** — Formulation of site criteria and evaluation of alternative sites
- **Site Utilization and Land-Use Studies** — Analysis of a site's development potential and applicable development controls
- **Architectural Research** — Primary and secondary research for project basis and conclusions
- **Architectural Programming** — Analytical problem-seeking process leading to space program requirements
- **Space Planning** — Determining adequate size and configuration for proposed projects
- **Space Management Studies** — Analysis of space requirements based on organizational structure
- **Value Management** — Cost management technique to minimize cost reduction impacts on performance
- **Design Brief Preparation** — Statement of project terms of reference for bidding out architectural services
- **Promotional Services** — Coordinating activities to develop financial support or public acceptance for a project

### Method of Compensation

Pre-Design Services may be compensated through:
- Multiple of Direct Personnel Expenses (MDPE): \(\text\{Fee\} = (\text\{Direct Cost\}) \times M\), where M (multiplier) ranges from 1.5 to 2.5
- Professional Fee Plus Expenses
- Lump Sum or Fixed Fee
- Per Diem, Honorarium Plus Reimbursable Expenses
- Mixed Methods

## SPP Document 203 – Specialized Architectural Services

SPP 203 governs architectural services that require **specific expertise** to complement or supplement regular design services, addressing specialized interior and exterior components of a project.

### Scope of Specialized Services

The document covers 14 or more distinct specialties, including:

1. **Architectural Interiors (AI)** — Design of interior spaces
2. **Acoustic Design** — Sound management and noise control
3. **Architectural Lighting Layout and Design** — Lighting systems design
4. **Site Development Planning (SDP)** — Site layout optimization
5. **Site and Physical Planning** — Master development, subdivision planning, urban design
6. **Comprehensive Development Planning (CDP)** — Multi-scale territorial planning
7. **Historic and Cultural Heritage Conservation and Planning** — Preservation work on heritage sites
8. **Security Evaluation and Planning** — Building security systems
9. **Building Systems Design** — Integration of MEP and other building systems
10. **Facilities Maintenance Support** — Post-completion maintenance guidance
11. **Building Testing and Commissioning** — Verification of system performance
12. **Building Environmental Certification** — Green building standards compliance
13. **Forensic Architecture** — Investigation of structural or systemic failures
14. **Special Building/Facility Planning and Design** — Healthcare, hospitality, transportation, industrial, and similar specialized building types

The architect acts as the **prime professional**, coordinating all allied design professionals and ensuring all inputs comply with the architectural design concept.

## SPP Document 204-A – Fulltime Supervision Services

SPP 204-A covers the engagement of an **Architect-in-charge of construction (Aicc)** who is directly and professionally responsible and civilly liable for the supervision of a construction project.

### rationale

When projects were small, the architect was assisted by a **Clerk-of-Works**. As projects grew in complexity, a **Construction Supervision Group (CSG)** became necessary for full-time, on-site inspection. The CSG is normally recommended by the architect and hired by the owner; they are answerable to both.

### Scope of Services

- Quality Control of construction works
- Evaluation of Construction Work against design documents
- Preparation of Daily Inspection Reports
- Filing of documents

### Manner of Providing Services

The architect may enter into contract with the owner in two ways:
1. Working in a **dual capacity** as Architect-of-record and as Consulting Architect / CSG for fulltime supervision
2. Working as **Consulting Architect** for fulltime supervision services only

### Method of Compensation

Compensation is primarily a **percentage of Project Construction Cost (PCC)**, detailed in the Architect's Guidelines, or through Multiple of Direct Personnel Expenses.

***

## SPP Document 204-B – Construction Management Services

SPP 204-B covers **Construction Management (CM)**, where the architect assumes broader managerial responsibilities over the entire construction process.

### Role and Functions

The Construction Manager's responsibilities include:
- **Coordination** of all construction activities and specialist consultants
- **Cost and Time Control** — monitoring budget and schedule adherence
- **Quality Control** — ensuring workmanship and material compliance
- **Record Keeping** — maintaining comprehensive project documentation

The Construction Manager oversees the CSG (as defined in SPP 204-A) but must not directly interfere with the contractor's operations or impose designs that affect the schedule or the architect's design concept. The CM is **legally responsible to the owner** for all construction aspects and must preferably be an architect with management skills and construction experience.

***

## SPP Document 205 – Post-Construction Services

SPP 205 governs architectural services that **"wrap up" the entire range of architectural engagement** after a building is turned over to the owner.

### Scope of Services

- **Building and Equipment Maintenance** — Ensuring all building systems are properly maintained after turnover; the in-house architect acts as building administrator
- **Building and Grounds Administration** — Billing of tenants, monitoring security and janitorial services, regulatory compliance
- **Post-Construction Evaluation** — Assessing whether the original program and building systems are meeting facility needs (includes forensic investigation of defects, seepage, and other emerging issues)

### Method of Compensation

Compensation is either a **salary** or a **management fee of 4–6% of gross rentals**, with the owner separately paying repair and maintenance expenses.

***

## SPP Document 206 – Comprehensive Architectural Services

SPP 206 defines **Comprehensive Architectural Services (CAS)** as the full continuum of professional services from pre-design through post-construction.

### Concept

The concept of CAS crystallized in response to increasingly complex building projects, recognizing that continuity of services across all phases is necessary for a completely viable project. Under CAS, the architect assumes the **dual role of Project Manager (PM) and Construction Manager (CM)**, functioning as creator, author, and coordinator of the building design.

## SPP Document 207 – Design-Build Services

SPP 207 governs projects where the architect takes on **both design and construction** responsibilities.

### Concept

Design-Build simplifies and expedites project delivery while providing creative, cost-effective solutions. The architect balances the three key project elements — **time, quality, and cost** — by assuming professional responsibility and civil liability for both the design and the construction of the project.

### Modes of Design-Build

1. **Design-Build by Administration (DBA)**: The architect directly administers the construction work, hiring personnel, procuring materials, managing accounts, and overseeing all construction activities
2. **Design-Build by Guaranteed Maximum Cost (DBGMC)**: The architect guarantees the maximum construction cost to the owner

### Method of Compensation

- DBA: 7% of Project Construction Cost (in addition to fees for Regular Design Services under SPP 202)
- DBGMC: 10% of PCC (exclusive of Regular Design Services fees); for residential projects, 10%

SPP 208 establishes the principles, procedures, and governance for **Architectural Design Competitions** in the Philippines.

The ADC promotes design innovation, creativity, and excellence in architecture. It is primarily used for civic or monumental projects where a wider range of design options is desired.

### Classifications of ADCs

1. **Project ADCs** — For actual projects proposed for implementation; may be single-stage or two-stage; open or by invitation
2. **Ideas Competition** — A design and planning exercise to elucidate a design problem without necessarily leading to construction

## SPP Document 209 – Professional Architectural Consulting Services (PACS)

SPP 209 defines the scope and conditions for **Professional Architectural Consulting Services (PACS)**, a specialized area for Professional Consulting Architects (PCAs).

### Purpose and Policy

The document emphasizes utilizing local Filipino expertise and Filipino Professional Consultants (FPCs) to minimize foreign dependence. It covers all consulting services rendered by a PCA or consulting architectural firm (RAF) to government, private sector, civil society organizations, or the international community for projects on Philippine soil.

### Qualifications of a PCA

A Professional Consulting Architect must be:
- A Philippine citizen (or holder of appropriate permits for foreign PCAs)
- A Registered and Licensed Architect (RLA)
- A member in good standing of the IAPOA

### Scope of PACS

PACS includes, but is not limited to:
- Program development and project conceptualization
- Research and development advisory work
- Documentation and technical assistance
- Construction management advice
- Training and capacity-building
- Teaching and academic roles in architecture

### Methods of Compensation

Compensation structures for PACS include:
- Hourly rates
- Retainer fees
- Lump sums
- Percentage of project costs

Owners are responsible for prioritizing qualifications over other factors and must hire qualified Filipino architects when available.

## Relationship Between the 2010 SPP and UAP Document 210

While SPP Documents 201–209 cover the **standards of practice** (scope, manner, and conditions of service), the **2016 UAP-IAPOA Document 210 (Methods of Compensation and Schedule of Fees)** provides the detailed, updated **fee schedules and recommended professional fees (RPF)** for all SPP-covered services. Both documents must be read together for full compliance with professional fee determination.

The RPF under UAP Doc. 210 is structured around six primary methods of compensation:
1. Percentage-based fees
2. Unit cost method
3. Value-based compensation
4. Lump sum or fixed fee
5. Time basis (hourly/per diem)
6. Mixed methods

***

## Compliance, Enforcement, and the Role of UAP/IAPOA

The UAP, as the IAPOA, plays a central role in the administration and enforcement of the SPP. Key compliance points include:

- Only **RLAs with valid PRC licenses** may legally practice architecture and render services under the SPP
- **Foreign architects** must secure TSP and DoLE work permits, and must collaborate with a local RLA
- **BPO/KPO firms** providing architectural services for projects in the Philippines must be PRC-registered architectural firms
- All **architectural design documents** are the intellectual property of the architect (Sec. 33, R.A. No. 9266), and must not be reproduced or used without authorization
- **Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR)** mechanisms are available for conflicts arising from architect-owner agreements
- Non-compliance with SPP provisions may result in penalties, disciplinary action, and revocation of the architect's PRC license
