## What You Need to Know for the LEA

## Learning Goal
Use grading as an earthwork and drainage coordination task: classify soil suitability, manage fill and prepared bases, control erosion, and avoid grading decisions that conflict with water drainage and easement rules.

## Core Concept
Grading modifies terrain through earthwork. In the checked Sitework Lecture and Building Technology - Siteworks sources, earthwork begins with soil classification and material behavior. The AASHTO system classifies inorganic soils for suitability as subgrade material in terms of drainage and bearing capacity. The sources define particle-size groups, Atterberg limits for fine soils, fill materials, subgrade/base-course materials, and paving over prepared bases.

The same sitework source family provides erosion and drainage tools. Riprap is a stone layer or facing used to prevent erosion, scour, or sloughing of a structure or embankment. Gabions are wire-enclosed riprap mats or baskets, and geotextiles can provide reinforcement, separation, filtration, and in-plane drainage. These are not decorative landscape items; they respond to soil, water velocity, bank slope, access, or base-course conditions.

PD 1067 adds the legal drainage boundary. Drainage systems must use approved outlets, artificial drainage from higher to lower land must minimize damage, lower estates receive natural flow while owners cannot improperly impede or increase that flow, and banks and shores are subject to public-use easements. Grading near rivers, streams, shores, drainage outlets, or protected areas must therefore be checked against the Water Code before earthwork is accepted.

A source-safe grading answer asks what material and water problem is being solved. Choose the option that protects drainage and bearing capacity, uses fill and base materials correctly, controls erosion where needed, and avoids work that violates water-margin or drainage constraints.

## Source-Safe Exam Takeaways

- AASHTO soil classification is relevant because subgrade suitability depends on drainage and bearing capacity.
- Atterberg limits help describe fine-soil moisture behavior, not site aesthetics.
- Fill, subgrade, base course, and paving are linked earthwork decisions.
- Riprap, gabions, and geotextiles are erosion, stability, filtration, or drainage measures.
- Water Code drainage, natural-flow, and easement limits can control where grading may occur.
