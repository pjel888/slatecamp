# Punch List

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Define the punch list from the checked CIAP source.
- Apply the thirty-day owner issuance rule and the sixty-day limit on additions.
- Connect punch-list items to the Period of Making Good of Known Defects.
- Separate corrective work from owner-preferred changes or new scope.

## What a Punch List Is

CIAP Document 102 uses the term directly. After substantial completion, the owner issues one or more lists of defects found or discovered on the completed work. These lists are the punch list or punch lists.

The punch list is not a wish list for new work. It is a close-out tool for defects in completed work and for corrective work connected to those listed defects.

## Timing Rules

Under CIAP Document 102, the contractor must receive the owner's punch list or lists not later than thirty days from the date of substantial completion. The owner may add to the punch-list items only as to corrective work on items in the original punch list or lists, and only not later than sixty days from the date of substantial completion.

Those time controls are often the exam trap. A late owner preference, design change, or added scope should not be treated as an ordinary punch-list item.

## Period of Making Good of Known Defects

The Period of Making Good of Known Defects or Faults is not more than thirty calendar days. It is counted from the contractor's receipt of the last item in the punch list submitted within the allowed period. During this period, the contractor completes the required corrective work.

If the defect is attributable to the owner, CIAP Document 102 treats the value of that work as additional work. If the contractor fails to do required corrective work, the owner may carry out the work after written notice and charge the cost to the contractor, including by withholding an amount not exceeding the contract cost of the corrective work.

## UAP Document 301 Boundary

UAP Document 301 also links substantial completion and known-defect correction, but its checked source uses different wording and time periods. For this article, apply the CIAP punch-list timing only when the question names CIAP Document 102 or quotes its rules.

## Real World Scenario

The owner discovers a new preferred finish change after substantial completion and tries to add it to the punch list sixty-five days later. The source-safe answer is to reject that as a normal punch-list addition. CIAP Document 102 limits additions to corrective work on original punch-list items and only within the allowed period after substantial completion.

## Exam Boundary

A punch list follows substantial completion; it does not create substantial completion by itself. Ask whether the item is a defect/corrective work item, whether it belongs to the original punch list, and whether it was raised within the source-stated time window.
