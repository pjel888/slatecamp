The punch list is the detailed inventory of incomplete or defective work items identified during the architect's Substantial Completion inspection. The contractor prepares and submits the initial punch list, which the architect reviews and may supplement during the punch walk inspection. The architect certifies the punch list as an official contract document that establishes the contractor's remaining obligations before Final Completion. authority includes the right to access the project, verify work quality against drawings and specifications, review the contractor's submitted punch list, add any additional items during the punch walk, and certify that punch list items have been corrected before issuing Final Completion.

## Core Concept

A punch list is created by the architect during the final inspection phase after Substantial Completion is declared. It itemizes every outstanding work item, minor defect, or non-conformance to contract documents that must be corrected before Final Completion can be certified. Punch list items are categorized by trade, location, and priority, and include estimated completion timeframes. The architect is responsible for determining what constitutes an appropriate punch list item versus what should have been completed before Substantial Completion certification.

The architect's certification of the punch list establishes a binding obligation on the contractor: complete all listed items by a specified deadline, or face contractual penalties and potential claims. The punch list is not a list of suggestions or nice-to-haves; it is a formal contract document with legal force. Items on the punch list are typically minor (hardware adjustment, paint touch-up, minor defects) that do not prevent occupancy. Significant items such as rework of mechanical systems or structural repair would indicate that Substantial Completion was premature and should not appear on a punch list.

After the contractor completes all punch list items, the architect conducts a final inspection to verify correction. The architect then issues the Certificate of Final Completion, which formally closes the construction phase. If the architect discovers during final inspection that punch list items were not properly corrected, the certificate is withheld until the contractor addresses the deficiencies. This inspection and certification cycle is the architect's final act of supervision and represents the architect's professional judgment that the work is complete and acceptable.

## Philippine Practice Context

Under UAP Document 301 (General Conditions) and standard Philippine construction practice, the punch list is a primary tool for managing final quality control and enforcing contractor compliance, with specific roles for both architect and contractor as defined in the contract documents. The architect who issues a Substantial Completion certificate without preparing a punch list creates significant risk: without documented outstanding items, defects discovered later may fall outside the contractor's obligation if the architect fails to formally identify them. Conversely, a poorly managed punch list that includes items the contractor never agreed to address creates disputes and withheld certifications.

The punch list must be prepared contemporaneously with the Substantial Completion inspection—not days or weeks later. This timing ensures accuracy and gives the contractor prompt notice of required work. The architect must distinguish between punch list items (minor work consistent with Substantial Completion definition) and items that suggest Substantial Completion was premature (e.g., structural work, major system malfunction). If major defects emerge during the punch list phase, the architect should question whether Substantial Completion should have been certified at all.

The contractor's obligation to complete punch list items at no additional cost is non-negotiable under standard contracts. The punch list is not a change order document; it represents work the contractor agreed to do under the original contract. If the contractor fails to complete punch items by the deadline, the owner has contractual remedies: withholding final payment, instructing others to complete items with costs charged against the contractor, or invoking performance bonds. The architect's role is to verify completion and certify Final Completion when satisfied that all items are done correctly.

## How It's Tested

**Question Pattern 1:** Punch list scope—What types of items belong on a punch list versus items indicating Substantial Completion was premature? (Tests distinction between minor and substantial work)

**Question Pattern 2:** Architect's certification timing—When must the architect create and deliver the punch list to the contractor? (Tests timing requirement and contractor notification duty)

**Question Pattern 3:** Punch list binding authority—Can a contractor refuse to complete punch list items without a change order? (Tests whether punch items are binding obligations with no additional compensation)

**Question Pattern 4:** Final inspection criteria—What must the architect verify during final inspection before issuing Final Completion certificate? (Tests architect's verification standard for punch item correction)

**Question Pattern 5:** As-built documentation—What architect responsibility exists regarding as-built drawings and testing documentation required for occupancy? (Tests architect's coordination and documentation duties)

<LawCallout code="ra9266" section="PD 1096 (National Building Code)" title="Certificate of Occupancy — Architect's Role">
The Certificate of Occupancy is issued by the Building Official (local government), NOT the architect. The architect's role includes: (1) Design compliance — sealed drawings must comply with NBC; (2) Construction administration — inspect for code compliance; (3) Occupancy coordination — assist owner with required documentation (as-builts, test reports, compliance certificates). The architect's Substantial Completion certificate does NOT authorize occupancy — only the Building Official's Certificate of Occupancy does.
</LawCallout>

<ExamTip>
  **LEA Priority — Certificate of Occupancy Key Points:**
  1. Issued by Building Official (government), not architect

  3. Based on inspection against National Building Code
  4. Architect is liable if design or construction supervision failed to ensure code compliance
  5. Owner cannot legally occupy without Certificate of Occupancy
</ExamTip>
