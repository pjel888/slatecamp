# Spatial Organization by Francis D.K. Ching

## Source-Audited Scope

Batch 055 limits this article to the checked spatial-organization terms and descriptions in the local theory source families: centralized, linear, grid, radial, and clustered organization, with detailed local support for centralized, linear, and radial schemes.

## Board Exam Quick Check

For LEA theory questions, match the spatial organization to the relation among spaces: a dominant central space, a directional sequence, an intersecting regular grid, radiating arms, or a flexible cluster.

## Core Concepts

- The checked theory source lists centralized, linear, grid, radial, and clustered organizations.
- A centralized organization is a stable concentrated composition with secondary spaces grouped around a large dominant central space.
- Linear organizations express direction, movement, extension, and growth.
- Linear organizations can be straight, segmented, curvilinear, horizontal, diagonal, or vertical.
- A radial organization combines centralized and linear organization through a dominant central space with linear arms extending outward.

## LEA Use

Use the central-space clue for centralized plans, the sequence or direction clue for linear plans, and the central-with-arms clue for radial plans. Use grid and clustered labels only when the question source supports those exact ordering terms.
