# North American Indigenous Architecture

## Source-Audited Scope

Batch 054 narrows this article to the North American examples directly named in the checked local history source: an American cabin, a Kickapoo Indian wigwam, and an Eskimo igloo. Other North American traditions are not used as primary truth for this batch.

## Board Exam Quick Check

Use the material clue first. The checked source ties the American cabin to stripped logs sealed with clay, the Kickapoo wigwam to rush mats over a wooden frame with an animal-skin door, and the Eskimo igloo to hardpacked snow blocks built up spirally.

## Core Concepts

- The American cabin is described as stripped logs sealed with clay, with a clay-lined chimney and a roof of bark or shingles.
- The Kickapoo Indian wigwam is described as rush mats over a wooden frame, with an animal-skin door.
- The Eskimo igloo is described as hardpacked snow blocks built up spirally.
- These examples show direct material response: timber and clay for the cabin, rush mats and wood for the wigwam, and snow blocks for the igloo.

## LEA Use

For this Batch 054 scope, answer from the source-backed material pairs. If a question asks about pueblos, longhouses, tipis, or other traditions, require a separate checked source before answering from this article.

## Common Traps

- Do not import longhouse, pueblo, or tipi details into this Batch 054 answer set.
- Do not confuse the American log cabin with the Kickapoo wigwam.
- Do not describe the igloo as timber-framed; the checked source says hardpacked snow blocks.
