## What You Need to Know for the LEA

The LEA tests your ability to correctly classify dormitories as Group B residential (not Group A), understand their space program standards per DOT classifications, and apply occupant load calculations for sleeping and common areas.

## Core Concept

Dormitories and student housing facilities are classified as _Group B residential_ per **PD 1096**, the same occupancy group as hotels and pensions. This classification reflects the _transient residential_ nature of these facilities—occupants are temporary residents (students, workers, disaster evacuees) rather than permanent household members. This distinction is critical: dormitories are **not** classified as Group A-2 (multi-family residential).

The Department of Tourism (DOT) establishes voluntary accommodation star rating classifications with space standards that may guide market positioning but do NOT constitute National Building Code (PD 1096) minimum floor area requirements. DOT standards (1-Star: 16 sqm, etc.) are separate from NBC Rule VIII habitable room minimums (typically 6–10 sqm for dormitory rooms depending on type). For LEA: NBC standards (PD 1096) are binding; DOT standards are advisory classification only.

**Occupant Load Determination:** Sleeping areas are computed at **9.3 sqm per person** per **RAPD 1096 (National Building Code of the Philippines, Rule VIII Section 805 or applicable occupancy load tables) or RA 9514 (Fire Code of the Philippines 2019 Revised IRR, Table 3.1-2 if applicable to fire load calculations)Common areas (lounges, dining, recreation) are calculated separately using retail/mercantile load factors (1 person per 18.58 sqm per RA 9514 Table 3.1-2) or education factors (1 person per 4.6 sqm) depending on function. This dual calculation is essential for determining exit requirements, sprinkler design, and emergency planning.

Dormitories may also include commercial support spaces (bookstore, cafeteria, clinic)—these are classified separately as Group E (Business/Mercantile) or Group D (Institutional), but the primary occupancy remains Group B.

| 2-Star | 20 | Standard dormitory | University on-campus residence |

### Occupant Load Calculation (RA 9514)

**Sleeping Areas:**
- Dormitories/Hotels: 1 person per 18.60 sqm of GROSS floor area (RA 9514 Table 10.2.3.1-2, Residential Use—Hotels & Dormitories). For institutional sleeping departments in health care settings: 1 person per 11.10 sqm GROSS floor area. Note: 9.3 sqm/person is Business Use, NOT sleeping rooms.

**Common Areas:**
- Lounge/recreation: 1 person per 18.58 sqm (mercantile)
- Dining: 1 person per 1.9 sqm (food service, seated)
- Study areas: 1 person per 4.6 sqm (education)

**Example (200-unit 3-Star dormitory):**
- Sleeping occupant load: 200 units × 25 sqm = 5,000 sqm ÷ 9.3 sqm/person = **538 occupants** (sleeping)
- Common dining (1,500 sqm): 1,500 ÷ 1.9 = **789 occupants** (simultaneous dining capacity)
- Total design occupancy for emergency egress: **1,000+ persons** (use largest simultaneous load)

### Accessibility & Egress Standards (BP 344, RA 9514)

- Accessible room door: 800 mm (0.80 m) minimum clear width per BP 344 base; 900 mm for certain vestibule entry doors per 2013 IRR amendment
- Accessible sleeping room: 4.0 m × 5.0 m minimum (BP 344 2024 Revised IRR Section [X]) [verify exact section number]
- Ramp gradient: 1:12 maximum (steeper slopes not permitted). Note: 1:20 gradient is for dropped sidewalks to prevent water collection; accessible ramps are limited to 1:12 per BP 344 Section 1.2.2 and 2024 Revised IRR.
- Exit width: [verify exact requirement from RA 9514 2019 Revised IRR or PD 1096 Rule IX; typical standard is 1 mm per occupant or similar ratio, not 7.6 mm per person]
- Travel distance: 60 m maximum (sprinklered) / 45 m (unsprinklered)
- 2 exits required at occupancy ≥ 50 persons
- Stairwell tread: min 250 mm, riser: max 200 mm

### Parking & Site Requirements (PD 1096 Section 803)

| Use | Ratio | Calculation |
|---|---|---|
| Dormitory (student) | 1 space per 5–10 beds | 200-bed dorm = 20–40 spaces |
| Support office | 1 per 125 sqm GFA (2004 Revised IRR) | Admin/clinic spaces |
| Staff residential | 1 per 2–3 staff units | Faculty quarters |

Standard parking stall: **2.5 m × 5.0 m** per PD 1096; Accessible stall: **4.0 m × 5.0 m** (2024 Revised IRR)

## Philippine Practice Context

A typical 300-bed university dormitory in the Philippines (3-Star, 25 sqm/unit) contains:
- Sleeping units: 300 × 25 sqm = 7,500 sqm
- Common areas (30% of housing): ~2,250 sqm (lounges, dining, study)
- Support: 1,500 sqm (management, laundry, storage)
- **Total GFA: ~11,250 sqm**
- **Occupant load (sleeping): 7,500 ÷ 9.3 ≈ 806 persons**
- **Occupant load (dining): 2,250 ÷ 1.9 ≈ 1,184 persons simultaneous**
- **Design capacity: 1,200 persons** (use maximum for egress planning)
- **Parking: 30–60 spaces** (at 1 per 5–10 beds)

Common layout: 4–6 stories with double-loaded corridors, 2–4 shared bathrooms per 8–10 rooms, one main stairwell plus emergency exit stairs. Fire rating: 2-hour separation between dormitory and support commercial spaces.

## How It's Tested

**Occupancy Classification Pattern (Difficulty 0.7):**
"A university builds a 500-bed on-campus residence hall. Under PD 1096, what is the primary occupancy group classification?"
- Answer: **Group B** (residential hotel/dormitory) — NOT Group A-2, despite permanent student residency, because occupancy is transient semester-to-semester

**Space Program Pattern (Difficulty 0.7):**
"A 400-bed 2-Star dormitory is proposed. What is the minimum total sleeping area required?"
- Answer: 400 beds × 20 sqm/bed = **8,000 sqm minimum** (per DOT 2-Star standard)

**Occupant Load Analysis Pattern (Difficulty 0.9):**
"A 200-unit 3-Star dormitory has 1,200 sqm of dining commons. Calculate the occupant load for dining and determine if 2 exits are adequate for emergency egress."
- Dining load: 1,200 sqm ÷ 1.9 sqm/person = **632 occupants**
- 2 exits required at ≥ 50 persons ✓
- Exit width needed: 632 occupants ÷ 165 = 3.83 m total exit width required per PD 1096 Section 1207 (approx 6.06 mm per occupant, not 7.6 mm). Provided by 2 doors at 2.4 m clear width each = 4.8 m total (exceeds 3.83 m minimum) ✓

<Tip>**Memory rule:** Dormitory = Group **B** (transient residential, not A). Never confuse with permanent multi-family (A-2). DOT star ratings: 1★=16, 2★=20, 3★=25, 4★=30, 5★=40 sqm. Occupant load formula: sleeping area sqm ÷ 9.3 = occupants (then add common areas separately for total design capacity).</Tip>
