# Architecture shapes the physical environment in which people live

## Source-Audited Scope

Use this topic as a design-responsibility topic grounded in theory sources. The checked local sources support responsibility through practical/aesthetic integration, planning for human needs, environmental protection and comfort, and attention to social, economic, physical, sensory, and cultural context. It is not a substitute for a legal ethics article.

## Board Exam Quick Check

When the stem asks about the architect's broader responsibility, choose the option that integrates people, environment, context, and performance. Avoid options that isolate appearance, cost, or technical systems from user welfare and environmental consequences.

## Core Concepts

- Architectural design must combine practical and aesthetic concerns to satisfy individuals or communities.
- Human needs are a central design concern, and successful building design is informed by science, sociology, and economics.
- Environmental protection and comfort are part of architectural theory and design scope.
- Design problems should be understood in their social, economic, and physical context.
- Environmental concepts in architecture include the relationship between people, buildings, and the designed environment.

## LEA Use

Responsibility questions are usually application questions. A source-safe answer does not claim a new legal penalty or exact code duty unless a law is cited. It explains why the better architectural decision serves users, context, environmental comfort, and the building's practical purpose together.

## Active Recall

1. What must design combine? Practical and aesthetic concerns.
2. What context should a design problem consider? Social, economic, and physical context.
3. What should environmental design support? Protection, comfort, and compatibility with site and place.
