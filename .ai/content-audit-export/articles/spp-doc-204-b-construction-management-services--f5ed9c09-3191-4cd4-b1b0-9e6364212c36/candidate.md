## Overview

**SPP Document 204-B** governs **Construction Management (CM) Services** — a broader, more managerial form of construction oversight that goes beyond the on-site inspection duties of the Construction Supervision Group (CSG) under SPP 204-A. It is part of the 2010 Standards of Professional Practice (SPP), promulgated as the Supplemental Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of **Republic Act No. 9266** (The Architecture Act of 2004), and replaces the earlier **1979 UAP Doc. 204-B**.

---
Where SPP 204-A focuses on **on-site quality control and inspection**, SPP 204-B positions the Construction Manager (CM) as the **owner's primary point of accountability for the entire construction process** — covering programming, coordination of all parties, cost control, time management, and quality assurance from a project-wide management perspective. Construction Management Services arose in response to the increasing complexity of construction projects and the corresponding demand for more effective cost control and faster project implementation consistent with high quality of work.

The Construction Manager must be a **State-regulated professional** — preferably a **Registered and Licensed Architect (RLA)** with managerial capabilities and extensive experience in the field of construction. Since construction management is **not part of the regular services of the Architect** under SPP 202, the services rendered by the Architect as Construction Manager shall be **separately compensated**.

## Background: Why Construction Management Was Instituted

Due to the increasing complexity of construction projects, the construction industry faced two key pressures:

1. **The need for more effective cost control** — Large and multi-phase projects require systematic tracking of budgets, procurement, and change orders beyond what periodic site visits can address
2. **The need for faster project implementation** — Tight schedules and multi-contractor environments require proactive coordination rather than reactive inspection

Construction Management Services were instituted to fill this gap — bridging the owner's expectations of on-time, on-budget, quality construction with the operational realities of managing complex projects.

***

## Who May Serve as Construction Manager

The Construction Manager (CM) may be:

1. **A member of the Owner's own staff** — an in-house professional hired directly by the owner's organization
2. **An independent individual** — a sole practitioner hired specifically for the project
3. **A firm** — a construction management company hired by the owner

An **Architect** is explicitly recognized as a suitable CM. The architect's training in coordinating various specialties — structural, electrical, mechanical, plumbing, interiors, and site development — allows the architect to supervise and assure proper monitoring of all construction activities with respect to quality, workmanship, and cost.

Architects who are also contractors, or who have been in responsible charge of construction works, are considered **especially capable of becoming Construction Managers**. This practical construction background, combined with professional design education, makes the architect uniquely qualified for the CM role.

***

## Functions and Description of Tasks

The responsibilities of the Construction Manager (CM) **include all the functions of the Construction Supervision Group (CSG)** as defined under SPP 204-A, plus broader management responsibilities. The four core CM functions are:

### 2.1 Coordination and Supervision

The CM coordinates all parties involved in the construction project — owner, architect-of-record, engineers, specialist consultants, contractors, sub-contractors, and suppliers. Coordination activities include:

- **Pre-Construction Phase**: Regular consultations with the Owner, Architects, and Engineers on all aspects of project planning — during and concurrent with the design development phase
- **Scheduling**: Preparation and monitoring of the overall project implementation schedule, including sequencing of all major trades
- **Interface Management**: Ensuring that the work of different contractors and sub-contractors does not conflict in terms of sequence, access, space, or systems
- **Meeting Facilitation**: Convening and minuting all construction coordination meetings, progress review sessions, and technical workshops
- **Procurement Coordination**: Assisting the owner in pre-qualifying contractors, evaluating bids, and recommending awards

### 2.2 Cost and Time Control

The CM is the owner's primary safeguard against cost overruns and schedule delays. Cost and time control activities include:

- **Budget Monitoring**: Tracking actual expenditures against the approved project budget and the Statement of Probable Project Construction Cost (SPPCC)
- **Change Order Management**: Evaluating all proposed changes, their cost implications, and schedule impacts before submission to the owner for approval
- **Progress Billing Review**: Reviewing and verifying contractor payment applications against actual work accomplished
- **Forecasting**: Providing the owner with regular updated forecasts of final project cost and completion date
- **Schedule Recovery**: Identifying and recommending corrective actions when work falls behind the approved program

### 2.3 Quality Control of Work

While the CSG handles day-to-day field quality control, the CM provides a higher-level quality assurance function:

- **QCP Oversight**: Reviewing and approving the Quality Control Plan (QCP) of the CSG
- **Material and Equipment Approval**: Coordinating the review and approval of material submittals, shop drawings, and equipment specifications
- **Inspection Coordination**: Scheduling and coordinating third-party testing and inspections (soil testing, concrete testing, etc.)
- **Non-Conformance Management**: Reviewing and dispositioning non-conformance reports from the CSG
- **Commissioning Coordination**: Overseeing the commissioning and testing of all building systems prior to turnover

### 2.4 Keeping of Records

The CM maintains comprehensive project records covering all management activities:

- **Project files** — all contracts, purchase orders, correspondence, and approvals
- **Progress reports** — weekly and monthly reports to the owner covering cost, schedule, and quality status
- **Meeting minutes** — records of all coordination, progress, and technical meetings
- **Change order log** — chronological record of all approved and pending changes
- **Procurement log** — status of all material and equipment procurement
- **As-built documentation** — coordination of final as-built drawings and warranties for turnover

## The CM and the CSG: How They Relate

A critical clarification in SPP 204-B is the relationship between the **Construction Manager (CM)** and the **Construction Supervision Group (CSG)**:

- The CM's responsibilities **include** the CSG's functions — meaning CM services are a **superset** of CSG services
- The CM may **hire the CSG to be under his employ**, directly managing and compensating the CSG team
- Alternatively, the CM may **supervise the CSG hired directly by the Owner**

This dual arrangement allows flexibility: the owner may prefer to hire and pay the CSG directly (for accountability and cost transparency), while still placing the CSG under the CM's technical and managerial direction.

***

## Limitation of Authority

The SPP explicitly limits the CM's authority to prevent liability overreach and protect the integrity of both the design and the construction contract:

**The Architect as CM shall NOT:**

1. **Involve directly with the Contractor's work** in such a way that it may be construed that the CM is assuming the Contractor's liability as provided for under **Article 1723 of the Civil Code** — meaning the CM must not direct, supervise, or control the contractor's construction methods to the point where the CM could be held solidarily liable for structural defects

2. **Impose methods, systems, or designs** that will substantially affect the construction schedule or **impair the design concept of the Architect-of-record** — this preserves the design integrity of the Aor and prevents the CM from effectively re-designing the project through construction directives

These limitations are analogous to those of the CSG under SPP 204-A, but are expressed at the management level rather than the field level.

***

## Legal Responsibility

SPP 204-B draws a clear and important distinction between the CM's **scope of responsibility** and the **limits of liability**:

### 4.1 Direct Responsibility to the Owner

The Architect as CM is **directly responsible to the Owner** on all aspects of construction work:
- Programming
- Coordination
- Quality control
- Cost control
- Time management

This direct responsibility is broader than the CSG's field-level accountability — the CM answers for the entire management process, not just on-site inspection.

### 4.2 Limits of CM Liability

The CM **assumes no liability** for equipment failure or structural collapse under two specific conditions:

<ComparisonTable>

| Condition | Liability Exclusion |
|---|---|
| Equipment failure or building collapse due to **deficiencies in the plan/design** | CM is NOT liable, **provided the CM did not participate in the plan/design review and approval** |
| Equipment failure or building collapse due to **deficiencies in the manner of construction** | CM is NOT liable, **provided the CM faithfully discharged his functions during construction/project implementation** |

</ComparisonTable>

These liability exclusions incentivize the CM to faithfully perform supervision duties (condition 2) while also staying out of design review (condition 1), unless separately retained as the Aor or design consultant. When the CM also serves as Aor, condition 1 no longer applies, and the CM-Aor faces full exposure under Article 1723 of the Civil Code.

## Qualifications

The Construction Manager may be an individual or a firm. The required qualifications are:

1. **State-regulated professional** — The individual or the principal of the firm must be a licensed professional under Philippine law; being an RLA (Registered and Licensed Architect) is the preferred qualification
2. **Managerial capabilities** — The CM must demonstrate organizational and managerial competence in coordinating multi-discipline construction teams
3. **Extensive experience in construction** — Practical exposure to construction processes, materials, systems, sequencing, and contracting is essential

The SPP notes that **architects who are also contractors, or who have been in responsible charge of construction works, are usually capable of becoming Construction Managers** — because such individuals combine design understanding with operational construction knowledge.

## Method of Compensation

Since construction management is not part of the architect's regular services under SPP 202, CM services are **always separately compensated**. The same six methods applicable to SPP 204-A apply here:

<ComparisonTable>

| Method | Best Used For | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **% of Project Construction Cost (PCC)** | All regular construction projects | Separate from SPP 202 design fee |
| **MDPE** | Variable-scope management work | Multiplier 1.5–2.5 on personnel hours |
| **Professional Fee + Expenses** | Ongoing/repeat client relationships | Fixed sum + reimbursements |
| **Lump Sum / Fixed Fee** | Government projects; well-defined scope | Predictable total cost |
| **Per Diem + Honorarium** | Short or limited-duration engagements | Daily rate + out-of-pocket |
| **Mixed Methods** | Complex, multi-phase CM engagements | Combination of above methods |

</ComparisonTable>

The specific percentage rates for CM services under the PCC method are prescribed in the **Architect's Guidelines** and are distinct from — and in addition to — the fees for SPP 202 design services and SPP 204-A supervision services.

## SPP 204-A vs. SPP 204-B: Key Distinctions

<ComparisonTable>

| | SPP 204-A (Fulltime Supervision) | SPP 204-B (Construction Management) |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary Role** | On-site inspection and quality control | Project-wide management of cost, time, quality, and coordination |
| **Scope** | CSG tasks: QC, evaluation, daily reports, filing | All CSG tasks + coordination, cost/time control, broader record keeping |
| **Personnel** | CSG — qualified architects on-site daily | CM — senior professional overseeing the CSG and all parties |
| **Reporting** | Daily Inspection Reports to Owner and Architect | Weekly/monthly management reports to Owner |
| **Covers CSG?** | IS the CSG | INCLUDES and may employ the CSG |
| **Contractual Liability** | CSG answerable to Owner and Architect | CM directly responsible to Owner on all construction aspects |
| **Legal Liability** | Solidary liability under Art. 1723 if supervising | Excluded from liability if faithfully discharged and not in design review |
| **Fee Basis** | % of PCC (separate from SPP 202) | % of PCC (separate from SPP 202 and 204-A) |

</ComparisonTable>

## SPP 204-B and Project Management (PM) under SPP 206

When an architect is hired to provide **Comprehensive Architectural Services (CAS)** under SPP 206, the architect assumes both the role of **Project Manager (PM)** and **Construction Manager (CM)**. In this context:

- **Project Management (PM)** refers to management activities **over and above** normal architectural and engineering (A&E) services, carried out across all phases — pre-design, design, and construction
- The PM oversees time, cost, and quality relative to the construction of the project
- The **presence of a Project Manager does not relieve the designers and contractors** of their respective normal duties and responsibilities
- If the PM also serves as CM to oversee time, cost, and quality control during construction, the PM shall provide the services detailed under SPP Documents 204-A and/or 204-B

The PM fee under SPP 206 is generally **2–5% of the Estimated Project Cost**, using combination methods; if the PM also performs regular architectural services for the same project, those are compensated separately under SPP 202.

## Practical Notes for Architects and Clients

**For Architects:**
- Never combine the Aor and CM roles without a clearly written, separately compensated agreement — doing so increases exposure to solidary liability under Article 1723 of the Civil Code, since both design deficiencies and construction deficiencies may now be attributable to the same professional.
- The CM role requires genuine managerial competence — scheduling, cost reporting, contract administration, and stakeholder management — beyond what most design-track architects practice. Consider supplementing with a formal project management credential or partnering with an experienced CM firm.
- Keep the CM's management records meticulously. The liability exclusion under SPP 204-B Sec. 4.2 is conditioned on the CM having "faithfully discharged his functions" — contemporaneous documentation is the primary evidence of faithful discharge.

**For Clients / Project Proponents:**
- Engaging a CM under SPP 204-B is most valuable for **large, multi-phase, or multi-contractor projects** where the owner's in-house team lacks the capacity to coordinate all parties directly.
- The CM fee is a **separate cost** from the architect's SPP 202 design fee and the CSG's SPP 204-A supervision fee — budget for all three independently.
- Specify in the Owner-CM Agreement whether the CM will directly hire and pay the CSG, or whether the owner will hire the CSG separately with the CM providing technical oversight — the two arrangements have different billing and accountability implications.
