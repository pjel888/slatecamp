_An Immersive LEA Review Module — Optimized for Board Exam Mastery_

## The Real-World Hook (Dual Coding Theory)

Picture yourself as the lead architect on a high-stakes public hospital project.[file:4] A wheelchair user enters the washroom.[file:4] They transfer from their wheelchair to the toilet.[file:4] Their hand reaches for the grab bar — which must be positioned **300mm above the top of the toilet seat**, not at some arbitrary height off the floor (Sec 6.3.b, 2024 IRR of BP 344).[file:4] If you mounted that bar at a fixed 800mm AFF (above finished floor), the bar could end up too high or too low relative to the actual seat height, depending on the toilet model.[file:4] They may lose balance and fall.[file:4] **The law is not arbitrary. Every millimeter is calibrated to the human body.**[file:4]

Chunk the rules this way: **Clearance → Support → Heights.**[file:4]

---

## Core Concepts (Mnemonic Anchor: "C-S-H")

- **Clearance (C):** The "1.8 Rule." **1800 x 1800 mm** unobstructed floor space is the baseline clear space, used for wheelchair turning and for accessible toilet compartments and roll-in showers (see Sec 6.4 and Sec 6.22 of the 2024 IRR).[file:4] Many designs deepen the cubicle to around **1800 x 2250 mm** for door swing and maneuvering, but the key exam minimum is **1.8 x 1.8 m clear**.[file:4][web:6][web:7]

- **Support (S):** The "300-Above Rule." The **top of the horizontal grab bar** on the side wall is positioned **300mm above the top of the toilet seat** (Sec 6.3.b).[file:4] With the toilet seat at 450mm AFF, this places the bar roughly at 750mm AFF, but the code’s controlling dimension is **from the seat, not from the floor**.[file:4]

- **Heights (H):** The "450/800 Rule" — but for **different fixtures**.[file:4] Toilet seat: **450mm AFF** (top of seat) (Sec 6.7).[file:4] Lavatory rim: maximum **800mm AFF** (Sec 6.9).[file:4] These belong to two different fixtures; do not mix them in exams or design.[file:4]

_Chant this three times aloud:_ **"1.8 turns the chair. 300-above steadies the grip. 450 sits them down. 800 is the wash basin, not the bar."**[file:4]

<Callout variant="lea-watch" title="The &quot;800 Rule&quot; Confusion">

The "800 Rule" Confusion:
The number 800mm is real — but it belongs to the **lavatory rim height** (Sec 6.9), NOT to toilet grab bars.[file:4] The 2024 IRR does not assign any fixed AFF height to toilet grab bars; they are referenced from the **seat height**.[file:4] If an exam option says "grab bars at 800mm AFF," treat it as a distractor unless the question explicitly states a 450mm seat and asks only for an approximate resulting AFF dimension.[file:4]

</Callout>

<Callout variant="authority" title="Why 300mm above the seat, not a fixed AFF value?">

Why 300mm above the seat, not a fixed AFF value?
During a lateral transfer from wheelchair to toilet, the user’s elbow is flexed and the hand grips a bar slightly above seat height.[file:4] Referencing the bar **300mm above the seat top** keeps the bar in a consistent biomechanical zone even when seat thickness or mounting varies, whereas a rigid AFF value (like 800mm) would float relative to the seat and could become unusable or unsafe.[file:4]

</Callout>

The low urinal lip allows both seated wheelchair users and ambulant persons with limited balance to use the fixture, while bilateral grab bars at 1500mm AFF provide stable support when standing.[file:4]

## Link to PD 1096 and NBC Rules VII & VIII

While PD 1096 and its IRR do not fix these accessibility dimensions, they establish the **general building framework** — use or occupancy classification (Rule VII), minimum room sizes and air space (Rule VIII), exits, stairs, ramps, and guard rails — within which BP 344 requirements must be integrated.[file:1][file:3] For example:

- **Ramps** under PD 1096 must meet exit/ramp requirements (width as required for corridors, slope, landings), and BP 344 then adds **accessibility refinements** such as dual handrails at 700mm and 900mm, tactile strips, and 100mm raised edges (amended IRR, Appendix A).[file:1][file:2]
- **Guard rails** in PD 1096 (Rule XII, 1100mm min) are complemented by BP 344’s requirements for handrail heights, shapes, and extensions, ensuring both fall protection and functional grip for PWD users.[file:1][file:2]

When designing an accessible washroom in any occupancy under Rule VII, treat BP 344 as a **mandatory overlay** on top of the general dimensional and safety controls of the National Building Code.[file:1][file:2][file:3][file:4]
