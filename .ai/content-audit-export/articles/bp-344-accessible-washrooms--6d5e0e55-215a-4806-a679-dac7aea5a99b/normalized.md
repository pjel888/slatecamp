# BP 344 Accessible Washrooms
## Learning Goal
After reading this article, you should be able to:

Apply BP 344 requirements for accessible washrooms: calculate minimum clear floor space (1.50 m × 1.50 m) for wheelchair turning; determine water closet clearance (900 mm wide × 1500 mm deep minimum with 450 mm side transfer space); apply grab bar placement and load requirements (horizontal 750-850 mm height, 1.3 kN capacity); and identify lavatory knee clearance and mirror height specifications.
---

## The Real-World Hook (Dual Coding Theory)

Picture yourself as the lead architect on a high-stakes public hospital project.[file:4] A wheelchair user enters the washroom.[file:4] They transfer from their wheelchair to the toilet.[file:4] Their hand reaches for the grab bar — which must be positioned **300mm above the top of the toilet seat**, not at some arbitrary height off the floor (Section 6, Toilets and Baths (grab bar height), 2024 IRR of BP 344).[file:4] If you mounted that bar at a fixed 800mm AFF (above finished floor), the bar could end up too high or too low relative to the actual seat height, depending on the toilet model.[file:4] They may lose balance and fall.[file:4] **The law is not arbitrary. Every millimeter is calibrated to the human body.**[file:4]

Chunk the rules this way: **Clearance → Support → Heights.**[file:4]

---

## Core Concepts (Mnemonic Anchor: "C-S-H")

- **Clearance (C):** The "1.8 Rule." **1800 x 1800 mm** unobstructed floor space is the baseline clear space, used for wheelchair turning and for accessible toilet compartments and roll-in showers (see Section 6, Toilets and Baths (turning space) and Section 6, Toilets and Baths (roll-in shower) of the 2024 IRR).[file:4] Many designs deepen the cubicle to around **1800 x 2250 mm** for door swing and maneuvering, but the key exam minimum is **1.8 x 1.8 m clear**.[file:4][web:6][web:7]

- **Support (S):** The "300-Above Rule." The **top of the horizontal grab bar** on the side wall is positioned **300mm above the top of the toilet seat** (Section 6, Toilets and Baths (grab bar height)).[file:4] With the toilet seat at 450mm AFF, this places the bar roughly at 750mm AFF, but the code’s controlling dimension is **from the seat, not from the floor**.[file:4]

- **Heights (H):** The "450/800 Rule" — but for **different fixtures**.[file:4] Toilet seat: **450mm AFF** (top of seat) (Section 6, Toilets and Baths (toilet seat height)).[file:4] Lavatory rim: maximum **800mm AFF** (Section 6, Toilets and Baths, lavatory spec).[file:4] These belong to two different fixtures; do not mix them in exams or design.[file:4]

_Chant this three times aloud:_ **"1.8 turns the chair. 300-above steadies the grip. 450 sits them down. 800 is the wash basin, not the bar."**[file:4]

## Link to PD 1096 and NBC Rules VII & VIII

While PD 1096 and its IRR do not fix these accessibility dimensions, they establish the **general building framework** — use or occupancy classification (Rule VII), minimum room sizes and air space (Rule VIII), exits, stairs, ramps, and guard rails — within which BP 344 requirements must be integrated.[file:1][file:3] For example:

- **Ramps** under PD 1096 must meet exit/ramp requirements (width as required for corridors, slope, landings), and BP 344 then adds **accessibility refinements** such as dual handrails at 700mm and 900mm, tactile strips, and 100mm raised edges (amended IRR, Appendix A).[file:1][file:2]
- **Guard rails** in PD 1096 (Rule XII, 1100mm min) are complemented by BP 344’s requirements for handrail heights, shapes, and extensions, ensuring both fall protection and functional grip for PWD users.[file:1][file:2]

When designing an accessible washroom in any occupancy under Rule VII, treat BP 344 as a **mandatory overlay** on top of the general dimensional and safety controls of the National Building Code.[file:1][file:2][file:3][file:4]

## Exam Tip

LEA Trap: Accessible water closet clearance: 900 mm wide × 1,500 mm deep min + 450 mm side transfer space (left or right). Grab bars: horizontal at 750-850 mm height, 1.3 kN capacity. The side transfer side is the most tested dimension — the space must be on BOTH sides of an accessible stall but the BARRIER-FREE approach requires transfer from either side.
