## Subject Coverage

Structural Conceptualization spans 12 interconnected subtopics forming a logical dependency chain:

- **Mechanics of Materials and Statics** — equilibrium, stress/strain, shear and moment diagrams
- **Loads and Load Combinations** — dead, live, wind, seismic loads and NSCP combination formulas
- **Reinforced Concrete Conceptualization** — dominant PH material; beams, columns, slabs, shear walls
- **Steel Conceptualization** — wide-flange sections, connections, beam behavior
- **Timber Conceptualization** — grading, allowable stresses, connections
- **Foundation Systems and Site Conditions** — shallow foundations, bearing capacity
- **Deep Foundations and Geotechnical Fundamentals** — piles, caissons, driven vs. bored
- **Lateral Force Systems and Seismic Design** — shear walls, moment frames, braced frames, NSCP seismic provisions
- **Wind Load Design and Classification** — pressure calculations, exposure categories, PH wind zones
- **Masonry Systems and Design** — unreinforced and reinforced masonry
- **Composite Structures and Special Structural Systems** — composite beams, long-span systems
- **Reading Structural Drawings and NSCP Application** — plans, sections, structural details

## Suggested Study Sequence

### Phase 1 — Build the Foundation (Weeks 1–2)

Start with **Statics and Mechanics of Materials**. If you cannot draw a free body diagram, nothing downstream makes sense. Then cover **Loads and Load Combinations** — understand dead, live, wind, and seismic loads physically.

### Phase 2 — Learn the Materials (Weeks 3–4)

Cover **Reinforced Concrete**, **Steel**, and **Timber** in sequence. For each: How does it behave under tension, compression, bending, shear? What are its failure modes? What does the NSCP allow?

### Phase 3 — Understand the Systems (Weeks 5–6)

Move from materials to systems: **Foundation Systems**, **Deep Foundations**, **Lateral Force Systems**, **Wind**, **Masonry**, and **Composite Structures**.

### Phase 4 — Integrate and Apply (Weeks 7–8)

Practice reading **structural drawings** and applying NSCP provisions to multi-material scenarios.

## Common Exam Traps

- **Skipping statics fundamentals.** You must understand equilibrium, internal forces, and shear/moment diagrams before approaching NSCP provisions.
- **Treating load combinations as memorization.** Understand *why* NSCP requires 1.2D + 1.6L — it's about probability and consequence.
- **Neglecting seismic and lateral systems.** The Philippines is on the Pacific Ring of Fire — seismic design is not a minor subtopic.
- **Not practicing with actual structural drawings.** You need to read structural plans, identify column grids, read rebar schedules, and understand section cuts.
- **Confusing NSCP chapters.** Chapter 2 = seismic, Chapter 4 = concrete, Chapter 5 = steel, Chapter 6 = timber, Chapter 7 = masonry.
