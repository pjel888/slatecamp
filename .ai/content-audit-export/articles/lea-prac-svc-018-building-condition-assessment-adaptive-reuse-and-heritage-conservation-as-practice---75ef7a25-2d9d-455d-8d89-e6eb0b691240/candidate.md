# Building Condition Assessment, Adaptive Reuse, and Heritage Conservation

## Learning Goal

Use RA 10066 and UAP SPP 203 to keep existing-building and heritage work source-safe: define adaptive reuse and conservation from law, and treat heritage-related practice as specialized architectural service work.

## Source-Safe Scope

This article uses a conservative source set. RA 10066 supplies the statutory vocabulary for adaptive reuse, built heritage, and conservation. SPP 203 supplies the professional-practice category by listing specialized architectural services, including historic and cultural heritage conservation and planning, facilities maintenance support, building testing and commissioning, building environmental certification, and building appraisal.

The local sources support a careful exam frame: existing-building work is not automatically the same as basic new-building design. It should be scoped and documented as a professional service, with heritage and adaptive reuse terms anchored in RA 10066.

## Adaptive Reuse

RA 10066 defines adaptive reuse as using buildings, structures, or sites of value for purposes other than their original use in order to conserve the site, its engineering integrity, and the authenticity of its design. Do not reduce adaptive reuse to decoration or a simple change in occupancy label.

## Built Heritage

RA 10066 describes built heritage as architectural and engineering structures, and their settings and landscapes, with notable historical and cultural significance. For exam purposes, the setting can matter along with the structure.

## Conservation

RA 10066 uses conservation for all processes and measures that maintain cultural significance, including preservation, restoration, reconstruction, protection, adaptation, or a combination of these. It also describes conservation as an integrated and holistic approach across disciplines and technologies.

## Professional Practice Cue

SPP 203 places historic and cultural heritage conservation and planning under specialized architectural services. It also lists building appraisal, building testing and commissioning, and facilities maintenance support. When the project involves an existing or heritage building, the architect should clarify the service scope, evidence needed, deliverables, and limits of advice rather than assuming the work is covered by ordinary design deliverables alone.
