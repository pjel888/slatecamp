# Design Brief and Architectural Programming

In the checked Design Process source, the design brief is best treated as a programming task: define the architectural problem and organize the information needed before design solutions are chosen.

## What The Brief Must Clarify

- The existing state: setting, physical site conditions, data, client or user profile, and constraints.
- The future state: mission, goals, performance requirements, and concepts.
- The program document: issues, facts, values, goals, performance requirements, and concepts.

## How To Read It

A brief is not the finished design. It is the organized statement of what the project must solve. The source describes programming as gathering, organizing, analyzing, interpreting, and presenting information relevant to the project.

## Exam Use

When a question gives a project brief, identify what fact is objective, what value or goal is being pursued, and what performance requirement must be met. Then choose the option that answers the requirement without inventing unsupported conditions.

## Common Trap

Do not answer from a preferred design style before reading the brief. The checked source puts problem definition before proposal-making.
