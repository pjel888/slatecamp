# SPP Document 206: Standards of Professional Practice on Comprehensive Architectural Services

## Overview

**SPP Document 206** governs **Comprehensive Architectural Services (CAS)** — the broadest and most integrative SPP document in the entire 2010 series. It replaces the **1979 UAP Doc. 206** and forms part of the Supplemental Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of **Republic Act No. 9266** (The Architecture Act of 2004).

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to:

Identify the scope of SPP Doc 206 Comprehensive Architectural Services — the full spectrum of pre-design through post-construction services combined into a single commission; explain how the 15% ceiling on basic fee for comprehensive services is calculated; determine when a comprehensive service agreement is appropriate vs separate SPP Doc agreements for each phase.
---
SPP 206 is not a standalone service but an **umbrella framework** that consolidates the entire range of services from SPP 201 through SPP 205 under the management of a single professional — the **Project Manager (PM)**. Understanding SPP 206 requires thorough knowledge of all preceding SPP documents, as CAS is only meaningful when each of its component services is fully understood.

***

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The Evolution of Comprehensive Architectural Services

The concept of Comprehensive Architectural Services **crystallized through the years in response to the demands of emerging complex building projects**. The architectural profession's transition from providing "basic" and "additional" services to services of a comprehensive nature came from a critical realization: **continuity of services related to design and construction is necessary for the execution of a completely viable project**.

In this extended dimension, **the Architect is the prime professional** — functioning as creator, author, and coordinator of the building design that becomes the basis for construction. The Architect must be knowledgeable in other fields in addition to building design in order to properly assist and serve the Client.

A critical policy principle in SPP 206 is that **the Architect is NOT expected to perform all the services personally**. Rather, the Architect acts as the **agent of the Client** in procuring and coordinating the necessary services required by a project. CAS is not about one person doing everything — it is about one professional orchestrating everything on behalf of the owner.

### 1.2 What is Project Management (PM)?

When the Owner hires an Architect or a firm to **coordinate the whole range of CAS**, this constitutes **Project Management (PM)**. PM is formally defined as:

<Callout variant="note">

*"Management activities over and above the normal architectural and engineering (A&E) services carried out during the pre-design, design, and construction phase. The overall objective is to have control over time, cost, and quality relative to the construction of a project."*

</Callout>


Three foundational principles govern the PM role:

1. **The PM does NOT relieve designers or contractors** of their respective normal duties and responsibilities in design and construction
2. **The PM complements** the functions of Architects, Engineers, and Contractors in meeting the broad and complex requirements of projects
3. **The PM's primary responsibility** is the exercise of **overall cost control** — planning, programming, and monitoring activities, and advising on material costs and construction methods

## 2. Scope of Services

The scope of SPP 206 is the entire continuum of services from SPP 201 through SPP 205, plus a specifically defined PM scope.

### 2.1 Comprehensive Architectural Services

CAS encompasses all five service clusters from the preceding SPP documents:

#### 2.1.1 Pre-Design Services (SPP Doc. 201)

All 12 pre-design services under SPP 201:

- Consultation
- Pre-Feasibility Studies
- Feasibility Studies
- Site Selection and Analysis
- Site Utilization and Land-Use Studies
- Architectural Research
- Architectural Programming
- Space Planning
- Space Management Studies
- Value Management
- Design Brief Preparation
- Promotional Services

#### 2.1.2 Regular Design Services (SPP Doc. 202)

All 6 phases of the regular design service under SPP 202:

- Project Definition Phase
- Schematic Design Phase
- Design Development Phase
- Contract Documents Phase
- Bidding or Negotiation Phase
- Construction Phase

#### 2.1.3 Specialized Architectural Services (SPP Doc. 203)

All 23 specialized services under SPP 203:

- Architectural Interiors (AI)
- Acoustic Design
- Architectural Lighting Layout and Design
- Site Development Planning (SDP)
- Site and Physical Planning Services (including Master Development Planning, Subdivision Planning, and Urban Design)
- Comprehensive Development Planning (CDP)
- Historic and Cultural Heritage Conservation and Planning
- Security Evaluation and Planning
- Building Systems Design
- Facilities Maintenance Support
- Building Testing and Commissioning
- Building Environmental Certification
- Forensic Architecture
- Building Appraisal
- Structural Conceptualization
- Preliminary Services
- Contract Documentation and Review
- Post-Design Services (including Construction Management Services)
- Dispute Avoidance and Resolution
- Architectural Research Methods
- Special Building/Facility Planning and Design
- Building Components
- Management of Architectural Practices

#### 2.1.4 Construction Services (SPP Docs. 204-A and 204-B)

- Fulltime Supervision Services (SPP Document 204-A)
- Construction Management Services (SPP Document 204-B)

#### 2.1.5 Post-Construction Services (SPP Doc. 205)

- Building and Facilities Administration
- Post-Construction Evaluation

### 2.2 Project Management Scope

In addition to coordinating all CAS services, the PM has a specifically defined management scope across two phases:

#### Pre-Construction Phase

<Callout variant="note">

*"As early as during the design development phase, perhaps even concurrently with the Architect's commission, the Project Manager (PM) should conduct regular consultations with the Owner and with the Architects and Engineers (for A&E services) on all aspects of planning for the project."*

</Callout>


This means the PM's engagement begins **before construction starts** — ideally during the design development stage — so that programming, procurement planning, budget controls, and contractor pre-qualification are all in place before the project goes to bid.

#### Construction Phase

<Callout variant="note">

*"If the Project Manager (PM) also serves as the Construction Manager (CM) to oversee time, cost, and quality control during the construction of the project, he shall provide the services detailed under SPP Documents 204-A and/or 204-B."*

</Callout>


The PM's default role during construction is **coordination and oversight**. If the PM also takes on the CM role, the full SPP 204-A/204-B scope applies and additional compensation is owed.

## 3. Manner of Providing Services

### 3.1 Comprehensive Architectural Services

The normal engagement model is straightforward: **the Architect enters into a single contract with the Owner** to perform Comprehensive Architectural Services. By the very nature of the CAS contract, the Architect **assumes the dual role of the Project Manager (PM) and the Construction Manager (CM)** — or effectively, the overall coordinator whose functions are outlined under SPP 206.

To perform the variety of CAS services, the Architect **must make full use of his own capability as well as services offered by other professionals**. The Architect may:

1. **Expand his own staff** by hiring the experts needed, or
2. **Form a multi-disciplinary team** consisting of professionals such as (but not limited to):

<ComparisonTable>

| Professional Type | Role in CAS Team |
|---|---|
| Architects | Design, documentation, site supervision |
| Engineers | Structural, M/E/P systems design |
| Market Analysts | Feasibility and demand studies |
| Accountants | Cost tracking and financial reporting |
| General Contractors | Construction implementation |
| Real Estate Consultants | Site analysis, land use advisory |
| Sociologists | Community impact, occupancy behavior |
| Planners | Urban design, land use planning |
| Bankers | Project financing and cash flow |
| Lawyers | Contracts, permits, legal compliance |

</ComparisonTable>


This multi-disciplinary team model is the defining feature of CAS — the Architect as PM does not work alone, but assembles and leads the best team for the specific project.

### 3.2 Project Management (PM)

The PM's organizational relationships under SPP 206 are specifically defined:

#### 3.2.1 PM and the Construction Manager / Fulltime Supervisor

If a PM is hired by the Owner, the PM may:

- **Hire the CM**, with the CM compensated either by the PM directly or by the Owner directly (on salary or percentage of construction cost), OR
- **Serve as the CM himself**, assuming the full SPP 204-B scope

In like manner, the **Fulltime Supervisor (under SPP 204-A)** can either be a staff member of the PM or hired directly by the Owner.

#### 3.2.2 The Owner-Architect-Engineer-Contractor Team

The PM operates as a **member of the Owner-Architect-Engineer-Contractor (OAEC) Team**. In this Team Approach, each member has precedence in their own field of expertise:

<ComparisonTable>

| Team Member | Primary Responsibility | Authority Domain |
|---|---|---|
| **Architect & Specialist Consultants** | Prime responsibility for plan/design of the project | Design decisions |
| **Engineers** | Responsible for respective engineering plans | Structural, M/E/P design |
| **Contractor** | Responsible for men, equipment, and delivery of the project | Construction methods |
| **Owner** | Makes decisions on the project; assures funds are available | Final approvals, financing |
| **Project Manager (PM)** | Primary responsibility for **overall cost control**; plans, programs, and monitors activities; advises on material costs and construction methods | Cost, schedule, quality oversight |

</ComparisonTable>


The PM sits atop the coordination hierarchy — not as a superior to the designer or contractor in their respective domains, but as the **integrating authority** who ensures all team members deliver on schedule, within budget, and at the required quality level.

## 4. Method of Compensation

SPP 206 is notably concise in its compensation provisions — reflecting that the detailed fee schedules are contained in the **Architect's Guidelines** rather than in the SPP text itself.

### 4.1 PM Compensation: Percentage of Project Construction Cost

The primary compensation method for the Project Manager is a **percentage of the Project Construction Cost (PCC)**:

<Callout variant="note">

*"The Project Manager is compensated on a percentage basis, as shall be described in the Architect's Guidelines."*

</Callout>


The **Recommended Professional Fee (RPF) for Project Management Services is 2% to 5% of the Project Construction Cost (PCC)**. The exact rate within this range depends on:

- The complexity and scale of the project
- Whether the PM also serves as CM and/or CSG
- The extent of specialized services engaged under SPP 203
- The duration of the project lifecycle

### 4.2 Separate Compensation for Design Services

A critical rule: **if the Architect as PM also performs regular design services for the same project, those design services shall be compensated SEPARATELY** as stipulated in SPP Document 202.

This means a PM who also acts as the Aor is entitled to **two separate fees**:
- **PM Fee**: 2–5% of PCC under SPP 206
- **Design Fee**: As per SPP 202 minimum basic fee schedules (% of PCC based on project group)

The two fees are cumulative and are not mutually exclusive.

### 4.3 Additional Compensation Methods

While the percentage method is primary, the Architect's Guidelines also recognize additional methods applicable to CAS/PM services:

<ComparisonTable>

| Method | Applicability |
|---|---|
| **Multiple of Direct Personnel Expense (MDPE)** | Variable-scope pre-construction PM activities |
| **Professional Fee Plus Expenses (PFPE)** | Ongoing PM engagement with defined deliverables |
| **Lump Sum / Fixed Fee** | Government projects; well-defined PM scope |

</ComparisonTable>

## 5. CAS vs. Individual SPP Engagements

A critical question in practice is: **when should a client engage CAS under SPP 206 vs. engaging individual SPP 201–205 services separately?**

<ComparisonTable>

| Factor | Individual SPP Engagements | Comprehensive Architectural Services (SPP 206) |
|---|---|---|
| **Project Complexity** | Suitable for straightforward single-phase projects | Best for complex, multi-phase, multi-contractor projects |
| **Owner's Capacity** | Owner has staff to coordinate consultants and contractors | Owner lacks capacity for multi-party coordination |
| **Continuity** | Multiple separate contracts; risk of coordination gaps | Single contract; PM maintains continuity across all phases |
| **Cost Accountability** | Each SPP contract has separate fee | PM fee (2–5% PCC) added on top of individual service fees |
| **Design Responsibility** | Aor has full design responsibility | Aor retains design responsibility; PM adds management layer |
| **Construction Oversight** | CSG/CM hired separately | PM coordinates CSG/CM, may assume both roles |

</ComparisonTable>


The key insight is that **CAS does not replace the individual services** — it adds a management layer on top of them. The total cost of a CAS engagement includes both the individual SPP fees and the PM fee.

## 6. CAS and the Architect as Prime Professional

SPP 206 articulates the most complete expression of the **Architect's role as Prime Professional** in the construction process. The three dimensions of this primacy are:

### 6.1 Creator
The Architect conceives the design — translating the Owner's program and vision into spatial, aesthetic, and technical proposals that form the basis for all subsequent work.

### 6.2 Author
The Architect produces the contract documents — drawings, specifications, and reports — that become the legal and technical basis for construction and for all regulatory approvals.

### 6.3 Coordinator
As PM under CAS, the Architect coordinates the entire team — from market analysts and lawyers in the pre-design phase, through engineers and contractors during construction, to maintenance contractors and appraisers in post-construction — ensuring that each specialist's contribution integrates into a coherent, viable project.

This three-dimensional primacy (Creator–Author–Coordinator) is what distinguishes the Architect's CAS role from that of a pure project manager from another profession — the architect brings both the **creative and technical authority** over the design and the **management authority** over the project lifecycle.

## 8. Practical Notes for Architects and Clients

**For Architects:**

- The PM fee of **2–5% of PCC** is in addition to — not inclusive of — the SPP 202 design fee and any SPP 203/204/205 specialist fees. The total professional fees on a full CAS project can be substantial; ensure that the owner understands and budgets for the full fee structure before engagement.
- When assembling the CAS team (Section 3.1.2), the Architect as PM should formalize the team structure through **sub-consultancy agreements or joint venture arrangements** — clearly defining scope, fee-sharing, liability, and deliverables for each team member.
- The PM does NOT absorb liability from designers or contractors — each professional retains liability within their own scope. This must be reflected in the Owner-PM agreement and in each sub-consultant agreement to prevent liability ambiguity.

**For Clients / Project Proponents:**

- Engaging an Architect under SPP 206 as PM means **paying two separate fee streams**: the PM coordination fee (2–5% PCC) and the individual SPP 202 design fee (per project group). Clients should request a full fee schedule upfront that itemizes all SPP fees applicable to their project.
- CAS is most valuable for **large, complex, or phased development projects** where the owner cannot effectively coordinate architects, engineers, specialists, contractors, and administrators directly. For a simple residential or small commercial project, engaging all SPP services individually may be more cost-effective.
- The **Owner remains the ultimate decision-maker** even under CAS — the PM advises, coordinates, and manages, but the Owner retains authority over all final approvals and funding decisions. CAS does not transfer project ownership decisions to the PM.

## Exam Tip

ALE Trap: Comprehensive = SPP 201 + 202 + 204 + 205 in a single contract. Basic fee = 15% of construction cost (max). Not included: specialized services (203). Comprehensive is a BUNDLE — the 15% ceiling applies to the bundle, not to each phase. The most tested: if a client asks for 'all services', it likely triggers SPP 206 (Comprehensive), not just SPP 202.
