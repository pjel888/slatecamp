# SPP Document 206: Comprehensive Architectural Services

## Board Exam Quick Check

For SPP 206 questions, first decide whether the item is asking about Comprehensive Architectural Services (CAS) or Project Management (PM). The source treats CAS as a full service range and PM as the coordination of that range. Do not import fixed fee ranges unless the checked source states them.

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to identify what SPP 206 covers, explain why PM does not replace the duties of designers or contractors, and recognize the architect's coordinating role in a comprehensive commission.

## Source-Audited Core

SPP 206 defines Comprehensive Architectural Services as the professional service range covering pre-design services, regular design services, specialized architectural services, construction services, and post-construction services. It is a full-lifecycle framework, not a shortcut that erases the separate service categories in SPP 201 through SPP 205.

In this extended service dimension, the architect is the prime professional. The source describes the architect as creator, author, and coordinator of the building design that becomes the basis for construction. The same source also states that the architect is not expected to perform every service personally. Instead, the architect acts as the client's agent in procuring and coordinating the services required by the project.

Project Management begins when the owner hires an architect or firm to coordinate the whole CAS range. PM involves management activities beyond normal architectural and engineering services during pre-design, design, and construction. Its objective is control of time, cost, and quality.

The presence of a PM does not relieve designers and contractors of their normal duties and responsibilities. The PM complements architects, engineers, and contractors. Under the team approach, the architect and specialist consultants keep prime responsibility for the plan and design, engineers keep responsibility for their engineering plans, the contractor is responsible for delivery through labor and equipment, the owner makes project decisions and assures funds, and the PM's primary responsibility is overall cost control.

## Scope Map

- SPP 201: pre-design services, including consultation, feasibility, site analysis, programming, space planning, value management, design brief preparation, and related work.
- SPP 202: regular design services, from project definition through construction phase.
- SPP 203: specialized architectural services, including interiors, acoustics, lighting, site development planning, heritage, security, building systems, commissioning, environmental certification, forensic architecture, appraisal, structural conceptualization, post-design services, and related specializations.
- SPP 204-A and SPP 204-B: full-time supervision and construction management.
- SPP 205: building and facilities administration and post-construction evaluation.

## PM Timing and Compensation

The source says the PM should conduct consultations with the owner and A&E team as early as the design development phase, perhaps concurrently with the architect's commission. If the PM also serves as CM during construction, the PM provides the services detailed under SPP 204-A and/or SPP 204-B.

The source-safe compensation statement is narrow: the PM is compensated on a percentage basis as described in the Architect's Guidelines. If the architect as PM also performs regular design services for the same project, those regular design services are compensated separately under SPP 202.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating CAS as a replacement for SPP 201 through SPP 205.
- Assuming the PM absorbs the legal duties of designers and contractors.
- Memorizing a fee range not stated in the checked local source.
- Saying the architect must personally perform every CAS component.
