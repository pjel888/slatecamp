# De Stijl, Constructivism, and Expressionism

## LEA Point

This bundle is a movement-comparison test. De Stijl, Constructivism, and Expressionism all reject academic historicism and applied ornament, but they do not mean the same thing.

## Source Anchors

- De Stijl is anchored by UNESCO through Rietveld Schroder House, built in 1924 by Gerrit Rietveld in Utrecht, with flexible space and visual/formal qualities as a manifesto of De Stijl ideals.
- Constructivism is anchored through Tatlin's Monument to the Third International and industrial/revolutionary abstraction, plus Melnikov House as a 1927-1929 avant-garde house-studio by Konstantin Melnikov.
- Expressionism is anchored by AIP through Einstein Tower, Erich Mendelsohn's 1919-1924 solar observatory, often cited as an architectural Expressionism example.
- Local HOA/theory anchors support Rietveld, Tatlin, Melnikov, and Mendelsohn as exam-recognition names.

## Exam Pattern

If the stem says primary colors, orthogonal planes, flexible spatial arrangement, and Rietveld, answer De Stijl. If it says Tatlin, revolutionary monument, industrial apparatus, or Melnikov's cylinders, answer Constructivism. If it says Mendelsohn, Einstein Tower, sculptural/curving form, and emotional intensity, answer Expressionism.

## Guardrails

Do not keep the older Futurism title for this article; the linked questions and available source anchors are about Expressionism. Do not call Einstein Tower a residence. Do not describe De Stijl as symmetric decoration; its exam cue is disciplined orthogonal abstraction and flexible space.

## Real World Scenario

For a museum gallery comparing early modern movements, a source-safe display would put Rietveld Schroder House, Tatlin Tower, Melnikov House, and Einstein Tower side by side so students compare form language before memorizing dates.
