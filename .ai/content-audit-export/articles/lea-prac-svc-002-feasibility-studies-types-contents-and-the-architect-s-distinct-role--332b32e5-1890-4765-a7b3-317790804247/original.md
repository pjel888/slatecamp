## Feasibility Studies: Types, Contents, and the Architect's Distinct Role

Feasibility studies represent a critical pre-design service that determines whether a proposed development is viable. SPP 201 distinguishes between pre-feasibility studies, which use secondary information for early decision-making, and detailed feasibility studies, which analyze the project comprehensively against present and future trends.

### Pre-Feasibility Studies vs. Feasibility Studies

Pre-feasibility studies involve procurement, analysis, and use of secondary information gathered for the project to aid the client in early decision-making. They represent the architect's initial assessment of a project's soundness. Feasibility studies, by contrast, provide detailed analysis of the project based on pre-feasibility findings and determine the viability of a proposed development. These studies require primary data gathering and analysis to forecast how a project will perform over time.

### Contents of a Detailed Feasibility Study

A comprehensive feasibility study sets the proposed development against present and future trends to forecast performance. The architect examines financial viability, market demand, technical feasibility, legal and regulatory compliance, and environmental impact. The study typically includes site analysis, market research findings, financial projections, risk assessment, and recommendations for proceeding or modifying the project concept.

### The Architect's Distinct Professional Role

The architect's role in feasibility studies differs fundamentally from the roles of financial consultants or market analysts. The architect brings technical and design expertise to assess physical feasibility, site constraints, regulatory compliance, and constructability. The architect must clearly define the scope of feasibility work within the pre-design services proposal and distinguish between advisory activities and formal study deliverables. This clarity protects both the architect's professional standing and the client's understanding of what the architect will provide.
