# Feasibility Studies and the Architect's Distinct Role

## Learning Goal

Use UAP SPP 201 to distinguish consultation, pre-feasibility studies, feasibility studies, site analysis, research, and programming as pre-design services.

## Why Pre-design Matters

SPP 201 states that involving the architect in the earliest project stages is advantageous to the client because the architect can provide objective project analysis and establish parameters to optimize building needs against resources and constraints.

That is the professional-practice point: the architect's pre-design work helps the client decide whether and how to proceed before design production becomes the main task.

## Consultation

Consultation occurs when the client asks the architect for oral or written advice and direction, conference attendance, evaluations, appraisals, and similar activities for a contemplated project. SPP 201 treats this as valuable input whether or not the client proceeds.

## Pre-feasibility Studies

Pre-feasibility studies are preliminary studies using secondary information gathered for the project. They help the client with early decision-making and represent the architect's initial assessment of the project's soundness.

## Feasibility Studies

Feasibility studies are a detailed analysis based on pre-feasibility studies to determine the viability of a proposed development. SPP 201 states that they set the project against present and future trends and require primary data gathering and analysis.

## Related Pre-design Services

SPP 201 also includes site selection and analysis, site utilization and land-use studies, architectural research, and architectural programming. Site selection includes formulation of site criteria and assistance in site evaluation. Land-use studies analyze site potentials, surrounding context, and applicable development controls. Architectural research uses primary and secondary research as a basis for conclusions. Programming is an analytical problem-seeking process used to identify project requirements.

## Compensation Cue

For this SPP 201 section, the method of compensation is based on adding technical service costs, using man-hours multiplied by rate, then applying a multiplier for overhead and profit. The multiplier range stated in the source is 1.5 to 2.5 depending on office setup, overhead, experience, and project complexity.

## Real World Scenario

A client is considering a mixed-use project on a constrained site but has not yet decided whether to acquire the property. The architect should not jump straight into schematic design. A source-safe response is to define the needed pre-design service: consultation for advice, pre-feasibility when the work relies on secondary information, feasibility when primary data and present-or-future trends are analyzed, and site selection or land-use study when the decision depends on site criteria, context, and development controls.

In an LEA item, choose the answer that keeps those services before regular design. The architect may help the client decide whether the project is viable, but that decision support is still different from producing SPP 202 design-phase deliverables.
