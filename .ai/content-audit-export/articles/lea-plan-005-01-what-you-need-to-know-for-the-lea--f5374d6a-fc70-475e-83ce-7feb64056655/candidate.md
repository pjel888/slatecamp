# Landscape Design

Landscape design organizes outdoor land so it supports use, climate response, drainage, access, ecology, and visual quality. In the checked sources, landscape work is not a decorative afterthought; it is part of site planning and open-space performance.

## What landscape design includes

Landscape design combines softscape, hardscape, grading, drainage, plant selection, open space, paths, paving, shade, and site furnishings. Sitework sources connect landscape decisions to soils, fill, drainage, paving, and surfacing. BCI connects landscape decisions to existing trees, native plants, ground cover, sun, wind, views, circulation, and outdoor use.

## Planting as performance

Plant material can define space, provide shade, filter wind and dust, screen views, reduce erosion, moderate temperature, and improve permeability when used with suitable ground cover. Existing healthy trees and native plants should be evaluated before removal because they may already provide shade, character, habitat, and soil protection.

## Open space as infrastructure

The Open Space Development Guidelines treat open spaces as ecological and social infrastructure. They include blue open spaces, green open spaces, and non-blue non-green open spaces. The guidelines support protecting, managing, enhancing, regenerating, or creating open spaces, and they connect open-space planning to stormwater, accessibility, maintenance, and community engagement.

BP 220 and PD 957 add subdivision-specific duties: parks, playgrounds, and open spaces are not leftover land. Site planning must consider drainage, streets, site assets, and nonbuildable open-space functions.

DOE guidance supports landscape decisions that improve orientation response, shading, ventilation, and solar-heat control.

## LEA answer method

For exam items, choose the landscape answer that improves site performance and user experience while staying inside the checked source facts. Avoid unsupported claims about fixed percentages, national-tree rules, or copied design examples unless the item gives a source.

## Source-safe takeaways

- Landscape design integrates softscape, hardscape, grading, drainage, planting, open space, access, and shade.
- Plants and ground cover can support shade, erosion control, permeability, screening, wind filtering, and outdoor comfort.
- Open spaces are ecological and social infrastructure, not unused land.
- BP 220 and PD 957 connect landscape decisions to parks, playgrounds, drainage, and preservation of site assets.
- DOE climate-response guidance supports shading, ventilation, and solar-control decisions.
