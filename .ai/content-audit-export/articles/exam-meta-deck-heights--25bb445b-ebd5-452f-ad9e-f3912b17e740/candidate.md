## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Treat this as a source-checked height ladder for BP 344 and PD 1096 guard, handrail, and route-clearance questions. The controlling rule depends on the element named in the question: accessible stair or ramp handrail, protective railing or guard, general stair handrail, corridor or exit balcony width, or projecting signage clearance.

BP 344 requires handrails on accessible ramps and stairs at 700 mm and 900 mm above the walking surface or stair tread. BP 344 also requires protective railings at a minimum height of 1100 mm for stairs, ramps, balconies, landings, or porches that are more than 750 mm above adjacent grade.

## Core Concept

PD 1096 supports the same guard trigger for balconies, landings, and porches: railings are provided when they are more than 750 mm above grade. For general stair handrails under PD 1096, handrails are placed not less than 800 mm and not more than 900 mm above the nosing of treads.

For circulation clearances, PD 1096 sets every corridor or exterior exit balcony serving a required exit for an occupant load over ten at not less than 1.10 m wide. BP 344 separately requires 2200 mm minimum vertical clearance for signage or protrusions that extend into a walkway or route.

## How It Is Tested

Read the element before choosing the number. If the question says accessible ramp or accessible stair handrails, use the BP 344 dual rail heights: 700 mm and 900 mm. If it asks for protective railing at an elevated balcony, landing, porch, stair, or ramp, use the 1100 mm minimum guard height once the drop is more than 750 mm. If it asks for ordinary PD 1096 stair handrail height, use the 800 mm to 900 mm band.

## Common Mistakes

Do not collapse all rail numbers into one answer. A handrail assists movement; a guard or railing prevents falling. The BP 344 accessible handrail rule uses 700 mm and 900 mm, while the protective railing rule uses a 1100 mm minimum height when the adjacent grade difference is more than 750 mm.
