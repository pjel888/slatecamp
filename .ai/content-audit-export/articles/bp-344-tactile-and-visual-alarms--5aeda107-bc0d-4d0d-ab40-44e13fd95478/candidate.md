# BP 344 Tactile and Visual Alarms

## Learning Goal

Use BP 344 2024 Revised IRR provisions for tactile surfaces, accessible signage, emergency call signals, visual alarms, and audio-visual evacuation signals.

## Signage and Tactile Information

BP 344 requires audio, visual, and tactile directional and information signage at points that can be conveniently seen, heard, and felt by persons with disabilities. Signage must be simple and easy to understand, use contrasting colors, and have minimum luminance contrast of 30 percent from surroundings.

Signage labeling public rooms, areas, and places must have tactile symbols, letters, or numbers embossed with a minimum height of 1 mm, and Braille symbols must be incorporated in signs indicating public places and safety routes.

## Tactile Floor Surfaces

Tactile floor surfaces must guide people with visual disabilities and must be situated at least 600 mm clear from tripping hazards, walls, columns, and other confusing elements. Tactile floor surfaces must have a minimum luminance contrast of 50 percent from adjacent floor finishes.

Warning tactile blocks have a depth of 600 mm and are placed perpendicular to the hazard or target object with a 300 mm setback. The IRR identifies uses at stairs, ramps, elevators, escalators, accessible toilets, and tactile information maps.

## Alarm Signals

Accessible toilet emergency call buttons must emit an audible or visible alarm signal. Strobe light alarms in accessible toilet compartments alert persons with hearing disabilities and must follow the Fire Code and NFPA 72 limits stated in the source.

For fire alarm systems, BP 344 states that audio and visual alarm systems must be installed on all floors in compliance with the latest Fire Code. Evacuation notification signals must be audible and visible in accordance with NFPA 72 or another approved means determined by the City or Municipal Fire Marshal.

## Practice Cue

Do not treat tactile surfaces, Braille, visual alarms, and audible signals as interchangeable. BP 344 combines multiple channels so persons with visual, hearing, mobility, and sensory disabilities receive usable information.
