## Board Exam Quick Check

When a question asks what the architect may do during construction, do not assume unlimited owner authority. UAP Document 301 frames the architect as the owner's representative during construction only to the extent expressly provided in the Contract Documents or otherwise in writing and shown to the contractor.

## Learning Goal

After reading this article, you should be able to:

- Identify the contract source of the architect's construction-stage authority.
- Recognize the architect's power to decide quality, acceptability, interpretation, performance, and progress questions.
- Explain why the architect must act impartially between owner and contractor.
- Separate project-representative assistance from contractor responsibility.

## Authority Comes From the Contract

UAP Document 301 states that the architect observes the work on behalf of the owner during the construction period. The same provision limits the authority to act for the owner to what is expressly provided in the Contract Documents or otherwise given in writing and shown to the contractor.

That limitation matters in exam scenarios. The architect can administer the contract and make contract-based decisions, but the architect does not become a free-standing substitute for the owner on every financial, scope, or legal decision.

## Decisions the Architect May Make

UAP Document 301 gives the architect authority to decide questions about the quality and acceptability of materials furnished and work performed, the manner of performance and rate of progress, interpretation of drawings and specifications, and acceptable fulfillment of the contract terms. It also states that the architect may stop the work when stoppage is necessary, in the architect's reasonable opinion, to ensure proper execution of the contract.

The source also defines common contract words. Unless otherwise stipulated, terms such as approved, directed, acceptable, and satisfactory point to the architect's judgment within the contract administration role.

## Limits and Contractor Responsibility

The architect's authority does not erase the contractor's own obligations. UAP Document 301 states that the architect's failure to order stopping of the work does not relieve the contractor from complying with the Contract Documents or from the contractor's responsibility to protect persons and property.

Project representatives, resident architects, resident engineers, or construction inspectors may assist the architect and engineers in general supervision and direction of the work. If a dispute arises between those representatives and the contractor, the question is referred to the architect. That assistance still does not relieve the contractor from performing the work in accordance with the drawings, specifications, and other Contract Documents.

## Impartial Contract Administration

UAP Document 301 says the architect is, in the first instance, the interpreter of the contract conditions and judge of performance. In that role, the architect should side neither with the owner nor with the contractor, but should use contract powers to enforce faithful performance by both.

The UAP Code of Ethics supports the same professional posture. It describes the architect's advice as unprejudiced and the architect's judicial functions between client and contractor as impartial.

## Real World Scenario

A site architect is asked to approve a cheaper substitute material after contract award because the contractor missed a specification conflict during bidding. The source-safe response is not "approve it to keep the project moving." UAP Document 301 points first to the Contract Documents, written architect decisions on drawing/specification conflicts, and the architect's limited authority as the owner's representative. The architect may interpret contract requirements and refer the matter through the contract process, but the contractor remains responsible for conforming work and cannot treat silence or missed stoppage as a waiver.

## How It Is Tested

Expect substitution, defective work, progress, payment, and interpretation scenarios. The safest answer usually asks: What do the Contract Documents authorize? Is the architect deciding quality, interpretation, performance, or progress? Does the decision require owner financial authorization? Does the contractor remain responsible for the work?

## Common Mistakes

- Treating the architect as having unlimited owner authority.
- Treating the architect's failure to stop work as a waiver of contractor responsibility.
- Treating project representatives as independent final decision-makers.
- Forgetting that the architect must act impartially when interpreting the contract.

## Source Anchors

- UAP Document 301: contract-document conflicts before proposal, architect status, decision authority, project representatives, and schedule of time limits.
- UAP Code of Ethics: unprejudiced client advice, impartial judicial function between client and contractor, and fair assistance to the contractor.
