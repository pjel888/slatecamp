# Architect's Guidelines

SPP 202 organizes regular design services around project classification and compensation method. The project groups are based on degree of complexity and creative skill, within technical, functional, economic, and aesthetic constraints. For LEA work, the safe approach is to classify from the source descriptions before discussing compensation.

## Project groups in SPP 202

Groups 1 to 5 describe increasing categories of building or facility character, from simplest utilization through moderate complexity, exceptional character, residences and small residential projects, and monumental buildings or facilities. Group 6 covers reuse of plans, designs, and related contract documents for repetitive construction without amendments. Group 7 covers housing projects with several residential units on one site using one set of documents. Group 8 covers work that requires extensive detail, including built-in components, interiors, and development planning or design. Group 9 covers alterations, renovations, rehabilitation, retrofit, expansions, or additions to existing buildings in Groups 1 to 5. Group 10 covers opinion, advice, clarifications, or explanation on technical architectural matters.

## Compensation methods

SPP 202 lists six methods of compensation for regular design services: percentage of Project Construction Cost, multiple of direct personnel expense, professional fee plus expenses, lump sum or fixed fee, per diem or honorarium plus reimbursable expenses, and mixed methods. The source also states that each project should be examined to determine the most appropriate compensation method.

## Conditions that affect fees

The source identifies additional conditions that matter in practice. Additional services require additional compensation. Specialist services and items such as scale models are paid separately, with coordination compensation for the architect. Owner changes after approval require payment for extra time and resources. If work is abandoned or suspended after detailed design and contract documents, the source treats the architect as having completed most of the work for fee purposes. Project Construction Cost includes the building work and systems in the architect's and consultants' plans and specifications, but excludes professional fees and construction inspector salaries.
