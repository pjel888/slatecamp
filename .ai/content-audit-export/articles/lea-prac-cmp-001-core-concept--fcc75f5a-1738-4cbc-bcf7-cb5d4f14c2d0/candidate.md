The LEA tests your ability to classify a project into the correct group and apply the correct fee percentage to the Project Construction Cost (PCC). ## Core Concept

The Architect's Guidelines establish **ten Project Classification Groups** that determine the architect's professional fee as a percentage of the Project Construction Cost (PCC). Groups 1 through 5 are classified by **complexity** (Groups 1–3) and **building type** (Groups 4–5). Groups 6 and 7 address **repetitive projects** with declining fee structures. Group 8 covers **interior design and site planning**. Group 9 applies a **multiplier** for renovations. Group 10 uses an entirely different compensation method.

The percentages stated for Groups 1–3 represent **DAEDS (Detailed Architectural and Engineering Design Services)**, which applies when a single architect coordinates all design disciplines under one contract. When engineering services are procured separately through independent contracts, the architect provides **DADS (Detailed Architectural Design Services)** at approximately 60% of the DAEDS rate. For example, Group 1 DAEDS is 5%, while Group 1 DADS is 3%.

Classification is the first step in fee determination under the percentage-of-PCC method. The architect identifies which group the project belongs to, then applies the group's percentage to the PCC to calculate the basic fee. Getting the group wrong means getting the fee wrong.

### Groups by Complexity (Groups 1–3)

Groups 1 through 3 classify projects according to their level of architectural and engineering complexity. All three groups share a common base: the stated percentage applies to projects with a PCC of **₱50 million or less**. For projects exceeding ₱50M, a **sliding scale** reduces the percentage on the excess amount.

**Group 1 — Simplest Utilization and Character** covers buildings of the most straightforward design and construction: warehouses, simple factory buildings, storage facilities, and other utilitarian structures requiring minimal architectural elaboration. The base fee is **5% DAEDS of PCC for projects ≤₱50M**.

**Group 2 — Moderate Complexity** covers buildings with standard architectural requirements: typical office buildings, standard commercial structures, and institutional buildings of moderate scope. The base fee is **6% DAEDS of PCC for projects ≤₱50M**.

**Group 3 — Exceptional Character** covers buildings of distinctive architectural character or those housing special equipment: churches, theaters, museums, laboratory buildings, and structures requiring specialized design solutions. The base fee is **7% DAEDS of PCC for projects ≤₱50M**.

### The Sliding Scale for Groups 1–3

<Warn>The sliding sclea is one of the most tested computation topics in the Compensation block. For Groups 1–3, the base percentage applies only to the first ₱50M of PCC. The excess is charged at progressively lower rates.</Warn>

The sliding scale applies identically to Groups 1, 2, and 3 — only the base rate for the first ₱50M differs. Each successive bracket is 1% lower than the previous one. For example, Group 1's sliding scale is 5% → 4% → 3% → 2% → 1% → 0.5%, while Group 2's is 6% → 5% → 4% → 3% → 2% → 1%:

<Example>A Group 1 warehouse project with a PCC of ₱150M: Fee on first ₱50M = ₱50M × 5% = ₱2,500,000. Fee on next ₱50M (₱50M–₱100M bracket) = ₱50M × 4% = ₱2,000,000. Fee on remaining ₱50M (₱101M–₱200M bracket) = ₱50M × 3% = ₱1,500,000. Total fee = ₱6,000,000.</Example>

### Groups by Building Type (Groups 4–5)

**Group 4 — Low-Rise Residentials** covers residential buildings of low-rise construction: single-family houses, duplexes, townhouses, and low-rise apartment buildings. The fee is **10% of PCC**. No sliding scale is specified for this group.

**Group 5 — Monumental Buildings and Facilities** covers buildings of monumental character and civic importance: government buildings, convention centers, and large public facilities of landmark significance. The fee is **12% of PCC**.

### Repetitive Project Groups (Groups 6–7)

Groups 6 and 7 recognize that repeating a design requires less architectural effort than creating it the first time. Both groups use **declining fee schedules** rather than a flat percentage.

**Group 6 — Repetitive Building** applies when a single building design is repeated multiple times (e.g., identical school buildings, standardized branch offices). The fee structure is:

<Note>Group 6 (Repetitive Building) uses percentages of the _basic fee_. Group 7 (Repetitive Housing) starts from 10% of PCC and adds increments. These are different base calculations — do not confuse them.</Note>

### Specialized Groups (Groups 8–10)

**Group 8 — Interior Design + Site Planning** covers projects where the architect's scope is limited to interior design services or site development planning, rather than full building design. The fee is **15% of PCC**.

**Group 9 — Renovations for Groups 1 to 5** applies when the architect is engaged to renovate an existing building that would fall under Groups 1–5 if it were new construction. Renovation work demands more architectural effort than new construction due to existing conditions, structural constraints, and as-built documentation requirements. The fee is **150% of the basic fee** that would apply to the project's group classification as new construction.

<Example>A Group 2 renovation project with a PCC of ₱40M: Basic fee if new construction = ₱40M × 6% = ₱2,400,000. Renovation multiplier = 150%. Renovation fee = ₱2,400,000 × 150% = ₱3,600,000.</Example>

**Group 10 — Technical Expertise** covers specialized consulting engagements requiring the architect's technical expertise outside the standard service categories. Compensation for Group 10 projects is determined using the **Multiple of Direct Personnel Expense (MDPE)** method rather than a percentage of PCC. No specific fee percentage is assigned.

## Philippine Practice Context

In Philippine practice, most private-sector projects fall into Groups 1–5. The sliding scale for Groups 1–3 becomes critical for large commercial and industrial projects where PCC commonly exceeds ₱50M. Group 9's 150% multiplier is frequently applied in the adaptive reuse and renovation market, particularly for heritage structures and building retrofits in Metro Manila's older commercial districts. Group 7's repetitive housing schedule is directly relevant to the mass housing programs of NHA and DHSUD. Architects negotiating fees must correctly classify the project first — classification errors compound through every subsequent fee calculation.

## How It's Tested

The LEA tests Project Classification in three patterns. First, the exam presents a building type and asks which group number it belongs to — the student must match building descriptions to the correct group. Second, the exam presents a PCC amount for a Group 1, 2, or 3 project exceeding ₱50M and asks the student to compute the fee using the sliding scale. Third, the exam presents a renovation scenario and asks the student to apply the 150% multiplier after first identifying the correct base group.

<Tip>Memorize the groups as three clusters: **Complexity** (1-2-3 at 5-6-7% DAEDS), **Type** (4-5 at 10-12%), and **Special** (6-7 repetitive, 8 interiors at 15%, 9 renovations at 150%, 10 MDPE). The complexity cluster shares the sliding sclea; the others do not.</Tip>
