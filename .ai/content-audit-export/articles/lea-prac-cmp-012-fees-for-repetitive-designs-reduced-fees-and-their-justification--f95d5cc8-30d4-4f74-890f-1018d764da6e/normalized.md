## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to:

- Describe reduced fees for repetitive designs.
- Explain the justification for fee reductions on repeat projects.
- Identify the percentage reduction for subsequent units.
- Apply reduced fee rules to multi-unit project scenarios.



### Definition and Characteristics of Repetitive Designs

Repetitive designs involve designing a building type once and then reproducing that design or similar variations for multiple units or locations. Examples include: apartment buildings where the same unit plan is repeated on multiple floors; residential subdivisions where the same house design is built on different lots; hotel projects where the same guest room type is repeated throughout; or institutional projects where standard classroom or office modules are repeated.

The repetition must be substantial and deliberate to justify reduced fees. Building two or three units of the same design typically qualifies. Building a few variations of the same prototype (some three-bedroom units, some four-bedroom units, all derived from the same design concept) also qualifies. Conversely, designing a completely unique building followed by a different unique building (even if both are the same building type) is not repetitive design and does not justify reduced fees.

### Scope of Work for Initial Design

For repetitive design fee reduction to apply, the architect must first complete the design for the initial prototype building. This involves all standard design phases: Schematic Design, Design Development, Contract Documents, Bidding Assistance, and Construction Administration for the first building. The architect receives full SPP 202 compensation for the initial design work.

The initial design establishes the building prototype, develops construction details, refines specifications, establishes building systems performance, and works through construction coordination issues. The architect's full design effort is expended during the initial design; this work is not reduced in fee.

### Scope and Fee Reduction for Repetitive Units

After the initial design is complete, the architect undertakes reduced scope for subsequent repetitions. Reduced scope typically includes: adapting the prototype design to different site conditions (different slope, utility connections, or orientation), making minor modifications to accommodate client preferences or variations in unit size or program, preparing modified construction documents that reference the initial prototype but incorporate adaptations, and responding to regulator requirements that vary by location.

The architect does not repeat fundamental design decisions (building form, structural system, mechanical and electrical strategy, material selections) that were established in the initial design. Instead, the architect adapts the proven prototype to new conditions. SPP 202 Group 6 establishes reduced fee percentages for repetitive designs: 50% for 2nd instance, 40% for 3rd instance, 30% for 4th and succeeding instances of the initial prototype design fee. These percentages apply to the full Recommended Professional Fee (RPF) of the initial design, regardless of variations from the prototype.

### Calculating Repetitive Design Fees

If the initial building prototype design has a full SPP 202 DAEDS RPF of 10% of the Project Construction Cost (PCC), and the project involves constructing the same design on three additional identical sites, the reduced RPF percentages per SPP 202 Group 6 apply directly to the base percentage. Therefore, the 2nd site applies an RPF of 5% of PCC (50% of the initial 10% RPF), the 3rd site applies an RPF of 4% of PCC (40% of the initial 10% RPF), and the 4th and succeeding sites apply an RPF of 3% of PCC (30% of the initial 10% RPF).

The actual fee amount depends on the PCC of each site's construction cost. For example, if each site has a PCC of 50 million pesos, the total project fees per SPP 202 Group 6 would be calculated as follows: Initial design is 5 million (10% of 50M); the 2nd site is 2.5 million (5% of 50M); the 3rd site is 2 million (4% of 50M); and the 4th site is 1.5 million (3% of 50M). The total fee would be 11 million for four identical sites. This is substantially lower than designing four completely independent buildings at full fee (4 × 5 million = 20 million), reflecting the reduced design effort for repetitions.

The contract should clearly specify which design is the "initial" design receiving full fees and which designs are "repetitive" designs receiving reduced fees. This clarity prevents disputes about what fee percentages apply.

### Justification for Reduced Fees

Reduced fees for repetitive designs are justified because the architect's intellectual and creative effort is concentrated in the initial design. Developing the building concept, refining the design through Schematic and Design Development phases, working through coordination and constructability issues, and preparing detailed specifications all occur during the initial design. Subsequent repetitions benefit from this completed design work.

Repetitive designs also reduce the architect's financial risk. The initial design's problems and lessons learned inform the subsequent designs, making them more straightforward and lower-risk to execute. The architect has higher confidence in cost and schedule for repetitive designs based on the initial prototype's experience.

### Variations and Site-Specific Adaptations

Repetitive designs often require adaptations for different sites, climates, or code jurisdictions. A prototype design successful in one location may require modifications when constructed in a different climate zone (different insulation, HVAC design) or jurisdiction (different code requirements, accessibility standards, or regulatory processes).

These adaptations justify the reduced fees established in SPP 202 Group 6 (50% for 2nd instance, 40% for 3rd instance, 30% for 4th and succeeding instances), as the architect adapts rather than redesigns the prototype. If site-specific adaptations are unusually complex, the architect may negotiate fees above the SPP 202 Group 6 percentages, but such negotiation should be explicitly documented in the contract.

### Multiple Projects vs. Multiple Phases of One Project

Repetitive design fee reduction applies differently to projects with repetitive units within a single project versus designs applied across multiple separate projects. For multi-unit projects on a single site (apartment complex with multiple identical towers, residential subdivision with multiple identical houses), the architect typically designs the prototype at full fee and reduces fees for subsequent repetitions within the project.

For projects where the same design is applied across multiple separate client projects (an architect designs a prototype office building that the developer builds in multiple cities), fee negotiation should address whether each new city location receives full fees (as a separate client project) or reduced fees (as a repetitive design). This distinction should be established in the contract.

### Client Communication and Justification

When proposing reduced fees for repetitive designs, the architect should clearly explain: (1) which design is the initial prototype receiving full fees, (2) which designs are repetitions receiving reduced fees, (3) the reduction percentage and justification (extent of variation from the prototype), and (4) how site-specific adaptations are reflected in the fee structure. Clients appreciate cost-effective design fees for repetitive work; clear documentation prevents disputes about what constitutes a "repetition" and what additional design work is required beyond the reduced scope.
