# Fees for Repetitive Designs: Reduced Fees and Their Justification

SPP 202 treats repetitive design first as a project-classification issue, not as a universal discount formula. Group 6 covers projects where the plan, design, and related contract documents are reused for repetitive construction of similar buildings without amending the drawings and specifications. Group 7 is different: it covers housing projects with several residential units on a single site using one set of plans, design, specifications, and related documents.

## Learning Goal

After studying this article, you should be able to distinguish Group 6 and Group 7 and avoid invented reduced-fee percentages.

## Group 6: Reuse Without Amendment

Group 6 applies when the same plan/design and related contract documents are reused for repetitive construction of similar buildings, and the drawings and specifications are not amended. The no-amendment condition matters. If the architect must redesign for new site constraints, change specifications, revise code responses, or produce new contract documents, the problem is no longer a clean source-safe Group 6 reuse scenario unless the item gives more facts.

## Group 7: Housing on One Site

Group 7 covers a housing project involving several residential units on a single site with one set of plans/design, specifications, and related documents. Do not answer every repeated residential unit as Group 6. If the item says one site and one set of plans for several residential units, Group 7 is the more source-aligned classification.

## Fee Boundary

The checked Batch 106 source supports the classifications and the general compensation-method list. It does not provide a numeric reduced-fee schedule in the checked line ranges. A question may still give a contract-specific reduced percentage, but SlateCamp should not teach a remembered discount as an SPP rule unless a verified source is added.

## Real World Scenario

A developer asks an architect to reuse the same small commercial building design for three separate lots. If the drawings and specifications are reused without amendment, the facts point toward Group 6. If each lot needs substantial redesign for different setbacks, utility service, soil conditions, or code constraints, the architect should not treat the later work as mere repetitive reuse. For a one-site subdivision using one set of residential unit plans, the better source classification is Group 7.

## Exam Traps

- Do not use a 50 percent, 70 percent, or any other reduced-fee formula unless the problem supplies it.
- Do not classify amended drawings or changed specifications as clean Group 6 reuse.
- Do not confuse Group 6 repetitive construction with Group 7 one-site housing using one set of plans.
- Do not let the word repetitive override the actual source facts.
