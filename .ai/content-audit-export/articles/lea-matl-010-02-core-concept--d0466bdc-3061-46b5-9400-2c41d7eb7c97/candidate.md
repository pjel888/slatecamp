## What You Need to Know for the LEA

Fajardo treats concrete hollow block work as a materials system, not only a block count. A CHB estimate can include blocks, cement and sand for laying, filler for hollow cores, plastering materials, footing materials, reinforcing bars, and tie wire. This batch keeps the article focused on the source-backed estimating sequence.

## Core Concept

For block quantity, Fajardo gives two basic approaches: the fundamental method and the area method. The fundamental method counts blocks by wall length and block layers. The area method multiplies net wall area by the standard value of 12.5 concrete hollow blocks per square meter for the common 20 cm by 40 cm face module.

Fajardo's example shows that a 2.60 m high wall with 60.00 m perimeter has 156 sq m of wall area, and 156 x 12.5 equals 1,950 pieces. The same result appears by dividing length by block length and height by block height, then multiplying the two counts.

## How It's Tested

Most questions ask which area should be counted. If posts, openings, or non-block areas are given, use net wall area before multiplying by 12.5. If the question asks for mortar, core fill, or footing, move to the matching table and mixture instead of treating the block count as the whole estimate.

## Common Mistakes

Do not count gross wall area when posts or openings are excluded. Do not use the block thickness to find the block count; thickness matters in mortar and fill estimates. Do not mix different block sizes in one table lookup unless the problem tells you to separate the phases.
