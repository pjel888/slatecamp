# Roman Architecture

## LEA Point

Roman architecture is the arch, vault, dome, and concrete tradition that turned compression into large public interiors and infrastructure. The safest exam anchors are the arch and voussoir, barrel and groin vaults, the Pantheon dome and oculus, the Colosseum amphitheatre, Roman concrete, basilicas, and aqueducts such as Pont du Gard.

## Source Anchors

- UNESCO Historic Centre of Rome anchors the ancient monumental context that includes the Pantheon and Colosseum.
- UNESCO Pont du Gard anchors Roman aqueduct engineering at monumental scale.
- The local Fletcher conversion anchors pozzolana concrete, arch-vault-dome construction, amphitheatre organization, and aqueduct/bridge logic.

## Structural Pattern

The Roman arch redirects load through wedge-shaped voussoirs into supports. A barrel vault extends an arch through space, a groin vault intersects two barrel vaults, and a dome rotates the arch into a centralized covering. Roman concrete made these forms more flexible because it could cure into monolithic masses rather than depending only on precisely cut stone blocks.

## Exam Guardrails

- Do not reduce Roman architecture to applied orders; the high-yield structural shift is arch, vault, dome, and concrete.
- Do not confuse the Pantheon with the Colosseum. The Pantheon tests unified domed interior and oculus; the Colosseum tests amphitheatre, cavea, circulation, and arcaded facade logic.
- Do not turn Pont du Gard into a decorative monument only. Its exam value is hydraulic infrastructure carried by arches.
- Avoid unsupported exact spectator counts, exact dates, and exact dimensions when the source map only needs the building type and structural idea.

## Real World Scenario

A board item shows a water channel crossing a valley on stacked stone arches. The source-safe answer is Roman aqueduct engineering, with Pont du Gard as the recognition anchor, not a temple facade or a purely decorative arcade.
