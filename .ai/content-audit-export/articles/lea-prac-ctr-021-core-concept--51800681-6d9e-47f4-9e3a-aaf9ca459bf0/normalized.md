The Construction Industry Arbitration Commission (CIAC) was established in 1985 by Executive Order No. 1008 and holds original and exclusive jurisdiction over construction disputes in the Philippines. CIAC jurisdiction is mandatory for construction disputes when the parties have included an arbitration clause in their contract; CIAC may assert jurisdiction even if another arbitral forum was specified. The CIAC is the primary dispute resolution path for construction contracts in the Philippines and operates under its own Rules of Procedure, with finality of awards and limited grounds for court appeal.

## Core Concept

The CIAC is a specialized arbitration body administered by the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) and Construction Industry Authority of the Philippines (CIAP). It has original and exclusive jurisdiction over disputes "arising from or connected with" any construction contract entered into by parties involved in construction in the Philippines, whether public or private, regardless of contract amount or project stage. The three requisites for CIAC jurisdiction are: (1) a dispute arising from or connected with a construction contract, (2) the construction contract was entered into by parties in the Philippines, and (3) the parties agreed to submit the dispute to arbitration (through an arbitration clause in the contract or later agreement).

CIAC jurisdiction is mandatory. If the contract contains an arbitration clause, CIAC jurisdiction is acquired automatically once a dispute arises and is brought before CIAC, regardless of whether the parties later contest CIAC authority. The owner and contractor cannot bypass CIAC by filing in regular courts unless they specifically exclude CIAC jurisdiction in the contract. Even if the contract specifies another arbitral institution (e.g., ICC), CIAC may still assert jurisdiction per Section 4.1 of CIAC Rules of Procedure, as codified in landmark cases such as Hutama-Rsea v. Citra Metro Manila Tollways.

The CIAC arbitration process includes a three-arbitrator tribunal (unless parties agree otherwise), pre-arbitration settlement conferences, presentation of evidence, and an arbitral award. The arbitral award is immediately executory upon lapse of 15 days from receipt unless a temporary restraining order or preliminary injunction is issued by a court. Appeals of CIAC awards are limited to grounds specified in the Arbitration Law: excess of authority, non-compliance with procedure, corruption, and manifest bias. CIAC decisions have finality and are not subject to extensive court review like regular court judgments.

## Philippine Practice Context

The CIAC was created by Executive Order No. 1008 to provide specialized, efficient arbitration for construction disputes without the delays of regular court litigation. The policy recognizes that construction disputes require technical expertise and quick resolution to minimize project disruption. CIAC arbitrators are typically experienced engineers, architects, and construction professionals who understand industry practice, technical issues, and contract interpretations specific to construction.

CIAC jurisdiction operates on the principle of freedom of contract combined with mandatory arbitration. If parties include an arbitration clause in their construction contract, CIAC jurisdiction is triggered when a dispute arises. The requirement of "agreement to arbitrate" is satisfied by the clause in the original contract. Courts have consistently held that CIAC jurisdiction cannot be avoided by filing in regular courts; if CIAC jurisdiction is invoked by one party and CIAC accepts the case, courts must dismiss any parallel litigation and defer to CIAC. This prevents duplicate proceedings and ensures CIAC's role as the exclusive forum.

The landmark case Hutama-Rsea v. Citra Metro Manila Tollways established that CIAC may assert jurisdiction even if the contract specifies another arbitral body (ICC). This ruling prioritizes CIAC's statutory mandate over contract language and underscores that CIAC jurisdiction is non-waivable by the parties. Construction parties in the Philippines must recognize that if their contract involves Philippine work and contains an arbitration clause, CIAC has jurisdiction regardless of what the contract says about forum selection.

The CIAC award's finality creates pressure for thorough arbitration procedure. Because appeals to courts are limited and successful reversal is rare, parties must present complete cases before CIAC. The 15-day timeline for award executability (execution unless TRO is issued) means parties must be prepared to comply quickly. The CIAC process encourages settlement—many cases settle after pre-arbitration settlement conferences once parties understand the evidence and likely outcome.

## How It's Tested

**Question Pattern 1:** CIAC jurisdiction trigger—What conditions must exist for CIAC to have jurisdiction over a dispute? (Tests the three requisites)

**Question Pattern 2:** Jurisdiction vs. contract forum—If a contract specifies ICC arbitration but CIAC is invoked, does CIAC have jurisdiction? (Tests mandatory jurisdiction principle)

**Question Pattern 3:** Arbitration agreement—Must the contract contain an arbitration clause for CIAC jurisdiction, or can parties later agree to arbitration? (Tests arbitration agreement requirement)

**Question Pattern 4:** Award executability—How long after a CIAC award is issued is it immediately executory? (Tests 15-day rule and enforcement timeline)

**Question Pattern 5:** CIAC vs. court litigation—What happens if a dispute is filed in regular court and one party invokes CIAC jurisdiction? (Tests CIAC's exclusive jurisdiction and court deference)
