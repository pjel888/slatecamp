# American Colonial Era
## Learning Goal
After reading this article, you should be able to:

## The Real-World Scenario (The Hook)

You are standing at the foot of the Luneta (Rizal Park), looking north toward the walled city of Intramuros. A grand, tree-lined boulevard stretches before you, civic buildings framing the vista like a Beaux-Arts painting. This is the ghost of Daniel Burnham's 1905 master plan — a vision that reshaped Manila's bones and launched the first generation of Filipino architects trained on American soil.

## Core Concepts

<Tip>

**Memory Anchor: "B-P-P = Burnham, Parsons, Pensionados."** Remember the chain: Daniel Burnham authored the 1905 plan, William E. Parsons implemented it as Consulting Architect of the BPW, and the Pensionado architects (Toledo, Arellano, Mapua, Barretto) carried the legacy forward. If a question asks "who designed the Burnham Plan?" → Burnham. "Who built it?" → Parsons. "Who was the first Filipino licensed architect?" → Mapua.

</Tip>


- **American Colonial Period (1898–1946):** The era during which U.S. governance transformed Philippine architecture through new building technologies (reinforced concrete, steel framing), institutional building programs, and American-trained Filipino professionals.
- **Daniel Burnham's City Beautiful Movement:** An urban planning philosophy emphasizing monumental civic spaces, axial boulevards, and park systems to project order, beauty, and democratic ideals — applied to Manila and Baguio in 1905.
- **Pensionado Architects:** Filipino scholars sent to study in U.S. universities under the **Pensionado Act of 1903**, who returned to lead the professionalization of Philippine architecture, blending Neoclassical, Beaux-Arts, and Art Deco training with local conditions.
- **Bureau of Public Works (BPW):** The government agency that implemented Burnham's plans and oversaw all public construction; its Consulting Architect position was the most powerful architectural post in the colony.

## The Hard Data (Cheat Sheet)

### Burnham Plan of Manila and Baguio (1905)

| Element | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Plan authors** | **Daniel Burnham** and **Pierce Anderson** (arrived Dec. 1904) |
| **Implementing architect** | **William E. Parsons**, Consulting Architect of the Bureau of Public Works (1905–1914) |
| **Cities planned** | **Manila** (capital redesign for 800,000 people) and **Baguio** (summer capital) |
| **Key Manila features** | Bayshore drive (now Roxas Boulevard), radial avenues from civic center at Luneta, park system, waterfront promenades, planned (but unbuilt) Philippine Capitol facing Rizal Monument |
| **Key Baguio features** | Hilltop civic center at Burnham Park, radial roads, The Mansion House (Governor's summer residence) |
| **Design model** | Modeled after Burnham's 1901 Washington D.C. McMillan Plan — compare: Rizal Monument axis vs. Washington Monument axis; planned Philippine Capitol vs. U.S. Capitol |
| **What was NOT built** | The Philippine Capitol Building (never constructed due to funding constraints and changing policies toward Commonwealth status) |

### William E. Parsons — Key Works

| Building | Significance |
|----------|-------------|
| **Gabaldon School Buildings** | Standardized, mass-produced schoolhouse design; elevated floors, huge capiz-shell windows, classrooms opening to hallways and outdoor areas. Named after Assemblyman **Isauro Gabaldon** |
| **Manila Hotel** (1912) | Premier colonial-era hotel on the bayfront |
| **Philippine General Hospital** | Became the model for U.S. Army hospitals in tropical countries |
| **Philippine Normal School** (now PNU) | Teacher-training institution |
| **The Mansion House, Baguio** | Official summer residence of the Governor-General |
| **Manila Army and Navy Club** | Social hub of the American colonial establishment |

### The Pensionado Architects — First Generation

| Architect | University | Key Contributions & Style |
|-----------|-----------|--------------------------|
| **Carlos Barretto** | Drexel University (graduated 1908) | First Filipino architect with an academic degree from abroad; second registered architect in the Philippines; pioneered the Division of Architecture at BPW; organized the Philippine Institute of Architects post-WWII; **Art Deco** style; designed the 1935 Carnival infrastructures |
| **Antonio Toledo** | Ohio State University (graduated 1911) | Master of the **Neoclassical / Beaux-Arts** style; first architect-educator; designed **Manila City Hall**, **National Museum** (Leyte Capitol), **Cebu Provincial Capitol**, **Bureau of Customs**, **Department of Tourism Building** |
| **Tomas Mapua** | Cornell University (graduated 1911) | **First registered architect in the Philippines** (License No. 1); founded **Mapua Institute of Technology** (1925) — the first architectural school in the Philippines; designed the **De La Salle University Main Building** |
| **Juan Arellano** | Pennsylvania Academy of Fine Arts → Drexel University (graduated 1911) | Promoted the shift to **Art Deco and Streamline Modern**; designed the **Metropolitan Theater**, **Manila Post Office Building**, **Jones Bridge**, **National Museum Building** (formerly Legislative Building); led the development of **Quezon City** |

### Second Generation — The Bridge to Modernism

| Architect | Key Contributions |
|-----------|------------------|
| **Andres Luna de San Pedro** | Son of painter Juan Luna; introduced Art Deco and exotic design motifs; designed the **Regina Building**, **Crystal Arcade** |
| **Pablo Antonio** | Pioneer of Modern Philippine Architecture; **Art Deco** master; designed the **FEU Campus** buildings (1938–1950) — largest surviving ensemble of Art Deco architecture in Manila (UNESCO citation, 2005); also designed the **Ideal, Lyric, Life, and Scala Theaters**. Named **National Artist for Architecture** (1976) |
| **Fernando Ocampo** | Co-founded the **UST School of Fine Arts and Architecture** (1930); designed the **Manila Cathedral** (post-war reconstruction) and UST Central Seminary Building |
| **Juan Nakpil** | **National Artist for Architecture** (1973, first architect so honored); Art Deco master; designed **Quiapo Church dome and second belfry**, **Rizal Theater**, **UP Administration & Library Buildings**; co-founded the Philippine College of Design (1941) |

### New Building Technologies Introduced

<Example>

**Philippine Application:** Your adaptive reuse of a Gabaldon school in Bulacan preserves the elevated floor (flood protection), capiz-shell windows (natural light without glass), and open hallway-to-classroom plan (cross-ventilation). These are direct applications of William Parsons' tropical design principles — raised floors, local materials, and climate-responsive orientation — all required by BP 344 today.

</Example>


| Technology | Impact |
|------------|--------|
| **Reinforced concrete (terroconcrete)** | Replaced traditional wood-and-stone construction for major buildings |
| **Steel-framed skeleton construction** | Enabled taller, more open-plan structures |
| **Kahn Truss System** | Trussed bars placed within concrete moulds for floor slabs and beams |
| **Concrete hollow blocks (CHB)** | Became the standard wall material for Philippine construction |
| **Prefabricated & precast components** | Standardized modular systems, especially in Gabaldon schoolhouses |

## LEA Exam Traps and Common Mistakes

<ExamTip>

**LEA Exam Pattern — Architect-to-Project Pairings:** The exam frequently tests which architect designed which building. Focus on: Toledo = Manila City Hall, National Museum (Neoclassical); Arellano = Metropolitan Theater, Manila Post Office (Art Deco); Mapua = De La Salle Main Building, founded MIT (License No. 1); Nakpil = Quiapo Church dome (first National Artist for Architecture).

</ExamTip>


- **Trap: Confusing Burnham with Parsons.** Burnham and Pierce Anderson authored the 1905 plan, but **William E. Parsons** was the architect who actually implemented it on the ground. Burnham spent only weeks in the Philippines; Parsons stayed from 1905 to 1914. Parsons also created his own City Beautiful plans for **Cebu** and **Zamboanga**.
- **Trap: Assuming the Burnham Plan was fully realized.** The planned Philippine Capitol Building — the centerpiece of Burnham's civic axis — was **never built**. The bayshore drive (Roxas Boulevard) and the radial avenue system are the most visible surviving elements.
- **Trap: Mixing up Pensionado architects.** The LEA loves to test architect-to-project pairings. Remember: **Toledo = Manila City Hall / Neoclassical**; **Arellano = Metropolitan Theater / Art Deco**; **Mapua = Mapua Institute of Technology / License No. 1**; **Barretto = Carnival Buildings / Art Deco**. Do NOT confuse Juan Arellano (architect) with Arcadio Arellano (separate architect, designed the Gota de Leche Building).

---


<Warn>

⚠️ COMMON TRAP: The Philippine Capitol Building (the centerpiece of Burnham's civic axis) was NEVER BUILT. Many students assume it was constructed alongside the other Burnham Plan elements. It was approved in the 1916 Jones Law but killed by budget constraints and the shift toward Commonwealth. Roxas Boulevard and the radial road system are the most visible surviving elements of the plan.

</Warn>

## Board-Prep Mnemonics

**"TAMB" — The Four Original Pensionado Architects:**
- **T** — Toledo (Neoclassical master, Manila City Hall)
- **A** — Arellano, Juan (Art Deco, Metropolitan Theater)
- **M** — Mapua (First licensed architect, founded MIT)
- **B** — Barretto (First pensionado abroad, Carnival buildings)

**"BPA" — The Burnham Chain of Command:**
- **B**urnham designed the plan
- **P**arsons implemented the plan
- **A**rchitects (Pensionado) carried the legacy forward

## Active Recall Questions

**Question 1:**
Who was the Consulting Architect of the Bureau of Public Works responsible for implementing Daniel Burnham's plans in Manila and Baguio?

A) Pierce Anderson
B) Antonio Toledo
C) William E. Parsons
D) Tomas Mapua

> **Answer: C) William E. Parsons.** Burnham and Anderson authored the 1905 master plan, but Parsons was recommended by Burnham himself and served as Consulting Architect from 1905 to 1914, adapting and implementing the plans on the ground.

**Question 2:**
Which architect designed the Manila Metropolitan Theater and helped shift Philippine architecture toward Art Deco?

A) Antonio Toledo
B) Juan Arellano
C) Pablo Antonio
D) Andres Luna de San Pedro

> **Answer: B) Juan Arellano.** Arellano trained at the Pennsylvania Academy of Fine Arts and Drexel University. He designed the Metropolitan Theater, the Manila Post Office, and the National Museum Building (formerly the Legislative Building), all in the Art Deco style.

**Question 3:**
What was a key architectural feature of the Gabaldon School Buildings designed by William Parsons?

A) Reinforced concrete walls with glass curtain facades
B) Elevated floors, large capiz-shell windows, and classrooms opening to outdoor hallways
C) Earthquake Baroque buttresses and thick coral-stone walls
D) Nipa-thatch roofing with bamboo post-and-beam framing

> **Answer: B) Elevated floors, large capiz-shell windows, and classrooms opening to outdoor hallways.** Parsons adapted his designs to the tropical Philippine climate by maximizing natural ventilation and light — using local capiz shells instead of glass, elevated floors for airflow, and open hallway plans connecting to outdoor areas. These were mass-produced standardized templates under the Gabaldon Act.
