## What You Need to Know for the LEA
## Learning Goal

Identify formwork types (conventional timber, prefabricated panels, slip forms, climbing forms, tunnel forms) by material, reuse potential, and application, and apply NSCP 2015 lateral pressure and deflection limits for formwork design.
## Core Concept

Pre-construction planning ensures site readiness, regulatory compliance, and contractor capability. Post-construction verification confirms that the completed building meets design, code, and contractual requirements before occupancy.

### Pre-Construction Phase

_Site Investigation and Soil Testing_ — Geotechnical investigation (boring, laboratory testing) determines soil bearing capacity, groundwater elevation, and foundation design requirements. Proper investigation prevents foundation failure, excessive settlement, and costly rework. NSCP 2015 Chapter 18 (Soils and Foundations) requires foundation design to be based on site-specific soil properties. Geotechnical investigation is required when soil conditions are uncertain or when design complexity warrants site-specific data.

_Permits and Regulatory Approval_ — PD 1096 (National Building Code of the Philippines) specifies permitting process: Barangay Clearance, SWAB (Single Window Approval Board if applicable), Building Permit application with approved plans and calculations. The Local Building Official (LBO) issues the permit after plan review. Failure to obtain permits invites demolition orders and legal liability.

_Demolition and Site Preparation_ — Existing structures are demolished per contract. Hazardous materials (asbestos, lead paint in heritage buildings) require proper abatement per DENR Environmental Management Bureau (EMB) standards and Department of Health (DOH) occupational health guidelines. Utilities (electrical, water, gas, telecommunications) are relocated if needed. Excavation spoil is managed per environmental regulations.

_Temporary Facilities_ — Site offices, worker camps, storage yards, safety railings, and scaffolding are installed. Site access and traffic control are planned to minimize public disruption. Temporary utilities (electrical, water) support construction operations. Temporary facility design and worker safety are governed by DOLE Construction Safety Standards and occupational safety regulations, not NSCP 2015. NSCP 2015 addresses structural design loads and seismic requirements.

### Post-Construction Phase

_Punch List_ — The contractor systematically identifies incomplete or non-conforming work items during the final inspection with the owner/engineer and prepares a punch list. Contractor corrects all items to the owner's satisfaction. Punch list closure is a contractual requirement before final payment.

_Substantial Completion_ — When the building is sufficiently complete for occupancy and use as intended, substantial completion is declared. Minor punch list items (cosmetic, non-functional) may remain. The owner typically occupies and derives beneficial use at this stage.

_Final Inspection_ — The Local Building Official conducts a final inspection confirming code compliance. Inspection covers structural integrity, fire-life safety, accessibility, egress, utilities, HVAC, and all systems. Violations must be corrected before occupancy permit issuance.

_Occupancy Permit_ — Also called Certificate of Occupancy (CO), this is the final authorization from the LBO to occupy the building. Without CO, the building cannot be legally occupied. CO is issued only after all code violations are corrected and final inspection is passed.

_As-Built Drawings_ — The contractor prepares accurate drawings reflecting actual construction (field changes, as-installed conditions). As-builts are essential for operations, maintenance, future renovations, and legal documentation. Discrepancies between design and as-built must be clearly noted.

_Warranty Period_ — Contractor warrants structural and major system performance for a specified period (typically 1–5 years per contract). Defects appearing during warranty are the contractor's responsibility for repair/replacement. Proper documentation (inspection reports, punch list) supports warranty claims.

## Key Provisions

| Pre-Construction Phase | Purpose | Philippine Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Geotechnical Investigation | Soil bearing, groundwater, foundation design | NSCP 2015 Chapter 18; Geotechnical Report required |
| Building Permit Application | Regulatory approval, plan review | PD 1096; LBO review and issuance required |
| Site Demolition | Clear site, remove hazards | DENR asbestos/hazmat abatement standards |
| Temporary Facilities | Support construction operations | NSCP 2015 Chapter 34 (Construction Standards) |

|---|---|---|
| Punch List | Identify incomplete work | Before substantial completion |
| Substantial Completion | Owner occupancy, beneficial use | When building is ~95% complete |
| Final Inspection | LBO code compliance verification | Before occupancy permit |
| Occupancy Permit | Authorization to occupy | After final inspection, corrections completed |
| As-Built Drawings | Document actual construction | Before final acceptance |
| Warranty Period | Contractor defect responsibility | 1–5 years typical per contract |

## Philippine Construction Context

Building permits in the Philippines are issued by city/municipal LBOs per PD 1096. PD 1096 Chapter 1 (General Provisions) and the 2004 Revised IRR govern the building permit application process. Specific rule and section numbers should be verified against the current IRR edition. Processing time varies by LGU — Metro Manila OBOs typically require 30-60 working days. Common delays: incomplete plan submissions, calculations not conforming to NSCP 2015, lack of environmental clearances.

Final inspections by LBOs are increasingly thorough, with checklists for structural (X-ray tests for rebar cover verification), fire-life safety (egress widths, stair rise/run, door hardware), accessibility (ramp slopes, toilet grab bars), and electrical code compliance. Non-compliance is documented; correction must be verified before CO issuance.

Occupancy permits (Certificate of Occupancy) are the final legal gate. Buildings cannot be legally occupied without CO. Landlords and tenants are liable for illegally occupied structures.

Warranty periods in Philippine construction practice: structural elements 5 years, major systems (HVAC, electrical, plumbing) 2–3 years, finishes 1–2 years. Contract specifications define actual periods.

## How It's Tested

The LEA evaluates pre/post-construction knowledge through:

1. _Permitting process_ — Sequence the steps (site investigation, permit application, LBO review, plan approval) correctly per PD 1096.
2. _Regulatory requirements_ — Identify which Philippine standards apply (NSCP 2015, environmental, accessibility).
3. _Defect resolution_ — Describe punch list process and contractor responsibility before final payment.
4. _Occupancy compliance_ — Explain why occupancy permit is required and consequences of illegal occupancy.
5. _Documentation_ — Importance of as-built drawings and defect records for warranty and future maintenance.
