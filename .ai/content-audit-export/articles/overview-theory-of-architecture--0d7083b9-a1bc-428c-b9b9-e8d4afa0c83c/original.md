# Theory of Architecture — Overview & Study Strategy

Theory of Architecture is the conceptual heartbeat of the LEA. Every other subject — from Architectural Design to Building Utilities — eventually traces its "why" back here. Before you can design a humane space, size a room for human comfort, or argue for a material choice, you need a framework for thinking about what architecture actually *is*. This subject builds that framework.

## What This Subject Covers

The PRC's nine subtopics move through a logical arc — from the philosophical to the practical, from the ancient to the contemporary.

**The Nature of Architecture** grounds you in what separates a building from architecture: intention, meaning, and the relationship between form and human experience. This is Vitruvius territory — *firmitas, utilitas, venustas* (firmness, commodity, delight) — the oldest framework we have, and still one of the most useful for exam scenarios.

**Elements of Architecture and Principles of Design** shifts into the formal language: space, form, line, light, texture, and how they interact through proportion, balance, rhythm, emphasis, and unity. Francis D.K. Ching's *Architecture: Form, Space, and Order* is the primary reference here. Treat it less like a textbook to memorize and more like a visual grammar to internalize.

**Architectural Philosophies and Theories of the Masters** covers the big thinkers — Le Corbusier's Five Points, Wright's Organic Architecture, Kahn's "silence and light," Venturi's complexity and contradiction. Know not just *what* they said, but *why* it mattered in its historical moment.

**Architectural Movements and Contemporary Discourse** maps the timeline from Modernism through Postmodernism, Deconstructivism, Critical Regionalism, and parametric design. This overlaps significantly with Architectural History — think of THEO as the "ideas" side and HIST as the "timeline" side of the same coin.

**Design Perception — Anthropometrics, Proxemics, and Kinesthetics** is where theory meets the human body. Edward T. Hall's proxemics (intimate, personal, social, public zones) and Neufert's anthropometric data connect directly to space planning. The LEA board has historically tested specific distances and zone dimensions.

**Space Planning Theory and Architectural Programming** bridges theory and practice. Programming is the analytical process that precedes design — identifying user needs, functional relationships, and spatial requirements before pencil meets paper.

**Tropical Architecture and Climate-Responsive Design** is particularly important for Filipino architects. Passive cooling strategies, sun path analysis, prevailing winds, shading devices, and the principles of the bahay kubo as vernacular climate response — all of this is fair game.

**Innovation — Emerging Theories and the Filipino Architect's Identity** is the newest and perhaps most personally meaningful area. What does it mean to design *for* and *from* Filipino culture? Critical Regionalism (Frampton) applied locally. Contemporary Filipino masters and their philosophies.

**The Pritzker Architecture Prize** — the board consistently tests laureates, their home countries, and their signature works or philosophies. Know at least the last 10–15 years of winners.

## Why Theory Matters Before Everything Else

<Callout>Theory is not abstract decoration on top of "real" architectural knowledge. It is the interpretive lens through which all other subjects become coherent. A structures problem is also a theory problem when you are asked why an architect chose exposed concrete. A site planning problem is also a theory problem when climate response is the rationlea.</Callout>

Students who skip deeply understanding Theory often find that their other subjects feel like disconnected facts. Students who internalize Theory find that everything else starts to *connect*.

## Study Strategy: Three Passes

### Pass 1 — Principles First (Weeks 1–2)

Start with Ching. Work through *Architecture: Form, Space, and Order* not to memorize captions but to build visual literacy. Can you identify proportion in a plan? Can you recognize hierarchy in a section? The LEA presents images and asks you to name the principle at work. Practice this actively.

Simultaneously, ground yourself in Vitruvius's triad. Nearly every LEA theory question can be anchored to one of those three legs.

<Tip>Make a two-column sheet: left column is the design principle (rhythm, balance, emphasis, etc.), right column is a real building or space where you can clearly see it. Retrieving examples forces deeper processing than re-reading definitions.</Tip>

### Pass 2 — Philosophers and Movements (Weeks 3–4)

Now layer in the thinkers. For each major philosopher or movement, answer three questions:
1. What was the central argument or position?
2. What were they reacting *against*?
3. What is a canonical built example?

This three-part structure works because architectural movements are almost always defined by opposition — Modernism against historicism, Postmodernism against Modernism's austerity, Critical Regionalism against placeless globalism.

### Pass 3 — Filipino Identity and Integration (Weeks 5–6)

This final pass ties everything together through a Filipino lens. How do global theories land in a tropical, archipelagic, culturally layered context? Who are the Filipino architects who synthesized these ideas — Locsin, Mañosa, Tan? Where does the Pritzker list stand today?

This is also when you revisit Anthropometrics with fresh eyes — not as dry dimensions but as a theory of human dignity embedded in spatial design.

## Common Pitfalls

**Confusing Theory with History.** The LEA has two separate but overlapping subjects. THEO asks "what does this principle mean and how does it apply?" HIST asks "when did this happen and who built it?" A Baroque *style* question belongs in History; a Baroque *spatial principle* (axial progression, dramatic light) question belongs in Theory.

**Memorizing without understanding.** The board regularly presents novel scenarios — an unfamiliar building image, a paraphrased principle — precisely to test whether you understand or just memorized. If you can only recognize a term when it appears verbatim, you have studied the surface.

<Warn>Do not spend disproportionate time on the Pritzker list at the expense of principles. The laureate questions are worth only a small fraction of the Theory section. Know the list, but do not let it crowd out Ching and the philosophical foundations.</Warn>

**Underestimating Proxemics and Anthropometrics.** These subtopics reliably appear in computational or scenario-based questions. Know the four proxemic zones and their approximate distances. Know standard human dimensions for accessibility and comfort.

## A Word on the Filipino Architect's Identity

<Note>The PRC increasingly emphasizes Filipino architectural identity — not as a narrow nationalist question, but as a sophisticated design challenge. How does a Filipino architect honor vernacular intelligence (the bahay kubo's ventilation strategy, the elevated floor, the pitched roof) while practicing in a contemporary, urbanizing, climate-challenged context? Study this not as trivia but as a genuine design philosophy.</Note>

This subject rewards students who are genuinely curious about ideas. Approach it that way, and the exam questions will feel less like tests and more like conversations you have already been having.
