## Subject Coverage

Nine subtopics tracing a logical arc from philosophical to practical:

- **Nature of Architecture** — Vitruvius: firmitas, utilitas, venustas; what separates building from architecture
- **Elements and Principles of Design** — Ching's vocabulary: form, space, order, proportion, scale, balance, rhythm, emphasis, unity
- **Architectural Philosophies and Theories of the Masters** — Le Corbusier's Five Points, Wright's Organic Architecture, Kahn's "silence and light," Venturi's complexity and contradiction
- **Architectural Movements and Contemporary Discourse** — Modernism through Postmodernism, Deconstructivism, Critical Regionalism, parametric design
- **Design Perception** — Edward T. Hall's proxemics (intimate/personal/social/public zones), Neufert's anthropometrics, kinesthetics
- **Space Planning Theory and Architectural Programming** — user needs analysis, adjacency requirements, spatial quantities
- **Tropical Architecture and Climate-Responsive Design** — passive cooling, sun path, prevailing winds, bahay kubo principles
- **Innovation and Filipino Architect's Identity** — Critical Regionalism applied locally, contemporary Filipino masters
- **The Pritzker Architecture Prize** — laureates, home countries, signature works

## Study Strategy: Three Passes

### Pass 1 — Principles First (Weeks 1–2)

Start with Ching's *Architecture: Form, Space, and Order*. Build visual literacy: can you identify proportion in a plan? Recognize hierarchy in a section? Ground yourself in Vitruvius's triad.

### Pass 2 — Philosophers and Movements (Weeks 3–4)

For each major thinker/movement, answer: (1) What was the central argument? (2) What were they reacting against? (3) What is a canonical built example?

## Common Exam Traps

- **Confusing Theory with History.** THEO asks "what does this principle mean?" HIST asks "when did this happen and who built it?"
- **Memorizing without understanding.** The LEA presents novel scenarios to test whether you understand or just memorized.
- **Over-investing in Pritzker list at expense of principles.** Laureate questions are a small fraction of the Theory section.
- **Underestimating proxemics and anthropometrics.** Know the four proxemic zones and approximate distances. Know standard human dimensions.

<Warn>Do not spend disproportionate time on the Pritzker list at the expense of principles. The laureate questions are worth only a small fraction of the Theory section. Know the list, but do not let it crowd out Ching and the philosophical foundations.</Warn>
