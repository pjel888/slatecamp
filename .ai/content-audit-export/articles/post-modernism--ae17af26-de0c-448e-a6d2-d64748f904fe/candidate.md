# Post-Modernism

## Board Exam Quick Check

Classify Post-Modernism by its reaction to strict Modernist purity, not only by date. The checked local sources tie the topic to historical reference, color, pattern, ornament, ambiguity, familiar narratives, and buildings by Robert Venturi, Philip Johnson, and Michael Graves.

## Source-Grounded Core

Post-Modernism appears in these sources as a challenge to the narrow language of late Modernism and the International Style. The local history source describes 1960s and 1970s architects turning back to historical precedents and everyday culture. Venturi's 1966 work is presented as a gentle manifesto that preferred rich meaning and both-and thinking over single, reductive clarity.

The LEA reviewer source summarizes the same shift in simpler board-review language: Postmodern architecture renewed appreciation for architectural traditions and enlivened facades with color, pattern, and ornament. It identifies Robert Venturi with the phrase less is a bore and with an architecture of ambiguity, decoration, messy vitality, and both-and thinking.

## People And Works

- Robert Venturi is the anchor for the theory side. The checked sources connect him to Vanna Venturi House in Pennsylvania and to a critique of orthodox Modernism.
- Philip Johnson is a bridge figure: he was associated with the International Style before becoming a major Post-Modern promoter. The 20th Century Architects source identifies his AT&T Headquarters in New York as a first postmodern skyscraper.
- Michael Graves is tied to the public visibility of the style. The same source says he abandoned Modernism in the mid-1970s and brought Post-Modernism to the public through the Portland Building.

## LEA Answer Pattern

Choose Post-Modernism when the question describes historical allusion, color, symbolic facade treatment, plural meanings, playful references, or a deliberate break from the glass-box language of orthodox Modernism. Do not confuse it with the International Style, which the sources describe through functional architecture, simple geometric forms, large glass areas, untextured surfaces, and steel or reinforced-concrete construction.

## Common Exam Traps

- Do not classify a building as Post-Modern merely because it was built after Modernism. Look for the source-backed design language: reference, ambiguity, ornament, color, or symbolic facade treatment.
- Do not miss Philip Johnson's shift. He is source-linked to both the International Style and later Post-Modern work.
- Do not treat Portland Building and AT&T Headquarters as generic office examples. In this topic, they are board-review anchors for public Post-Modern expression.
