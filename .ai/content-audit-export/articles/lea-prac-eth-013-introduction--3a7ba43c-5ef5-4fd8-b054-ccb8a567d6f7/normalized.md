# Ethical Fee Competition and Professional Charges

Fee ethics in Philippine architectural practice is not just a pricing issue. It is a client-duty, profession-duty, and statutory-compliance issue.

The Code of Ethics starts the client relationship with disclosure and agreement. The architect may present professional experience to a prospective client, but should not offer free preliminary sketches or other services without an agreement for legitimate compensation. At the start of the relationship, the architect must ascertain the exact nature and scope of services and the corresponding professional charges.

The same client-duty section requires professional fees to be commensurate with the work involved and with the architect's professional standing and experience, based on the Basic Minimum Fee under the Standards of Professional Practice. It also requires compensation solely through the professional fee charged directly to the client, and it requires disclosure of business interests that may affect the client's interest.

The profession-duty section is the exam anchor for fee undercutting. The architect must not render professional services without compensation except for small civic or charity projects. The architect must not knowingly compete with other architects on the basis of a difference in professional charges, and must not use donation as a device for competitive advantage except for worthy civic or religious projects.

RA 9266 gives these ethical and SPP duties legal weight. Its IRR says a person who violates RA 9266, its IRR, the Code of Ethical Conduct, the Standards of Professional Practice, or policies of the Board and Commission may be penalized. The IRR also prohibits compelling a registered and licensed architect to perform services without first executing a written contract or service agreement.

For exam questions, separate legitimate agreement-making from unethical fee conduct. A source-safe answer should favor written scope, professional charges, commensurate fee, no free preliminary services, no fee-based undercutting, no donation as competitive advantage, and no hidden compensation from contractors or suppliers.
