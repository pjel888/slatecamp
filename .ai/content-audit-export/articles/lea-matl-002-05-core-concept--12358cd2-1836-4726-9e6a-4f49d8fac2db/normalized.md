## Core Concept

Portland cement is a hydraulic cement made by burning clay and limestone and pulverizing the clinker into a fine powder. Cement paste is cement mixed with water; it coats, sets, and binds aggregate particles in concrete.

The water-cement ratio is the weight ratio of mixing water to cement in a unit volume of concrete. Building Construction Illustrated states that this ratio controls strength, durability, and watertightness. Under Abrams law, compressive strength varies inversely with the water-cement ratio: too much water leaves weak, porous concrete after curing, while too little water can make the mix dense but difficult to place and work.

The Salvan source gives the same practical warning: more water than actually required for hydration may bleed, cause segregation or laitance, evaporate to leave voids, decrease strength, increase permeability, and weaken the cement paste.

Curing is tied to measured strength. Salvan describes compressive-strength testing around 7-day and 28-day curing periods. Building Construction Illustrated states that concrete is normally specified by compressive strength at 28 days after placement, with 7 days noted for high-early-strength concrete. Surface sealing agents can prevent water evaporation so moisture is retained for hydration.
