# Physical and Aesthetic Considerations

Site planning starts with evidence about the land. The source-safe LEA approach is to read physical and aesthetic conditions as constraints and opportunities, then connect them to code, energy, drainage, access, and user experience.

## Physical factors to audit

Building Construction Illustrated lists site-analysis concerns such as legal boundaries, setbacks, easements, program area requirements, slopes, subsoil, drainage, water table, existing trees and native plants, climate, sun, wind, rain, access, utilities, views, noise, and adjacent land uses.

Topography matters because contour spacing and slope influence buildability, drainage, access, erosion, and outdoor-use potential. In the checked BCI material, closely spaced contours indicate steeper terrain, and slope ranges affect what activities are practical.

The BP 220 and PD 957 subdivision standards require attention to site suitability, hazards, slope, grading, drainage, street alignment, and preservation of site assets such as trees, topsoil, and ground cover. Roads should be planned with site contours where possible, and drainage must move water safely.

DOE energy-conserving design guidance adds climate response. Orientation, solar heat gain, daylighting, natural ventilation, window placement, wind movement, and sun shading affect building performance. Do not invent fixed monsoon-month rules or street height-to-width ratios unless the item supplies a checked source.

## Aesthetic factors are not decoration only

Aesthetic decisions include views, approach, landscape character, shade, visual screening, outdoor space, and how buildings fit the site. They should support physical performance rather than fight it.

## LEA answer method

When an item gives a site, start with hazards and legal constraints, then read slopes and drainage, then check access and utilities, then evaluate solar, wind, shade, views, noise, and neighboring uses. Choose the answer that respects both the physical evidence and the relevant planning standard.

## Source-safe takeaways

- Site analysis is evidence gathering before design judgment.
- Slope, contour spacing, drainage, soil, vegetation, access, utilities, climate, views, and noise all affect planning decisions.
- BP 220 and PD 957 require safe, suitable, drainable sites and preservation of site assets where applicable.
- DOE guidance supports orientation, ventilation, daylighting, and shading decisions.
- Aesthetic quality is stronger when it works with terrain, climate, landscape, views, and user movement.
