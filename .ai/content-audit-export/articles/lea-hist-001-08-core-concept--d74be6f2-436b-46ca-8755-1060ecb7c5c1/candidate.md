# Indus Valley Civilization Architecture

## LEA Point

Indus Valley architecture is an early urban planning and sanitation tradition. The source-safe exam anchors are Mohenjo-daro and Harappa, standardized brick, planned streets, wells, drains, citadel/lower town organization, and the Great Bath. The safest interpretation is functional urban order rather than royal monumentality.

## Source Anchors

- UNESCO Moenjodaro anchors the archaeological site, planned urban fabric, brick construction, water management, and Great Bath context.
- Authoritative museum and archaeology references support the mature Harappan urban period and the caution that script, rulers, and religious meanings are still not fully resolved.

## Core Pattern

Indus cities are tested less through temples and palaces and more through repeatable urban systems: brick modules, street alignment, wells, bathing areas, and covered drainage. That pattern makes the civilization distinct from the pyramid and ziggurat monument traditions of Egypt and Mesopotamia.

## Exam Guardrails

- Do not claim the Great Bath has one proven ritual meaning. Treat it as a major public water structure with debated function.
- Do not frame the civilization as less advanced because it lacks obvious royal palaces or monumental temples.
- Do not force a violent invasion explanation for decline when the safer source boundary is gradual urban change with environmental, economic, and settlement factors debated.
- Avoid exact acreage and pool dimensions unless a future capture supports them.

## Real World Scenario

A board item describes a brick-lined tank, stairs, drainage, and citadel context at Mohenjo-daro. The source-safe answer is the Great Bath and the Indus urban sanitation tradition, not a Roman bath or a Mesopotamian temple precinct.
