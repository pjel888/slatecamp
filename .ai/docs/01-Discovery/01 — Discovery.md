# 01 — Discovery

Research, validation, and market intelligence for SlateCamp.

> **Strategic framing:** Architecture is our *beachhead* — not our ceiling. The platform is being built from day one to support any high-stakes professional licensure exam. We start with Architecture because the spatial complexity of the ALE stress-tests every layer of the product. Once the engine works here, expansion to other PRC-regulated professions is a data migration and content licensing exercise.
>

---

## 🎯 Problem Statement

The journey from undergraduate studies to professional licensure in the Philippines is **fragmented, expensive, and psychologically punishing.** Students rely on:

- Hundreds of blurry, unverified PDFs from multiple review centers
- Outdated textbooks that don't reflect current building codes or exam formats
- Blind memorization with zero feedback on actual mastery
- Expensive physical review centers (₱15,000–₱40,000+) with no data-driven outcomes

**There is no single platform that structures the entire syllabus, measures competence, and proves readiness — across any profession.**

This is not an architecture problem. It is a *licensure industry* problem.

## 🗺️ Market Strategy — Wedge → Expand

### Phase 1 Beachhead: Architecture Licensure (ALE)

Architecture is the ideal first market because:

- The ALE syllabus is uniquely complex (spatial reasoning, building codes, structural principles) — if SlateCamp works here, it works anywhere
- The community is tight-knit and vocal — topnotchers have 100K+ followings, meaning organic growth is achievable
- Philippine architecture schools are concentrated and reachable for B2B pilots
- Spatial learning tech (3D Lab) differentiates us in a way text-heavy competitors cannot replicate

## 🏔️ Market Landscape

### Philippine Licensure Market Overview

- The **Professional Regulation Commission (PRC)** administers over **43 licensure exams** annually
- Combined annual examinees across all professions: estimated **200,000–300,000+**
- The review industry is worth an estimated **₱5–10B annually** — dominated by offline review centers with no digital OS equivalent
- Digital penetration in this space is near zero beyond generic quiz apps and YouTube reviewers

### Architecture Licensure Exam (ALE) — Beachhead Data

- ALE passing rates fluctuate between **40–65%** per exam cycle
- A single failure costs **6+ months** of review time and **₱30,000+** in direct costs
- The emotional and social stigma of failing is a massive psychological driver
- Top schools (UST, PLM, FEU, NU, Mapua, UP) account for the majority of examinees — a concentrated B2B target

### Competitive Landscape

| **Competitor / Alternative** | **Type** | **Weakness** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical Review Centers (e.g., RAIA, UAP affiliates) | In-person lectures | No personalized analytics, expensive, geographically limited, schedule-bound |
| PDF / Photocopy Culture | Informal peer sharing | Unverified content, no structure, no feedback loop, often outdated |
| Generic Quiz Apps (Quizlet, Kahoot) | General-purpose | No taxonomy mapping, no spatial learning, no licensure-specific content |
| YouTube / TikTok Reviewers | Free content | Fragmented, no adaptive testing, no mastery tracking, no accountability |
| Cerebry (SEA) | Adaptive EdTech | Math-focused, no Philippine PRC content, no spatial tools |
| Snapask (SEA) | On-demand tutoring | Human-dependent, not scalable, no structured curriculum |

**SlateCamp's moat:** No competitor combines a taxonomy-mapped wiki, adaptive quizzing engine, 3D spatial lab, AI tutor, personal notes, AND predictive analytics into a single integrated loop — for any profession, not just one.

## 👤 User Research — Key Personas

### 🎯 Beachhead Personas (Architecture)

### Persona A: The Anxious Reviewee (Primary B2C)

- **Demographics:** 22–28 years old, recent architecture graduate, preparing for the ALE
- **Behavior:** Studies 6–10 hours daily during review season; buys 2–3 review center packages; hoards PDFs
- **Pain:** "I've been studying for months and I still don't know if I'm actually ready."
- **Trigger:** Fear of public failure and wasted time/money
- **Job to be Done:** *Tell me exactly what I don't know and fix it before exam day*

### Persona B: The Undergraduate (Pipeline)

- **Demographics:** 18–22 years old, 3rd–5th year architecture student
- **Behavior:** Struggles with spatial concepts in studio; uses YouTube to supplement lectures
- **Pain:** "I can't visualize how building codes translate to actual structures."
- **Trigger:** Failing midterms or struggling in design studios
- **Job to be Done:** *Help me understand 3D concepts that my professors can't explain clearly*

### Persona C: The University Dean (B2B)

- **Demographics:** Department head or dean at an architecture school
- **Behavior:** Tracks board passing rates obsessively; low rates threaten school rankings
- **Pain:** "70% of our graduating batch is bleeding red in Structural Design and I can't intervene until it's too late."
- **Trigger:** Declining board passing rates and enrollment
- **Job to be Done:** *Give me a diagnostic dashboard for my entire cohort 6 months before the exam*

---

### 🌐 Expansion Personas (Future Markets)

### Persona D: The Nursing Candidate (NLE)

- **Demographics:** 20–26 years old, BSN graduate
- **Behavior:** Relies heavily on review centers (PACU, Kaplan Philippines); buys review books
- **Pain:** "There's so much content to memorize and I have no way to know if I'm focusing on the right topics."
- **Job to be Done:** *Prioritize what matters most and tell me my readiness score*

### Persona E: The CPA Candidate

- **Demographics:** 23–30 years old, BS Accountancy graduate
- **Behavior:** Self-studies heavily, buys CRC-ACE or similar review packages
- **Pain:** "I've taken this exam twice. I don't know what I'm doing wrong."
- **Willingness to Pay:** High — CPAs command premium salaries
- **Job to be Done:** *Diagnose exactly where I keep failing and build a fix plan*

### Persona F: The Future International User

- **Demographics:** Nurse or medical graduate in PH preparing for NCLEX or USMLE
- **Pain:** Wants a single platform that bridges PH licensure and international certification prep
- **Job to be Done:** *Don't make me buy two different review tools*

## ✅ Validation Signals

- Filipino students spend **₱15,000–₱40,000+** per review season on centers, materials, and transportation
- The "PDF hoarding" culture proves desperate demand for structured content
- Licensure Exam topnotchers have massive social media followings (100K+), proving the market worships expertise
- No existing digital platform owns the "licensure exam OS" category in the Philippines
- Architecture is the ideal wedge: spatial complexity stress-tests the platform; text-heavy exams (Law, CPA, Nursing) become a content and taxonomy exercise after the engine is proven
- The PRC administers 43+ exams — each one is an addressable expansion market with minimal platform re-engineering
