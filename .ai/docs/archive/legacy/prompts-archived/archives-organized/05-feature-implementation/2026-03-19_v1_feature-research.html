02 — Critical Gaps

Five features that are missing right now
and block exam performance.

These are not future phase ideas. They are documented failures in the current product — gaps that are causing students to underperform on the actual ALE. The evidence is direct, cited, and sourced from all four documents.

01
P1 CRITICAL
Area C Preparation System — Development Controls Calculator + Design Problem Library
Area C (Architectural Design and Site Planning) is 40% of the ALE and is entirely unaddressed by any current SlateCamp feature. Every quiz on the platform is multiple-choice. The ALE's largest exam component — AMBF/PSO/TGFA calculations, timed floor plan design, BP 344 compliance checklists, and occupant load calculations — has zero product support.
What to build: (1) A Development Controls Calculator — input lot data, building type, occupancy; output AMBF, PSO, TGFA, required parking slots, accessible parking count, occupant load. (2) A Design Problem Library — timed prompts organized by building type (residential → commercial → institutional) with a BP 344 compliance checklist that the student runs after completing their layout. (3) An Area C Endurance Track — position the Endurance Cycle explicitly as Area C stamina training: "Day 2 of the ALE is 3+ hours. Your Endurance window is currently 72 minutes. Today pushes it to 80."
Direct Source Evidence
"Most first-time failures cite running out of time on Area C as a primary cause. The Endurance Cycle and Proof Simulation serve this; they must be positioned as Area C performance tools."
Filipino Architect Encyclopedia + Architect Journey UX Research
02
P1 CRITICAL
Retaker Onboarding — A Shame-Sensitive Distinct Flow
Retakers are the highest-willingness-to-pay segment on the platform. They have lived the pain of inadequate preparation and will pay for anything that gives them a genuine system. But the encyclopedia documents the "hiya" (shame) culture explicitly — ALE failure is not discussed openly. A retaker hitting the same onboarding flow as a first-timer is a conversion-killing mistake.
What to build: An onboarding branch for users who self-identify as retakers. The word "retake" and "failure" never appear — only "returning reviewer" and "more data, better strategy." The retaker flow shows their heatmap from the previous cycle if available, asks "What subject felt hardest?" and uses that as the diagnostic seed. First screen copy: "You know the ALE. You know what needs to change. SlateCamp gives you the data to change it." The Proof Simulation is surfaced on Day 1 for retakers — not gated behind 30 sessions.
Direct Source Evidence
"Retakers will not self-identify publicly. ALE failure is not discussed openly. Never make failure visible to others. Handle retaker onboarding with extreme sensitivity — the word 'retake' never appears in user-facing copy."
Architect Journey UX Research — Section on Hiya Culture
03
P1 CRITICAL
Personal Cram Card Generator — Auto-Built From Wrong Answer Patterns
Every serious ALE reviewer manually compiles a personal "cram card" — the 15–25 numerical values they keep getting wrong. BP 344 clearances, Fire Code travel distances, PD 1096 stairway dimensions, SPP fee percentages, CPD units, DFU values. This is documented real reviewer behavior. SlateCamp has the wrong-answer data to do this automatically — and isn't using it for this purpose.
What to build: After 20+ quiz sessions, auto-generate a "Cram Card" — the top 15–20 numerical values where the user's answer accuracy is lowest AND the exam frequency is high. Format: one printable A4 PDF. Update weekly. Each entry shows: the value, the code reference, and a short "remember this because" hook derived from the wiki_items exam_tip type. Shareable to Campfire. The moment this feature exists, it becomes the most word-of-mouth-driven feature on the platform — reviewers share their cram cards with batchmates.
Direct Source Evidence
"Compile personal cram card: critical numbers, code provisions, common exam traps. Every serious reviewer manually compiles this list — SlateCamp should do it automatically."
Filipino Architect Encyclopedia + Architect Journey UX Research
04
P1 CRITICAL
Exam Day Notification System — Pre-Exam Protocol + Post-Day-1 Lockout
The encyclopedia documents specific behaviors that harm ALE performance, yet no product feature addresses exam-day psychology. The most documented: candidates who analyze Day 1 questions with peers during the lunch break and overnight create anxiety that tanks Day 2 performance. SlateCamp can intervene — at zero build cost — with the right notification at the right moment.
What to build: (1) T-7 days: "Stop learning new content. Review what you know cold." (2) T-1 day: Physical prep checklist — ID, pencils, printed PD 1096 Rules VII & VIII, venue confirmation. (3) Evening before Day 2: "Do NOT discuss Day 1 questions with anyone tonight. This is documented to reduce Day 2 performance. Sleep. Tomorrow is the real challenge." (4) Post-exam morning: "Results take 3–7 days. You did the work. Now rest." These notifications cost nothing to build and directly address documented exam-failure behavior.
Direct Source Evidence
"Do not discuss specific questions after Day 1 — this is prohibited and creates unnecessary anxiety about items you cannot change. Lunch break: do not analyze your answers with peers."
Filipino Architect Encyclopedia — ALE Exam Day Section
05
P1 CRITICAL
Subject Floor Alert — "You Will Fail Even With a High Overall Score"
The ALE requires a minimum 50% per subject regardless of overall weighted average. A student at 74% overall with 47% in Structural Design will fail. The Readiness Score currently has no sub-score floor warning. This is the most common surprise failure pattern documented in the encyclopedia — and the platform has the data to prevent it but no feature to surface it.
What to build: A persistent dashboard alert that fires whenever any subject sub-score approaches or falls below 50%: "⚠️ Structural Design is at 49%. The ALE requires 50% per subject to pass — regardless of your overall score. This one subject will determine your result." The alert must be impossible to dismiss without taking action (routing to the Structural trail). Color the subject's heatmap section in a distinct warning state. The Readiness Score card on mobile shows a floor-breach indicator badge when any subject is below threshold.
Direct Source Evidence
"70% general weighted average required, with no subject below 50%. The subject floor requirement is what produces surprise failures among students who score well overall."

Capturing students 5 years early
is the only moat a competitor can't buy.

The encyclopedia documents that 5th-year students doing thesis work are 12–18 months from the ALE, deeply engaged with building codes daily, and have zero structured preparation support. A student acquired in thesis year arrives at ALE review with mastery data already in the system — a heatmap that isn't starting from zero.

"The first-mover who captures the undergrad is the only competitor who can honestly say: 'Your ALE preparation started 5 years ago.' The student who starts using SlateCamp in 3rd year of college doesn't switch to review mode at graduation — they arrive with years of mastery data already in the system."

05 — DAE Phase Features (Years 7–8 of the Architect's Life)

The invisible opportunity —
2 years that compete with forgetting.

The encyclopedia identifies the DAE period as "professionally energized, academically fading." DAE architects apply PD 1096 and BP 344 every day — but in practice context, not exam format. When they return to ALE review, they discover that 2 years of real-world code application didn't translate to exam performance. They know the codes as practitioners but not as examinees.

01
Phase 2
DAE Maintenance Mode — Context-Aware Daily Practice
A low-frequency study track: 10–15 questions per day, matched to the DAE architect's current work context. The key insight from the encyclopedia: a DAE architect doing accessible toilet details all week knows BP 344 in practice — but can they answer "What is the minimum turning radius for a wheelchair in an accessible toilet?" in ALE multiple-choice format? The practice knowledge is there; the exam-format translation is not.
What to build: A weekly "What are you working on?" prompt — select current practice context from: Building Permit Preparation / Construction Supervision / Design Development / Pre-Design Research / Government Work. SlateCamp surfaces 10 questions tagged to that context's relevant codes. "You're doing building permit work? Here are 10 questions on PD 1096 Rule 7, the OBO process, and development controls." The logbook-like UX mirrors their actual DAE workflow. Free tier: 5 questions/day. Pro tier: full DAE Mode access.

P2 High
Code Update Alert System — When Laws Change, Alert the Right Users
The encyclopedia documents that architects practice under updated codes with no systematic update mechanism. BP 344 was revised with a 2024 IRR. NSCP editions update. PEC revises. 03
P3 Medium
Bloom Cognitive Tracker — "You Know It But Can't Use It"
Raw accuracy tells you how often a student gets questions right. bloom_level from the v5.0 taxonomy tells you what type of thinking they're demonstrating. The encyclopedia describes the highest-value ALE question type: "The trend of questions is more analytical and technical — you have to answer it as an architect and think like one." The moment after the exam is
the biggest retention opportunity.

08 — Campfire & Community Features

Community features built around existing Filipino architect social bonds — not invented from scratch.

The encyclopedia documents strong existing social structures: school affiliation identity (UST vs. PUP batchmates), regional practice networks, firm networks, and UAP chapter membership. Campfire should tap these existing bonds — not try to create artificial community dynamics.

Campfire Feature 01
Structured Passer Story Template
Don't just invite passers to share — give them a structured format: Starting Score / Final Score / Trail Duration / 3 Things That Helped Most / 1 Thing to Do Differently. This produces consistently useful intelligence for current reviewers, not generic testimonials. The structured format also makes the data extractable for anonymized product insights.
Campfire Feature 02
Private Retaker Support Space
A private Campfire channel accessible only to users who self-identify as retakers. No school names, no real names required. No public leaderboards. The hiya culture means this space must be explicitly private and judgment-free. "I failed January 2025. Here's what I found when I analyzed my score." This becomes the most emotionally bonded segment of the community — and the most retention-loyal.
Campfire Feature 03
School Cohort Heatmaps (Anonymous Aggregate)
Filipino architecture school identity is real — UST vs. Mapua vs. PUP batchmates. Anonymous aggregate cohort heatmaps by school: "UST June 2026 cohort: strongest in History, weakest in Structural." Individual data is always private. School-level aggregate is safe and motivating. This feeds the Dean's Dashboard B2B pitch and creates organic word-of-mouth within school networks.
Campfire Feature 04
Regional Architecture Channels
Philippine architecture is regionally organized — Cebu, Davao, and Metro Manila architects have different practice contexts, different LGU permit quirks, different heritage frameworks. Regional Campfire channels let architects share local practice knowledge: "Quezon City OBO now requires X." This is content a national platform can't generate without community contribution. It creates regional stickiness that national marketing can't buy.
Campfire Feature 05
Study Pod Accountability Loop
Groups of 4–6 students link accounts. Shared score delta view (not individual scores — privacy preserved). Pod milestone: all members clear 70% → unlock Proof Simulation one week early. Social accountability drives 2–3x lower churn per the business plan. The pod model also creates word-of-mouth acquisition: you can only form a pod with people you invite to SlateCamp.
Campfire Feature 06
Topnotcher Effect Cascade
When a PRC Topnotcher (Top 10) used SlateCamp, the entire Campfire activates. Structured story, heatmap trajectory visualization, interview post. The ALE topnotcher carries outsized cultural weight in Philippine architecture — every batchmate knows who their school's topnotcher was. A single verified topnotcher story generates more acquisition than ₱50,000 in Meta ads.
09 — B2B & Institutional Features

Three institutional channels — each one
a structural distribution moat.

The encyclopedia reveals a complete institutional ecosystem around Philippine architecture: 94 accredited schools, 3 major review centers, UAP chapters, DPWH, DOH, and the PRBoA. Each is a B2B opportunity with a different relationship model and different data need.

Architecture Schools — Phase 2 Core

Dean's Dashboard — Cohort-Level ALE Intelligence

Architecture deans track ALE performance as an institutional KPI — school ranking, topnotcher count, passing rate. No data product currently gives them cohort-level intelligence before the exam. SlateCamp's aggregate heatmap data is the most actionable information a Dean of Architecture has ever had access to.

What to build: Dean's Dashboard — anonymized aggregate heatmap for the school's enrolled cohort. Subjects where the cohort is systematically weakest. Comparison against the national SlateCamp cohort. Early warning: "Your June 2026 cohort shows a systematic gap in Structural Design — 4 months before the exam." Per-seat institutional pricing. Entry point: comp exam integration — schools that administer comp exams feed scores to SlateCamp and get cohort analytics back.

Review Centers — Coexistence Play

White-Label Intelligence Layer for JPT & ATLAS-CDC

The encyclopedia shows that JPT and ATLAS-CDC are the primary review infrastructure. They provide structure and community; they have no personalization or data layer. SlateCamp's correct positioning: "Don't build it — license SlateCamp instead." Review centers become distributors, not competitors.

What to build: A review center partnership program. Review centers recommend SlateCamp to their enrollees + use the Readiness Score data to personalize their own program delivery. Revenue share or referral model. The white-label engine (Business Plan Revenue Stream 4): review centers run SlateCamp's mastery engine under their own brand. They bring the students; SlateCamp runs the digital layer at ₱400–₱800/student/cycle.

UAP & PRC — Accreditation & Intelligence Play

CPD Accreditation Application + ALE Performance Intelligence Product

The business plan identifies two high-value institutional plays at the UAP and PRC level. First: apply for PRC CPD accreditation for SlateCamp's own content. If approved, completed SlateCamp modules count toward the 45-unit CPD requirement — expanding the TAM from ~3,000 new passers/year to ~25,000+ active RLAs who need CPD renewal every 3 years. Second: SlateCamp's exam_outcomes table, when populated, will contain the first-ever correlational dataset between specific learning behaviors and ALE pass rates in the Philippines. This dataset is valuable to the PRBoA for syllabus calibration and to UAP for CPD content planning — and they will pay for access to it.

What to build for UAP: A presence at UAP NATCON (annual national convention) — the highest-density gathering of Filipino architects. Low cost (sponsorship). High ROI: access to senior architect market (CPD), school representatives (B2B), and next-cycle reviewers (acquisition). What to build for PRC: The ALE Performance Intelligence Product (Business Plan Revenue Stream 6) — anonymized performance data sold as intelligence to UAP, architecture schools, DepEd/CHED, and publishers. Pricing: ₱20K–₱200K per institutional buyer per year.

Architecture Firms — Scholarship-Sponsorship Channel

Firm Sponsorship as Ethical Talent Pipeline Investment

The UAP Code of Ethical Conduct (Doc 200) prohibits direct advertising by architects — architecture firms cannot run ads offering employment to unlicensed architects. Sponsoring SlateCamp access for unlicensed employees and target recruits is the ethical channel for talent pipeline investment. The business plan maps this at ₱75K–₱1M per firm per year depending on tier.

What to build: Four sponsorship tiers: Blueprint (₱75K, 15 sponsored seats) / Foundation (₱150K, 40 seats) / Cornerstone (₱350K, 100 seats) / Architect Patron (₱1M, unlimited + NATCON branding). Firms get: sponsored access for unlicensed staff, aggregate readiness dashboard for their sponsored users, priority recruitment access to SlateCamp passers through Campfire, and a listing in the "Firms That Support Filipino Architects" directory.

SME Author Program

Senior Architects as Content Authors — CPD Credit + Compensation

The encyclopedia documents that senior architects teach part-time at BSA programs and maintain deep expertise in specific practice domains. An SME author program — licensed architects who author and verify Wiki content and question rationales in exchange for CPD authorship credit and compensation — qualitatively differentiates SlateCamp's content from anything a generic EdTech company can produce and creates institutional legitimacy.

What to build: SME authorship program: licensed RLA authors receive CPD credit for authorship hours (if PRC-accredited) + ₱500–₱2,000 per published wiki article and ₱200–₱500 per question rationale reviewed. Verified author badge on their articles. This is the content strategy that makes SlateCamp's wiki impossible to replicate by a competitor without equivalent licensed architect relationships.

Year 2
3D Lab Extended — Earthwork, MEP Cutaways, and Structural Bay Progression
The encyclopedia documents that MEP systems are "invisible in buildings — hidden in walls and ceilings" and that "students read about them without ever seeing how they're spatially organized." The encyclopedia also extensively covers the structural coordination challenges early-career architects face. The 186-module 3D Lab roadmap includes these but the product architecture hasn't specified how they differ from code-compliance modules.
What to build for MEP: A cutaway 3D building model revealing MEP layers — toggle each system on/off: structural frame → plumbing stacks → electrical conduit → HVAC ductwork → fire protection piping. Show how they navigate conflicts. This is the single most spatially educational model in the entire 3D Lab — it teaches coordination, not just code compliance.

What to build for Structural: Progressive bay complexity — start with a simple RCC frame, add seismic bracing, add floor loads, show how member sizes change. Connect to NSCP 2015 provisions. "This beam is undersized for Zone 2 seismic loads — here's why."
Source: Filipino Architect Encyclopedia — Section 17 Project Coordination + 3D Visual Learning Research

Year 2
Heritage Architecture Track — RA 10066 and Adaptive Reuse Specialty Content
The encyclopedia documents that senior architects increasingly deal with heritage building adaptive reuse — RA 10066 clearances from NHCP for structures over 50 years old, heritage zone projects, pre-war structure interventions. The encyclopedia even notes: "Every Filipino architect carries two things: the memory of a design that was built exactly as drawn, and the memory of one that wasn't." Heritage architecture is a growing practice specialty and a legal compliance area increasingly tested in the ALE under Professional Practice.
What to build: A heritage architecture Wiki section — RA 10066 provisions, NHCP documentation requirements, conservation methodology, Philippine National Artists for Architecture (Nakpil, Locsin, Antonio, Mañosa) with their building details and significance. The National Artists content is high-frequency in ALE History questions and deeply meaningful professionally. Add a Heritage Assessment Tool: input structure age and location → outputs applicable laws, required clearances, and conservation options. This positions SlateCamp in the heritage space where no digital resource currently exists.
Source: Encyclopedia Section 15 Elder Practice + Encyclopedia Section 16 Code Stack (RA 10066)

Multi-Profession Expansion — NLE as the First Scale Move
The business plan maps the Nursing expansion as a 16x revenue opportunity — 100,000+ NLE candidates per year vs. 6,400 ALE candidates. (3) Design the SME recruitment playbook for nursing content authors — same model as the architecture SME author program, applied to RNs and nursing educators. (4) Begin community outreach to NLE Facebook groups (the same strategy that works for architecture groups). Revenue model and pricing will mirror ALE with minor adjustments for the larger market volume.
