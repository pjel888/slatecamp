## Error Description

The article teaches that the maximum ramp slope under BP 344 is 1:12 for permanent ramps and disparages 1:20 as "too shallow." The 2024 Revised IRR of BP 344 (NCDA) states the general standard is "Gradient not steeper than 1:20." The 1:12 standard only applies to swimming pool transfer walls.

## How the Question Propagates the Error

**Question Text:** "What is the recommended ramp slope ratio according to BP 344?"

**Correct Answer (option b):** "1:12 preferred, steeper allowed temporarily with conditions"

**Rationale (incorrect):** "BP 344 specifies maximum ramp slope of 1:12 (1V:12H) for permanent ramps; maximum 1:20 allowed temporarily with handrails and landings."

**Option c (wrong answer, disparages correct value):** "1:20 (too shallow), generally not recommended for accessibility purposes"

The question explicitly teaches the OUTDATED 1:12 standard as correct and characterizes the correct 1:20 standard as "too shallow" and "generally not recommended."

## Verified Correct Answer

The 2024 Revised IRR of BP 344 states gradient not steeper than 1:20 as the general standard for ramps. 1:12 appears only for swimming pool transfer walls.

## Impact

Students learning from this question will be taught the wrong ramp slope standard. If they apply 1:12 to a real project, they will build a ramp that is steeper than legally permitted under the 2024 Revised IRR.

## Recommended Correction

1. Change correct answer from option b to option c (or create new option with 1:20)
2. Rewrite rationale to cite the 2024 Revised IRR's 1:20 standard
3. Include code citation: BP 344 2024 Revised IRR
