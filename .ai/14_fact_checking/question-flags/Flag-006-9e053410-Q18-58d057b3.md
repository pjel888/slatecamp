## Error Description

The article teaches that "Certificate of Use" is the document issued after an approved change of occupancy in an existing building. The correct document per PD 1096 Section 309.9 is a "Certificate of Occupancy" ("other Certificates of Occupancy due to changes in use"). The term "Certificate of Use" does not appear in the PD 1096 IRR.

## How the Question Propagates the Error

**Question Text:** "After an approved change of occupancy, what document does the Building Official issue?"

**Correct Answer (option b):** "A Certificate of Use"

**Rationale (teaches the wrong mnemonic):** "Mnemonic: 'COU ≠ CO — Certificate of U-se for existing building, change of U-se; Certificate of O-ccupancy for new/O-riginal occupancy.' The letter that changes in the document name tells you which building status it applies to."

The question explicitly teaches "Certificate of Use" as the correct answer and the rationale reinforces this with a mnemonic.

## Verified Correct Answer

Section 309.9 of the PD 1096 2004 Revised IRR states: "other Certificates of Occupancy due to changes in use." The correct document is a Certificate of Occupancy, not a Certificate of Use.

## Impact

Multiple questions in this article's question bank teach "Certificate of Use" as correct terminology. Students learning this will answer exam questions incorrectly and may use the wrong terminology in professional practice.

## Recommended Correction

1. Change correct answer from "Certificate of Use" to "Certificate of Occupancy"
2. Remove the "COU ≠ CO" mnemonic from rationale
3. Rewrite rationale to reference Section 309.9 correctly
