## Flag 2: POTENTIALLY INACCURATE CLAIM — "Must Prove Reciprocity OR Substantial Equivalence"

**Impact:** MEDIUM
**Location:** Paragraph 1, lines 5-6
**Exact Quote:** "A person who is not a Philippine citizen at the time of applying to take the licensure examination must prove either reciprocity exists or substantial equivalence in education, training, and experience before being permitted examination."

**Analysis:** VERIFIED AGAINST ACTUAL RA 9266 TEXT. The actual Section 27 states:

> "A person who is not a citizen of the Philippines at the time he/she applies to take the examination shall not be allowed to take the licensure examination unless he/she can prove... that, by specific provision of law, the country of which he/she is a citizen, subject or national either admits citizens of the Philippines to the practice of the same profession without restriction or allows them to practice it after passing an examination on terms of strict and absolute equality with citizens, subjects or national of the country concerned, including the unconditional recognition of prerequisite degrees/diplomas..."

The article's claim that the applicant must prove "either reciprocity exists or substantial equivalence in education, training, and experience" is **NOT SUPPORTED** by the actual text. RA 9266 Section 27 requires proof of **reciprocal treatment by the foreign country** — specifically that the foreign country either (a) admits Filipinos without restriction OR (b) allows them to practice after examination on terms of strict and absolute equality. The phrase "substantial equivalence in education, training, and experience" does **not appear** in Section 27. The article appears to have conflated RA 9266 with other professional regulatory laws (e.g., nursing or engineering laws that use "substantial equivalence" language) or with general PRC rules.

**Verdict:** CONFIRMED INCORRECT. The article invents an alternative pathway ("substantial equivalence") that does not exist in RA 9266 Section 27.

## Flag 4: CONTRADICTORY CLAIM — Section 27 vs Section 38 Confusion

**Category:** LEGAL_INACCURACY / CONTRADICTS_QUESTION
**Impact:** HIGH
**Location:** Paragraph under "Application to Foreign Architects in the Philippines", lines 29-31
**Exact Quote:** "Foreign architects from reciprocal countries may be eligible to take the Philippine licensure examination under Section 27, but must secure temporary/special permits for practice under Section 38, with Filipino counterpart requirements. However, all foreign architects, including those from reciprocal countries, must secure a temporary/special permit to practice under Section 38, which is project-specific and requires a Filipino counterpart."

**Analysis:** VERIFIED AGAINST ACTUAL RA 9266 TEXT. The actual Section 38 states:

> "Foreign nationals who have gained entry in the Philippines to perform professional services as architects or consultants in foreign-funded or assisted projects of the government or employed or engaged by Filipino or foreign contractors or private firms, shall, before assuming the duties, functions and responsibilities as architects or consultants, secure a special/temporary permit from the Board..."

Section 38 applies to **foreign nationals performing professional services on specific projects** — it does NOT apply to foreign nationals who have already passed the Philippine licensure examination and obtained a Philippine architect's license. Once a foreign national from a reciprocal country passes the Philippine exam and is registered under RA 9266, they are a **licensed Philippine architect** and do not need a Section 38 temporary/special permit to practice.

The article's claim that "all foreign architects, including those from reciprocal countries, must secure a temporary/special permit to practice under Section 38" is **CONFIRMED INCORRECT**. It conflates:
- **Section 27 pathway**: Foreign national → proves reciprocity → takes Philippine exam → passes → becomes licensed Philippine architect → practices without Section 38 permit
- **Section 38 pathway**: Foreign national (licensed in home country but NOT in Philippines) → gets temporary/special permit for specific project → works with Filipino counterpart

These are separate, parallel pathways. The article incorrectly implies that Section 38 is a mandatory step for ALL foreign architects, including those who have already been licensed through the Section 27 examination pathway.

**Verdict:** CONFIRMED INCORRECT. The article fundamentally misrepresents the relationship between Section 27 and Section 38.

**Evidence:** RA 9266 Section 38, actual text retrieved from lawphil.net on 2026-05-02.

**Confidence:** HIGH (verified against primary source — clear statutory language)

**Recommended Article Change:** Completely rewrite the "Application to Foreign Architects in the Philippines" section to clarify:
1. Section 27: For foreign nationals seeking **full Philippine licensure** via examination (requires reciprocity proof)
2. Section 38: For foreign nationals seeking **temporary project-specific practice** without Philippine licensure (requires special/temporary permit, Filipino counterpart, applicable to both reciprocal and non-reciprocal countries at Board discretion)
3. These are alternative pathways, not sequential requirements
4. A foreign architect who obtains full Philippine licensure through Section 27 does NOT need a Section 38 permit
