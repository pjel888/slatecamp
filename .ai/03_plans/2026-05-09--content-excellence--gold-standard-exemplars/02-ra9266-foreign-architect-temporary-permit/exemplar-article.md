# RA 9266 Foreign Architect and Temporary Permit Gate

## Learning objective

Given a professional-practice scenario involving a foreign architect, identify whether the person may lawfully perform architectural services in the Philippines and which RA 9266 IRR requirement controls the answer.

## Definitions that come first

The RA 9266 IRR defines a foreign architect as an architect who is not a Filipino citizen and is not registered and licensed in the Philippines, but is duly registered and licensed in the architect's home country. It also defines the Filipino counterpart as the local Philippine architect, partnership, or corporation that must work in association with the foreign architect, partnership, or corporation on a project on Philippine soil.

Source: RA 9266 IRR, Section 3, Definition of Terms, Items 30 and 31, PDF p.5, extracted from `vitruvius-connection/001_Raw-Source-Layer/001-Laws/RA-9266/RA-9266 IRR Architecture Act of 2004.pdf`.

## The permit gate

Section 38 is the exam hinge. A foreign national who enters the Philippines to perform professional services as an architect or consultant in the covered projects must secure a special/temporary permit from the Board, subject to approval of the Commission, before assuming the duties, functions, and responsibilities as architect or consultant.

Source: RA 9266 IRR, Section 38, Coverage of Temporary/Special Permits, PDF p.20.

## Board shortcut

Being licensed abroad is not the same as authority to practice architecture in the Philippines. In an LEA stem, check three gates: home-country licensure, Filipino counterpart, and special/temporary permit approval. If any gate is missing, the option that treats foreign licensure alone as sufficient is wrong.
