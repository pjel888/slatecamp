# PD 957 License to Sell: Registration Is Not Yet Selling Authority

## Learning objective

Given a subdivision or condominium buyer-protection stem, decide whether the fact pattern is about registration, license to sell, public offering materials, or performance bond requirements under the PD 957 IRR.

## The rule that decides the answer

Section 11 of Rule IV states that no owner or dealer shall sell any disposable subdivision lot or condominium unit in the registered project without a license to sell issued by the Board. Registration alone is not the answer when the stem asks whether the developer may start selling units/lots.

Source: PD 957 IRR, Rule IV, Section 11, PDF p.47, extracted from `vitruvius-connection/001_Raw-Source-Layer/001-Laws/PD-957/PD 957 IRR - Subdivision and Condominium IRR.pdf`.

## Timing is attached to registration

The same extracted Section 11 language says the license to sell is issued within two weeks from registration of each project, after proper application, required work program, performance bond under Section 12, and payment of the prescribed license fee.

Source: PD 957 IRR, Rule IV, Section 11, PDF p.47.

## Public offering materials are part of the paper trail

The registration/application evidence includes a copy of any circular, prospectus, brochure, advertisement, or communication used or to be used for public offering of the subject project and for circulation upon Board approval. This is not a substitute for the license to sell; it is part of the regulatory evidence trail.

Source: PD 957 IRR, Rule IV application documentary requirement excerpt, PDF p.43.

## Board shortcut

When the stem says “registered project,” do not automatically choose “may sell.” Ask whether the license to sell has been issued. The exam trap is treating registration, advertising submission, and selling authority as one step.
