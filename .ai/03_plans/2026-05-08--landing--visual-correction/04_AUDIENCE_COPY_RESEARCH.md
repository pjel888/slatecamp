# 04 — Audience and Copy Research: What Users Actually Care About

Date: 2026-05-08
Status: copy direction, no implementation

## Target audience reminder

SlateCamp is for Filipino architecture students, apprentices, and ALE candidates who need a supplementary architecture knowledge resource.

It is not positioned as a primary review center.

It should feel like:

- a reliable field guide
- an architecture knowledge atlas
- a Pokedex-style reference for concepts, codes, topics, and weak areas
- a calmer companion beside school, review center, notes, mock boards, and apprenticeship

## Core audience emotion

Users do not primarily care about:

- SRS
- active recall
- spaced repetition algorithms
- quizzing methodology
- mastery models
- adaptive engines
- taxonomy analytics

They care about:

- assurance
- comfort
- reliability
- knowing what matters
- finding topics quickly
- not feeling lost
- knowing what to do next
- understanding rather than panic-memorizing
- having a dependable place to return to when reviewers, PDFs, recordings, and notes feel scattered

## Research signals

Research from architecture/ALE and broader Philippine board-prep discussions shows recurring concerns:

- anxiety before board exams
- mock board scores shaking confidence
- unfinished recordings/materials
- uncertainty about what is enough
- code memorization overwhelm
- balancing apprenticeship/work and review
- expensive or disappointing review-center experiences
- need for structure, clarity, and trust

Useful vernacular/emotional states:

- kabado
- andami pang hindi naaral
- pangangapa
- scattered notes/reviewers
- mock boards shook confidence
- familiarization vs memorization
- finding what to focus on next

## Positioning principle

SlateCamp should say:

> We help you find, understand, and organize architecture knowledge so you can review with less panic and a clearer next step.

It should not say:

> We are the review center, the official source, or the system that guarantees readiness.

## Copy territories to use

Strong:

- Architecture knowledge atlas
- Field guide for architecture review
- Find the concept fast
- Know where a topic fits
- Use it beside school and review center
- Less pangangapa
- Understand before you memorize
- Turn scattered notes into a map
- Review with a calmer next step
- Source-backed where verified
- Visual explanations for spatial rules
- Practice to check what stayed
- Weak areas to revisit

Acceptable, with care:

- readiness signals
- weak-topic analytics
- repair list
- board-season companion
- ALE season support

Use sparingly:

- quiz
- dashboard
- analytics
- 3D Lab
- active learning

Avoid leading with:

- SRS
- active recall
- spaced repetition
- algorithm
- mastery engine
- diagnostics engine
- AI-powered learning platform
- pass probability
- guarantee
- official syllabus
- review-center alternative

## Ethical fear marketing

Fear is allowed only if it names a real anxiety and immediately gives agency.

Good pattern:

> The scary part is not knowing what you missed until too late. SlateCamp helps you find weak topics early and return to your review with a clearer next step.

Bad pattern:

> Are you sure you will pass? Don't risk your future.

Better SlateCamp tone:

- calm but honest
- direct but not shame-based
- reassuring but not guaranteeing
- Filipino-student-aware without trying too hard to be slangy

## Suggested copy posture

### Hero posture

Do not write a feature list.

Write for the anxious student who has too many tabs, reviewers, notes, and recordings.

Possible directions:

- "Find your way through architecture review."
- "A calmer atlas for architecture knowledge."
- "Know what matters. Find it faster. Review with less pangangapa."
- "Architecture knowledge, mapped for the moments you feel lost."

Subcopy should include:

- supplementary positioning
- school/review center support
- knowledge/resource/field-guide category
- no guarantee / educational boundary, but not in a heavy-handed way above the fold

### Problem section posture

Use the user's world:

- review recordings pile up
- codes and tables blur together
- mock boards expose gaps
- school notes are scattered
- you need to know what to revisit, not just study harder

### Product section posture

Use actual product verbs:

- browse the wiki
- open related topics
- practice a topic
- revisit weak areas
- see visual examples
- check readiness signals

### Trust section posture

Be explicit:

- supplementary platform
- not affiliated with PRC/PRBoA/universities/government agencies
- not a review center
- no pass guarantees
- verify official/current requirements with primary sources or qualified professionals

## Landing copy anti-patterns

Reject copy that sounds like:

- "Optimize retention with active recall and SRS."
- "Maximize your readiness with adaptive algorithms."
- "The all-in-one platform for passing the ALE."
- "World-class review system."
- "Official syllabus-backed review center."
- "Every missed answer becomes a guaranteed repair path."
- "Full 3D lab free after signup" if access is gated.

## CTA language

Better CTAs:

- Explore the atlas
- Start with the free atlas
- Find a topic
- Open the field guide
- Start with a practice check
- Browse resources

Use "Create account" only when the route truly requires signup.

Avoid generic CTAs repeated everywhere:

- Get started
- Learn more
- Start now

## Copy acceptance criteria

A future copy pass must satisfy:

- A tired architecture student understands the category in 5 seconds.
- It does not sound like generic edtech SaaS.
- It does not lead with methods users do not care about.
- It respects review centers/school instead of positioning against them.
- It offers relief without promising exam outcomes.
- It is legally safe and source-boundary aware.
- It matches actual product behavior.
