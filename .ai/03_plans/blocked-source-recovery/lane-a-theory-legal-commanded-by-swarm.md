## 6. Risks

### Shared risks (both clusters)
1. **vitruvius-connection inaccessibility:** The source layer was not available during this investigation. The crawl summary from 2026-05-09 confirms its existence. Before reopening, the source layer should be re-verified as accessible.
2. **Theory vs. law source asymmetry:** Theory clusters cannot be grounded the same way as legal clusters. Legal clusters cite specific code sections; theory clusters cite academic texts and syllabus scope. This creates a lower "source confidence" ceiling.
3. **Syllabus interpretation risk:** The IRR says "sociocultural and technological trends" — this is broad enough to include spatial justice and digital architecture, but broad enough that a different SME could argue these topics are not required. Source records should note this interpretive nature.

### 008-02 specific risks
4. **Paywalled primary source:** Soja's book costs $24.95. The publisher page and WorldCat record serve as citation authorities, but full-text verification of specific claims is not possible without purchase/library access. The current article does not quote Soja directly — it summarizes his framework — so full-text verification may not be critical.
5. **Western-centric theory:** Soja's framework is built on Los Angeles case studies. The article's Philippine application (bayanihan, informal settlements) extends beyond what Soja directly addresses. The ESSC source partially bridges this gap but is not academically peer-reviewed.

### 008-03 specific risks
6. **No single canonical textbook:** Unlike spatial justice (Soja), digital architecture has no single definitive text. The topic is synthesized from multiple professional standards and technology concepts. Source records should reflect that the authority is distributed across syllabi + SPP + professional practice evidence.
7. **Rapidly evolving technology:** BIM/VR/AI tools evolve faster than professional standards. The IRR (2004/2010) predates widespread BIM adoption in the Philippines. Source citations should use the syllabi's "technological trends" language to accommodate this.
