# SlateCamp landing reference sites — deep design analysis addendum

**Date:** 2026-05-07
**Status:** Research-only addendum / no implementation
**Branch:** `feat/landing-3d-hero-best-representation`
**Purpose:** Reframe the landing direction after Philip clarified that the Three.js hero does **not** need to be a literal floating architectural model, and that SlateCamp should be positioned more as an **architecture knowledge atlas/resource** than as a generic study tool. The visual can be abstract, magical, spatial, and brand-defining as long as it evokes architecture, assurance, comfort, reliability, and organized knowledge for Filipino LEA/ALE examinees.

---

## 1. Correction to the previous brief

The earlier brief was too constrained around a literal architectural proof object: a lot, setbacks, a building mass, one slider, and a compliance story.

That direction is useful as one possible product-proof module, but it is **not the only valid landing hero direction**.

Philip's updated direction is stronger:

> Use Three.js like the reference sites use it: as atmosphere, identity, spatial emotion, and brand world. It can be abstract if it evokes architecture, knowledge, review comfort, and reliability.

### Updated visual north star

> **A magical architecture-knowledge field:** an abstract spatial system where dots, grids, sheets, light, fragments, and pathways suggest that scattered exam knowledge is becoming organized, navigable, and less frightening.

This should feel like:

- an architecture knowledge atlas / code atlas / field guide coming alive;
- a calm review buddy beside review centers, school, books, notes, mentors, and self-study;
- a source-backed field guide, not a review-center replacement;
- assurance and comfort, not a lecture about SRS or quiz algorithms;
- magic through order, light, and spatial transformation — not fantasy clutter.

## 2. Reference site analysis

### A. Infinite Field — WebGL as a world, not a widget

**Observed core:** One expressive canvas defines the whole experience. The site uses a glowing orange, terrain-like/ribbon-like field against darkness, sparse text, and huge restraint. It does not show a literal product UI. It creates a *place-feeling* first.

**Design intent:**

- Create a portal into an abstract digital field.
- Make the visitor pause before reading.
- Build memory through one iconic moving form.
- Let atmosphere carry brand meaning.

**Emotional effect:**

- Mystery.
- Calm intensity.
- Premium restraint.
- Spatial depth without literal architecture.
- A sense of entering an authored digital environment.

**How Three.js/canvas is used:**

- As the primary identity object.
- As a mood engine.
- As a spatial metaphor.
- As a pacing device that slows the user down.
- Not as a tool, configurator, game, or dashboard.

**Transfer to SlateCamp:**

SlateCamp can have one memorable abstract field instead of a literal building model. Possible visual metaphors:

- a living blueprint field;
- dotted exam topics forming a stable route;
- warm light moving through cool slate grids;
- scattered study fragments aligning into a review map;
- topographic contour lines becoming subject pathways;
- floating resource sheets orbiting a calm central axis;
- a constellation of codes, quizzes, notes, and tools becoming one field guide.

**Hard lesson:** If the main visual is powerful, the first viewport must be quiet. Do not place four feature cards, long support copy, a mini tool, and multiple CTAs around it.

---

### B. Matter — physical-world intelligence through cinematic restraint

**Observed core:** Matter uses a full-bleed, physical-world hero with a short category-defining headline: “Superintelligent Vision for the Physical World.” It sells a large transformation first, then grounds it in sensor/model specifics below.

**Design intent:**

- Make a complex technical product feel consequential.
- Position the company as a new perceptual layer for the physical world.
- Use industrial context and analytical overlays to create authority.
- Start with awe, then explain.

**Emotional effect:**

- Seriousness.
- Frontier confidence.
- Technical authority.
- Controlled mystery.
- “This understands more than the surface.”

**Motion/video role:**

- The moving/physical background proves the domain.
- The visual suggests machine perception and scale.
- The image carries meaning before the copy explains the mechanism.

**Transfer to SlateCamp:**

SlateCamp should not open with mechanism-first language like “SRS,” “active recall,” “adaptive quizzes,” or “review analytics.” Those are internal methods.

The Matter-style move is to open with the user-level transformation:

- “Stop studying blind.”
- “Architecture knowledge you can actually rely on.”
- “Know what stuck. Know what to repair.”
- “Your architecture field guide for board-review season.”

Then ground it below the fold:

- source-backed wiki;
- quizzes and weak-area repair;
- 3D visual explanations;
- practical calculators/tools;
- supplementary review-center support.

**Three.js implication:** The hero visual should feel like SlateCamp *sees* and organizes architecture knowledge. It can be an abstract field that turns unclear fragments into visible relationships.

### C. Save.design — a workspace world for a specific expert audience

**Observed core:** Save.design is not just a bookmarking/gallery tool. It positions itself as a “technical design inspiration workspace.” The copy is concise and action-based: save references, experiment with code, document process, grow with the community.

**Design intent:**

- Speak to a niche audience that values craft and technical depth.
- Make the website itself prove taste and implementation literacy.
- Turn “inspiration” from passive browsing into an organized workspace.
- Use dark technical atmosphere, grids, small mono labels, and canvas visuals to make the product feel serious.

**Emotional effect:**

- Insider-tool credibility.
- Technical calm.
- “This was made by people who understand my workflow.”
- Trust through taste and specificity.

**Motion/visual role:**

- Canvas/generative visuals create a world of technical creativity.
- Grid and node motifs imply saving, mapping, connecting, and organizing.
- Scroll pacing creates discovery rather than dumping all features instantly.

**Transfer to SlateCamp:**

SlateCamp needs the same category precision, but for LEA/ALE examinees:

- Not “study app.”
- Not “SRS platform.”
- Not “quizzing method.”
- Not “review center.”

Better category:

> **Architecture knowledge resource platform and supplementary review buddy.**

Save.design's verb pattern should be adapted into student outcomes:

- Look up.
- Understand.
- Practice.
- Repair.
- Review again.

Possible SlateCamp workflow line:

> “Look up the rule. See what it means. Test what stuck. Know what to fix next.”

**Three.js implication:** Make the hero visual feel like an organized workspace for architecture knowledge: sheets, dots, notes, guide lines, weak-topic markers, and resource nodes forming a coherent map.

### D. Atuin — sculptural object, human tone, section rhythm

**Observed core:** Atuin uses a huge, sculptural, likely WebGL-style form overlapping large typography. The site pairs technical competence with human warmth. It uses big type, simple labels, modular sections, color blocks, and a strong menu.

**Design intent:**

- Make a software studio feel technically capable but human-made.
- Use one sculptural object as a signature, not a literal service diagram.
- Alternate quiet hero space with energetic section rhythm.
- Keep navigation simple and confident.

**Emotional effect:**

- Confident.
- Playful but controlled.
- Technically credible.
- Human and approachable.
- Designed, not templated.

**Interaction/motion style:**

- The 3D object behaves like a digital sculpture/monument.
- Plus icons and menu interactions make discovery simple.
- Motion appears structural rather than decorative.

**Transfer to SlateCamp:**

SlateCamp can use a bold abstract Three.js object that overlaps the headline or sits behind it if readability is protected. It does not need to be a building. It can be:

- a folded blueprint ribbon;
- a hovering cluster of review sheets;
- an abstract section cut made of translucent planes;
- a glowing “knowledge core” surrounded by subject fragments;
- a spatial graph of exam topics;
- a calm field of dots that organizes into a path.

The important lesson is rhythm: the landing page should alternate modes:

1. quiet magical hero;
2. direct assurance proposition;
3. “why students feel lost” pain section;
4. “how SlateCamp helps” system section;
5. specific tools/resources;
6. review-center support positioning;
7. sample content/proof;
8. final CTA.

### E. OCI / Made by Buzzworthy — compliance anxiety converted into confidence

**Observed core:** OCI is about building codes, zoning, permits, approvals, and regulatory complexity. The site makes compliance feel premium and navigable through blueprint blue, giant type, tiny technical labels, grid discipline, and fear-to-assurance copy.

**Design intent:**

- Turn intimidating regulatory complexity into a guided process.
- Communicate fluency, accountability, speed, and confidence.
- Make code/zoning feel strategic, not bureaucratic.
- Use architectural drawing language as trust infrastructure.

**Emotional effect:**

- Authority.
- Technical calm.
- “They know the system.”
- Precision.
- Relief from regulatory uncertainty.

**Copy strategy:**

OCI names the anxiety indirectly, then answers it:

- navigating code, construction, compliance;
- eliminating delays;
- guiding projects with confidence;
- trustworthy, quick, accountable;
- turning agency hurdles into milestones.

**Transfer to SlateCamp:**

This is the strongest reference for SlateCamp's *psychology*.

For examinees, the fear is:

- “Ang daming aaralin.”
- “Hindi ko alam kung saan magsisimula.”
- “Baka outdated or unreliable ang source ko.”
- “Weak ako sa codes/utilities/HOA/structures.”
- “Late ko na madidiscover weak topics ko.”
- “May review center ako, pero kailangan ko ng buddy between sessions.”

SlateCamp's answer:

- organized subject map;
- source-backed wiki and summaries;
- quizzes and weak-area repair;
- 3D visuals for spatial/code concepts;
- practical calculators/tools;
- calm next actions;
- supplementary support beside review centers/school/books/mentors.

**Three.js implication:** The abstract visual should convert “exam chaos” into “mapped clarity.” It can begin as scattered points/sheets/rules and gently organize into a coherent architecture field.

### F. ArkitekWeb / Awwwards preview — premium restraint plus spatial drama

**Observed core:** The Awwwards page is a gallery wrapper, but the ArkitekWeb preview shows a dark, cinematic, architecture-adjacent web experience with abstract light forms, glossy materiality, and high-contrast typography. Tags indicate 3D, transitions, microinteractions, GSAP, Three.js, and Blender.

**Design intent:**

- Make the site feel like a crafted digital artifact.
- Use ambiguity, materiality, and light instead of literal explanation.
- Use a single abstract form as a signature.
- Pair dramatic visuals with minimalist editorial typography.

**Emotional effect:**

- Premium.
- Architectural.
- Spatial.
- High-craft.
- Slightly mysterious.

**Transfer to SlateCamp:**

SlateCamp can evoke architecture through:

- proportion;
- voids;
- light and shadow;
- planes;
- grids;
- sheet edges;
- material surfaces;
- coordinate markers;
- sections and fragments;
- slow spatial transitions.

It does **not** need to show a building, a lot, or a literal 3D lab interface in the hero.

## 3. Cross-site design principles

### Principle 1 — One iconic visual system

All strong references use one dominant visual idea. They do not put a dozen visual metaphors above the fold.

For SlateCamp:

- Choose one hero world: **architecture knowledge field**.
- Do not combine product screenshot + 3D model + feature cards + stats + multiple CTAs in the first viewport.

### Principle 2 — Abstract is valid if it carries meaning

The visual can be abstract as long as the metaphor is legible:

- scattered → organized;
- unclear → mapped;
- anxious → calm;
- isolated → guided;
- rules → visible knowledge;
- review chaos → field guide.

### Principle 3 — Copy must do the clarity work

If the visual is abstract, the copy cannot also be vague.

Good:

> “Stop studying blind.”
> “Look up the rule, see what it means, test what stuck, and know what to fix next.”

Bad:

> “A revolutionary learning-science-powered architecture review ecosystem.”

### Principle 4 — Motion must reduce anxiety

SlateCamp motion should not be spectacle for its own sake. It should show knowledge becoming clearer:

- dots align into subjects;
- notes sort into resources;
- weak areas glow softly;
- a path appears through exam topics;
- scattered rules resolve into a field guide.

### Principle 5 — Architecture cues should be native, not cliché

Use:

- dotted lines;
- dashed boundaries;
- title-block labels;
- sheet numbers;
- section markers;
- redline annotations;
- translucent planes;
- contour/grid fields;
- quiet material surfaces.

Avoid:

- generic blueprint wallpaper;
- stock skyline/building renders;
- spinning logo;
- CAD mini-tool in the hero;
- over-detailed compliance demo above the fold.

### Principle 6 — Fear marketing must end in comfort

SlateCamp can name anxiety, but must not exploit it.

Allowed:

> “The danger is discovering weak spots too late.”
> “SlateCamp helps you see what to repair next.”

Not allowed:

> “Failing is likely unless you use us.”
> “Guaranteed pass.”
> “Official board source.”

## 4. Recommended Three.js creative concepts

### Concept A — The Living Field Guide

**Visual:** A field of dots, sheets, and faint lines floats in a soft architectural space. At first it looks scattered. As the hero settles, the fragments align into subject zones: codes, HOA, utilities, BT, planning, structures, professional practice.

**Message:** SlateCamp turns scattered review materials into a navigable architecture field guide.

**Motion:** Slow alignment, soft parallax, subtle cursor gravity, no camera controls.

**Best for:** The Pokedex / atlas positioning.

---

### Concept B — Review Constellation

**Visual:** Star-like topic nodes hover over a dotted blueprint grid. Lines appear between related topics. A warm path moves from “forgotten” to “reviewed” to “repair next.”

**Message:** Know what stuck, what slipped, and what to study next.

**Motion:** Topics softly pulse; the route animates once; weak areas glow amber, repaired topics glow calm blue/green.

**Best for:** Assurance and weak-area repair.

---

### Concept C — The Floating Sheet Stack

**Visual:** Translucent review sheets / drawing plates float in 3D space. Each sheet has faint non-readable marks: title block, dotted guides, redlines, section bubbles. The stack gently organizes into a clean atlas.

**Message:** Source-backed architecture knowledge, organized like a drawing set.

**Motion:** Sheets drift, align, and settle. A few labels appear outside canvas in DOM.

**Best for:** Reliability and source-backed resource library.

---

### Concept D — Blueprint Aurora

**Visual:** A soft wave/aurora/ribbon made from dotted architectural grid lines flows behind the headline. It is abstract, magical, and light, but still drawn from plan-line language.

**Message:** Architecture knowledge can feel calm and alive, not buried in PDFs.

**Motion:** Very slow waveform. Reduced-motion users get a static gradient/grid render.

**Best for:** Premium emotional first impression.

---

### Concept E — From Chaos to Review Path

**Visual:** Small fragments representing notes, quizzes, rules, diagrams, and tools begin scattered. They gently snap into a path or map.

**Message:** Every study session gets a direction.

**Motion:** One-time load animation, then idle stillness.

**Best for:** Fear-to-assurance conversion.

## 5. Updated implementation posture — still no implementation yet

If Philip approves this direction, implementation should **not** start from the previous setback-slider component.

Instead, first choose one of the abstract concepts above and write a short art-direction spec:

- visual metaphor;
- geometry primitives;
- colors/tokens;
- motion behavior;
- DOM copy around it;
- reduced-motion fallback;
- performance budget;
- what it must not become.

### Recommended default

Best synthesis from all references:

> **Concept B + C hybrid: Review Constellation inside a Floating Sheet Stack.**

This combines:

- Infinite Field's one expressive atmospheric system;
- Matter's physical-world intelligence and short thesis;
- Save.design's technical workspace world;
- Atuin's sculptural confidence and section rhythm;
- OCI's fear-to-assurance / compliance confidence;
- ArkitekWeb's premium spatial drama.

### Hero copy paired with this concept

**Eyebrow:** Architecture knowledge atlas
**Headline:** Navigate architecture knowledge with confidence.
**Support:** Source-backed resources, visual explanations, quizzes, 3D labs, and practical tools for school, boards, and practice.
**CTA:** Open the atlas
**Quiet link:** Get a free readiness check

## 6. Updated acceptance criteria

### Must feel like

- Premium but approachable.
- Architectural but not literal.
- Magical but not fantasy.
- Reliable, calm, and comforting.
- A knowledge atlas / field guide / Pokedex-style review buddy.
- Built for Filipino LEA/ALE review needs.

### Must not feel like

- a 3D Lab tool embedded in the hero;
- a black video player card;
- a generic SaaS dashboard;
- an official PRC/PRBoA source;
- a review-center replacement;
- a lesson about SRS/active recall/quizzing methods;
- a decorative blob unrelated to architecture knowledge.

### First viewport must contain only

1. compact primary navigation;
2. one eyebrow;
3. one headline;
4. one short support sentence;
5. one primary CTA plus optional quiet link;
6. one abstract Three.js visual system.

Move below the fold:

- feature pillars;
- long review-center explanation;
- detailed legal disclaimers;
- multiple tool controls;
- dense stats;
- secondary nav if it crowds the hero.

---

## 7. Bottom-line recommendation

The winning direction is **not** “a floating architecture model.”

The winning direction is:

> **A calm, magical, abstract architecture-knowledge field that shows scattered board-exam material becoming organized, reliable, and reviewable.**

This is more ownable for SlateCamp and more aligned with the user's market:

- Students do not care that the product uses SRS.
- Students do not care that a hero widget can simulate setback violations.
- Students care that the exam feels less chaotic.
- Students care that knowledge feels reliable.
- Students care that they know what to study next.
- Students care that SlateCamp supports their main review system instead of pretending to replace it.

That is the design brief the landing page should serve.
