## 3. Verification of Previously Raised Concerns

### 3.1 BP 344: 1:12 maximum-gradient concern (Claude flag)

Claude raised concern that the 1:12 maximum for accessible ramps was missing from the exemplar, which could miscalibrate students. I investigated:

- The raw 2024 BP 344 PDF was searched for `1:12`. The **only occurrence** is in a context about *"swimming pool transfer wall clear transfer space"* — not the accessible ramp table (Table A.1.1).
- Table A.1.1 lists 1:20 (general, 15m landing interval) and 1:15 (special, 10.50m/700mm/10m interval). It does **not** list 1:12.
- **Verdict:** The 1:12 concern is a false lead for this PDF edition. The exemplar's 1:20/1:15 framing is correct. **No fix required.** The 1:20 general gradient + 1:15 special row with dimensional limits are faithful to the raw source.

**However** — I flag this as a hidden-assumption risk: if a different BP 344 edition or IRR printing includes 1:12 in the ramp table, students using mixed sources could be confused. The source ledger should explicitly record that *this PDF (2024 Revised IRR) does not include 1:12 in the accessible ramp table*, so future reviewers don't re-litigate this.

### 3.2 PD 1096: Eaves 750mm section-number concern (Claude flag)

Claude flagged that eaves ≥750mm from property lines might belong under Rule VIII (Light and Ventilation), not Rule VII Section 704. I checked the raw DPWH PDF:

- PDF p.81, under **SECTION 704. Location on Property**, subsection **1. General**, item **c**: *"Eaves over required windows shall not be less than 750 millimeters from the side and rear property lines."*
- This is **visibly and unambiguously** under Rule VII, Section 704.1(c).

**Verdict:** The Rule VII locator is correct. Claude's concern, while reasonable (eaves are often discussed under light/ventilation), does not hold against the raw DPWH 2004 IRR PDF. The claim-ledger locator (`Rule VII / Section 704.1(c) / PDF p.81`) is now precise and verified. **No fix required; locator already refined.**

### 3.3 UAP: uap-c4 85/15 + Article 1723 conflation (Claude flag — highest-risk claim)

Claude's single most important concern. I re-read the current claim-ledger:

> *"Under UAP Document 401-402 Article 7.08 on suspended or abandoned work, completion of the Contract Documents Phase is treated as 85% of the architect's work in that agreement; the remaining 15% is stated as 10% for Article 1723 liability and 5% for construction-phase service."*

And the raw source (PDF p.8 of UAP Document 401-402):

> *"If the Architect has prepared all of such documents, he has completed this Contract Documents Phase of the service, which is equivalent to EIGHTY FIVE PERCENT (85.0%) of his work. The remaining FIFTEEN PERCENT (15.0%) of his work is broken down as follows: a) TEN PERCENT (10.0%) for the Architect's liability under Art. 1723 of the New Civil Code; and b) FIVE PERCENT (5.0%) for the Architect's construction phase service..."*

**Verdict:** The current claim-ledger text is **faithful to the raw source** and properly scoped to *"Article 7.08 on suspended or abandoned work"* and *"in that agreement."* The critical guards are:
- The claim does **not** generalize the 85/15 split as a universal UAP fee-allocation rule.
- The claim is explicitly scoped to *"suspended or abandoned work"* context.
- The claim uses *"is treated as"* / *"is stated as"* rather than a prescriptive *"equals."*

**The fix applied is sufficient for calibration. I concur with the synthesis: this claim is calibration-safe.** That said, this claim remains the most easily-misread artifact in the entire corpus if a downstream content producer strips the Article 7.08 scoping. I recommend adding a `DO_NOT_GENERALIZE` guard comment in the claim-ledger notes field.
