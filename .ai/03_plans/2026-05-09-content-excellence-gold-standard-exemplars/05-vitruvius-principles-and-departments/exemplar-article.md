# Vitruvius: Principles versus Departments of Architecture

## Learning objective

Distinguish Vitruvius's fundamental principles of architecture from his departments of architecture, so an LEA theory stem does not trap you with the wrong list.

## The principles list

In Book I, Chapter II, Vitruvius states that architecture depends on Order, Arrangement, Eurythmy, Symmetry, Propriety, and Economy. He defines eurythmy as beauty and fitness in the adjustments of the members, and symmetry as the agreement between the members of the work and the relation between parts and the whole.

Source: Vitruvius, Ten Books on Architecture, translated by Morris Hicky Morgan, Book I, Chapter II, PDF pp.33-34, extracted from `vitruvius-connection/001_Raw-Source-Layer/002-History/Vitruvius - 10 Books of Architecture.pdf`.

## Economy is not just cheapness

Vitruvius describes economy as proper management of materials and site, with a thrifty balancing of cost and common sense in construction. In exam terms, do not reduce economy to minimum cost. It is fit use of resources under real site and supply constraints.

Source: Vitruvius, Ten Books on Architecture, Book I, Chapter II, PDF p.36.

## The departments list

In Book I, Chapter III, Vitruvius says there are three departments of architecture: the art of building, the making of time-pieces, and the construction of machinery. Building is then divided into fortified towns and public works on one side, and structures for private individuals on the other.

Source: Vitruvius, Ten Books on Architecture, Book I, Chapter III, PDF p.36.

## Board shortcut

When the stem says principles, think Order, Arrangement, Eurythmy, Symmetry, Propriety, Economy. When it says departments, think building, time-pieces, and machinery. The wrong answer is often a correct Vitruvian term attached to the wrong category.
