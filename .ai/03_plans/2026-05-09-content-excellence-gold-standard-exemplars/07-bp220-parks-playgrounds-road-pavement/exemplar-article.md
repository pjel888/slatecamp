# BP 220 Parks, Playgrounds, and Road Pavement

## Learning objective

Given an economic or socialized-housing subdivision question prompt, identify whether the issue is the hierarchy of basic settlement needs, parks and playgrounds allocation, minimum parks/playgrounds area, or road-pavement standard under the Revised IRR of BP 220. The numbers are simple only after you know which control is being tested.

## Governing source

Use the Revised IRR of BP 220, 2008. The hierarchy of basic needs appears in Rule II, PDF p.9. Parks and playgrounds allocation appears in Table 1, PDF p.12. Road pavement standards appear in Section 5.b.3.4, PDF p.17.

## Basic needs are listed in order

The Revised IRR lists the basic needs of human settlements in descending order as follows: water; movement and circulation; storm drainage; solid and liquid waste disposal; park/playground; and power. The same passage states that provision of these basic needs shall be based on the actual setting within which the project site is located.

Source: BP 220 Revised IRR 2008, Rule II, Basic Needs of Human Settlements, PDF p.9.

This order can be tested as a planning-priority question, not merely as a memory list. If the prompt asks which item comes first in the hierarchy, the answer is water. If it asks whether park/playground is above storm drainage, the answer is no. The hierarchy puts storm drainage before solid and liquid waste disposal, then park/playground, then power.

## Parks and playgrounds are density-linked

Table 1 allocates parks and playgrounds by density for both economic housing and socialized housing. For 150 lots or dwelling units per hectare and below, the allocation is 3.5% of gross area. The table then increases the allocation: 151-160 uses 4%; 161-175 uses 5%; 176-200 uses 6%; 201-225 uses 7%; and above 225 uses 9%.

Source: BP 220 Revised IRR 2008, Rule II, Table 1: Parks and Playgrounds Allocation, PDF p.12.

The trap is to memorize 3.5% as if it applies to every project. It applies only to the 150-and-below density band. When density rises, the required allocation rises. The table is also parallel for economic and socialized housing in the cited excerpt, so do not change the percentage simply because the prompt switches between those two project types.

## Minimum area is a separate floor

The same Table 1 note states that in no case shall an area allocated for parks and playgrounds be less than 100 square meters. It also states that there is an additional 1% increment for every 10, or fraction thereof, above 225. The parks and playgrounds area is also described as non-alienable and non-buildable for a community hall, but buildable for a basketball court, and it is exclusive of areas allocated for community facilities.

Source: BP 220 Revised IRR 2008, Rule II, Table 1 note, PDF p.12.

This means the computation has two layers. First, determine the percentage from the density band. Second, check the absolute floor of 100 square meters. For high-density projects above 225 lots or dwelling units per hectare, do not stop at 9%; check whether the additional 1% increment rule is triggered by the density stated in the prompt.

## Worked allocation example

If an economic-housing question gives 140 dwelling units per hectare and asks for the parks/playgrounds allocation percentage, use the 150-and-below row: 3.5% of gross area. If the same problem produces a computed area below 100 square meters, the minimum-area note prevents the area from dropping below 100 square meters. If the prompt gives 232 dwelling units per hectare, the “above 225” row and the 1% increment note become relevant.

For board-review work, write the density band and the area floor on separate scratch lines. A project can land in the correct percentage band and still fail the 100 square meter minimum, while a pavement question may have nothing to do with open-space allocation.

## Road pavement has material and performance values

Section 5.b.3.4 states that major roads, minor roads, and motor courts for economic and socialized housing projects shall be paved with asphalt with a minimum thickness of 50 mm, or concrete with a minimum thickness of 150 mm and a minimum compressive strength of 20.7 MPa at 28 days. It also states that sidewalks or alleys shall be of macadam finish.

Source: BP 220 Revised IRR 2008, Rule II, Section 5.b.3.4, PDF p.17.

Do not mix the asphalt and concrete values. Asphalt is tied to 50 mm minimum thickness. Concrete is tied to 150 mm minimum thickness and 20.7 MPa at 28 days. If the prompt asks for concrete performance, answering only 150 mm is incomplete because the cited line also gives compressive strength.

## Decision sequence

Use three questions before choosing a BP 220 answer. First, is the prompt asking about priority of basic needs? If yes, use the descending order list and start with water. Second, is it asking about parks/playgrounds allocation? If yes, find the density band, then check the 100 sq m minimum and the above-225 increment rule. Third, is it asking about road pavement? If yes, decide whether the material is asphalt or concrete before choosing the thickness and strength value.

## Exam traps

- **Trap 1: 3.5% is used for every density.** It is only the 150-and-below row.
- **Trap 2: the 100 sq m minimum is forgotten.** The table percentage does not eliminate the minimum area floor.
- **Trap 3: parks/playgrounds are confused with community facilities.** The cited excerpt treats them separately.
- **Trap 4: road-pavement material values are swapped.** Asphalt is 50 mm; concrete is 150 mm with 20.7 MPa at 28 days.
- **Trap 5: hierarchy order is ignored.** Water comes first in the listed basic needs.
