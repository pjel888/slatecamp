# UAP SPP 201 and Document 401-402: Pre-Design Is Not Basic Service

## Learning objective

Given a Professional Practice stem, distinguish pre-design services from regular/basic architectural services and from excluded/separately engaged services.

## The rule that decides the answer

SPP Document 201 is explicitly the standard of professional practice on Pre-design Services. It covers services before or outside the basic production of construction documents, including consultation, pre-feasibility studies, architectural programming, and space planning.

Source: UAP SPP Document 201, PDF pp.36, 42, 43, 48, and 49, extracted from `vitruvius-connection/001_Raw-Source-Layer/001-Laws/UAP/SPP 201.pdf`.

## Consultation is still professional service

SPP 201 describes consultation as giving oral or written advice and direction, attending conferences, making evaluations and appraisals, and similar activities. The service is valuable whether or not the client later pursues the project.

Source: UAP SPP Document 201, Consultation, PDF p.42.

## Architectural programming solves requirements before design

SPP 201 describes architectural programming as an analytical problem-seeking process leading to the statement and identification of horizontal and vertical requirements. It includes a space program with characterizations such as ambiance and cost range.

Source: UAP SPP Document 201, Architectural Programming, PDF p.48.

## Boundary trap: what the agreement does not cover

UAP Document 401-402 states that the agreement does not cover Fulltime Construction Supervision, Project or Construction Management, Pre-Design Services such as Space Planning and Research, and allied architectural design services such as Architectural Interiors or Site/Physical Planning. It is also not a Design-Build or Construction Agreement.

Source: UAP Document 401-402, Article 5/6 excerpt, PDF p.1, extracted from `vitruvius-connection/001_Raw-Source-Layer/001-Laws/UAP/UAP Document 401-402.pdf`.

## Compensation trap

In UAP Document 401-402 Article 7.08 on suspended or abandoned work, if the Architect has prepared all plans, specifications, and other project design documents, the Contract Documents Phase is treated as equivalent to 85% of the architect’s work in that agreement. The same Article 7.08 excerpt states that the remaining 15% is broken into 10% for the architect’s liability under Civil Code Article 1723 and 5% for construction-phase service.

Source: UAP Document 401-402, Article 7.08 / Article 8 context, PDF p.8. Scope note: this is the Article 7.08 suspended-or-abandoned-work allocation in the proforma agreement, not a standalone universal fee schedule.

## Board shortcut

For UAP SPP questions, classify the service before picking the fee or responsibility: pre-design, regular/basic design, fulltime supervision, construction management, design-build, or allied/specialized service. Many wrong answers are real services placed under the wrong agreement.
