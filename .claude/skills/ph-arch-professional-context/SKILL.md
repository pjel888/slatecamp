## The Philippine Architecture Professional Landscape

### Who Practices Architecture in the Philippines

**Registered Licensed Architects (RLAs):** Professionals who have passed the LEA and hold a valid PRC Certificate of Registration and Professional ID Card. As of recent PRC records, there are approximately 25,000–30,000 registered architects in the Philippines, though active practitioners are a smaller subset.

**Architecture Students and Fresh Graduates:** The primary SlateCamp market. Approximately 5,000–7,000 LEA candidates sit each exam cycle.

**Undergraduates (BSA):** Secondary market. ~15,000–20,000 students enrolled in BS Architecture programs nationally across ~100+ CHED-authorized schools.

---

### Where Filipino Architects Work

**Private Architectural Practice (majority)**
- Small architectural firms (2–10 people) — the most common practice structure in the Philippines
- Mid-size firms (10–50 people) — typically Metro Manila-based, serve commercial and institutional clients
- Large firms (50+ people) — rare; usually Joint Ventures (JV) with foreign architects on major projects
- Solo practitioners — common for residential and small commercial projects in provinces

**Government Practice**
- DPWH (Department of Public Works and Highways) — largest employer of government architects; handles national infrastructure
- DHSUD (Department of Human Settlements and Urban Development) — housing programs, subdivision regulation
- Local Government Units (LGU) — city/municipal architects, building officials
- State Universities and Colleges (SUCs) — campus facilities management
- Other agencies: BCDA, PCSO, NHA, DOH (hospital infrastructure)

**Specialty Practice Areas**
- Residential design (dominant by project volume)
- Commercial/retail (malls, mixed-use — significant in Metro Manila)
- Healthcare architecture (hospital design — growing sector)
- Educational facilities
- Industrial/warehouse
- Tourism and hospitality
- Government infrastructure

**Adjacent Professions (NOT architecture practice under RA 9266)**
- Interior design (requires separate RA 8534 license)
- Landscape architecture (requires separate RA 8549 license)
- Urban planning (requires separate RA 10587 license)
- Construction management (no separate architect license required, but architects practice this)

## The DAE (Diversified Architectural Experience) Reality

Understanding DAE is critical for professional practice content because it is the most commonly misunderstood requirement.

### What "Diversified" Actually Means

The IRR of RA 9266 requires "diversified" experience — meaning exposure to different phases or types of architectural work, not just 2 years at one job doing the same task repeatedly.

**What counts as diversified DAE in actual PRC practice:**

Accepted activities (any combination adding to 2 years):
- Drafting/technical drawing under a supervising RLA
- Design development work (schematic through working drawings)
- Site inspection and construction supervision
- Specification writing
- Cost estimation (with architectural context)
- Client coordination and programming
- Building permit processing
- Government architecture service

**What typically does NOT count:**
- Pure administrative or secretarial work at an architectural firm
- Interior design work only (unless in combination with architectural work)
- Construction work without architectural supervision
- Any work not certified by a Registered Licensed Architect

### DAE Documentation in Practice

The affidavit of experience must be signed and sealed by the supervising RLA. Common problems during PRC application:
- Supervising architect's PRC registration lapsed — the certification may be questioned
- Logbook not maintained contemporaneously — looks fabricated
- Only one employer listed — may be questioned as not "diversified"

**SlateCamp content implication:** Professional practice quiz questions about DAE should reflect these real-world complications, not just the clean statutory requirements.

## CPD (Continuing Professional Development) in Philippine Architecture

### CPD Requirements for Architects

After passing the LEA, architects must accumulate CPD credit units to renew their PRC Professional ID Card every 3 years.

**Required CPD units per renewal period:**
- 45 CPD credit units per 3-year compliance period (verify with current PRC rules — may have been updated)
- At least 15 units must be technical/professional (architectural content)
- Remaining units from ethics, management, or related topics

**CPD providers:**
- UAP (primary provider) — national conventions, seminars, workshops
- CHED-accredited universities
- Professional organizations
- PRC-accredited private CPD providers

**CPD activities that earn units:**
- Attending UAP national convention (NATCON) — typically 15–20 units
- Completing UAP-organized seminars
- Presenting papers or giving lectures
- Publishing technical articles
- Completing graduate-level courses

**SlateCamp opportunity:** Post-licensure, CPD is the continuing education need. SlateCamp's long-term B2B institutional play includes CPD-accredited content for licensed architects.

## Philippine Construction Industry Context

Understanding the construction industry helps ground SlateCamp professional practice content in reality.

### Key Industry Players

**Developers:** Ayala Land, SM Prime, Megaworld, Robinsons Land, Vista Land — drive the bulk of commercial and residential design commissions in the Philippines. These are the primary clients for large architectural firms.

**Contractors:** DMCI, EEI Corporation, Megawide, FILINVEST — major contractors that architecture students will interact with during construction phase services.

**Construction materials ecosystem:**
- Cement: Holcim, Lafarge (Eagle), Republic Cement — all CHB and RC construction uses these
- Steel: Pag-IBIG funded projects specify ASTM/AISC; government projects specify NSCP
- Prefab/modular: growing sector for socialized housing

**Permit processing reality:**
In Philippine practice, building permit processing is a significant professional service in itself. Processing times in local government offices vary enormously — Manila/NCR can take 3–6 months for complex projects. Provincial areas faster. This is why architects maintain relationships with Building Officials and why permit processing appears in SPP Doc. 202 as a reimbursable expense.

## Philippine Project Types and Their LEA Relevance

### What Projects Filipino Architects Actually Design

**High-volume / LEA-relevant:**
- Residential (houses, townhouses) — most common project type, basis for many Area C problems
- Low-cost housing (BP 220 projects) — relevant to planning section
- Condominiums — PD 957 requirements, vertical living
- Commercial buildings (shop, office, retail) — Area C standard program
- Schools (DepEd standards apply) — public schools follow DPWH standard designs

**Medium-volume / LEA-relevant:**
- Hospitals and health centers (DOH guidelines + NBCP Group D occupancy)
- Hotels and resorts (tourism sector — major in Cebu, Boracay, Palawan)
- Industrial buildings (economic zones in CALABARZON, Cebu, Davao)

**Low-volume / still LEA-tested:**
- Transportation facilities (airports, terminals) — Area C complex projects
- Religious buildings (churches, mosques) — assembly occupancy under fire code
- Heritage conservation (NHCP involvement, RA 10066)

## Professional Fees in Philippine Practice

Understanding how architects are actually compensated makes SPP content credible.

### Reality of Fee Collection in Philippine Practice

The SPP recommends percentage-based fees, but actual practice is more complex:

**For residential (small to medium scale):**
- Common range: 8–12% of total construction cost
- Small projects (under ₱3M TCC): sometimes flat fee or per sq.m. rate
- Negotiation is common — SPP fee schedule is a reference, not always a floor

**For commercial/institutional:**
- 5–8% for standard commercial
- Higher for complex programs (hospitals, laboratories, specialized facilities)
- Joint ventures with foreign architects on large projects: Filipino firm typically gets 30–50% of fee

**Common fee disputes (relevant for ethics and practice questions):**
- Client refuses to pay design fees after design is complete but before construction begins
- Contractor provides value engineering that architect did not approve
- Client makes changes during construction without architect's approval
- Architect signs and seals plans for a project they did not actually design (ethical violation + legal liability)

## Government Architecture Practice

A significant number of Filipino architects work in government — DPWH, LGUs, SUCs. Government practice has specific characteristics relevant to professional practice content:

**DPWH architects:**
- Must comply with DPWH standard designs for school buildings, health centers, public markets
- Building permits processed through DPWH regional offices for national projects
- COA (Commission on Audit) scrutiny of all costs — specifications must be defensible

**LGU-based Building Officials:**
- Must be a Registered Licensed Architect or Civil Engineer
- At least 5 years practice experience required
- Cannot simultaneously have private practice in their jurisdiction (conflict of interest)
- Responsible for enforcing PD 1096 at the local level

**DPWH Design-Build contracts:**
- Government's preferred procurement mode for infrastructure
- Architect of record is the winning contractor's design team — creates interesting accountability questions for professional practice exams
