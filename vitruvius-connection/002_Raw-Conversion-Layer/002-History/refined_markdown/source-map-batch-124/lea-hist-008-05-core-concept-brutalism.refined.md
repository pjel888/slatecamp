## Claim Map

- Britannica anchors Brutalism as mid-20th-century architecture characterized by raw concrete, bold geometric forms, utilitarian aesthetics, postwar social/institutional use, and decline in favor by the 1980s.
- Architectural Review anchors Reyner Banham's 1955 New Brutalism essay and 1966 book context as the critical codification surface; use as movement-history locator, not as article-body quotation.
- City of London/Barbican anchors the Barbican Estate to construction beginning in 1965, 11-year completion, Chamberlin, Powell and Bon, Grade II listing, 40-acre estate, raised podium, highwalk/estate elements, and bush-hammered concrete Brutalist qualities.
- Safdie Architects anchors Habitat 67 to 365 prefabricated concrete boxes/modules forming 158 residences and a pioneering prefabricated-housing model.
- Boston.gov anchors Boston City Hall to completion in 1968, Kallmann McKinnell and Knowles, Brutalist civic identity, concrete departure from Boston brick/brownstone, and democratic governance/accessibility intent.
- Kimbell Art Museum anchors Kahn's 1972 building to concrete vaults, natural light, travertine, white oak, and careful proportion; use as refined concrete modernism adjacent to Brutalist material questions, not as raw fortress Brutalism.
