## Safe Exam Claims

1. A bearing wall supports imposed loads and transmits compressive forces through the wall plane to the foundation.
2. Bearing walls should be continuous from floor to floor and vertically aligned from roof to foundation.
3. Masonry construction uses units of stone, brick, or concrete block with mortar to form durable, fire-resistant walls efficient in compression.
4. The most common structural masonry units are precast concrete masonry units or concrete block.
5. Standard CMU blocks have two or three cores.
6. Mortar joints vary from 1/4 inch to 1/2 inch, typically 3/8 inch or 10 mm.
7. Masonry bearing walls should be reinforced in seismic zones by embedding steel bars in thickened joints or cavities with grout for greater strength and lateral resistance.
8. A strong bond should develop between reinforcing steel, grout, and masonry units.
