## Safe Exam Claims

1. Structural steel girders, beams, trusses, and columns are used to construct skeleton frames, and steel is normally cut, shaped, and drilled in a fabrication shop.
2. Wide-flange W shapes have largely superseded classic S shapes, and beams may also be channels, structural tubing, or composite sections.
3. Steel framing is efficient when columns support a regular grid of girders, beams, and joists.
4. The general objective is to use the lightest steel section that resists bending and shear within allowable stress limits and without excessive deflection.
5. Lateral buckling can be induced by compressive stresses in a slender portion, and increasing steel beam flange width increases resistance to lateral buckling.
6. Connections usually use transitional elements such as steel angles, tees, or plates, and actual connections are more often bolted or welded.
7. Bolted connections are point connections, welded connections are line connections, pin joints allow rotation, and rigid or fixed joints restrain rotation and translation.
8. Steel column-beam connections can be made moment-resistant by bolting or welding beam flanges to the column.
9. Braced frames use vertical trusses with diagonal bracing to increase stiffness and moderate drift; they can be simply connected with pin joints.
10. Web stiffeners are required where concentrated loads might cripple joist webs, such as at joist ends or interior supports.
