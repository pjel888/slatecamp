## Safe Exam Claims

1. Structural diaphragms transmit inertial forces to lateral-force-resisting members; structural walls resist combinations of earthquake-induced shear, moment, and axial force.
2. The cited section applies to structural walls serving as earthquake-force-resisting parts and to diaphragms, struts, ties, chords, and collector members that transmit earthquake forces.
3. Structural wall reinforcement ratio is not less than 0.0025 along longitudinal and transverse directions, and spacing each way does not exceed 450 mm.
4. Reinforcement provided for shear strength is continuous and distributed across the shear plane.
5. At least two reinforcement layers are used in a wall if the assigned in-plane factored shear force exceeds the cited threshold.
6. Continuous reinforcement in structural walls, diaphragms, trusses, struts, ties, chords, and collectors is anchored or spliced according to reinforcement-in-tension provisions.
7. Boundary members are provided at wall and diaphragm boundaries and opening edges when the cited stress condition requires them, and required boundary members have transverse reinforcement.
8. Transverse reinforcement terminating at wall edges without boundary elements uses a standard hook engaging the edge reinforcement or equivalent U-stirrups, subject to the cited exception.
9. All construction joints in walls and diaphragms conform to the cited construction-joint provision, and contact surfaces are roughened as specified by the source.
