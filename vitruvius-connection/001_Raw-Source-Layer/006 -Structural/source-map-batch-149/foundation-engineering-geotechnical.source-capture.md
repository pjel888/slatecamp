Safe claims captured:
- Subsurface exploration provides ground stratigraphy, soil/rock properties, groundwater conditions, geotechnical parameters, and potential construction problems for foundation design.
- Foundation engineering checks both safety/stability and performance/serviceability; a stable structure can still be unusable if settlement is excessive.
- Soil behavior depends on density, void ratio, water content, effective stress, drainage condition, compressibility, strength, and permeability.
- Fine-grained/cohesive and coarse-grained/granular soils behave differently; clay and sand are examples used by the source.
- Groundwater level affects bearing capacity and settlement and should be observed during exploration.
- SPT is an in-situ testing and sampling method using a split-spoon sampler; the N value is the sum of blows for the last two 152.4 mm intervals, about 300 mm total penetration.
- Split-spoon samples can support basic physical property tests such as grain-size distribution and specific gravity.
- In saturated clay, consolidation is time-dependent as excess pore pressure dissipates and load transfers to the soil skeleton; secondary consolidation continues after primary consolidation.
- The captured Standard Proctor statement is limited to MSE backfill: lifts not more than 300 mm, compacted to 95 percent of maximum dry unit weight and within +2 percent of optimum water content.
