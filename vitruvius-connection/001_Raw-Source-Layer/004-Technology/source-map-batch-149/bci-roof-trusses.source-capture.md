Safe claims captured:
- A truss is a structural frame based on the rigidity of the triangle and uses linear members primarily in axial tension or compression.
- Top and bottom chords are principal members; web members connect upper and lower chords; panel points are joints between web members and chords.
- A truss should be loaded at panel points to keep members primarily axial; otherwise secondary stresses can develop.
- Purlins not bearing at panel points subject the top chord to local bending.
- Steel trusses are fabricated by welding or bolting angles and tees; connections usually use gusset plates.
- Warren, Belgian, Fink, Pratt, and Howe trusses are distinguished by web-member arrangement and tension/compression behavior.
- Heavier wood trusses may use layered members joined at panel points with split-ring connectors; loads should be applied at panel points.
- Vertical sway bracing, horizontal cross-bracing, lateral bracing, and manufacturer/engineer guidance are part of truss coordination.
- Wood trussed rafters are pre-engineered and shop-fabricated; truss manufacturer guidance governs configurations, allowable spans, loads, and construction details.
