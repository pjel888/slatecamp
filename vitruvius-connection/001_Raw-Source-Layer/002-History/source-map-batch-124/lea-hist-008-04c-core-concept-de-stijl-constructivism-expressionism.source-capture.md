## Captured Claim Anchors

- UNESCO anchors the Rietveld Schroder House as a 1924 Gerrit Rietveld work in Utrecht and a manifesto of De Stijl ideals, with flexible spatial arrangement and modern movement significance.
- AIP anchors the Einstein Tower as Erich Mendelsohn's first important work, designed/built 1919-1924, often called a main example of architectural Expressionism, and a functional solar observatory.
- Britannica anchors Tatlin's Monument to the Third International / Tatlin Tower as Vladimir Tatlin's famous abstract revolutionary monument project, commissioned in 1919 and exhibited as a model.
- Britannica/local anchors Constructivism to industrial materials, abstraction, and machine/technology/social-purpose commitments rather than De Stijl's orthogonal harmony.
- Moscow/heritage and local anchors support Melnikov House as a 1927-1929 avant-garde house-studio by Konstantin Melnikov with twin/intersecting cylindrical form; the article title is corrected from Futurism to Expressionism because the linked questions and source anchors test Expressionism.
